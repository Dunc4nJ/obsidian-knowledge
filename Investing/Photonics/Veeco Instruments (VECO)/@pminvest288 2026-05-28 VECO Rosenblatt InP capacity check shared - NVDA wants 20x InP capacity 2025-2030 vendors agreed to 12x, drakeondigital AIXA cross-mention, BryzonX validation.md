> **ROSENBLATT — Industry Update — May 28, 2026 — Optical**
> **Indium Phosphide Supply & Demand Model: Multi-Year Bullish With Scale Up CPO Timing Risks**
> *Mike Genovese, Comm and Cloud Infrastructure, (212) 607-3169, mgenovese@rblt.com*
>
> **Summary**
>
> Our proprietary Indium Phosphide (InP) supply and demand model suggests the companies making Electro-Absorption Modulated Lasers (EMLs) and Continuous Wave (CW) lasers for Optical transceivers and ultra-high power (UHP) CWs for Co Packaged Optics (CPO) are on track to increase capacity for these products by ~12x from 2025 to 2030. The companies', including Lumentum, Coherent, Broadcom, Sumitomo, Mitsubishi, and AAOI, lasers and photodiodes are produced from InP, a specialized compound material. Most InP manufacturing happens on 2-inch, 3-inch or 4-inch wafers, although 6-inch manufacturing has recently been increasing. Because InP requires a rare material, indium, and is difficult to grow, one 6-inch InP wafer for optical components costs thousands of dollars, or orders of magnitude more than a typical silicon 12-inch wafer used for computing applications.
>
> Optics are key to the distributed nature of the AI computing fabric. In other words, they are needed to create xPU clusters within and across Data Centers. Supply of InP-based Datacom components was ~50% behind demand exiting 2025. Our model shows that, under the base case scale up CPO timing scenario, Datacom demand will still be ~50% behind supply exiting 2030 after a 12x supply increase. This suggests an industry that will continue to grow in a non-cyclical manner well past 2030. Our concern is the scale up CPO supply chain has a lot of potential timing challenges, including wafer, tool and measurement, DSP, PIC, and laser capacity. If scale up CPO were to be delayed beyond the current 2H27 build, 2028 ship expectations, it would likely be a buying opportunity in industry leaders like LITE and FN as timing stretches but the total opportunity size continues increasing.
>
> In the near-term, our top picks include COHR for the revenue acceleration and stepped-up GMs expansion we expect to be driven by the company's increasing laser production on 6-inch wafers positively impacting 800G and 1.6T transceiver sales. We also like AAOI, which is positioned to grow into a nearly 10% transceiver market share player, from under 5%, and to participate in the UHP CW CPO laser market. Finally, we like VIAV for the underappreciated bottlenecks in OCS and CPO Test expertise and capacity. We are concerned that buy side GM expectations for CIEN have become too high and do not factor in price increases from suppliers LITE and COHR. Finally, we expect CRDO 1.6T AECs to be weaker than generally expected, and view the company as a niche, not strongly relevant, optical player.
>
> **Related Companies / Share Price**: AAOI $179.83 / CIEN $582.08 / COHR $380.18 / CRDO $221.23 / FN $686.90 / LITE $902.31 / VIAV $53.06
