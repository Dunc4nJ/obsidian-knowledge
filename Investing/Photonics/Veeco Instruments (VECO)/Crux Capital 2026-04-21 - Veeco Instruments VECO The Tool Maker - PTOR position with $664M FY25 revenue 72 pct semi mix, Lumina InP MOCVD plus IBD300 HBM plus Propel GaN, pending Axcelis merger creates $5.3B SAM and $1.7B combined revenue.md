# Veeco Instruments ($VECO): The Tool Maker

Crux Capital's April 21, 2026 thesis on [[Veeco Instruments (VECO)]] as a Process-Tool-of-Record (PTOR) semiconductor equipment maker positioned at the intersection of advanced logic, High Bandwidth Memory, optical components, and GaN power semiconductors. ## Original Content

Veeco Instruments is a semiconductor equipment company. It makes the specialized machines that chip manufacturers use to build the world's most advanced semiconductors, and it holds qualified positions at some of the largest and most sophisticated fabs on the planet.

Chipmakers need specialized tools to manufacture a chip, each one handling a different step in the process. Veeco builds the tools for some of the hardest and most precise of those steps. Once a tool like Veeco's gets approved and embedded into a production line, replacing it is expensive, risky, and rare. That stickiness is the foundation of the business.

What makes Veeco interesting right now is where those tools are showing up. Advanced logic chips, High Bandwidth Memory, optical components for data center connectivity, GaN-based power semiconductors.

These are some of the fastest-growing areas in the entire semiconductor industry.

### What Veeco Actually Does

Think of a semiconductor chip as a layer cake, except each layer is only a few atoms thick, and building each one requires a different specialized machine.

Some machines deposit materials. Some etch away material. Some blast the chip with precise amounts of energy to change how it behaves electrically. Veeco's tools handle some of the most critical and difficult of those steps.

There are three main areas where Veeco competes.

*Veeco slide — "Critical Role in Semi Manufacturing": Representative Process Steps from Front End (Deposition → Lithography → Etch → Ion Implantation → Annealing) to Back End (Inspection/Metrology → Advanced Packaging). VECO tag-products labelled below each step: Deposition = IBD 300, IBD EUV (Driving business today); Annealing = LSA (Laser Spike Annealing), NSA (Nanosecond Annealing); Advanced Packaging = Wet Processing, Litho (New products). Bottom band: "Veeco Technologies are Critical for Several Semi Manufacturing Process Steps." Footnote glossary: IBD = Ion beam Deposition; EUV = Extreme ultraviolet; LSA = Laser Spike Annealing; NSA = Nanosecond Annealing.*
![[cruxcapitalgroup-veco-the-tool-maker-002.png]]

**Annealing.** After certain layers of a chip are built, the structure needs to be "activated" using heat. The challenge is that the layers are incredibly thin, and applying too much heat for too long damages everything around them. Veeco's annealing tools solve this by delivering precise bursts of laser energy measured in fractions of a millisecond.

*Veeco slide — "Nanosecond Annealing Opportunity": cross-section temperature/time chart shows NSA (Future) heating at very-low-microns shallow vs LSA (sub-millisecond) at deeper layers vs Traditional Lamp Based (full wafer). Bullets: Scaling challenges driving need for new annealing capabilities; NSA broadening Laser Annealing adoption to new leading-edge Logic and Memory applications — shallow anneals enabling 3D devices, material modification steps to improve performance by changing device structure and properties; potential for additional logic HVM orders in 2026. Footer tag: "Nanosecond Annealing a substantial opportunity to expand to new leading-edge applications."*
![[cruxcapitalgroup-veco-the-tool-maker-003.png]]

Think of it like soldering something without melting everything next to it. Veeco holds what the industry calls Process Tool of Record status at top chip manufacturers, which means their tool is the qualified, approved, go-to solution for that step. Getting displaced from that position is very difficult once you are there.

**Ion Beam Deposition.** This is a process for depositing ultra-thin films of material onto a surface with exceptional precision. One of Veeco's most important positions here is in EUV mask blank deposition. EUV stands for Extreme Ultraviolet lithography, and it is the printing technology used to make the world's most advanced chips.

*Veeco slide — "IBD300 – 300mm Front End Semi Opportunity": Illustration of grain size and distribution: TODAY = Incumbent Technology (non-uniform, randomly oriented grains of small size = higher resistivity); FUTURE = IBD (uniformly oriented large grains = lowest resistivity). Bullets: Industry leader in Ion Beam Technology; Lower resistance metals essential for customer scaling and have direct impact on device performance, speed, and battery life; IBD achieving superior thin film properties vs incumbent technologies including lower resistivity; Ideal for advanced applications where low resistance is most critical. Footer band: "IBD achieving superior thin film properties versus incumbent technologies for advanced node applications."*
![[cruxcapitalgroup-veco-the-tool-maker-004.png]]

The "masks" used in EUV printing are essentially stencils, and Veeco's tools are used to build those stencils. A newer platform called the IBD300 targets a specific problem in AI memory chips, depositing metal layers that conduct electricity more efficiently, which translates directly to faster chips.

**MOCVD.** This stands for Metal Organic Chemical Vapor Deposition, which sounds complex but is essentially a way of growing very precise crystal structures out of gases. Veeco uses this to make equipment for two specific areas that are both tied directly to AI infrastructure, optical components and power efficiency. More on both of those below.

That is the setup, so let's dig into why we're talking about this now.

### The Photonics Connection

Optical components are often times built from a material called Indium Phosphide, or InP. And InP components are often grown using MOCVD equipment, specifically Veeco's Lumina platform.

[Image — Veeco March 2, 2026 press release screenshot; transcribed verbatim below.]

> PLAINVIEW, N.Y., March 02, 2026 (GLOBE NEWSWIRE) -- Veeco Instruments Inc. (NASDAQ: VECO) today announced that a global leader in optical components laser manufacturing placed orders for multiple Lumina® Metal Organic Chemical Vapor Deposition (MOCVD) systems and multiple Spector® Ion Beam Sputtering (IBD) Optical Coating systems. These tools will be used to fabricate indium phosphide (InP) lasers for innovative optical communication solutions in the datacom industry. This order establishes Lumina as the customer's production tool of record for InP epitaxy, while the Spector systems are used to deposit high-quality optical coatings on laser diode facets.

As AI clusters get bigger and demand more bandwidth between chips, more optical components get built. More optical components means more Lumina tools going into fabs. Veeco recently secured orders for multiple Lumina systems specifically tied to data center optical communication.

### The Power Efficiency Angle

Here is a problem that is really coming to the forefront as of late. Data centers are running out of power.

*Veeco slide — "Compound Semiconductor Projected SAM Growth": Total SAM $0.7B (2025) → ~$1.2B (2029), projected ~12% CAGR. GaN Power: $100M (2025) → $300M (2029) — Key Growth Drivers: Single wafer MOCVD platform for 200 and 300mm GaN wafer sizes; Shipped 300mm evaluation for GaN on Si Power Device customer to leading Power Device customer. Photonics: $150M (2025) → $400M (2029) — Drivers: Batch platform for distributed feedback (DFB) red MicroLED; Single wafer platform for disruptive Red, Green and Blue MicroLEDs on same wafer using GaN on Si; Batch platform for solar and other applications. Right-side bars show segments GaN / Photonics / RF & Other / Service.*
![[cruxcapitalgroup-veco-the-tool-maker-005.png]]

Not because the grid is broken, but because the amount of electricity that AI infrastructure demands is growing faster than utilities can supply it. Every watt matters. And a surprisingly large share of the electricity going into a data center never reaches the actual compute chips because it gets lost as heat during the conversion from grid power to the voltages the chips need.

A material called Gallium Nitride, or GaN, can help solve a significant part of this problem. GaN-based power components are much more efficient at converting electricity than the silicon components that are used today. Less energy wasted as heat means more energy available for compute.

Veeco's Propel platform makes GaN components at the 300mm wafer scale, which is the size needed for cost-effective high-volume manufacturing. In 2025, a major power semiconductor company placed an order for a pilot production line using Propel, with full-scale manufacturing expected in 2027.

### The Memory Angle

Every AI accelerator, whether it is an NVIDIA GPU or a custom chip from a hyperscaler, uses a type of memory called HBM, which stands for High Bandwidth Memory. HBM is what allows the chip to access data fast enough to keep the compute cores busy.

HBM is built by stacking multiple chips directly on top of each other, which is far more complicated than traditional flat chip manufacturing. The connections between the stacked layers have to carry enormous amounts of electrical signal, and making those connections as efficient as possible is one of the key engineering challenges in HBM manufacturing.

Veeco's IBD300 and annealing tools are specifically designed to address this. By depositing metal layers with a more uniform structure than competing technologies can achieve, they reduce electrical resistance in the interconnects, which means faster memory and less energy wasted.

Veeco recently shipped an evaluation system to a second HBM manufacturer. That means two of the world's leading HBM producers are now testing Veeco's tools in their fabs. If those evaluations convert to full production orders, the revenue impact is meaningful. Management estimates that a single application win at a memory customer with typical production volumes is worth $30 to $40 million in annual revenue. For context, Veeco's total 2025 revenue was $664 million.

In 2020, semiconductor-related revenue was 37% of the company's total. By 2025, that figure had reached 72%, or $477 million out of $664 million total. The company deliberately moved resources toward advanced logic, memory, and compound semiconductors and away from slower-moving legacy markets.

The headline 2025 revenue number looks slightly lower than 2024's $717 million, but the reason matters. The decline came almost entirely from China sales normalizing after an unusually strong 2024 cycle, not from any problem with the core business. Revenue from the rest of Asia, primarily Taiwan and Korea where the most advanced chip manufacturing happens, actually grew from $235 million to $330 million year over year. That is where Veeco's best customers and most important tool qualifications live.

### The Market Opportunity Is Getting Bigger

One of the strongest parts of the Veeco story is that the market it serves is expanding, not just because demand is growing, but because entirely new applications are creating demand for tools that did not exist at scale before.

*Veeco "Why own Veeco?" infographic — four positioning pillars: 1) Long-Term WFE Growth, 2) Enabling Technologies For Industry Inflections, 3) SAM Expansion to Deliver Outperformance, 4) Investment Strategy to Drive Long-Term Value.*
![[cruxcapitalgroup-veco-the-tool-maker-007.png]]

Veeco estimates its total served market will grow from roughly $2.5 billion in 2025 to roughly $4.4 billion in 2029. That is a 15% annual growth rate for the pool of revenue Veeco can go after.

The biggest drivers of that expansion are the nanosecond annealing market growing as 3D chip architectures become more common, the IBD300 market growing as HBM adoption accelerates, and the GaN power semiconductor market opening up as the industry scales to larger wafers.

### The Axcelis Merger

The biggest single event on Veeco's horizon is a pending merger with a company called Axcelis Technologies.

*Axcelis + Veeco merger slide — "Combining Capabilities to Create a Compelling Investment Opportunity": Axcelis provides fundamental steps in semiconductor device production, develops enabling process applications through design/manufacture/complete-lifecycle support of ion implantation systems; Veeco plays an integral role in fabrication and packaging of advanced semiconductor devices, offers laser annealing, ion beam, metal organic chemical vapor deposition (MOCVD), single wafer etch & clean and lithography technologies. Combined Company Highlights: $1.7 billion Revenue, 44% Gross Margin, ~$230 million Combined R&D Spend, $387 million Adj. EBITDA, >$900 million Combined Cash, 18% Combined Revenue CAGR (2019–2024).*
![[cruxcapitalgroup-veco-the-tool-maker-008.png]]

Axcelis makes ion implantation equipment. In plain terms, ion implantation is the process of firing atoms into a chip at precise locations and depths to change how it conducts electricity. It is performed dozens of times during the manufacturing of a single advanced chip, and it is one of the most foundational steps in the entire process. Axcelis is the leading independent company in this space.

If the merger closes, Veeco becomes a significantly larger and more diversified semiconductor equipment company. It gains exposure to a broader range of customers, a bigger installed base of tools already running in fabs, and a meaningful service and support revenue stream that comes with maintaining equipment over its lifetime.

*Axcelis + Veeco merger slide — "Expansion of Served Addressable Markets Across Technologies": Axcelis ~$2.9B (2024) Ion Implantation; Veeco ~$2.4B (2024) split across Wet Processing $0.5, Annealing $0.7, MOCVD $0.5, Ion Beam $0.4, Other $0.3 (numbers approximate from chart); Combined ~$5.3B (2024). Footer: "Well positioned within end markets that are expected to experience significant growth through 2030 driven by AI and power demand."*
![[cruxcapitalgroup-veco-the-tool-maker-009.png]]

Perhaps most importantly, the combined company has more ways to participate in every new fab buildout. When a chipmaker spends billions to build a new factory, they need tools from multiple equipment vendors. A larger Veeco with Axcelis's capabilities becomes a more essential partner in that process.

*Axcelis + Veeco merger slide — "Broad and Complementary Portfolio of Capital Equipment Solutions for Semiconductor Customers": Front end (Deposition: IBD300 low-resistivity metals, IBD EUV mask blank deposition and pellicle; Ion Implantation: Purion GSD; Annealing: LSA low thermal budget anneals, NSA), Back end (Lithography: AP300; Wet Processing: WaferStorm — "first choice for HBM & CoWoS"). Footer: "Combination creates a leader in Semiconductor Equipment across Implant, Annealing and Deposition products." Footnote glossary: IBD = Ion Beam Deposition; EUV = Extreme Ultraviolet; LSA = Laser Spike Annealing; NSA = Nanosecond Annealing.*
![[cruxcapitalgroup-veco-the-tool-maker-010.png]]

The merger is subject to regulatory approval and standard closing conditions. But if it clears, it materially changes the scale and scope of what Veeco is.
