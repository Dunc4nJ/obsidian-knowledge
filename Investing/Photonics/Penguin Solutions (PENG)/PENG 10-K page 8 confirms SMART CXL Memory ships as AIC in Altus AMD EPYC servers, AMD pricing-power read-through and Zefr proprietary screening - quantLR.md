## Key Takeaways

- **PENG's own 10-K names AMD EPYC explicitly as the host platform for SMART CXL memory.** Verbatim from page 8: *"SMART CXL Memory is available as a memory expansion add-in-card (AIC) in our Penguin Solutions Altus® AMD EPYC™ based servers, providing expansive memory for AI workloads."* This corrects a structural ambiguity in the prior thesis stack — until now the vault notes treated PENG's CXL play as platform-agnostic, but the 10-K language makes it specifically [[Advanced Micro Devices (AMD)]]-coupled at the server level. NVIDIA Grace/Blackwell GPUs sit on top of EPYC hosts; the CXL pool sits in the AIC slot of the EPYC server. The thesis dependency is **EPYC, not NVIDIA**.
- **Zefr is the moat-language-the-bulls-have-been-missing.** *"Our Integrated Memory segment also offers Zero Failure Rate ('Zefr®') memory modules… A Zefr memory module undergoes a rigid proprietary screening process which is performed on OEM original memory modules or SMART Modular Technologies memory modules to deliver ultra-high reliability for demanding workloads."* This is the **proprietary IP** that lets PENG charge integrator-plus margin on top of commodity DRAM/flash — quantLR's "premium pricing when generic supply is constrained" claim has a direct 10-K source. Any model that treats PENG's 31% gross margin as pure pass-through-distribution is wrong; Zefr is the reason the margin is structural, not arbitrage.
- **The supply-chain lift quantLR is calling**: AMD just printed strong (Q1 2026 earnings reportedly tonight per the Pennycheck reply: *"AMD commentary tonight on epyc!!"*). EPYC is in PENG's Altus servers. Specialty memory modules go into the same data-center / networking / HPC verticals AMD called out. **Tight memory supply rewards screened/packaged modules over generic** because hyperscalers can't risk fail-rate slip when DRAM is allocated. PENG benefits at three layers simultaneously: (a) Advanced Computing (Altus EPYC servers), (b) Integrated Memory (specialty modules), (c) MemoryAI/CXL (the AIC line item from the 10-K).
- **DDIMM and 256GB density flag is a roadmap signal**: the 10-K page also mentions "differential DIMMs ('DDIMM'), load reduced DIMMs, registered DIMMs, unbuffered DIMMs, small-outline DIMMs, and mini-DIMMs… up to 288 pins and densities of up to 256 gigabytes." DDIMM is the JEDEC-standard form factor for high-capacity server memory (think SK Hynix MRDIMM-class densities). 256 GB single-DIMM density puts PENG at the leading edge — same memory-capacity tier as the highest-end DDR5-RDIMM products SK Hynix and Samsung sell into hyperscalers.
- **quantLR's "2-3 innings of memory supercycle" calibration matters for sizing.** The QT'd memory take frames peak margin/pricing-power at 2028-2032, with current SanDisk +251% YoY revenue growth as proof that revenue is still in early innings of the up-cycle (supply relief 2-3 years out). If that timing read is correct, PENG's +65-75% Memory growth FY26 guide is sustainable through FY27-FY28 — which materially extends the runway the asymmetric trade has to play out. Counterpoint research's "late 2027 memory peak" call (cited in the [[PENG synthesized thesis - facts, business drivers, and dependencies across the five legs (IM cyclical, MemoryAI CXL, PMA photonic, Sovereign AI, Marvell-Encyclopedia earnout)|synthesized thesis]]) lines up directionally with quantLR's 2028-2032 peak window.

## Verbatim transcription of the screenshot

The image is a screenshot of the PENG Q4 2025 10-K viewed in a financial-document reader (Quartr-style interface — top breadcrumb reads "Penguin Solutions > Q4 2025 - 7 Oct, 2025"). Page 8 of 182, Annual report 10-K. The visible body text (continuing from a list of memory-module formats on the prior page):

> ('DDIMM'), load reduced DIMMs, registered DIMMs, unbuffered DIMMs, small-outline DIMMs, and mini-DIMMs for industrial, government, networking and communications, enterprise storage and computing and other vertical markets. These memory modules come in configurations of up to 288 pins and densities of up to 256 gigabytes. We utilize advanced printed circuit board and device packaging and stacking technologies to achieve cost-effective, high-density solutions. Our products are designed to meet the quality requirements of enterprise class systems pursuant to the stringent specifications required for various high-speed applications.
>
> Our Integrated Memory segment also offers Zero Failure Rate ("Zefr®") memory modules to support powerful HPC platforms that process massive amounts of data. A Zefr memory module undergoes a rigid proprietary screening process which is performed on OEM original memory modules or SMART Modular Technologies memory modules to deliver ultra-high reliability for demanding workloads. SMART Zefr aims to deliver the industry's highest levels of memory uptime and reliability and, for example, enhances the reliability and uptime of our Stratus ztC Endurance platform.
>
> Additionally, we support leading-edge and emerging interconnect standards such as Compute Express Link ("CXL"), an industry standard, open protocol for low latency GPU interconnects. SMART CXL Memory is available as a memory expansion add-in-card (AIC) in our Penguin Solutions Altus® AMD EPYC™ based servers, providing expansive memory for AI workloads.
>
> We also design and manufacture embedded and removable flash memory products in a variety of form factors and capacities, incorporated into storage and hybrid-integrated memory solutions in standard and rugged formats. Our flash memory products include solid-state drives ("SSDs"), Serial Advanced Technology Attachment (SATA) and PCIe NVMe products in 2.5" enclosures, M.2, EDSFF, and other module form factors. We also offer flash component products such as embedded MultiMediaCard and embedded and removable USB products, and SD and microSD Card configurations. Our flash memory capabilities include customized firmware development.

The third paragraph (CXL/Altus/AMD EPYC) is the load-bearing one — it is highlighted in the original screenshot. This is the verbatim 10-K language that proves the AMD EPYC dependency at the system level.

## Why this is positive for PENG, plainly

The note is short but the inference chain is meaningful. AMD's strong commentary on EPYC server demand (Q1 2026 print, May 5 evening) directly lifts the Advanced Computing segment of PENG that builds Altus EPYC servers. Independently, a tight memory-supply environment lets a value-added screening operator like SMART (with Zefr) charge a premium over commodity module sellers. **Both effects compound.** The 10-K screenshot is the proof that the dependency is structural and disclosed, not a thesis-time inference — when sell-side or institutional readers eventually run the AMD-PENG-CXL supply-chain screen, this is the page they'll cite.
