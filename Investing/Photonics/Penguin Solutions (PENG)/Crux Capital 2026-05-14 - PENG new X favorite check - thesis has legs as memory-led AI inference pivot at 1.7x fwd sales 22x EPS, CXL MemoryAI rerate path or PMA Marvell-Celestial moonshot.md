## Original Content

I keep getting asked about $PENG

And I understand why.

Penguin Solutions is genuinely confusing at first glance. You've got the AI infrastructure business, the memory business, the LED business, a CXL play, a photonic memory story, SK Telecom and SK hynix connections, NVIDIA and Dell ecosystem relationships, and a Celestial and Marvell angle developing in the background.

That is quite overwhelming, and I spend hours every day researching this stuff.

So this is my attempt to lay it out simply. Thesis map. Discussion framework. Not a full deep dive or recommendation. I only heard about this company for the first time last week. I could definitely be missing some things, so discussion here is welcome!

The main question I am trying to answer for myself is if $PENG is still being viewed like a memory and systems company while the company is actually trying to become an AI memory infrastructure platform.

That is the whole setup. Let's go.

---

## What even is Penguin?

I want to think of it as three businesses.

Advanced Computing is the part that helps customers build large AI and high-performance computing systems. When Penguin talks about AI factories, it means the full system needed to run AI at scale: servers, GPUs, CPUs, networking, storage, memory, software, deployment, and ongoing support. This is the "build and manage the AI system" part of the company.

Integrated Memory is the SMART Modular business. Memory is what computers use to store and move data while they are working. DRAM is fast working memory. Flash is storage. Specialty memory is built for more demanding use cases. This segment also includes the more interesting AI-related pieces: CXL and MemoryAI. CXL is a technology that helps CPUs and GPUs share memory more efficiently. MemoryAI is Penguin's product line built around that idea, especially for AI inference workloads that need fast access to lots of memory.

Optimized LED is the Cree LED business. Still generates revenue. Far less central to anything I care about here.

So the general map for me is: Advanced Computing builds the AI system. Integrated Memory supplies the memory expertise inside it. CXL, MemoryAI, and eventually the Photonic Memory Appliance are where those two pieces start coming together.

That overlap is why Penguin is harder to categorize than a normal AI server name. And that overlap is also the interesting part of the thesis.

## Why this is becoming relevant now

*The rest of this post is for paid subscribers. I dive into why PENG is relevant, what the current financial situation is like, how they are positioning, who they are partnered with, where I can see them going, what I am watching, and what my strategy is.*

The AI infrastructure story is shifting and I think this is underappreciated.

We are moving into the inference era. Inference is different from training because customers care about latency, responsiveness, context length, memory capacity, deployment speed, and uptime. Once AI moves from experimentation into production, the system around the GPU becomes more important.

Penguin's CEO said AI workloads are moving toward real-time inference and that demand is expanding beyond hyperscalers into enterprise, neocloud, and sovereign AI customers. He also said model training was largely compute-bound while inference and agentic AI are more memory-bound and latency-sensitive.

Here is a point that I hope hits home.

HBM gets most of the attention because it sits close to the GPU. But the full AI system also needs CPUs, DRAM, flash, CXL memory expansion, storage, networking, and software around the accelerator. Management is saying there is a lot more memory demand around AI systems than just the HBM attached to the GPU.

If inference workloads need more context, faster responses, and better GPU utilization, memory becomes a much bigger part of the AI infrastructure conversation. Training was the GPU buildout. Inference may become the memory and data-movement buildout.

Penguin is trying to position itself directly into that transition.

Management also said AI adoption should accelerate over the next year as more companies move from testing AI to deploying it inside their own operations. That fits the whole enterprise and sovereign AI angle.

## The current numbers

Q2 FY26 revenue was $343 million, down 6% year over year. Adjusted gross margin was 31.2%. Adjusted EPS was $0.52.

The segment mix is fascinating.

Advanced Computing did $116 million, 34% of revenue, down 42% year over year.

Integrated Memory did $172 million, 50% of revenue, up 63% year over year.

LED did $56 million, 16% of revenue, down 7%.

Down, up, down.

That the tension. The future narrative is AI infrastructure and memory architecture. The current financial strength is coming from Integrated Memory.

Penguin raised its full-year outlook but the raise was mainly driven by memory. FY26 revenue growth is now expected around 12% at the midpoint, with adjusted EPS around $2.15. Advanced Computing is guided down 15-25%. Memory is guided up 65-75%. LED is guided down 5-15%. Management also lowered full-year gross margin guidance to 28%, due to higher memory mix and higher memory costs inside AI hardware.

So today, $PENG is a memory-led AI infrastructure transition story. That is where I am at so far.

Look at this though. If you combine Advanced Computing and Integrated Memory, Penguin is already operating above a $1 billion annualized run-rate. I would treat that as a broad AI infrastructure and memory-adjacent run-rate rather than a clean pure-AI number. The cleaner proof point is that non-hyperscale AI and HPC revenue grew 50% year over year in the first half. So they already have scale. The question is whether that scale starts shifting toward higher-value AI memory infrastructure.

## Why Advanced Computing looks messy right now

Advanced Computing revenue looks bad, but some of that weakness is timing and mix rather than the thesis breaking.

Penguin is moving away from hyperscaler concentration and winding down the Penguin Edge business. That creates near-term drag. Management said the wind-down of Penguin Edge and the absence of prior hyperscale hardware sales create a major headwind to Advanced Computing growth this year.

At the same time the pieces they want to grow are showing better signals. First-half non-hyperscale AI and HPC revenue grew 50% year over year and represented more than 40% of Advanced Computing revenue, versus roughly 20% in the prior-year first half. New logos across financial services, biomedical research, and energy.

On timing, management said revenue can lag bookings by three to six months, and larger engagements can take 12 to 18 months from prospecting to design, award, contracting, build, and deployment.

So bookings are the leading indicator here. Management is saying bookings are strong but revenue recognition is delayed. If that is true, Advanced Computing could look very different over the next few quarters. If bookings weaken, the thesis changes. That distinction matters to me and I am watching it closely.

## The AI factory platform

Management described the AI factory platform as six pieces: ClusterWare, MemoryAI, Advanced Computing systems, OriginAI factory architectures, end-to-end services, and the partner ecosystem.

Penguin helps customers design, deploy, and manage their own AI factories while also bringing memory products into that stack. The upside is less about GPU access and more about becoming part of the infrastructure layer customers use to run inference.

The CEO background fits this pivot. Kash Shaikh came from Dell's AI and HPC infrastructure world and said he had competed against Penguin from the other side. He knows what enterprise customers need when they try to build production AI infrastructure. His excitement seems tied to the combination of memory and AI infrastructure, not a generic AI label.

A lot of the Advanced Computing business still has systems-integration DNA. Penguin helps customers take servers, GPUs, CPUs, networking, storage, software, and support and turn that into a working production system. That alone would deserve a lower multiple. The reason the story gets more interesting is that Penguin also owns the memory layer. CXL, MemoryAI, and eventually PMA are the pieces that could move the company from AI systems builder toward AI memory infrastructure platform.

## CXL and MemoryAI are the first real proof point

This is the piece I care about most near term.

Penguin launched MemoryAI and its CXL-based KV cache server. CXL, or Compute Express Link, creates shared memory between CPUs and GPUs. A KV cache stores inference context. When a model is answering a long prompt it keeps referring back to prior context. If that context sits closer to the compute the system responds faster. If it keeps reaching back to slower storage, latency goes up and efficiency goes down. That is why memory pooling and KV cache infrastructure are really important as inference grows.

Penguin's MemoryAI KV cache server supports up to 11TB of CXL-based memory and is designed for enterprise-scale AI inference.

And it is already getting customer activity. Penguin received a substantial CXL card order from a generative AI company building inference workloads. It also sold CXL-powered KV cache servers to a tier-one financial institution for an on-premise AI factory.

That's real. CXL is here now. MemoryAI is a real product line. Customer proof is early but it exists.

That is the key proof point for me. Management is saying MemoryAI gives them something differentiated in customer conversations, because it directly addresses the memory side of inference rather than only the compute side.

The CFO was asked whether CXL systems should carry higher margin than regular modules. He said yes, because it is more of a solution with software and differentiated hardware.

That's cool. The old memory business is cyclical and pricing-driven. MemoryAI and CXL could be more solution-driven. That is the bridge from commodity memory exposure toward AI infrastructure value capture.

## The PMA and Celestial / Marvell angle

This is the longer-term upside. And yes, this is the part everyone is getting excited about.

Penguin is developing a Photonic Memory Appliance, or PMA. The simplest version: CXL is electrical memory pooling. PMA is optical memory pooling.

Yay lasers. Moon it! Also, sigh. $POET.

Celestial AI developed photonic fabric technology designed to move data using light. Marvell acquired Celestial, which validates the importance of optical connectivity inside next-generation AI systems. Marvell owns the photonic fabric and silicon connectivity layer. Penguin appears to be working on the appliance and system layer around memory.

You ready for a really bad penguin analogy that might actually confuse you more? Great.

Think of the AI data center like a giant penguin colony. The GPUs are the penguins trying to work as fast as possible. The memory is the fish they keep needing to grab. Celestial and Marvell are building the high-speed ice tunnel that moves the fish around the colony. Penguin wants to build the giant fish warehouse connected to that tunnel, so all the penguins can access more fish faster and keep working without waiting around.

Got it? Yikes. Anyway…

On the call, Penguin confirmed it is continuing to advance PMA development in partnership with Celestial AI, now inside Marvell. Management said photonic connectivity increases the capacity to share memory and can take CXL to the next level.

This is the longer-term version of the same thesis. CXL helps CPUs and GPUs share memory electrically. PMA could help AI systems access a much larger pool of memory optically.

## The Celestial investment

Penguin was an early investor in Celestial AI. When Marvell acquired Celestial, Penguin received $10.5 million in cash and 281,834 Marvell shares valued at $22.2 million. Penguin later sold those shares and recorded a $27.5 million gain.

Nice. But backward-looking.

The better story is what comes next. Penguin has early conviction in photonic memory architecture on its track record. It has a stated PMA development effort. And management is directly tying that PMA work to Celestial, now inside Marvell. That is the part I care about.

## NVIDIA and the partner ecosystem

Penguin is working with the major platform players rather than competing with them. When asked about NVIDIA's own AI factory reference designs, management said they view them as an advantage, complementary to Penguin's full-stack platform, not a threat to it.

That gives the thesis a cleaner shape. Penguin is selling the design, integration, memory, software, and deployment layer around AI infrastructure. It benefits as enterprises and sovereign customers try to turn GPU capacity into real production systems.

---

## SK Telecom and SK hynix

Penguin signed a collaboration agreement with SK Telecom and SK hynix to pursue AI data center solutions, following SKT's $200 million strategic investment. SK hynix is one of the most important memory companies in the world. SK Telecom gives Penguin a sovereign AI channel. Penguin pointed to South Korea's Haein sovereign AI factory as one of its deployment examples, alongside Deepgram and Georgia Tech.

In a tight memory market, that relationship could be more than branding. Management has said the SK hynix relationship can help with access, and access to memory is becoming more important as AI systems scale.

## What could this become?

At around $48, Penguin has a market cap of roughly $2.6 billion. Management is guiding to roughly $1.5 billion of FY26 revenue at the midpoint, so the business trades around 1.7x forward sales and roughly 22x guided adjusted EPS.

Cheap or expensive depends entirely on what this becomes.

If Penguin remains mostly a memory-cycle company plus a lumpy AI systems integrator, the multiple probably stays more limited. In that version the company may grow into a $1.7 to $2.0 billion revenue base but the market values it like a memory and systems business. That is fine, not exciting.

The more interesting version is that CXL and MemoryAI become real product lines and Penguin starts looking more like an AI inference memory infrastructure company. That could support a larger revenue base and a better multiple, maybe $2.0 to $2.5 billion of revenue with the market applying a 2x to 3x sales multiple.

Then there is the moonshot. If PMA becomes real, Penguin has a role in the photonic memory appliance layer around Celestial and Marvell. In that case investors start asking whether this deserves to be valued like a differentiated AI memory infrastructure company. That is the version that gets genuinely interesting.

So the rerate question.... Does $PENG stay a 1.5x to 2x sales memory and systems company? Or does it become a 2x to 3x-plus sales AI memory infrastructure company?

## TLDR

AI inference is creating a new memory bottleneck. Enterprises, neoclouds, and sovereign AI customers need more than GPUs. They need compute, memory, networking, storage, software, deployment, and ongoing support. Penguin already has the AI and HPC deployment layer. Penguin already has the memory layer. CXL and MemoryAI connect those two pieces today. PMA and Celestial and Marvell create the longer-term photonic memory option. The SK relationships add strategic credibility on both the memory and sovereign AI sides.

That combination is unusual. The current business is imperfect. But the direction is promising.

I personally took a position before diving into this one. A few respected buddies pitched it to me and it sounded good enough for a starter. I will be looking to add on dips in the low 40's if we get there and the market looks constructive.
