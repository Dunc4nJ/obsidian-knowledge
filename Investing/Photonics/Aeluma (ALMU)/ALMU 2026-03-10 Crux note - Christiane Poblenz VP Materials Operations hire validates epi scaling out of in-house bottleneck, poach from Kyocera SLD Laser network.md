# ALMU 2026-03-10 — Crux note: Christiane Poblenz hire as VP Materials Operations validates epi scale-out from R&D bottleneck per Klamkin commentary

Crux's same-day reaction to [[Aeluma (ALMU)]] naming Christiane Poblenz as VP of Materials Operations to lead scaling of large-diameter epitaxial wafers. Frames the hire as real-time validation of the commercialization-transition narrative CEO Jonathan Klamkin laid out on the most recent earnings call — specifically that ALMU is moving its in-house epitaxial deposition step toward external high-volume epi houses, and that the company is hiring "high-level people" rather than technicians as it transitions from R&D to manufacturing.

## Key Takeaways

- **Epi bottleneck is the binding constraint**: on the recent earnings call CEO Klamkin called out that doing the highly complex compound-on-silicon epitaxial deposition step in-house is the hurdle to "infinite scale." Poblenz's press-release brief explicitly says she is being brought in "to lead scaling efforts for Aeluma's large-diameter epitaxial wafers." Direct match of hire-to-bottleneck.
- **Commercialization hires are arriving as promised**: Klamkin had said on the call that the caliber of candidates ALMU was interviewing was "astounding" and that they needed senior operators who could "run an operation transition to manufacturing and deliver product." Poblenz, with 25 years of semiconductor wafer scale-up experience including through acquisitions, fits that exact archetype.
- **Talent poach from the existing ecosystem**: Poblenz comes from Kyocera SLD Laser, where she was Senior Director of Epitaxy. Both Kyocera and SLD Laser are prominently logoed alongside UCSB in ALMU's investor-deck ecosystem map — signaling that ALMU is successfully pulling talent from inside its strategic partner network rather than competing in the broader compound-semi labor market.
- **Automotive qualification optionality**: the press release flags Poblenz led epi operations through "automotive qualification." ALMU's near-term focus is mobile / AI / defense, but the long-term roadmap includes automotive sensors for ADAS and robotaxis. Having an exec who has already navigated that brutal qualification process is a multi-year option on the auto vertical.
- **Long-duration view**: Crux frames this as a 2030-timeframe investment — meaningful incremental progress, not a near-term re-rate catalyst. Reads through as bridge-building between R&D narrative and commercial-scaling execution.

## Original Content

Today Aeluma (ALMU) announced it has named Christiane Poblenz as its Vice President of Materials Operations to lead scaling efforts for Aeluma's large-diameter epitaxial wafers.

This new update is a great piece of real-time validation for Aeluma. It perfectly aligns with everything the company's leadership stated in their recent earnings calls and directly addresses several other specific questions I had regarding their bottlenecks, talent, and scaling strategy.

Let's breakdown exactly why Dr. Christiane Poblenz's appointment is significant

### 1. Solving the Epitaxial Bottleneck

In their latest earnings call Klamkin (CEO) discussed how Aeluma currently does the highly complex epitaxial deposition (putting the compound materials on the silicon wafers) in-house, and that transferring this step to massive external epi houses is a hurdle to achieving infinite scale. The press release specifically notes that Dr. Poblenz is an expert in epitaxy and is being brought in to "lead scaling efforts for Aeluma's large-diameter epitaxial wafers." This confirms Aeluma is actively working towards the next big task of moving their core IP out of their small facility and into high-volume production.

### 2. Promise of "High-Level" Hires

During the earnings call, Klamkin explicitly stated that as they transition from R&D to commercialization, they are no longer just hiring technicians but they need "high-level people that know how to run an operation transition to manufacturing and deliver product". He noted that the caliber of candidates they were interviewing was "astounding". Dr. Poblenz, with 25 years of experience scaling semiconductor wafers to production and acquisition, is exactly the type of veteran "commercialization hire Klamkin promised they were bringing on.

### 3. A Deep Connection to Aeluma's Existing Ecosystem

The update notes she was previously the Senior Director of Epitaxy at Kyocera SLDLaser. If you look at Aeluma's investor presentation detailing their partnerships and ecosystem, the logos for both Kyocera and SLD Laser are prominently featured alongside UCSB. Her hiring suggests Aeluma is successfully poaching or transitioning top-tier talent directly from within its close-knit network of strategic partners. This is important because as Aeluma gets closer to large volume orders they are going to need to expand their staff in a meaningful way.

### 4. Automotive Qualification Experience

While Aeluma is currently focused on mobile, AI, and defense in the near term, their long-term roadmap includes Automotive sensors for advanced driver assistance systems and robotaxis. The press release specifically highlights that Dr. Poblenz led epitaxy operations through "automotive qualification." Her specific experience navigating the incredibly strict automotive qualification process will be invaluable when Aeluma eventually targets that vertical.

In short, this press release perfectly bridges the gap between the R&D narrative Aeluma has been pitching and the commercial scaling reality they need to execute today.

There is still plenty that needs to happen and this is a very long term story (for me personally it's a 2030 timeframe investment) but it is good to see them making progress with the hires.
