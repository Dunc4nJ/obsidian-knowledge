# GSR via jukan05 - InP bottleneck is 6N-7N red phosphorus not indium

## Key takeaways

- **GSR adds a deeper bottleneck layer to [[Crux Capital 2026-01-08 - The InP Crisis - AXTI 40 pct of global InP supply, 60-day China export permits, $100M raise to double capacity in 9 months, POET ALMU LWLG TFLN as substrate disruptors]].** The Crux note frames [[AXT (AXTI)]] and indium/InP substrate capacity as the visible choke point; this GSR/jukan05 read argues the upstream constraint is qualified high-purity red phosphorus used in InP crystal growth.
- **The scarce input is process-qualified 6N/7N phosphorus, not commodity indium.** The claim is that indium can be a sentiment amplifier, while phosphorus purity directly drives dislocation slip, crystal growth yield, device performance, and customer qualification.
- **[[Nippon Chemical Industrial (4092.T)]] and [[Rasa Industries (4022.T)]] are the named Japanese choke points.** Ticker verification corrected the initial NCI assumption: Nippon Chemical Industrial is Tokyo-listed as 4092.T, while Rasa Industries is Tokyo-listed as 4022.T. Both now have Photonics scaffolds because the investable thesis is compound-semiconductor feedstock for InP photonics.
- **This matters across the InP demand stack.** If phosphorus qualification is the real bottleneck, it feeds directly into InP wafer and laser availability for [[Coherent (COHR)]], [[Lumentum (LITE)]], [[Applied Optoelectronics (AAOI)]], and InP/silicon-photonic sensing paths such as [[Ouster (OUST)]].
- **The reply context pushes back against simple self-sufficiency narratives.** Follow-up replies flagged Chinese expansion claims, MOCVD/[[Aixtron (AIXA.DE)]] as another layer, and the risk that buyers who skip qualification audits may learn that not all nominal 7N phosphorus is actually qualified 7N.

The Hidden Chokepoint: 7N Red Phosphorus Monopoly

While the market fixates on indium price increases, a deeper bottleneck has been almost entirely overlooked. What truly grips the indium phosphide supply chain by the throat is not indium, but 7N (99.99999% purity) high-purity red phosphorus—a material with global annual production of only tens of tons, near-monopolized by Japan’s NCI and Rasa Industries.

Indium’s impact on substrate cost has been severely overestimated. One kilogram of 4N crude indium contributes only 5-6% or less to direct metal cost at the terminal device level. Even if indium prices double, the impact on optical module manufacturers’ overall BOM is negligible. Indium is an emotional amplifier, not where pricing power resides.

Phosphorus is the true pricing power anchor. Indium phosphide crystal growth demands extreme phosphorus purity—any minute impurity triggers dislocation slip and destroys device performance. Purifying red phosphorus from 6N to 7N requires reducing total impurities from 1 ppm to 0.1 ppm, an exponentially difficult process that requires specialized high-pressure sealing systems and ppt-level trace analysis capabilities.

Global supply of 6N and 7N semiconductor-grade red phosphorus is almost entirely controlled by Japan’s NCI and Rasa Industries. NCI has produced red phosphorus since 1928, with nearly a century of accumulated process barriers. Research indicates Chinese indium phosphide manufacturers’ annual imports of NCI high-purity red phosphorus are approximately 10 tons.
