# LightCounting May 2026 — 2026 is first year SiP transceivers exceed 50% of $40B market; optical-chip TAM 4x to $15B by 2031; only 10-15% of scale-up migrates to NPO/CPO by 2030-31

LightCounting's May 2026 newsletter "2026 Is the Year of Silicon Photonics and Indium Phosphide," teasing their full May 2026 report on Silicon Photonics, LPO/LRO and NPO/CPO. The newsletter body below is the free, ungated portion; the underlying report is subscriber-only (no free figures beyond the one chart shown). This sets a notably *more conservative* scale-up-optical ramp than the bull narrative — a useful counterweight to [[TrendForce 2026-03-11 - CPO penetration in AI data centers rises from ~0.5 pct (2026) to ~35 pct (2030) on NVIDIA scale-up optical interconnects at Rubin Ultra-Feynman]] and the [[SemiAnalysis CPO book argues co-packaged optics is central to scale-up not scale-out, with Nvidia CPO endpoints injected at Feynman ~2028 not Rubin Ultra]] thesis.

## Key Takeaways

- **SiP+InP quantification.** 2026 is the first year transceivers using silicon-photonics modulators exceed **50% of the total ~$40 billion transceiver market**. On the optical-*chip* layer, SiP was ~1/3 of all optical chips in 2025 and grows to **42% (~$6.3B) of the optical-chip market by 2031**; InP remains the largest segment — 58% in 2025 falling to **46% (~$6.9B) by 2031**, including continuing EML contribution. EMLs ([[Coherent (COHR)]], [[Lumentum (LITE)]] InP) may be displaced by SiP modulators, but InP CW lasers (for pluggables and CPO/NPO), tunable lasers, and InP PIC for coherent keep growing. NPO/CPO adoption — enabled by [[Broadcom (AVGO)]], [[Nvidia (NVDA)]], [[TSMC (TSM)]] — is now actively *boosting* InP CW-laser demand. Optical-chip TAM grows ~4x from $4B (2025) to $15B (2031); transceivers + AOCs + NPO/CPO reach ~$80B by 2031.

- **DSP-content compression dated to 2027-2031, NOT the next 12 months.** The contrarian read here is timing: LightCounting frames the SiP/LPO/CPO substitution that erodes DSP content as a **2027-2031 phenomenon as LPO/CPO volumes "bite"** — explicitly *not* 2026. The chart's optical-chip TAM milestone ($4B 2025 → $15B 2031) and the LPO/LRO and NPO/CPO categories are quantified over that window. Nothing in the free text dates DSP-content erosion to the next 12 months; 2026 is still the SiP-modulator crossover year, with the volume-driven DSP pressure landing later. (See [[IEEE EPS 2026 Linear Pluggable Optics overview - LPO names MACOM Semtech MaxLinear as key proponents, low-power DSP-less but late at 800G and ceding 1.6T to LRO]] for the DSP-less LPO mechanism — relevant to [[MaxLinear (MXL)]], [[MACOM Technology (MTSI)]], [[Semtech (SMTC)]].)

- **Conservative scale-up assumption: only 10-15% migrates to NPO/CPO by 2030-31.** The headline upside-case caveat: "Most of the market growth is expected from applications of NPO/CPO in scale-up networks, yet this forecast is based on an assumption that **only 10-15% of all scale-up interconnects will migrate to NPO/CPO by 2030-2031**." LightCounting also assumes scale-up networks **keep copper within racks**, using optics only for multi-rack connections — same copper-survives-within-rack logic as the TrendForce/Broadcom view. This 10-15% scale-up migration is materially below the implied bull ramp and bounds the near-term CPO TAM.

- **Where the upside lives (and how to monitor it).** LightCounting flags three named triggers that would break the forecast to the upside: (1) multi-rack scale-up systems become widely adopted; (2) scale-up domains extend well beyond 1,000 GPUs; (3) scale-up networks start using optics *within* a rack instead of copper. These are the exact watch-items for anyone long the optical scale-up thesis — the bull case requires at least one to land before ~2030.

- **Pricing turned stable on shortages.** Notably, LightCounting *raised* its pricing assumptions: price declines are "minimal now because of the shortages," with prices "fairly stable in 2026-2027 unless the industry swings from shortages to a glut" — corroborating the laser/InP lead-time tightness in [[TrendForce - 800G-plus transceiver shipments jump 2.6x to ~63M units in 2026 as NVIDIA EML lock-in extends laser lead times beyond 2027]] and [[Crux Capital 2026-01-08 - The InP Crisis - AXTI 40 pct of global InP supply, 60-day China export permits, $100M raise to double capacity in 9 months, POET ALMU LWLG TFLN as substrate disruptors]].

> # LightTrends Newsletter

> ### 2026 Is the Year of Silicon Photonics and Indium Phosphide
>
> May 2026
>
> _LightCounting releases May 2026 report on Silicon Photonics, LPO/LRO and NPO/CPO_
>
> 2026 will be the first year for sales of transceivers using silicon photonics modulators to exceed 50% of the total $40 billion market. Despite many advantages of silicon photonics technology, it took almost a decade for it to make a significant impact on the optical transceiver market. Decisions made by several large companies, including Cisco, Huawei and Intel, helped to accelerate adoption of silicon photonics in the past. Deployments of Near-Packaged and Co-Packaged Optics (NPO/CPO) are accelerating adoption of silicon photonics now and boosting demand for InP CW lasers. Once again, several very large companies, including Broadcom, Nvidia and TSMC, are enabling this transition.
>
> Figure below shows our estimate for the market value of optical chips used in transceivers, AOCs, LPO, LRO, NPO and CPO. The market size was modest $4 billion in 2025, but it will grow by almost 4x to $15 billion in 2031. Sales of optical transceivers, AOCs, NPO and CPO will reach almost $80 billion by 2031.
>
> Silicon photonics chips accounted for 1/3 of all in 2025 and it will be contributing 42% (or $6.3 billion) to the total market for optical chips in 2031. The growth will continue well beyond 2031, as optical interconnects will proliferate from scale-out networks today to higher bandwidth scale-up networks before the end of this decade and to scale-in (networks inside a package for interconnects between chiplets) by the middle of the next decade. This is why the largest CMOS foundries like Samsung and TSMC have entered the silicon photonics market. It is not the $6.3 billion market for SiP chips by 2031 that attracts them, it is the path of the entire multi-trillion dollar semiconductor industry that crossed over from copper to optical interconnects. We are in the very early stages of this transition.
>
> *Figure: Sales of optical chips used in transceivers, AOCs, LPO/LRO and NPO/CPO by technology. 2025 ($4 billion): Silicon Photonics 32%, InP 58%, GaAs 10%. 2031 ($15 billion): Silicon Photonics 42%, InP 46%, GaAs 3%, TFLN/LiNbO3 bulk and other 9%. (Source: LightCounting)*
> ![[lightcounting-may2026-sip-001.png]]
>
> Progress in silicon photonics will create opportunities for other optical materials and technologies. InP modulators (EMLs) may be replaced by silicon photonics, but the market for InP lasers, including CW lasers for pluggable transceivers and CPO/NPO as well as tunable lasers and InP PIC for coherent transceivers, will continue to grow. It is the largest segment of the optical chip market: 58% of the total in 2025 and it will still account for 46% (or $6.9 billion) of the total by 2031, including continuing contribution from EMLs.
>
> This forecast now accounts for a significant consumption of lasers for development of new products, such as NPO/CPO, as well as limited production yields. We also increased our assumptions for pricing since the price declines are minimal now because of the shortages. We expect that prices will be fairly stable in 2026-2027 unless the industry swings from shortages to a glut of production capacity, which will probably happen at some point in the future.
>
> There is an upside to this forecast as well. Most of the market growth is expected from applications of NPO/CPO in scale-up networks, yet this forecast is based on an assumption that only 10-15% of all scale-up interconnects will migrate to NPO/CPO by 2030-2031. We also expect that the scale-up networks will continue to rely on copper interconnects within the racks and the optical interconnects will be only used for multi-rack connections.
>
> There is a significant upside to our forecast if and when:
> - multi-rack scale-up systems become widely adopted
> - the scale-up domains extend well beyond 1,000 GPUs
> - scale-up networks start using optical interconnects within a rack, instead of copper.
