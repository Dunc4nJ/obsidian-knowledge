## Key Takeaways

- Goldman's published CPO map (covered in [[Goldman 2026 AI optical cheat sheet maps EPS upside across lasers PCBs and CCL manufacturers]]) shows the **finished structure** — GPU, Switch ASIC, CPO chip, Optical engine, Laser sources — but **omits the foundational layers underneath**: substrate wafers, epitaxial growth, and the equipment that makes both possible. Per Sancet, that's where the asymmetric upside lives because those layers are smaller-cap, less covered, and equally indispensable.

- The **substrate layer** is the most under-priced gap. [[Soitec (SOI.PA)]] makes the SOI wafers under every photonic chip TSMC fabs for [[Nvidia (NVDA)]]; [[Tower Semiconductor (TSEM)]] is "the leading independent photonics foundry, currently tripling capacity" yet missing from Goldman's table; [[IQE (IQE.L)]] (~$400M cap) supplies the compound-semi epitaxial layers that lasers are grown on. Sancet flags IQE's market cap vs Sumitomo's $52B as a 50x asymmetry — same value chain position, vastly different price tags.

- The **equipment layer** is a higher-quality moat than Goldman implies. [[Aixtron (AIXA.DE)]]'s MOCVD reactors are used by EVERY listed laser maker ([[Sumitomo Electric (SMTOY)]], [[Lumentum (LITE)]], VPEC) — that's a true picks-and-shovels position. [[Aehr Test Systems (AEHR)]] handles wafer-level burn-in for compound-semi devices (essential, no real substitute). [[LPKF Laser (LPK.DE)]] enables the glass-based optical integration via LIDE tech that's emerging as a CPO packaging path.

- The **next-gen laser layer**: Sancet flags [[Sivers Semiconductors (SIVE.ST)]] as the InP laser maker positioned for silicon-photonics integration — omitted from Goldman's cheat sheet despite being a clear plays-the-laser-shift name.

- The **supercycle math** Sancet leans on: Goldman projects optical-networking TAM going from **$15B (GB300 NVL72, mainly 2026) → $154B (Rubin Ultra NVL576, mainly 2028)**, ~9x. CPO contributes ~$91B of that ($59% of the $154B), assuming 29% CPO penetration in scale-out. If those numbers hold, the omitted-layer companies above ride the same wave with smaller-cap leverage. Goldman's own data, in other words, supports the pitch — they just didn't tag the picks.
