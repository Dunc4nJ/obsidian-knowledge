# 2026-06-09 CPO-delay dispute — SemiAnalysis report sinks optical names then NVIDIA's Shainer rebuts switch-side delays while leaving the GPU-endpoint CPO thesis intact

On 2026-06-09 a SemiAnalysis note distributed to institutional clients — flagging that the rollout of co-packaged optics (CPO) would likely be delayed — sent the optical-networking complex sharply lower. Hours later, NVIDIA SVP of networking Gilad Shainer, interviewed at GTC/Computex in Taipei, said there would be "no delays" and that Spectrum-X Ethernet CPO switches enter mass production and customer delivery in 2H 2026. The crucial nuance for photonics-component investors: **Shainer's rebuttal is about SWITCH-side CPO**, while the SemiAnalysis concern that actually matters for DSP/transceiver vendors is **GPU-side ENDPOINT CPO** (NVIDIA's Feynman generation, ~2028). The rebuttal therefore *qualifies* rather than *contradicts* the extended-pluggable-window thesis — pluggable optics (and their DSPs) keep running through the endpoint until Feynman-class CPO arrives at the GPU.

## Key Takeaways

- **(a) What NVIDIA disputed — switch-side Spectrum-X CPO, "no delays," 2H 2026 ramp.** Per GuruFocus, the rebuttal targets "claims of delays in the delivery of its next-generation products, particularly the Spectrum-X Ethernet CPO switches. Shainer emphasized that there will be no delays, with mass production and customer deliveries expected to begin in the second half of 2026." Shainer drew "the distinction between small-scale commercial validation and large-scale network replacements, assuring stakeholders that small-scale introductions for supercomputing clients will commence in late 2026, followed by gradual expansion in 2027." The independent confirmation came from Tae Kim, who interviewed Shainer at Computex and reported the executive "did not indicate any delays at all regarding CPO shipments." This is consistent with [[Nvidia (NVDA)]]'s prior staging of optical NVLink and Spectrum-X photonics on its 2026–2028 roadmap.

- **(b) The scope distinction that DSP/transceiver vendors should care about — switch CPO vs GPU-endpoint CPO.** NVIDIA's rebuttal addresses the SWITCH side (Spectrum-X Ethernet, Quantum InfiniBand photonics switches) ramping 2H 2026. The SemiAnalysis worry that bears on [[Marvell Technology (MRVL)]], [[MaxLinear (MXL)]], [[Coherent (COHR)]] and [[Lumentum (LITE)]] is the GPU/XPU ENDPOINT going co-packaged — which per the SemiAnalysis CPO Book lands with the Feynman generation (~2028) via the NVL1152 ("all CPO" per Jensen Huang). So long as the endpoint stays pluggable, the DSP-bearing transceiver socket persists. Shainer confirming switch-side CPO is on time does NOT pull GPU-endpoint CPO forward; if anything it leaves the extended-pluggable-window thesis intact — the rebuttal qualifies the bear case, it does not refute it. This is the same distance-time-window framing in [[PhotonCap March 2026 - NVDA's $4B COHR-LITE bet and Broadcom Hock Tan's copper-within-rack view solve different distance-time windows (0-2m 2026-27 vs cross-rack 2028-30) not opposite answers]].

- **(c) The 2026-06-09 selloff magnitudes.** [[Applied Optoelectronics (AAOI)]] -14% (afternoon trading, leading the group); [[Coherent (COHR)]] -11%; [[Marvell Technology (MRVL)]] -9%; [[Corning (GLW)]] -9%; [[Lumentum (LITE)]] -8%; [[Ciena (CIEN)]] -7%; ANET -3%; [[Broadcom (AVGO)]] -1.5%. The DSP-vendor name the coverage explicitly tagged with a large move was MRVL (-9%), not MXL — [[MaxLinear (MXL)]] is exposed to the same TIA/DSP-socket thesis but its specific same-day move was not cleanly attributable to the CPO report in these sources (it was tangled with a same-week auditor-switch / shelf-registration story). The synchronized cross-name decline points to a sector-wide CPO-timing scare rather than company-specific news.

- **(d) What the June SemiAnalysis report reiterated vs its January CPO Book.** The June 2026 institutional note re-asserted the core position SemiAnalysis laid out in its January 2026 "Co-Packaged Optics (CPO) Book – Scaling with Light for the Next Wave of Interconnect": CPO is the eventual interconnect end-state, but the transition is gated and the near/mid-term socket stays pluggable (DSP transceivers vs LPO vs NPO vs CPO). The June reiteration applied that to current rollout timing — i.e., the *delay* framing — which the market read as bearish for pluggable-transceiver bulls on a too-fast-CPO reading, even though the Book's own thesis (endpoint CPO arriving with Feynman ~2028) is the bullish case for the extended pluggable window. The selloff was arguably the market mis-pricing a "CPO is delayed" headline as "optical demand is delayed."
