### 1. $LPK

LPKF Laser & Electronics is a German laser-equipment company with exposure across electronics manufacturing, development tools, laser plastic welding, solar equipment, and advanced semiconductor packaging.

I recently wrote an article on them here, stating that I took an initial position:

The part of the story getting the most attention is LIDE, or Laser-Induced Deep Etching. If you have seen this company on X, this is the bit that people are talking about. LIDE is used for high-precision glass processing, including through-glass vias and glass-core substrate applications. That puts LPKF directly in the advanced-packaging discussion as the industry explores glass substrates for future AI and high-performance computing packages.

Current financials are weak, but the upside case is tied to whether glass-based advanced packaging moves from qualification into production.

---

#### Q1 2026 snapshot

LPKF's Q1 was weak on the P&L but broadly in line with management's plan. Revenue declined 32% year over year, mainly because Solar is going through a planned weak year as the market transitions toward perovskite technology. Adjusted EBIT declined 68% year over year, but management emphasized that the North Star transformation program already softened the earnings impact from the lower revenue base. Order intake was the better part of the print, with a book-to-bill ratio above 1.4x and stronger demand in Development and Electronics.

It's important to understand that for many investors (myself included) this is a story stock right now. What that means is that current revenue and earnings etc. are not part of the upside thesis. The focus is on the optionality of their strong positioning in future architectures.

But the main message from the quarter is that reported earnings remain pressured, while the advanced-packaging commentary keeps improving. Management said the business is progressing as expected, LPKF is already being used for prototyping at multiple semiconductor customers, and Q1 brought a first capacity-expansion order from a Korean customer. The company is also in advanced talks with several customers for production ramp-up orders.

---

#### Overarching takeaway

LPKF's call was about moving from technology validation to production validation.

Management said the "positioning phase" in advanced packaging is basically over. The company has already established itself as a qualification partner for a large share of relevant customers. The focus is now shifting toward winning true production orders. That is the milestone we need to watch. LPKF has already shown that customers are testing the technology. Now they need to prove customers will buy production-scale systems.

The strongest validation point from the call was that over 80% of relevant advanced-packaging customers have bought LPKF equipment to qualify glass processes and improve yield. That is a lot! Management also said four or five players are now at a technical and yield level where they can start making first ramp-up investments. That makes the story more tangible for sure. LPKF is still early in revenue conversion obviously, but they are already embedded in customer qualification flows.

---

#### Advanced packaging

The biggest change is that LPKF is moving closer to customer capex. Management said 2026 is the year where the first capex for true production purposes should be positioned. The Korean capacity-expansion order is the first clear data point, and they are in advanced discussions with several other customers for production ramp-up orders.

LPKF is also trying to avoid being a one-product story. Management repeatedly described LIDE as the entry ticket into advanced packaging. The company now wants to broaden its role across the glass process chain. It is offering additional process steps, including glass singulation, ABF-layer ablation, glass-glass bonding, and glass-silicon bonding.

The broader product family is called Nexa and it includes LIDE systems, Tensor Ablation, and Tensor Bonding. The strategic goal is to make LPKF the company customers call whenever glass enters the advanced-packaging roadmap. So the upside case improves if LPKF can capture multiple tools or steps in the process flow rather than only one LIDE system.

---

#### Timing: ramp-up versus high volume

The timeline is really important for a story stock.

LPKF sees 2026 as the year for first production-ramp purchase orders. Those orders would install and ramp through 2027 and 2028. Management expects customers to need roughly a year to reach target yields before moving into true high-volume orders. The high-volume phase is framed around 2029 and 2030, with purchase orders typically arriving six to nine months before revenue conversion. So this should really give some clarity around what the investment landscape looks around this idea. More on that later.

Tensor Ablation could see production-related purchase orders in 2026. Tensor Bonding appears slightly later, with true production sales more likely in 2027. Co-packaged optics (CPO) is further out. Management is still in market-assessment mode there, with positioning expected in 2027–2028 and ramp timing more around 2029–2030.

So, to reiterate, LPK is less about immediate revenue acceleration and more about future production-option value. It can receive important order validation this year, but the larger revenue inflection appears more tied to 2027–2030.

---

#### TAM and demand read

LPKF's previously referenced TAM for LIDE / glass structuring was around €0.5B. Management now says that number likely needs to move higher because AI-related wafer-start assumptions are being revised upward. They have not yet disclosed the updated TAM. Separately, Tensor Ablation could add roughly €100M of addressable market, while Tensor Bonding and co-packaged optics remain unquantified upside layers.

---

#### Segment read

Advanced packaging remains the strategic driver. Management believes LPKF has a multi-year head start in equipment maturity, with several dozen tools already in the field and years of performance data. They also say their IP position is visible in the final product, which makes enforcement easier. Management noted that some Chinese competitors may be infringing on its IP, and LPKF has already taken legal action where necessary.

Development had a strong Q1. Demand improved in rapid prototyping, helped by Asia and defense-related demand. Management also said some delayed 2025 projects are now converting into orders.

Electronics saw stronger order activity, especially in SMT. Management pointed to a gradual easing of prior investment restraint and delayed 2025 demand moving into 2026.

Welding remains challenged by weak automotive demand, but they received a bulk order from Smart Robotics and believes the restructuring of that business can support a return to growth.

Solar is the main drag. The old cadmium-telluride investment cycle has faded, while perovskite high-volume equipment demand is still early. Management views 2026 as a weak transition year, but sees perovskites as a long-term opportunity.

---

#### North Star transformation

North Star is LPKF's restructuring and cost-reduction program. Management said the first wave is on track. Production in Furth has been ramped down, and Suhl is now the main operational base for that activity. They are targeting a sustainable double-digit EBIT margin by 2028.

LPKF also finalized a financing agreement with existing banks in Q1, securing financing through 2028 for restructuring and investment in growth technologies. That gives the company more room to execute while advanced packaging develops.

---

#### Guidance

LPKF maintained 2026 guidance for revenue of €105–120 million and adjusted EBIT margin of -3.0% to +4.5%. Management said the guidance excludes potential high-volume advanced-packaging orders because timing depends on downstream process qualification by customers.

This is really important to remember. Management is not baking large advanced-packaging volume orders into the guide, even though it clearly expects relevant purchase orders this year. That means meaningful production-order wins could represent upside to the formal outlook, but revenue timing still depends on customer ramp schedules.

---

#### Bull case

The bull case is that LPKF becomes a strategic equipment supplier for glass-based advanced packaging. The strongest proof points are the over-80% qualification presence, the first Korean capacity-expansion order, active ramp-up discussions, and the broader Nexa portfolio.

If glass substrates move into production for AI and high-performance computing packages, LPKF could move from a small industrial laser company into a more strategic semiconductor packaging equipment supplier. The upside improves if LPKF captures multiple process steps and not just LIDE.

#### Risk case

The biggest risk is timing. Customers still need to reach target yields across the full process chain. LPKF's own process step may be ready, but the full package flow has to mature before high-volume orders arrive.

The second risk is current fundamentals. Solar is weak, Welding is restructuring, and group earnings remain pressured.

The third risk is competition and IP, especially from China. LPKF has a strong IP position, but the market opportunity is large enough to attract aggressive competitors.

---

#### Bottom line

My allocation towards LPK is roughly 1.5% at ~15. I plan to gradually add until it's a 2% position and just hold for a few years to see if the option-style investment works. To be clear, to me this is a venture-style investment. It's a position where I invest with the expecation of significant gains, but also understand the high likelihood of significant downside along the way. This is also a heavily sentiment driven stock. If the story gets ahead of itself, and it starts to price in all the future growth, I may reassess my holding and act accordingly.

### 2. $AIXA

AIXTRON is a German semiconductor equipment company focused on deposition systems for compound semiconductors. Its tools are used in optoelectronics, GaN power, SiC power, LEDs, Micro-LEDs, and R&D applications.

The Q1 story, and the thesis for most of us, is centered on optoelectronics. AIXTRON's reported P&L looked weak, but the order book showed a major AI datacom laser inflection.

---

#### Q1 2026 snapshot

AIXTRON reported Q1 revenue of €59 million, down 47% year over year, in line with the company's quarterly guidance of €65 million ± €10 million. Gross margin fell to 18%, hurt by low volume, negative operating leverage, and a mid-single-digit restructuring charge tied to personnel reduction. EBIT was negative €22 million.

The order side was much stronger. Management emphasized €171 million of new orders, driven by optoelectronics. Optoelectronics accounted for well over two-thirds of total order intake in the quarter.

So the really distilled down overview of this call is: weak revenue quarter, strong demand quarter.

---

#### Main takeaway

AIXA is definitely directly tied to the AI optical connectivity buildout.

Management said Q1 marked a clear inflection point for optoelectronics. Demand came primarily from laser-related datacom applications. The driver is a shift in AI data-center architecture from copper and lower-speed optical links toward 800G and eventually 1.6T optical connectivity. This is great. We like to see our companies positioning in strength for future architectures and speeds.

That shift creates a large increase in required wafer starts. More wafer starts require more laser production capacity. More laser production capacity requires more epitaxy tools. That is where AIXTRON fits.

---

#### Optoelectronics

Management described the AI data-center transition in three layers.

First is scale-up, where copper links inside AI data centers are replaced by higher-speed optical connections.

Second is scale-out, where the number of optical connections inside the data-center architecture increases.

Third is scale-across, where traffic between data centers rises and requires more optical links.

We have broken these different layers a handful of times on this stack.

AIXTRON's G10-AsP platform is the main beneficiary. Management said G10-AsP represented the majority of Q1 volume orders. Customers are ramping aggressively for EML and PIC-based lasers used in AI-driven data-center infrastructure. Most systems ordered today are configured for 4-inch wafers but include 6-inch conversion kits, giving customers a transition path when larger wafers become available. That's great because 6-in should become more and more prevalent in the coming years. COHR and NOK are already pushing there.

The technical pitch is yield. Datacom lasers require multiple epitaxial growth steps, so small improvements in wafer-level uniformity can multiply through the process and impact final device yield. AIXTRON says its tools control uniformity at wafer level rather than batch level, which is why customers increasingly standardize on its platform for advanced photonics devices.

---

#### Opportunity sizing

The most important Q&A detail was the tool opportunity sizing.

Management estimated that the laser-communications market may need 80–100 G10 tools installed per year. When an analyst translated that into a potential €300–400 million annual equipment opportunity, management said that was "not too far off."

That is a really clean way to size the AIXA optoelectronics opportunity from the call. It turns the story into a potentially multi-year equipment cycle.

---

#### Visibility into 2027

Management said customer transparency improved meaningfully after NVIDIA's early-March investments into optical ecosystem players such as Coherent and Lumentum. Since then, multiple optoelectronics customers have engaged with AIXTRON around multi-tool orders for 2026, orders extending into 2027, and forecasts across 2026 and 2027.

Management stopped short of giving a detailed 2027 forecast, but said optoelectronics momentum extends well into 2027 and gives the company a solid base. If GaN and SiC power electronics recover on top of that, 2027 could become stronger.

---

#### Power electronics and other markets

GaN demand remained stable but soft. It contributed just under 10% of total order intake in Q1. Management expects demand to improve when customer fab utilization rises, possibly later in 2026 or in the first half of 2027.

SiC remained soft due to industry underutilization. Management said utilization is improving, but the timing of renewed volume demand remains uncertain. AIXTRON said its tools remained the leading choice in SiC epitaxy during 2025, even in a difficult market.

LED and Micro-LED had limited impact on Q1 order intake. Spending remains low and cyclical, with activity mainly tied to Chinese end users focused on AR and smart-glass applications.

---

#### Malaysia expansion

AIXTRON announced a new facility in Malaysia's Penang region. The site will focus on assembly and testing of selected 100mm, 150mm, and 200mm systems for Asian customers. The investment is around €40 million, with operations planned for 2027.

The strategic logic is proximity to Asian customers, supply-chain resilience, and cost competitiveness, while keeping core R&D and production strength in Europe.

---

#### Convertible bond

After quarter-end, AIXTRON placed a €450 million convertible bond with a five-year maturity and 0% coupon. Management said the transaction adds financial flexibility and can support organic growth, potential M&A, or opportunistic buybacks.

Management also said the convertible was not tied to a specific pending transaction. It was framed as an opportunistic financing move at attractive terms.

---

#### Guidance

AIXTRON reaffirmed its raised 2026 guidance: revenue of €560 million plus or minus €30 million, gross margin around 42%, and EBIT margin of 17–20%. The guide includes one-off personnel-reduction costs. Management also guided Q2 revenue to about €110 million plus or minus €10 million.

The guide implies a major ramp after Q1. Q1 revenue was only €59 million, so the year depends on converting optoelectronics backlog into shipments during Q2–Q4.

---

#### Bull case

The bull case is that AIXTRON is entering a multi-year optoelectronics equipment cycle. AI data centers need more high-speed optical links, laser manufacturers need more capacity, and AIXTRON appears well positioned with G10-AsP.

The 80–100 G10 tools per year comment is the key. If that market sizing holds, AIXA's optoelectronics opportunity could be much larger and more durable than a one-quarter order spike.

#### Risk case

The biggest risk is revenue conversion. Q1 was weak, and full-year guidance requires a strong ramp through the rest of 2026. Any customer shipment delay could pressure the guidance bridge.

The second risk is mix. Power electronics remains soft, and AIXTRON needs optoelectronics strength to carry the year. A GaN/SiC recovery would help, but timing is still uncertain.

#### Bottom line

I think AIXA is extremely important for the optical supercycle.

I do not have a position yet, but I would be interested in the 38-40 level if the thesis is still intact. I don't know if we get there or not, as it is a bit off the top. But that is where I like the risk:reward scenario for my portfolio.

My goal would be to build AIXA to roughly a 4-5% weighting in my optics portfolio.

### 3. $AXTI

AXT is a compound semiconductor substrate supplier. They manufacture indium phosphide, gallium arsenide, and germanium substrates.

The current story is overwhelmingly about indium phosphide, or InP. InP substrates are used in lasers and photodetectors for high-speed optical communications. That puts AXTI directly in the AI optical infrastructure supply chain.

---

#### Q1 2026 snapshot

AXT reported Q1 revenue of $26.9 million, up from $23.0 million in Q4 2025 and $19.4 million in Q1 2025. GAAP gross margin improved to 29.6%, and non-GAAP gross margin improved to 29.9%.

Management framed InP substrates as a key ingredient in high-speed optical data transmission required in AI-focused data centers. It also said longer-term capacity planning has become one of the most important discussions the company is having with customers and major supply-chain players.

---

#### Revenue mix

The mix shift was one of the most important parts of the quarter.

InP generated $13.6 million of revenue, primarily from data-center applications, making it slightly above half of total revenue. Gallium arsenide revenue was $5.4 million, Germanium was $0.2 million, and consolidated raw-material joint ventures generated $7.6 million.

That mix is why gross margin improved so sharply. Higher volume helped, but the richer InP mix was the real driver. Management said pricing benefits are being put in place, but investors should see more of that effect later in the year.

---

#### Main call takeaway

The main takeaway is that AXTI is definitely an InP capacity bottleneck story as much as X has been framing it as such.

Management said customer forecasts and backlog are at record levels. Long-term capacity planning has become one of the most important conversations AXT is having with customers and major supply-chain participants.

The key demand point was that AXT's InP backlog has climbed to more than $100 million. Management also said Q2 should be the largest InP revenue quarter in AXT's history.

---

#### Capital raise

AXT completed a very large equity raise to fund the InP ramp. The company completed a $550 million base public offering and then added $82.5 million through the underwriters' over-allotment option, bringing gross proceeds to roughly $632.5 million. The proceeds are mainly intended to support Tongmei's InP substrate capacity expansion, R&D, working capital, and general corporate purposes.

This is both bullish and risky. It is bullish because it validates the scale of the opportunity and gives AXT funding to expand. It is risky because it creates dilution and raises the execution bar.

---

#### Capacity expansion

Capacity is the center of AXTI's story.

Management said AXT is running ahead of plan to double InP capacity in 2026 from Q4 2025 levels. The company then plans to double InP capacity again in 2027 with a nearby facility dedicated to InP wafer production.

They gave a revenue-based capacity framework on the call. By the end of 2026, it expects roughly $35 million per quarter of InP factory output. By late 2027 or early 2028, it expects roughly $65–70 million per quarter of InP capacity.

That would take AXTI from a small InP supplier into a much larger strategic capacity provider, assuming demand holds and execution is clean.

---

#### Why AXT thinks scale is a competitive advantage

InP is difficult to grow. Management pushed back against the idea that capacity additions are simple. Adding theoretical capacity and actually delivering qualified wafers are two different things.

AXT's advantage is vertical integration. It designs and builds its own crystal-growth furnaces, controls critical raw materials through its supply-chain relationships, and has manufacturing space that can be repurposed for InP production. AXT also has partial ownership in ten companies in China that produce raw materials for its manufacturing process.

The raw-material strategy is especially important now. Management said JinMei has begun refining high-purity indium, giving AXT more direct control of a critical input for InP substrates.

---

#### Customer visibility and long-term supply agreements

Management said nearly all larger customers in the optical space are discussing long-term supply agreements with AXT, with some expected to reach resolution in the near future.

The hyperscaler angle is especially important. Management said hyperscalers and hardware companies are encouraging their suppliers to secure longer-term supply arrangements with AXT. That suggests end customers understand that InP substrates can become a strategic bottleneck in AI optical infrastructure.

AXT also said it is supporting nearly all leading customers in the optical space, including Tier 1 laser manufacturers and optical transceiver module makers globally and in China.

---

#### Demand drivers

AXT tied demand to several areas.

The first is U.S. cloud and AI infrastructure spending. Management said this is driving demand for EML and silicon-photonics-based optical transceivers, plus high-speed photodetectors. Management believes AXT material is already used across multiple U.S. hyperscalers.

The second is China. Management said China InP laser-market revenue more than doubled in Q1 versus Q4, and it expects that revenue to double again in Q2. China is especially valuable because domestic China shipments avoid export permit issues.

The third is the transition to larger wafer sizes and higher specifications. AXT is investing in 6-inch InP for both iron-doped and sulfur-doped specifications, while 4-inch demand is already becoming more real.

---

#### Wafer-size transition

Today, 3-inch wafers still dominate much of the laser market. But high-speed detectors have largely transitioned to 4-inch, and laser demand is also moving toward 4-inch.

6-inch remains further out for most, but customers are already warning AXT that the transition is coming. Management said the company is making important progress on 6-inch InP for both iron-doped and sulfur-doped specifications.

---

#### Pricing and margins

Gross margin improved because volume rose and the mix shifted toward InP. Management said pricing increases are also being implemented due to higher raw-material costs, especially indium.

Management expects Q2 gross margin to cross the 30% threshold. Longer term, management has discussed 35% as a reasonable external landing point, while internally targeting potential upside above that level as scale, mix, and pricing improve.

The pricing opportunity is strongest in larger wafers and higher-spec products. Smaller wafers and lower-end markets are more price-sensitive, while 4-inch and 6-inch wafers with tighter specs give AXT more pricing power.

---

#### Export permits

Export permits are the main quarterly swing factor.

If you are unfamiliar, AXT has a China-based manufacturing footprint, and some shipments require export permits. Management said Q1 permits came in slightly better than guidance and Q2 started solidly, but permit timing remains outside AXT's control.

For Q2, management said about $34 million of revenue is already realizable because permits are in hand or permits are unnecessary. They also said there could be significant upside if additional permits arrive in time.

U.S. permits remain uncertain. Management said it is still working on U.S.-based permit applications and has been asked by China's Ministry of Commerce for more data on some applications, which it views as an encouraging sign. They did say that they can ship to U.S. customers outside of the U.S.

---

#### Tongmei IPO

Tongmei's STAR Market IPO remains in process. AXT said the IPO remains subject to review and approval by Chinese authorities, and the process remains lengthy.

This is important because Tongmei is central to the manufacturing and capacity story. The IPO remains a potential future catalyst, but the timing is outside AXT's direct control.

---

#### Bull case

AXTI is becoming a strategic InP wafer supplier during a multi-year AI optical buildout. Demand is visible, backlog is above $100 million, margins are recovering, Q2 profitability is guided, and capacity is expanding aggressively.

The strongest version of the bull case is that AXT signs long-term supply agreements, doubles capacity in 2026, doubles again by late 2027 or early 2028, and becomes one of the few scalable InP suppliers for AI optical infrastructure.

#### Risk case

Export permits can delay revenue recognition. Capacity additions require clean manufacturing execution. The equity raise increases share count. China concentration adds geopolitical risk. The stock has also priced in a lot of optimism.

#### Bottom line

There is no doubt that AXT plays a vital role in the supply chain. Customer demand is significant, the capacity ramp is aggressive, and the fact that they can ship outside of geographical U.S. is very positive. My hold up, and what makes me not want a position, is valuation with the China risk. Even if they can hit their capacity ramps, I still can't underwrite a ~$5B valuation today. Or even in 2 years. Others can, but I can't.

### 4. Cohu

Cohu sells semiconductor test equipment, handlers, inspection tools, contactors, and software. The company helps customers improve semiconductor manufacturing yield and productivity.

I wrote an article on COHU here:

![COHU - The Chip Torturer](../_media/cruxcapitalgroup-must-read-earnings-axti-lpk-aixa-001.png)

#### COHU - The Chip Torturer

[Gaetano](https://substack.com/profile/410947583-gaetano)

·

Apr 23

[Read full story](https://cruxcapitalgroup.substack.com/p/cohu-the-chip-torturer)

The current story is centered on AI and high-performance computing. AI accelerators, CPUs, ASICs, network processors, and HBM stacks are getting hotter and more complex. That raises the need for thermal control, HBM inspection, power-device test, contactors, and analytics software.

---

#### Q1 2026 snapshot

Cohu reported Q1 revenue of $125.1 million, with approximately 60% recurring revenue. GAAP gross margin was 46.3%, and non-GAAP gross margin was 46.5%. Cash and investments ended the quarter at $489 million.

The call was stronger than the headline EPS. Management said orders rose 57% year over year, reflecting improved semiconductor market conditions and the increasing relevance of Cohu's technology portfolio across AI and high-performance compute.

---

#### Main call takeaway

The main takeaway is that COHU is becoming a manufacturing bottleneck beneficiary as AI chips get hotter and harder to test.

Management said AI accelerators and HPC processors generate immense heat during operation. Testing these chips requires precise temperature environments to ensure functional accuracy and long-term reliability. If a chip is tested at the wrong temperature, performance readings can be distorted, yields can suffer, and latent field failures can increase. That is why Cohu's thermal-control technology is becoming more valuable.

---

#### The $750 million AI/HPC pipeline

Cohu now sees a computing-segment opportunity pipeline of roughly $750 million. That includes about $650 million in test handlers and about $100 million from HBM inspection. Management raised FY26 high-performance computing revenue guidance to $80–100 million.

The opportunity spans 12 customers, with five customers in qualification and seven in early engagement. Great diversity.

The print looked mixed at first, but the call made the AI/HPC opportunity much larger and more tangible.

---

#### Eclipse and T-Core

Cohu secured two major Eclipse orders in Q1. The first supports AI data-center applications at a U.S. fabless customer developing server and inference devices. Management said closed-loop junction-temperature control was a key differentiator and that the win represents an estimated $100 million incremental revenue opportunity at that customer over three years.

The second order supports data-center computing, mobile, and automotive processors at another U.S.-based fabless customer. Management said Eclipse gives the customer and its OSATs a common platform across multiple markets, while T-Core helps maximize yield and utilization.

Cohu also added five customers in various stages of qualification, representing an incremental $200 million revenue opportunity starting late this year and into next year.

---

#### HBM inspection

Cohu said inspection and metrology orders rose 64% year over year. In HBM, management sees strong momentum for final inspection of HBM3 and HBM4 and is investing to support HBM5. It now expects Neon HBM revenue to grow about 80% year over year to roughly $20 million in 2026.

They also secured volume repeat orders for Neon inspection from a U.S.-headquartered customer and a Korean customer. Overall inspection revenue is estimated at about $70 million this year.

This gives COHU a second AI path beyond thermal handlers.

---

#### Power delivery and GaN

Management highlighted power delivery as an underappreciated AI infrastructure requirement. AI systems need efficient, precise power management, especially as data centers move toward 800V DC infrastructure and GaN-based power conversion. Cohu's Diamondx tester was qualified for power-device testing, expanding its AI-related footprint.

Cohu also saw increased adoption of high-current contactors for AI power applications and received a multi-unit order for a new silicon-photonics test solution. Management said these photonics switches form the backbone of cloud and AI Ethernet fabric, and Cohu is now testing them.

---

#### Software and recurring revenue

PAICe software is still small, but it adds a high-quality recurring layer.

Management gave a strong example where a $20 million system order came with $330,000 per year of software subscription. Over the lifetime of the systems, that could create about $5 million of recurring revenue.

So systems create the installed base, while software, consumables, contactors, services, and support create recurring revenue.

---

#### Q2 and FY26 guidance

Cohu guided Q2 revenue to $144 million plus or minus $7 million, up 15% sequentially and 34% year over year. Management raised full-year 2026 revenue growth guidance to 20–25% over 2025. Q2 gross margin is expected around 44%, with full-year gross margin in the mid-40% range as Cohu ramps supply chain and production capacity for HPC customers.

OpEx is expected to decline from Q1 to about $53 million in Q2, then remain in the low-$50 million range for the rest of the year.

---

#### Why margins dip despite better demand

Cohu said Q1 gross margin was roughly 50% on recurring revenue and roughly 40% on systems. Systems revenue is expected to rise faster as HPC orders convert, so blended gross margin faces a temporary mix headwind even while the business improves.

That explains why the stock initially sold off. The release showed a GAAP loss, thin non-GAAP EPS, and a Q2 gross-margin step-down. The call explained that the margin decline is mix-driven and tied to systems growth, rather than demand weakness.

---

#### Pipeline conversion and timing

Management said Q2's sequential increase includes about $10 million of HPC systems revenue, just under half of the quarter-over-quarter increase. That puts first-half HPC systems revenue at roughly $30 million.

Management said the already qualified $100 million opportunity is annualized, but only a portion is expected to convert this year. The five customers in qualification represent roughly $10–40 million per customer per year, with some qualifications expected by early Q3. Revenue could show up in Q4, though some may shift into early 2027 depending on lead times and revenue recognition.

---

#### Capacity and lead times

Management said thermal-handler cycle time is around 14 weeks, but a large order, such as $30 million, would ship over several weeks or months. Cohu is working with suppliers to solve choke points, especially around thermal heads, while also hiring in Malaysia and reworking the facility layout to open more capacity.

So the pipeline is growing, but revenue conversion depends on production capacity and supply-chain execution.

---

#### Demand broadening

Another strong Q&A detail is that AI demand is broadening beyond one GPU-centered customer.

Management said it is seeing more demand from large CPUs, ASIC accelerators, and network processors as inference grows.

---

#### Bull case

The bull case is that COHU becomes a core beneficiary of AI chip complexity. High-power chips need active thermal control. HBM needs more inspection. Power delivery needs more test. Silicon-photonics switches need test solutions. PAICe software adds recurring revenue on top.

The $750 million pipeline gives investors a much bigger opportunity to underwrite than the current revenue base.

#### Risk case

The risk case is margin and timing.

Systems mix can weigh on gross margin, especially during a fast ramp. Qualifications can push revenue from late 2026 into 2027. Supply-chain and thermal-head capacity could limit conversion. OpEx is also elevated because the company is investing to capture the HPC opportunity.
