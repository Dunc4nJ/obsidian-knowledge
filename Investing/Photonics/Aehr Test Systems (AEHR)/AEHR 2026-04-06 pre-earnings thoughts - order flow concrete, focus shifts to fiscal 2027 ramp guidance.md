### Some thoughts on the upcoming earnings

The stock broke 52 week highs today. Crazy runup the past week!

Earnings could really go either way.

I could see the market reacting negatively to what is presumably going to be a relatively low headline revenue number if investors are projecting to see a company already past the revenue inflection point.

I could also see the earnings call providing a clear picture of demand, significant order and revenue visibility, and the market continuing to reward the high multiple.

The earnings appear to be set for April 7th after the bell. Some sources say the 6th. This inconsistency is fairly common with smaller companies.

The headline revenue number is not what I am focused on. The conference call and the guidance are.

The market may move hard on the print, especially with a lot of newer eyes on the name after the recent AI and silicon photonics order flow. But the more interesting question for me is how management frames the path from here.

Important to understand that this company is trading at a very high multiple that is pricing in significant future growth. Before this inflection happens (IF it happens in a meaningful way) there should be high volatility. If the market decides it doesn't want to continue to pay for this future growth, share price will get hit. Just keep this in mind always when you are thinking about companies in this stage.

### Fiscal year

AEHR's fiscal year ends late May. Second-half fiscal 2026 runs through May 29, and fiscal 2027 starts May 30. Just keep that in mind here because it gets a little confusing when it does not line up with the calendar year.

---

### What we know

On the January call, management reinstated second-half fiscal 2026 guidance for revenue of $25 million to $30 million, with second-half bookings expected at $60 million to $80 million. Quarter-end backlog was $11.8 million. They then added $6.5 million of bookings in the first six weeks of Q3, bringing effective backlog to $18.3 million.

They also said the bulk of that expected bookings strength was tied to AI processors, with some silicon photonics and very little silicon carbide. That is important because it shows where the real near-term center of gravity is.

So even before the newer order announcements, they had already laid out a setup where bookings run well ahead of revenue. This quarter could feel a little strange because of that. The story has gotten a lot more exciting, but management's own framework already suggested the income statement would lag the narrative for a bit. At a $1.3 billion valuation, I think that gap is worth keeping in mind.

---

### What has converted since the last call

Since the last earnings call AEHR has announced several concrete orders.

February 11: an initial production purchase order for multiple Sonoma systems tied to a lead hyperscale customer's next-generation AI ASIC processors, with delivery scheduled for summer 2026.

February 26: a $14 million order from its lead AI processor customer for multiple new fully automated FOX-XP wafer-level burn-in systems, with shipment scheduled within six months.

March 3: a follow-on silicon photonics order from its lead customer for production wafer-level burn-in tied to AI optical I/O.

March 31: an initial order from a major new silicon photonics customer covering both engineering qualification and high-volume production systems, shipping in fiscal Q4.

The order flow has started to validate the story. So an appropriate question now is how much of that converts to near-term revenue versus how much is still setting up fiscal 2027.

---

### Sonoma

The January call still had some forecast language around Sonoma that has since started to convert. They said Q3-to-date Sonoma orders were already above $5.5 million, exceeding all of Q2, and that at least one customer planned to move into production in late calendar 2026. The lead packaged-part AI customer had provided a substantial forecast with requested shipments beginning in fiscal Q1 2027.

Since then, AEHR announced the initial production Sonoma order for that hyperscale AI ASIC program. That is a meaningful step but the delivery is scheduled for summer 2026, so the call is where I want to hear management clarify how much of the Sonoma ramp belongs to second-half FY26, how much belongs to early FY27, and how large the consumables opportunity could become once those systems are installed.

---

### Wafer-level AI

Management said the bulk of expected second-half bookings strength was tied to AI processors across both wafer-level and packaged-part burn-in. They also said a single AI processor at wafer level can represent 20 to 30 systems at $4 million to $5 million each, and that they had discussed capacity exceeding 20 systems a month. The February 26 order gave that part of the story more substance with multiple fully automated FOX-XP systems, each configured to test nine 300mm wafers in parallel, for AI processors used in training and inference.

They also said two additional AI processor companies were planning wafer-level benchmark evaluations, which makes this feel more like a developing pipeline than a one-off program.

The revenue print will tell us what landed and the call will tell us how much more is behind it.

---

### Silicon photonics

On the last earnings call they said their lead silicon photonics customer had firmed up its production ramp, but that it would begin early next fiscal year, later than previously expected. They also said another major silicon photonics customer was expected to book an initial FOX system soon, with delivery planned for May.

Since then, AEHR announced a follow-on order from the lead customer and a new major customer win in the hyperscale optical interconnect market. The March 31 release included both engineering qualification and high-volume production systems shipping in fiscal Q4, plus a forecast for additional systems with follow-on orders possible later this calendar year. This feels like one of the cleaner areas where the story is moving from future to an actual revenue bridge.

---

### Where my head is at

The order flow is becoming concrete. The narrative has clearly improved. But I am going into this report focused on the call more than the number. Specifically whether management can make the timing of the ramp feel clearer and more believable heading into fiscal 2027.
