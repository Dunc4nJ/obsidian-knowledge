## Original Content

Yesterday I wrote some pre-earnings thoughts. Read them here: [[AEHR 2026-04-06 pre-earnings thoughts - order flow concrete, focus shifts to fiscal 2027 ramp guidance|AEHR...Continued Upside?]] (Gaetano, Apr 6).

Today we got the full earnings with the call.

There were lots of good nuggets in there.

Let's unpack it.

---

### The Financial Inflection & Balance Sheet

As anticipated, the actual Q3 income statement lagged the massive order flow. Q3 revenue came in at $10.3 million, down 44% YoY, primarily due to a lower volume of high-margin WaferPak shipments. WaferPaks are the contact hardware Aehr uses to test entire wafers. This less favorable product mix dragged non-GAAP gross margins down to 36.5%, compared to 42.7% a year ago.

The real story was the operational pivot and exploding backlog. CEO Gayn Erickson highlighted this shift: "*We're very pleased with the strong momentum in our business across multiple market segments, highlighted by more than $37 million in quarterly bookings and a book-to-bill ratio exceeding 3.5x*". Adding in the $12.2 million booked in the first five weeks of Q4, Aehr's effective backlog has surged to a record $50.9 million.

One crucial update is that Aehr has officially tapped out its At-The-Market, or ATM, equity offering. They raised $10.5 million in Q3 and another $19.5 million shortly after the quarter ended. CFO Chris Siu noted that they have "*now fully utilized the $40 million available under the ATM and have sold over 1.13 million shares at an average price of $35.38*". This leaves them with a bolstered balance sheet, ending Q3 with $37.1 million in cash and cash equivalents, with the current ATM now fully utilized.

Because of the massive $50.9 million backlog, management provided highly optimistic guidance for the remainder of Fiscal 2026.

Management expects full-year fiscal 2026 revenue "*to be on the high side of the $45 million-$50 million range*" and second-half bookings to hit the "*high side of the $60 million-$80 million range*".

Gross margins are expected to improve in Q4 as scaled manufacturing better absorbs fixed costs. Consequently, Aehr expects "*to return to profitability on a non-GAAP basis in the fourth quarter*". For the full year, they project a non-GAAP net loss between -$0.13 and -$0.09 per share.

One other timing detail worth knowing is that Aehr is also shifting its fiscal year-end from late May to late June. So after Fiscal 2026 ends on May 29, 2026, Fiscal 2027 will begin on June 27, 2026.

---

### The Sonoma ramp + milestone

Aehr's Sonoma system is its package-level burn-in platform. It tests chips after they have already been packaged, stressing them under heat and power to catch weak parts before they go into expensive AI systems. Sonoma is becoming a foundational tool for the AI processor industry, driven by its ability to handle extreme power loads that traditional test equipment simply cannot sustain.

During the quarter, they completed development of a next-generation fully automated higher-power Sonoma system supporting up to 2,000 watts per device.

Erickson gave useful context on what that means competitively: "*We have some devices that we're going to be testing this spring that are almost 2,000 watts per device, right? Everybody's out there talking about how can you do what does it take to get to 1,000 watts? We're jumping right past that.*"

As AI ASICs and GPUs grow more complex, they draw massive amounts of power and generate extreme heat. Getting to 2,000 watts per device creates a meaningful technological moat in a market where the thermal demands of cutting-edge chips are outpacing what legacy test platforms were designed to handle.

To meet anticipated demand from a major hyperscaler planning to begin shipments in Q1 FY27, Aehr is scaling manufacturing capacity aggressively. They will begin shipping Sonoma systems from an additional contract manufacturer this quarter, adding capacity of more than 20 systems per month, which meaningfully expands its ability to support future growth.

---

### The AI wafer-level benchmark delay

Aehr's FOX system is its wafer-level burn-in platform. This means it tests chips while they are still on the wafer, before they are cut apart and packaged. That can matter a lot because catching bad die earlier can save customers from wasting money on expensive downstream packaging.

The highly anticipated wafer-level benchmark with a top-tier AI processor supplier took longer than expected. The details, though, point to a structural learning curve for the industry rather than any failure in Aehr's technology.

Erickson explained the root cause: "*This was due to a technical misunderstanding on the clock configurations, which created some challenges with the initial WaferPak designs. While we wish we'd been able to catch this earlier, we're taking device data now on their wafers with the current WaferPak design and redesigning the WaferPaks to meet the new requirements*."

On the Q2 call, Erickson added more color on why these misunderstandings emerge as companies shift from testing individual packages to testing whole wafers: "*They actually gave us some things that were implied based upon package that weren't totally applicable to wafer level. On the very first time you're doing wafer level burn-in, you just don't think about it from the challenges or the differences at what happens when you're talking about a device that shares common substrates.*"

The benchmark delay reflects how genuinely new wafer-level burn-in is for these customers, rather than a problem with Aehr's approach.

---

### The high-bandwidth memory opportunity

Among the more significant new developments is Aehr's active push into testing high-bandwidth memory. HBM is the critical, expensive memory stacked directly inside AI GPUs and sits at the center of how modern AI chips are architected.

Erickson said: "*We are seeing the added potential for HBM insertions with our FOX multi-wafer test and burn-in system roadmap that extends to flash, High Bandwidth Flash, DRAM, and HBM memories. This is a key focus for Aehr this year.*"

The financial logic for doing this at the wafer level is compelling. Modern AI chips use advanced multi-chip packaging like TSMC's CoWoS, combining compute die and memory stacks into a single assembled component.

Erickson said: "*The CoWoS substrate is more expensive than the silicon itself of the processor, which sounds crazy. They would be very interested in doing the wafer level to screen out the die before they have to throw away everything else.*"

Catching bad die before packaging means avoiding the write-off of a very expensive assembled component, which gives customers a very direct economic incentive to move screening earlier in the flow.

---

### TAM Comments

The key TAM section came in Q&A when Christian Schwab asked management how big the opportunity could be over a multi-year timeframe.

Gayn said they have thought about it, but are being cautious about formal projections. Then he got much more explicit.

He said, "*The TAMs are multi-hundreds of millions of dollars for package level burn-in. Wafer level burn-in, if you say it displaces package level, is even higher.*"

He then added that, including memory, "*The spend in burn-in probably total spend measured in multiple billions of dollars per year in the next couple of years on an annual basis.*"

He also said these TAMs are "*significantly larger than anything we ever talked about on the power semiconductor side.*"

---

### Silicon photonics as the near-term revenue bridge

While the larger AI and memory ramps are setting up for FY27, silicon photonics is converting to revenue right now.

On the new hyperscale data center customer, Erickson confirmed: "*These systems are all scheduled to ship in this fiscal fourth quarter, ending May 29th, 2026.*" Beyond new customer shipments, existing customers are scaling toward full automation. Aehr's lead silicon photonics customer has upgraded their lines to fully integrate Aehr systems and aligners with autonomous guided robots that carry 300mm FOUPs, enabling completely lights-out, hands-free operation. That kind of deployment depth signals a customer building for production volume.

---

All in all, solid guidance and visibility. But the reality is is that they are still trading at a very high multiple. So anyone investing in this company needs to decide for themselves whether they are willing to pay for the story rather than the current financial reality.
