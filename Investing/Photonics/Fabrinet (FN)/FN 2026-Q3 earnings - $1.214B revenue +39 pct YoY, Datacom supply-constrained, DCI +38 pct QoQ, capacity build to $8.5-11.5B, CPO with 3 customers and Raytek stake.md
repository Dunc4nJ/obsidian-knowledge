## Original Content

### FN Earnings: The Thesis Strengthened

_But Am I Taking A Position?_

Fabrinet's Q3 print was strong, and the call gave us a clearer picture of what is happening beneath the surface.

They reported $1.214B of revenue, up 39% year over year and 7% sequentially.

Non-GAAP EPS was $3.72. Both came in above guidance.

Q4 guidance came in at $1.25B–$1.29B of revenue and $3.72–$3.87 of non-GAAP EPS.

That implies continued sequential revenue growth, EPS roughly flat to up sequentially, and about 40% year-over-year revenue growth at the midpoint.

So the business read is fairly bullish.

The stock setup is more complicated in my opinion.

This quarter confirmed that AI infrastructure demand is real, but it also showed why the market may still debate the pace of growth, revenue visibility, margin pressure, and the right valuation to pay today.

Datacom demand is stronger than reported revenue, DCI is accelerating, HPC is expanding beyond the original program, and capacity is being built for a much larger business.

*Below is my full breakdown of what changed, what still needs to prove out, and the levels I am personally watching.*

*None of this is financial advice or a recommendation.*

---

#### My Main Takeaway

Fabrinet has more demand than it can ship.

That is especially true in Datacom. Revenue there declined sequentially, but management said demand was far above shipments and that Datacom revenue would have been a new record "by a wide margin" without supply constraints. The bottleneck also broadened. This is no longer only an EML/laser discussion. Management called out lasers, memory, and certain ASICs as constraints.

That is the key distinction that I think we really need to consider here.

The issue is supply availability and ramp timing, rather than weak demand.

That is a better problem, but it can still frustrate the market in the short term.

Also, I think they are positioned for really great growth if we jump forward a few quarters. This quarter and next feel like more of a transitional period in a way. Where the real excitement will come when constraints are eased, capacity is built up, and new architectures come into play.

#### Datacom: Weak Number, Strong Setup

Datacom was the soft spot in the reported numbers.

Revenue fell from $278M to $260M sequentially. That is the part the market may focus on first.

But the call was much more constructive than the number alone.

Management said supply shortages held back shipments and that demand remained exceptionally strong. More important, Fabrinet has now completed qualification and started shipping two Datacom transceiver programs directly to a hyperscale customer. These are both 800G scale-out programs, with initial ramp starting in Q4 and a steadier ramp expected through FY27.

They are also preparing multiple merchant transceiver programs, including scale-out applications, with production expected to begin in the second half of calendar 2026 / early FY27.

So Datacom has two stories:

1. Near-term revenue is supply constrained.
2. FY27 Datacom breadth looks better, with two hyperscaler-direct 800G scale-out programs already shipping in small quantities and merchant programs expected to begin production around early FY27.

---

#### Telecom / DCI

Telecom was the strongest part of the quarter.

Telecom revenue reached $628M, up 13% sequentially and 55% year over year. DCI reached $197M, up 38% sequentially and 90% year over year.

This supports the point from the pre-earnings report: Fabrinet's "telecom" bucket is increasingly an AI data-movement read-through.

DCI is connecting data centers to each other. That demand is already showing up in the numbers. Management also said 800ZR is still getting going, which means this cycle likely still has room.

---

#### HPC: Small Timing Push, Larger Opportunity

HPC revenue increased from $85.6M to $106.7M, up 25% sequentially.

The nuance is the $150M quarterly milestone.

Management now expects that level to be pushed out by about one quarter because the customer is transitioning to its latest-generation product. At first glance, that sounds like a negative, but the explanation was constructive.

Fabrinet has won additional follow-on programs with the same customer and is now supporting a broader set of products tied to accelerated computing infrastructure. Management said the timing shifted slightly, but the overall trajectory is stronger and revenue should continue growing beyond the first $150M quarterly milestone.

So my read is that near-term timing moved slightly right. Long-term scope improved.

That is a tradeoff I am fine with, at the right price.

---

#### Capacity

Management said the current footprint supports about $4.8B of revenue capacity. Pinehurst adds about $200M. Navanakorn starts around $250M and can expand toward $500M. Building 10 adds about $3B. Combined, that gets Fabrinet to roughly $8.5B of revenue capacity. Building 11 and Building 12 could add another $1.5B each, pushing potential capacity toward $11.5B+.

That is a huge number.

Q4 guide annualizes around $5.1B at the midpoint, so Fabrinet is building for a much larger business.

Management also framed the risk/reward well. Building 10 costs roughly $130M–$132M, and management said six months of operating profit at full run-rate could pay for the whole facility. If unused, they estimated a gross margin hit around 50 bps.

But it's important to know that this capacity does not directly solve the Datacom component bottleneck. Fabrinet still needs lasers, memory, ASICs, and other constrained parts to become available. But the capacity build makes sure FN can convert more demand once the component supply catches up.

---

#### Margins and Free Cash Flow

Non-GAAP gross margin was 12.1%, down from 12.4% last quarter. Management pointed to FX headwinds and ramp inefficiencies from scaling multiple new programs. Operating margin still held at 10.7%, helped by very low OpEx at 1.4% of revenue.

Free cash flow was negative $11M, with $53M of operating cash flow and $64M of capex.

For now, I view this as acceptable. They are building capacity into customer demand.

But this is the area I will keep watching. If growth slows while capex remains elevated, the market will care.

---

#### CPO and OCS

CPO became more real this quarter.

Fabrinet is already seeing small CPO revenue and is working with three customers. On the call, management said they are shipping to all three, across both scale-up and scale-out. They also made a $32M investment for a roughly 14% stake in Raytek Semiconductor to deepen advanced packaging capability for CPO.

This is still early, but it shows Fabrinet is actively positioning for the next architecture shift.

OCS remains earlier. Management said it is incremental to the new merchant Datacom wins and that they are focused on one or two opportunities.

---

#### What Changed After the Call

Before the call, I wanted answers on four things: HPC, Datacom, Telecom/DCI, and capacity.

Here is where I land now as a TLDR;

HPC: still ramping, $150M target pushed out about one quarter, broader scope with the same customer.

Datacom: reported revenue weak, but demand stronger than shipments. New hyperscaler-direct and merchant wins improve FY27 setup.

Telecom/DCI: very strong, with DCI up 38% sequentially and 800ZR still early.

Capacity: much bigger roadmap than expected. Fabrinet is planning for a materially larger business.

Margins/FCF: watch-outs, but tied to FX, ramp inefficiencies, and growth capex.

---

#### Stock Setup

This is where I want to separate the business from the stock.

The business momentum is strong. The call strengthened the long-term thesis.

But the stock already prices in a lot.

The market could still punish the name for a few reasons:

* Q4 guide implies mid-single-digit sequential revenue growth
* Datacom revenue declined sequentially
* Datacom supply constraints broadened
* HPC's $150M milestone moved out by roughly one quarter
* Gross margin and FCF are under pressure
* FY27 commentary was strong, but still qualitative

So I want exposure to FN. I just do not want to chase it after a major rerating, especially because I already have so much optics exposure.

#### My Strategy

I currently hold no position in FN.

The business is moving in the right direction, but I would prefer a better entry. The level I am most interested in is around the 200-day moving average, which is near $595 as of this writing. That is where the risk/reward would become much more attractive for my own strategy and position sizing.

If FN never gives that reset, I will reassess.
