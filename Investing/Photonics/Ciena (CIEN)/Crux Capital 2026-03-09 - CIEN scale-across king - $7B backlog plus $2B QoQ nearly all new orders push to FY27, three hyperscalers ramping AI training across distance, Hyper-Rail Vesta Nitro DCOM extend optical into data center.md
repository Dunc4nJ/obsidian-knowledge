# Crux Capital 2026-03-09 — CIEN "Scale-Across King": $7B backlog (+$2B QoQ), FY27-loaded order book, three hyperscalers ramping AI training across distance

## Key Takeaways

- **Headline numbers run hot.** [[Ciena (CIEN)]] reported $1.43B revenue (+33% YoY), adjusted EPS $1.35 (>2× YoY), adjusted GM 44.7% (ahead of expectations, full-year GM guide raised). Backlog jumped ~$2B in one quarter to ~$7B.
- **Order book pushed almost entirely into FY27.** CFO Marc Graff: "nearly all new orders we are taking now will be for fulfillment in fiscal 2027." Revenue would have been higher but for capacity constraints; demand expected to outstrip supply "for at least the next several quarters."
- **Management talks "durability."** CEO Gary Smith: "Demand is incredibly strong, with exceptional order activity in the quarter… durability of demand through 2027 and beyond." Crux flags this as the same tone heard from [[Lumentum (LITE)]], [[Coherent (COHR)]], [[Applied Optoelectronics (AAOI)]] — and that a *future post* will cover the "durability" theme across the cluster (Crux saying "we'll cover this in full in a post soon").
- **Scale-Across is the new AI infrastructure layer.** As clusters get larger and more power-constrained, hyperscalers spread training compute across multiple sites. Smith: customers using high-speed optical networks to create *"one single AI training environment that operates across distance."* He framed it as **"the very early stages of this wave of opportunity"** — Crux highlights this language as the antidote to "over-extended" fear narratives on X.
- **Three hyperscalers already using Ciena optics for AI training across distance** — and all three ramping. The first-disclosed hyperscaler has already ordered *multiple additional clusters*. This is a deployment curve, not a single-customer pilot.
- **Reconfigurable Line System (RLS) had a second consecutive record shipment+revenue quarter.** Plus a new product introduction: **Hyper-Rail** — Smith calls it "an order of magnitude increase in fiber density" within existing rack footprints. Standardization end-2026, ramp in 2027.
- **A second demand wave: service providers + neoscalers.** Service providers "have not invested in optical infrastructure for about five years" and are returning to spend now, simultaneous with AI traffic ramp. Managed Optical Fiber Networks (MOFN) — dedicated optical routes between data center locations — orders in India +40% YoY, MOFN now ~10-15% of service provider business.
- **Pushing into the data center itself.** Smith: *"The physics of copper inside the data center is reaching its limits."* Three product vectors:
  - **Vesta 200** — compact optical engine / module for high-speed equipment connections
  - **Nitro** — linear redriver, signal-conditioning for short electrical links (improve reach + cut power)
  - **DCOM** (Data Center Out-of-Band Management) — provisioning/management network for hyperscale infrastructure
  Different maturity stages but same direction: expand role across the full connectivity stack, not just WAN transport.
- **Margins benefited from mix, capacity infill, cost reductions, and "value exchange" with customers** — i.e., Ciena getting commercial terms that reflect the supply-tight environment. Graff said pricing actions from prior periods have more impact in 2H.

This is Crux's framing of the AI infrastructure trade *expanding outward* — from compute → power/cooling → photonics components → **transport and optical networking**. CIEN sits at the geographic-scale-out layer that becomes critical when training spans campuses → metros → regions, and Crux uses CIEN's "very early stages" language to push back against the "AI infra over-extended" narrative. The "durability through 2027 and beyond" plus the FY27-loaded backlog is a forward-bookings tell that cross-validates the [[Applied Optoelectronics (AAOI)]] / [[Lumentum (LITE)]] / [[Coherent (COHR)]] capacity-constrained reads. ## Original Content

Ciena just reported a very strong quarter, and the headline numbers were excellent. Revenue came in at $1.43 billion, up 33% year over year. Adjusted EPS was $1.35, more than double the prior-year quarter. Full-year guidance moved higher, and backlog increased by about $2 billion in one quarter to roughly $7 billion.

The numbers were strong but I am always more interested in what the call highlights.

What stood out most to me was management's description of demand. Gary Smith opened by saying, "Demand is incredibly strong, with exceptional order activity in the quarter." He also said that customer planning conversations are giving Ciena confidence in "the durability of demand" through 2027 and beyond.

That language gets my attention and it's what we are seeing across lots of the companies I cover like [LITE 0.00%↑](https://substack.com/search/%24LITE) [COHR 0.00%↑](https://substack.com/search/%24COHR) [AAOI 0.00%↑](https://substack.com/search/%24AAOI) etc. I also really like the comment about 'durability'. We will cover this in full in a post soon.

A company does not talk that way unless it is seeing a very deep and very broad demand environment. Marc Graff took it a step further when he said backlog increased by approximately $2 billion in the quarter to around $7 billion, and that "nearly all new orders we are taking now will be for fulfillment in fiscal 2027."

Nearly all new orders are now being pushed into 2027. That tells you a lot about where Ciena sits in the AI buildout. Demand is running well ahead of what the supply chain can deliver today. Graff said revenue in the quarter "would have been higher but for these constraints," and he added that demand is expected to continue outstripping supply for at least the next several quarters.

This is what a bottleneck looks like in real time.

The broader message from the call is that the AI buildout is moving further out into the network. For a while the focus has been on the obvious layers of the stack like GPUs, memory, power, cooling, lasers, transceivers. Ciena's quarter showed the next layer very clearly. As clusters get larger and more power constrained, hyperscalers are spreading compute across multiple sites. Gary Smith said customers are using high-speed optical networks to create "one single AI training environment that operates across distance." He also said Ciena believes it is in "the very early stages of this wave of opportunity." That's pretty wild to hear. When there is so much fear on X about these companies being over-extended, they are literally telling us they are in the very early stages. Take note.

Once AI training stretches across campuses, metros, and regions, the network becomes part of the architecture. High-speed optical connectivity across distance moves much closer to the center of the story. That is exactly where Ciena sits.

Management said three hyperscalers are already using Ciena's optical solutions for training applications across distance, and all three are ramping. The first hyperscaler the company disclosed previously has already added orders for multiple additional clusters. That gives the call a very different feel from a normal networking quarter. This is a company describing a real deployment curve.

Ciena also outlined how it plans to deepen its position as this demand grows. One of the clearest examples is its Reconfigurable Line System, or RLS. This is the optical transport infrastructure that sits underneath large cloud and AI networks and helps move huge volumes of data across long distances. Think of it as the high-capacity optical backbone that links major sites together. Ciena said RLS had its second consecutive record quarter for shipments and revenue, which tells you customers are already scaling this part of the network aggressively.

Management also introduced Hyper-Rail, which is essentially the next generation of that backbone architecture. Smith said it delivers "an order of magnitude increase in fiber density" within existing rack footprints. That means Ciena is trying to help customers move far more traffic through the same physical space, without having to go build a large amount of new supporting infrastructure around it. That matters in environments where power, land, and physical network buildout are becoming constraints of their own. Ciena expects Hyper-Rail to move through standardization at the end of 2026 and begin ramping in 2027.

If you really look, you can see the roadmap forming pretty clearly.

Ciena is building around a world where AI clusters are no longer confined to one building or even one campus. As those clusters stretch across distance, customers need more long-haul optical transport, more fiber density, and a more efficient way to move massive amounts of data between locations. That expands the role of companies providing the optical systems and interconnect layer, which is exactly where Ciena is strongest.

There was also a second demand wave running through the quarter, and this is an important part of the story. Ciena did not describe this as a quarter driven only by hyperscalers. The company also talked about strong activity from service providers and what it calls neoscalers, meaning newer AI and cloud infrastructure builders that are trying to stand up large-scale capacity of their own. Gary Smith said service providers "have not invested in their optical infrastructure for about five years." Now that spending is returning at the same time AI traffic and cloud interconnection needs are rising.

Management also talked about growth in Managed Optical Fiber Networks, or MOFN. This is basically when cloud companies and service providers work together to build or operate dedicated high-capacity optical connections. Instead of relying on generic public network capacity, they are building more controlled and purpose-built optical routes to connect important data center locations. Ciena said orders in India were up 40% year over year, reflecting that kind of activity, and estimated that MOFN now represents around 10% to 15% of service provider business.

Everything looks good for Ciena here.

The company is seeing several demand streams rise at the same time. Hyperscalers are expanding network requirements across campuses and regions. Service providers are returning to optical spending after years of relative underinvestment. Managed optical fiber networks are becoming more important as cloud and AI infrastructure gets more distributed. Neoscalers are entering the picture as well and treating connectivity as a core part of the buildout.

All of that is landing on Ciena at once.

The company also spent time discussing what is happening in and around the data center itself. Smith said "the physics of copper inside the data center is reaching its limits," which is another way of saying that traditional electrical connections become less attractive as speeds rise, power budgets tighten, and heat becomes harder to manage. Ciena's view is that more of those short-reach and mid-reach links will gradually shift toward optical technology over time.

That is where products like Vesta 200, Nitro, and DCOM come in.

Vesta 200 is an optical engine, which means it is one of the core building blocks used to move data with light instead of electricity. It is a compact optical module designed to help data center equipment handle very high-speed connections more efficiently. Nitro is a linear redriver, which is a signal-conditioning component used to help electrical links travel further while using less power. Ciena is pitching it as a way to improve short-distance connections inside the data center. DCOM stands for Data Center Out-of-Band Management. That is the network layer used to provision, configure, and manage servers and infrastructure behind the scenes. It is essential for running hyperscale environments reliably and efficiently.

These products are at different stages of maturity, but the strategic direction is easy to see. Ciena wants a larger role across the entire connectivity stack. It already has strength in the wide-area optical transport layer. Now it is working to extend that into more parts of the data center and campus environment as optical content moves deeper into the architecture.

Margins and profitability supported the broader story. Adjusted gross margin came in at 44.7%, ahead of expectations, and management raised full-year gross margin guidance. Graff said the quarter benefited from product mix, capacity infill demand, cost reductions, and early progress in what he called the "value exchange" with customers. That means Ciena is getting a better financial outcome from the strong demand environment through a combination of favorable mix, internal efficiency work, and improved commercial terms. He also said pricing actions from prior periods should have more impact in the second half.

So all in all, Ciena is sitting in the middle of a network demand surge driven by several overlapping AI and cloud infrastructure waves. The company is seeing strong orders, rising backlog, improving profitability, and enough demand to push a large share of new orders out into 2027. The call described a capacity-tight environment with real urgency behind it.

This quarter showed that the AI infrastructure trade keeps expanding through the stack. Pressure begins in compute, then moves into power and photonics, then moves outward again into transport and optical networking. Ciena gave one of the clearest public market signals yet that the network layer is becoming a core piece of that buildout.
