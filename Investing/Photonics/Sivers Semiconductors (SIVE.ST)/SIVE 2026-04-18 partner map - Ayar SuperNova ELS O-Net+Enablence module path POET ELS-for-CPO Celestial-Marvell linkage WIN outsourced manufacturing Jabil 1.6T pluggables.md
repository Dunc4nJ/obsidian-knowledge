Sivers has one of the more interesting partner sets in small-cap photonics, and that is one reason the stock keeps drawing so much attention.

These relationships give the best insight we have for where Sivers' lasers are actually being used, how close those uses are to real products, and which ones could contribute to revenue first.

They do different jobs. Some show that Sivers has a real seat inside an important future optical architecture. Some show how its lasers could move from a bare die into a fuller product. Some are really about whether the company can scale supply if the demand shows up. And now one of them points more clearly into pluggables as well.

### Ayar Labs

Ayar is a very important part of the whole partner map because it shows exactly where Sivers fits in a real optical-I/O architecture. Optical I/O means using light instead of short copper connections to move data between chips and systems so you get more bandwidth, lower latency, lower power than traditional electrical links. That is what Ayar is building toward.

Sivers' role is to supply the multi-wavelength laser array that feeds Ayar's SuperNova light source which is the laser layer of the stack. Ayar is looking for a dense, multi-wavelength source that can work inside a demanding optical-I/O system, and the fact that Sivers is in that role says something meaningful about the technical level the company has reached. It is also worth knowing that Ayar sits inside a serious ecosystem, with public associations including NVIDIA, HPE, Intel, GlobalFoundries, and Alchip and TSMC.

So let's look at the timeline. In July 2023, Sivers announced a $1 million order from Ayar for next-generation laser arrays including commercial terms for future volume pricing, with delivery expected across 2023 and 2024. Then in December 2024, the companies expanded the relationship around qualification and manufacturing readiness for high-volume optical-I/O deployment, with shipment volumes expected to ramp in the next 18 to 24 months. If you use that language at face value, the larger commercial ramp points roughly to mid-2026 through late-2027. Some development and order revenue has already come through, but the larger commercial opportunity still sits in that window as far as we know.

Going forward, the key questions are whether qualification is progressing on schedule, whether recurring shipments start showing up beyond the initial order, and whether Sivers remains part of Ayar's later-generation light-source roadmap.

### O-Net and Enablence

O-Net and Enablence are part of the same external-light-source path. O-Net handles the module side, Enablence provides the star coupler inside the module, and Sivers provides the laser arrays. A star coupler is the optical component that splits and routes light across multiple paths inside the module. Sivers' arrays are one part of a product stack where lasers, routing element, and module packaging all have to work together.

A laser company can have genuinely good technology and still get stuck if it only sells bare die and depends on others to figure out packaging and integration. O-Net and Enablence show one concrete path from Sivers' arrays into a module that could actually be sold into AI datacenter and HPC optical systems.

The timeline sequence is clear even if production targets are less defined. April 2025 brought the initial OEM partnership with O-Net. March 2026 brought the fuller three-way external-light-source announcement. The April 2026 earnings call had management saying the relationship was progressing well, with product demos pointed toward OFC. The dated path is roughly 2025 to establish the module partnership, early 2026 to announce the fuller product architecture, and spring 2026 still centered on demos and development progress.

That puts O-Net and Enablence squarely in 2026 as a product-development story, with the bigger revenue question moving more into 2027 and beyond unless the company provides more specific timing. The signals are whether demos become more concrete, whether named customer traction starts to emerge, and whether the language shifts from demos toward production intent.

### POET Technologies

POET is another external-light-source and light-engine route for Sivers. Sivers supplies the DFB lasers, and POET supplies the Optical Interposer platform that integrates those lasers into a fuller light-engine design aimed at pluggable transceivers and external light sources for co-packaged optics.

Read more about POET here: [POET Tech Deep Dive](https://cruxcapitalgroup.substack.com/p/poet-tech-deep-dive) (Gaetano, Apr 12).

If Ayar is one optical-I/O path and O-Net is one module path, POET adds another integration ecosystem entirely which is useful because it reduces the risk of the story depending too heavily on a single future customer or architecture. POET's whole pitch is about packaging and manufacturability, and if that platform works, Sivers benefits as the laser supplier inside it.

The November 2025 partnership announcement said prototypes were expected in the first half of 2026, with production readiness targeted for the end of 2026. H1 2026 for prototype work, late 2026 for production readiness, and 2027 onward as the period when a real revenue contribution becomes easier to argue. That makes POET a 2026 setup story and a 2027 revenue question.

The things to watch are whether the prototype work happened in the first half of 2026, whether production readiness still looks on track, whether POET starts talking about repeat programs rather than just prototypes, and whether POET's own customer traction starts pulling Sivers along. This is also where the Celestial and Marvell thread starts to appear.

### The Marvell question

POET has had a relationship with Celestial AI, and Celestial is now inside Marvell. A commissioned April 2026 Zacks note referenced expected 2026 shipments tied to Celestial AI and Marvell, which suggests the relationship is still active. It is management-guided communication through a paid third party rather than direct confirmation from Marvell, and that distinction carries real weight. We have yet to receive confirmation directly from POET, Celestial AI, or Marvell about the partnership status.

Marvell has other publicly disclosed optical relationships of its own, including work with Lumentum and broader ecosystem collaboration with Coherent, plus its own silicon-photonics light-engine capability. None of that rules out other suppliers over time, but it does mean Marvell is best understood as a company with its own optical roadmap already underway rather than an open slot waiting to be filled.

My view is that it is highly probable Sivers is designed into POET's solution for Celestial AI and Marvell, and I see no obvious reason why Marvell would replace POET or ask POET to change their laser vendor. But without direct confirmation, that has to be stated as a high-probability possibility rather than a guarantee. It is also worth acknowledging that companies like to multi-source. The Marvell linkage is very significant for Sivers. It also has to stay grounded in what the public record actually shows.

A formal announcement of this design in would be a big catalyst for both POET and Sivers.

### WIN

WIN is Sivers' outsourced manufacturing partner for high-volume DFB laser and laser-array production. This is a supply-side relationship. Its job is to tell you how Sivers could actually manufacture enough product if demand from Ayar, O-Net, POET, Jabil, or others begins to show up at scale.

Sivers already has internal capability in Glasgow, but their own language increasingly points toward a model where internal capacity handles development, qualification, and lower-volume work, while external foundry support is needed for bigger ramps. WIN is what makes the rest of the map more believable from a supply perspective. With it, you can begin to tell a capacity story alongside the customer story.

The collaboration was announced in March 2025. By the February 2026 earnings call, management described active qualification work with WIN and reiterated that 2027 is the year when these laser opportunities can begin driving revenue, with most of the readiness work happening during 2026. The central question is whether Sivers can support demand at scale, which means watching qualification progress, process transfer, and capacity availability as the year develops.

### Jabil

Jabil is the newest addition to the map and a very significant one because it pushes Sivers more clearly into pluggables. On April 15, 2026, Sivers announced that Jabil plans to develop a 1.6T linear receive optical transceiver using Sivers' DFB lasers. That places Sivers inside a live 1.6T pluggables conversation with a large-scale manufacturing and integration company.

Ayar, O-Net, and POET all lean toward optical-I/O, external-light-source, and future co-packaged-optics-style pathways. Jabil is a pluggables datapoint, and pluggables are where a lot of today's real datacenter optical volume still sits. So Jabil helps bridge the story from interesting future optical architecture to relevance in a product form factor that is already part of today's market.

The collaboration was announced April 15, 2026, with language around plans to develop the module using Sivers lasers. No production date, no shipment target, no design-win date yet. Jabil is best read as a 2026 validation and development milestone that could become a 2027 revenue story if the company starts disclosing qualification, demos, production intent, or orders. The watchlist is clear: any move from development language toward actual demos or qualification, any production timing, and any indication of who the end customers might be.

### What the latest earnings call changed

Management now talks about two photonics lanes more clearly than before. The first is the longer-dated optical-I/O, external-light-source, and co-packaged-optics lane associated with Ayar, O-Net, and POET. The second is pluggables, which management called a very attractive opportunity, with a growing qualified-opportunity list and laser sampling already underway at multiple customers. That makes the Jabil development feel less isolated and more like part of a deliberate commercial direction.

Management also said that after starting with younger ecosystem names, they are now seeing larger household-name companies enter discussions around future photonics roadmaps. That does not give us new named partnerships, but it does suggest the Sivers discussion set is widening in ways not yet fully visible in the public record.
