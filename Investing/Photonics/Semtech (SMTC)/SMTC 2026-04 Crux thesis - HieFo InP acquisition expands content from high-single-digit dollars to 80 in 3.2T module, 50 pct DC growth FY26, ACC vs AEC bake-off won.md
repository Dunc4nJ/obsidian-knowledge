### Fight for the AI Interconnect Layer

Inside AI data centers, every new jump in bandwidth pushes harder against power budgets, thermal limits, signal loss, and reach constraints. 800G made those issues more visible. 1.6T is turning them into first-order design problems. 3.2T pushes that even further.

As that happens, more value starts accruing to the suppliers that help hyperscalers move more data with less power across copper, optical modules, and the optical architectures forming around the rack.

Semtech is moving deeper and deeper into that shift.

*[Image — decorative Semtech section divider banner; not preserved]*

On the surface, Semtech sells enabling components that sit inside high-speed optical modules and copper interconnects. At a deeper level, it is trying to expand from a strong position in analog signal integrity into a broader interconnect stack that spans electrical, optical, and now upstream photonics through HieFo. That shift is the real reason this name has become interesting to me and why I took a position last month.

HieFo is the hinge.

"By combining HieFo's proven InP technology, including lasers and gain chips, with Semtech's industry-leading TIAs and laser drivers, we can offer customers a comprehensive solution for next-generation optical platforms, including co-packaged optics (CPO) and near-packaged optics (NPO), strengthening Semtech's position as a leader in high-bandwidth, low-power and low-latency networking solutions. Equally important, we believe strongly in the people and domestic capabilities we are bringing into Semtech through this acquisition, and we are committed to investing in their growth and success."

Before this acquisition, Semtech already had an attractive position in the signal chain. It supplied analog components that sat adjacent to the optical engine. After HieFo, Semtech moves upstream into the light source itself.

That quote tells you Semtech is building on top of an installed optical position rather than trying to force its way into the market from scratch.

HieFo adds indium phosphide gain chips and CW lasers, which are core light-generating building blocks inside modern optical interconnects. The immediate appeal is obvious. More content. The deeper appeal is that Semtech can now optimize more of the optical stack together.

### What Semtech actually does

The easiest way to understand Semtech is to picture the path data takes inside an AI rack. Signals leave an ASIC, travel across traces on a board, move through copper cables or optical modules, and then arrive at the next device. Every one of those transitions introduces losses, power tradeoffs, and signal integrity problems.

Semtech sells products that help preserve that signal.

*[Image — Semtech "High-Speed Connectivity for AI, Cloud and Enterprise Networks" Optical and Copper marketing slide; not preserved]*

In optical modules, Semtech has long been known for transimpedance amplifiers, or TIAs. A TIA sits on the receive side of an optical link. Light hits a photodiode, the photodiode generates current, and the TIA converts that tiny current into a usable voltage signal. At very high speeds, that conversion has to happen cleanly and efficiently because weak signal quality quickly turns into a power and performance problem.

On the transmit side, Semtech sells drivers that push the electrical signal into the modulator or laser with the right swing and quality so the outgoing optical link can perform at the required speed.

Semtech also has a major push in copper interconnects through CopperEdge, which includes linear equalizer and redriver solutions designed to extend reach and preserve signal quality across high-speed copper links. That covers active copper cables as well as board-level use cases where a signal has to travel farther across traces and connectors without blowing out the power budget. As speeds move to 200G per lane and beyond, those copper links become much harder to maintain. Semtech's argument is that linear signal conditioning can solve enough of that problem while using far less power than heavier DSP-based alternatives.

That combination already made Semtech exposed to the AI buildout.

HieFo extends that relevance into the light source itself.

### An attractive part of the AI stack

The AI networking buildout is creating pressure exactly where Semtech operates. Hyperscalers are building clusters where networking power is now a first-order design constraint. Once that happens, value starts accruing toward the suppliers that can lower power per link while still preserving bandwidth density and reach.

Semtech's role becomes easier to see as we break the architecture into copper and optical.

Inside the rack, copper remains extremely important. Passive DAC cables work well over very short distances, though their usable reach shrinks as lane rates rise. AECs can extend reach, but typically with a heavier power burden. Semtech's ACC push sits between those two. CopperEdge aims to give hyperscalers a lower-power way to preserve short-reach copper connectivity while extending reach beyond what passive copper can reliably support.

*Schematic Diagram of DAC, ACC, and AEC — DAC is passive copper end to end, ACC adds Driver/Receiver chips at each end, AEC adds heavier Retimer chips at each end.*
![[cruxcapitalgroup-semtech-story-worth-knowing-005.png]]

Management has been very direct about why that matters.

> "ACC consume 90% less power. That is really very compelling to this hyperscaler."

Inside a power-constrained rack, a product that keeps copper viable while cutting power that aggressively will get attention very quickly.

I think the more useful quote from an investor standpoint is the one that describes actual customer selection behavior.

> "In this leading hyperscaler in which we're ramping, we understand there was somewhat of a bake-off, direct attach copper, completely passive cable, ACC, and AEC. In the end, there's two cable types that are going to be used, direct attach copper and ACC. Due to power use and, you know, latency, AECs were not considered."

That is the kind of quote that moves ACC from a theoretical category win into a real rack-level design choice.

Management also framed the broader copper debate:

> "Recently, I heard something like you say, 'Oh, we need to put a terminal value on copper because this is gonna be all optics.' I can assure you know, that was my thinking about 20 years ago. We try to kill copper. The copper is getting stronger and stronger."

That does not mean optics stops taking share where optics belongs. It means copper still has a durable role in scale-up environments, especially where distance is short and every watt counts.

The scale of that opportunity becomes easier to appreciate once you think about what is actually inside the rack.

> "When you look at the rack design right now, any rack, they probably have anywhere from 100-200 cables inside. Each connector, each cable has two connectors on each end. You can translate into significant power savings."

On the optical side, Semtech sits in a different but equally important place. In linear pluggable optics, more of the processing burden shifts toward the analog front end and the host system. That increases the value of TIAs, drivers, and low-loss interconnect design.

*LPO vs CPO architecture — LPO has Switch ASIC + DSP on PCB connecting via electrical trace to a separate Linear Pluggable Optics Module (with Optical Engine). CPO co-packages the Optical Engine inside the Switch ASIC assembly, connecting directly to a Passive Faceplate Connector via Optical Fiber.*
![[cruxcapitalgroup-semtech-story-worth-knowing-006.webp]]

For Semtech, that is a structural tailwind.

> "By offering LPO, we have opportunities to offer drivers in addition to TIAs. That will increase our TAM by 150%."

That quote is especially useful because it quantifies what LPO does for Semtech. It is not just more relevant, it meaningfully expands the content opportunity.

Management also described where this is heading.

> "The important of LPO also that's a prelude, in my view, is the MPO or CPO because all gonna be DSP-less solution."

That line places LPO inside a broader architectural shift rather than treating it as an isolated product cycle.

And the customer signal around LPO has become increasingly strong.

> "I would say this technology at this point is not if, but more like a when and what platform they're going to be using it."

As optics move closer to the switch or XPU package, Semtech wants to stay attached to that value migration. Management talks about NPO and CPO for a reason. These architectures change where the optics live, though they still require lasers, drivers, TIAs, and modulators.

An important distinction management has made here is between CPO and NPO.

> "When in a CPO, we may not have much content in there, except for lasers, but in NPO, we will be all over the place with the laser drivers and TIAs and lasers and even silicon photonics modulator..."

This tells you where Semtech sees its role shrinking, where it sees it expanding, and why management is leaning so hard into NPO.

### The growth story over the next 12 months

Semtech's near-term setup is driven by four overlapping ramps.

The first is active copper cables. Management has repeatedly described ACC as a major growth vector, with initial hyperscaler deployments starting now and demand building through the year.

The second is 1.6T optical. Semtech already has strong exposure to 800G through TIAs, though the bigger opportunity is the transition to 1.6T where analog performance, power efficiency, and content per module all become more important.

The third is LPO, where design wins with several leading U.S. hyperscalers are already converting into shipments.

The fourth is HieFo contribution, which brings immediate revenue from gain chips and lasers while also expanding what Semtech can sell into future optical modules.

Management laid out the shape of that ramp very clearly.

> "We expect to start shipping CopperEdge for the 1.6T ACC hyperscaler deployment this quarter, with demand accelerating throughout the year. We also expect FiberEdge design wins for 1.6T transceivers with a significant ramp in the second half of the year."

This is not a smooth, linear growth story. It is a back-half weighted ramp tied to specific product transitions and deployment windows.

The guidance that follows is what makes the setup feel larger than a normal product cycle.

> "Given the breadth of our data center portfolio and design interaction across an expanding set of customers, we expect data center year-over-year revenue growth this fiscal year to exceed 50%."

Fiscal 2026 data center revenue already reached $223 million. To clear that hurdle, the business has to accelerate from here.

There is also more visibility here than a surface read of the financials might suggest.

> "We have this long-term system called a co-planning process with the lead customers... We have expanded the co-planning process from a year ago, 1 customer, to currently 7 customers."

That helps answer a central execution question. Semtech is not simply reacting to orders after the fact. It is planning capacity alongside customers well in advance.

Management also addressed the supply side more directly.

> "The current manufacturing partners, they are in allocation mode for the capacity. The good thing is, we build ahead a little bit, and that create a little briefing room and buffer that give us time to qualify additional foundry capabilities and capacity. We'll be all right, for the next 12, 18 months."

That does not eliminate execution risk, though it does suggest Semtech has prepared for a tighter supply environment.

There is also useful third-party support for the timing of the next speed transition.

> "According to Dell'Oro Group, 2026 will mark the first year of volume deployments of 1.6 Tbps switches, with the ramp expected to be even faster than 800 Gbps — surpassing 5 million ports within one to two years of shipments."

That helps tie Semtech's internal roadmap to a broader industry ramp rather than leaving the story entirely dependent on management promises.

A large portion of the market still seems to look at Semtech through older buckets. That leaves a few things underweighted.

The first is that Semtech's data center business already has real scale. Fiscal 2026 data center revenue reached $223 million, up 58% year over year, and fourth-quarter data center revenue hit a record $63 million.

The second is that Semtech appears to have genuine traction with hyperscalers. That is visible in design wins, the co-planning process, and the breadth of what it demonstrated at OFC.

> "They really see the analog design is not easy... Before they define a platform architecture, they wanted to check what are the boundary conditions, can this be achieved or not? We're the guy, and that's really a great position to be."

### The main risks

The bull case still depends on execution.

Architecture risk sits near the top of the list. LPO, NPO, MPO, CPO, ACC, and onboard equalization are all moving pieces in an ecosystem that is still forming. Semtech is positioned across several of those paths, which helps, though attach rates and timing can still shift.

ACC durability is a real debate. The mix between passive copper, active copper, and optics will keep evolving, and Semtech's thesis depends on active copper earning a durable lane where its power and latency advantages remain compelling.

There is also plain execution risk. Semtech has to turn design wins into production revenue, ramp 1.6T optical sockets, scale CopperEdge deployments, and integrate HieFo effectively.

HieFo itself still has to prove that its strategic logic turns into commercial results. The rationale is strong. The payoff depends on whether that laser technology broadens module share and drives higher content in volume.

---

### Final thoughts

Semtech is evolving into a higher-content AI interconnect supplier at exactly the point when networking power and signal integrity constraints are becoming central to hyperscaler design decisions.

The position is already more developed than the market may give it credit for. TIAs and drivers gave Semtech a strong foothold in optical analog content. CopperEdge extended that relevance into rack-scale copper. HieFo opens a path into the light source itself. The financial cleanup underneath that story makes the setup more investable than it would have looked a couple of years ago.

If the upcoming ramps convert cleanly, Semtech's role in this stack will look much larger twelve months from now than it does today. And if that happens, the market may have to stop viewing Semtech as a supporting analog name and start viewing it as one of the more important enablers in the AI interconnect layer.
