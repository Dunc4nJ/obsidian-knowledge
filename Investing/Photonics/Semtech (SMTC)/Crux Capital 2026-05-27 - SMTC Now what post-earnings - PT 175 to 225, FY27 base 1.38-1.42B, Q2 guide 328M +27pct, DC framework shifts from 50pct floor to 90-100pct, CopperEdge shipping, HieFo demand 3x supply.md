# Crux Capital 2026-05-27 — SMTC "Now what?" post-earnings — PT $175 → $225, FY27 base case $1.38-1.42B, Q2 guide $328M +27% YoY, DC framework shifts from "50% floor" to "90-100%+", CopperEdge shipping, HieFo demand 3× supply

Crux's post-print reaction to [[Semtech (SMTC)]] Q1 FY27 — the first earnings report after the May 17 "Best Way to Invest in Copper + Optics" revisit thesis. **"Semtech delivered the quarter I wanted to see."** The acceleration thesis (broad-based across FiberEdge / CopperEdge / 800G / 1.6T / LPO / LRO / lasers simultaneously) materialized; the old data-center growth target is now framed as a *floor* rather than a target; Crux raises the 12-month PT $175 → $225 (base) and lifts FY27 numbers across revenue, EBITDA, and EPS.

## Key Takeaways

- **Q1 FY27 actuals (vs. company guide bar)**: Revenue **$291M** (+16% YoY), **Adj EPS $0.51** (+34% YoY), Adj op margin **20.4%**, Adj EBITDA **$66.4M**. **Data-center revenue $71.6M (+14% QoQ, +39% YoY)** — the acceleration shape Crux explicitly wanted after the Q4 FY26 print only showed 26% YoY DC growth. **LoRa $44.5M** (+12% QoQ, +14% YoY) — beat Crux's expectation; targeting >15% sequential in Q2.
- **Q2 FY27 guide (the load-bearing signal)**: **Revenue $328M midpoint (+27% YoY)**, **Adj GM 54.0%**, semiconductor product GM 62.1%, **Adj EPS $0.61 (+49% YoY)**, Adj EBITDA $79.2M. The data-center component is guided **+35% sequential / +85% YoY**, putting Q2 DC ~**$96-97M**.
- **Crux's data-center framework explicitly shifts**: The old guide implied at least ~$335M DC in FY27. With H1 DC alone at ~$168M and management saying acceleration *continues* into H2, the implicit H2 need to hit the old floor is less than the Q2 run rate. **"I am no longer wondering whether data center grows more than 50% this year. It is whether it grows closer to 90%, 100%, or more. That is a very different conversation."** This is the framing change of the note.
- **Product-cycle proof points hit across lanes**:
  - **CopperEdge real shipment language confirmed**: 1.6T CopperEdge IC shipments started to cable partners for deployment at a US hyperscaler. Crux's deeper read: ACC cables are the *first* visible revenue lane but the platform story is **board-level signal integrity** (switch boards, GPU boards, ASIC boards, connectors, active backplanes) — "this quarter pushed the story toward the platform version."
  - **800G FiberEdge remains strong** with Semtech qualified into new sockets including **some sole-source positions**.
  - **1.6T begins entering revenue in Q2** via new DSP-based designs; **larger 1.6T ramp is H2-weighted, backed by design wins / bookings / backlog already in place**. Setup is "layering 1.6T on top of a strong 800G base" — not replacement.
  - **LPO 800G FiberEdge linear TIA + driver deployed across several leading hyperscalers in US and China**; LPO revenue grew sequentially. **Growing hyperscaler conviction around 1.6T LRO and LPO for the first layer of scale-out fabric driven by power savings.**
  - **XPO and NPO future optionality**: Semtech participating in MSAs; many XPO module designs already incorporate FiberEdge chips. HieFo gives Semtech a path to the light source as optics moves closer to the ASIC.
- **HieFo InP gain-chip — capacity is now the gating constraint, not demand**: **Demand exceeds supply by ~3×**. Management plans to **expand capacity 3-4× by year-end FY27, then another 3-4× by end of FY28** (compounded ~10-16× expansion over 2 years). CW lasers sampled to several major module manufacturers with strong feedback. Discussion of **coherent light, DWDM lasers, and CPO light-source opportunities** (2028+). Strategic shift: SMTC used to sit around the optical module (TIAs + drivers); HieFo gives them path to the **light-source layer** — if SMTC co-optimizes lasers + drivers + TIAs + other photonic/electronic components, **content-per-module goes from high single digits in 800G toward something structurally larger at 1.6T / 3.2T**. "How a small acquisition can change the long-term shape of the company." Pairs with [[AXT (AXTI)]] / [[Sivers Semiconductors (SIVE.ST)]] / [[Lumentum (LITE)]] InP-laser ecosystem analysis.
- **Updated valuation framework (the headline)**:
  - **12-month: downside $130-150 / base $225 / bull $275** (raised from prior $175 base — "the numbers changed, the guide changed, the visibility changed, and the framework has to change with it").
  - **24-month: downside $175-200 / base $325 / bull $425**.
  - **FY27 revenue**: conservative $1.30-1.34B / **base $1.38-1.42B** / bull $1.45-1.52B (Crux working number ~$1.40B; needs ~$390M/quarter in H2 with Q2 already guided $328M).
  - **FY27 data-center revenue**: conservative $400-425M / **base $440-475M** / bull $500M+. Doubling FY26's $223M is now the base case.
  - **FY27 Adj EBITDA**: conservative $305-325M / **base $340-365M** / bull $385-420M.
  - **FY27 Adj EPS**: conservative $2.35-2.55 / **base $2.70-2.95** / bull $3.20+.
- **Position management & psychology**: Crux is **already heavy** in SMTC after the post-print run; would only size up further on a pullback to **~$143**, which he does *not* expect. Plan: **stay long**. The 3 watch-items: (1) Q2 DC revenue actually hits the implied $96-97M, (2) management keeps using accelerating-H2 language, (3) CopperEdge / 1.6T FiberEdge / LPO / LRO / HieFo keep moving from strong language into visible revenue contribution.
- **Operating leverage and model cleanup**: Q1 Signal Integrity gross margin dipped from Q4 due to first quarter running the newly acquired InP facility (integration effect, not structural). Q2 guide tells the real story — R&D up in DC + LoRa while SG&A declines as % of revenue. **Cellular module divestiture (legacy Sierra Wireless drag) in final stages** — leaves Semtech as DC engine + LoRa engine + high-end consumer/sensing. - **Read-through for the broader AI networking stack**: Strong 800G + accelerating 1.6T + LPO/LRO adoption + CopperEdge traction + InP laser capacity pressure → AI networking cycle is still expanding. Implications for [[Credo Technology (CRDO)]], [[MACOM Technology (MTSI)]], [[Marvell Technology (MRVL)]], [[Broadcom (AVGO)]], [[Coherent (COHR)]], [[Lumentum (LITE)]], [[Applied Optoelectronics (AAOI)]], [[Viavi (VIAV)]] et al. **Crux's specific tactical call**: "another strong tailwind for **MTSI and CRDO** especially" because companies with **both copper + optics exposure are catching bids**; doesn't break the optics-only thesis ("they will get their bids again soon enough").

> Data center did $223 million in fiscal 2026. Management's prior guidance implied at least roughly $335 million in fiscal 2027. After this quarter, that number looks much more like a floor.

To hit the old $335 million-plus target, Semtech would only need roughly $167 million in the entire second half, which is less than the Q2 run rate. Management is saying the opposite. They expect acceleration into the second half.

### 800G & 1.6T

> This was still primarily an 800G quarter. Q1 was mostly 800G FRO and LPO. 1.6T is beginning to enter revenue but the bigger ramp is second-half weighted.
>
> This makes the setup really clean. Semtech does not need to replace a weakening 800G base with 1.6T. It is layering 1.6T on top of a 800G base that remains strong. Demand for 800G FiberEdge TIAs stayed strong across a wide range of transceiver programs, and both established and emerging module suppliers have qualified Semtech into new sockets, including some sole-source positions.
>
> Q2 is where 1.6T FiberEdge first begins showing up in revenue through new DSP-based designs. The larger 1.6T ramp comes in the second half, backed by design wins, bookings, and backlog already in place. Evaluation becomes design win, design win becomes booking, booking becomes backlog, backlog becomes shipment. Semtech is moving through that chain across several product families at the same time, which is exactly why the guide stepped up as sharply as it did.

### CopperEdge

> Last quarter, management said CopperEdge production shipments for a 1.6T ACC hyperscaler deployment should begin in Q1. This quarter, they confirmed CopperEdge 1.6T IC shipments started to cable partners for deployment at a U.S. hyperscaler. Real shipment language!
>
> I care about CopperEdge for a reason that goes beyond ACC cables. ACC is the first visible revenue lane. It's a copper cable with active signal-conditioning built into the cable ends, sitting between passive DAC and DSP-heavy AEC, offering better reach than passive copper, much lower power and latency than DSP-based alternatives. That is useful inside the rack.
>
> But the real CopperEdge story is board-level signal integrity. The same linear equalization and redriving can help signals move across switch boards, GPU boards, ASIC boards, connectors, and active backplanes. Management talked about onboard and active backplane momentum and expects additional design wins in coming quarters across all three use cases. One hyperscaler ramp is a necessary proof point but more hyperscalers evaluating and broader board-level use cases developing is what turns a proof point into a platform.
>
> This quarter pushed the story toward the platform version.

### LPO and LRO

> A traditional optical module often uses a DSP inside the module to clean up the signal. That saves the link, but it uses power, creates heat, adds cost, and captures value inside digital silicon. LPO removes or reduces that DSP burden. It saves power but makes the system less forgiving because there is less digital cleanup inside the module. That shifts more burden toward the analog front end, which is where Semtech lives.
>
> Management said its 800G LPO FiberEdge linear TIA and driver solutions are now deployed by several leading hyperscalers across the U.S. and China, and LPO revenue grew sequentially. They also said growing hyperscaler conviction around 1.6T LRO and LPO for the first layer of scale-out fabric is being driven by power savings. Early, but moving in the right direction.
>
> XPO and NPO belong in the future optionality bucket for now, but Semtech is participating in both MSAs and many XPO module designs are already incorporating FiberEdge chips. As optics moves closer to the ASIC, Semtech wants to stay attached across the signal chain. HieFo gives them a path to do that all the way to the light source.

### HieFo

> The acquisition is looking much more specific.
>
> HieFo gain-chip demand already exceeds supply by roughly 3x. Management expects to increase capacity 3x to 4x by year-end, then another 3x to 4x by end of next year, which is a serious capacity commitment, and it only makes sense if customer pull is real. On CW lasers, Semtech has sampled products to several major module manufacturers and is getting strong feedback. Management also discussed coherent light, DWDM lasers, and CPO-related light-source opportunities. Most of that is 2028-oriented, but I like to hear the future vision.
>
> The strategic shift is that Semtech used to sit around the optical module through TIAs and drivers. HieFo gives the company a path toward the light-source layer. If Semtech can co-optimize lasers, drivers, TIAs, and other photonic and electronic components, the content story gets much larger at 1.6T, 3.2T, and beyond. The bull case for content-per-module goes from high single digits in 800G toward something structurally larger at the next nodes. That is how a small acquisition can change the long-term shape of the company.

### Copper vs Optics

> I touched on this a good bit in my full report, so ill make this quick.
>
> Inside the rack, copper can make sense because distances are short, latency is critical, cost is relevant, and power is limited. As speeds rise, passive copper gets harder, which creates room for active copper and linear equalizers. Across racks, rows, buildings, and campuses, optics becomes essential. The right question is where each technology belongs, not which one wins.
>
> Semtech has exposure to both. CopperEdge helps electrical signals survive through copper paths. FiberEdge, TIAs, drivers, LPO, LRO, XPO, NPO, and HieFo help Semtech capture value as data turns into light. The AI infrastructure buildout needs both copper and optics playing specific roles across different parts of the stack. That is a better world for Semtech than one where either side wins completely.

> Signal Integrity gross margin dipped from Q4 because this was the first quarter running the newly acquired InP facility. I view that as an integration effect and not a structural issue. The Q2 semiconductor product gross margin guide tells the real story.
>
> The company is spending more on R&D in data center and LoRa while SG&A declines as a percentage of revenue. Spend where return can be high. Get leverage where overhead should scale slower than sales. When revenue grows, mix improves, and operating leverage shows up at the same time, earnings can grow faster than sales. Q2 guidance is already showing that dynamic.

### Capacity is now the gating factor

> Demand risk has come down significantly. Execution risk is now the central question.
>
> Management was direct…capacity is king. They started working on availability roughly 18 months ago and are building further, aiming to double or triple current capacity. That is a supply-constrained problem, which is the version of this problem I would rather have. But if supply lags demand, revenue gets delayed. If HieFo capacity expansion takes longer than planned, the laser opportunity moves slower. If module customers change architectures, some programs shift.
>
> One other thing worth flagging is that free cash flow was $28 million in Q1, down from Q4. Management called out fiscal 2026 annual bonus payments and capex stepping up as the company invests behind the ramp. The business is still generating cash and Semtech now has enough financial flexibility to support the AI interconnect ramp without the balance-sheet overhang that existed after the Sierra Wireless deal.

### The read-through for the broader AI networking stack

> This quarter is also useful for thinking about the rest of the optical and interconnect universe.
>
> Strong 800G demand, accelerating 1.6T, LPO and LRO adoption, CopperEdge traction, and pressure on InP laser capacity all suggest the AI networking cycle is still expanding. That has implications for Credo, MACOM, Marvell, Broadcom, Coherent, Lumentum, Applied Optoelectronics, VIAVI, and others across the AI connectivity stack.
>
> The broader point is that the AI infrastructure trade is still moving deeper into networking, optics, copper, signal integrity, and photonics. Semtech just gave us another strong data point for that thesis.
>
> I think this will be another strong tailwind for MTSI and CRDO especially. Companies that have exposure to both copper + optics are catching bids. But this does not break the optics thesis in any way. They will get their bids again soon enough.

### The bull case, the risks, and where I go from here

> The bull case is that Semtech becomes one of the cleaner public ways to own the AI interconnect bottleneck across copper, optics, analog signal integrity, and lasers. The strongest version is that 800G remains strong while 1.6T layers in, CopperEdge expands beyond ACC into boards and backplanes, LPO and LRO keep gaining share as hyperscalers chase power savings, HieFo turns into a constrained photonics asset with real customer pull, and LoRa provides a steady second engine throughout.
>
> If those ramps compound rather than replace each other, Semtech moves from $223 million of data-center revenue in fiscal 2026 toward $500 million-plus in fiscal 2027, then toward a much larger fiscal 2028 number as 1.6T, CopperEdge, and HieFo keep scaling. In that version, the market starts valuing Semtech as a scarce AI physical-layer platform.
>
> The bear case is that Q2 hits the guide but second-half language softens. CopperEdge stays tied mostly to one large U.S. hyperscaler. HieFo capacity takes longer than planned. LPO and LRO stay real but smaller than hoped. In that version, Semtech is still a better business than it was a year ago, but the multiple compresses because the ramp looks narrower than this quarter suggested.
>
> The base case is that Q2 data center lands around $96 million to $97 million, second-half 1.6T ramps cleanly, CopperEdge keeps broadening, and Semtech exits fiscal 2027 with data-center revenue somewhere in the mid-$400 million range.
>
> The three things I am watching most closely are 1) whether Q2 data-center revenue hits the implied guide, 2) whether management keeps talking about accelerating second-half demand, and 3) whether CopperEdge, 1.6T FiberEdge, LPO, LRO, and HieFo keep moving from strong language into visible revenue contribution. If all three stay on track, the updated model below may still be too low.
>
> My plan is to stay long. I am raising my model as well. I am already heavy in this position especially after the recent run with other names taking breathers. Therefore in order for me to size up from here, I would need a really solid entry around $143. I do NOT expect it to get there. This is just my own framework and reflective of my own strategy and oversized position.
