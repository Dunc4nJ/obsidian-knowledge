## What Soitec Does - In More Detail

Soitec makes engineered semiconductor wafers. A standard wafer is mostly a starting surface. An engineered wafer is built with specific layers, materials, thicknesses, and physical properties that shape the performance of whatever gets built on top of it.

*Engineered wafer concept — purpose-built substrate, not a commodity input.*
![[cruxcapitalgroup-soitec-deep-dive-must-read-004.png]]

In RF, power, and photonics, that is really important. The starting wafer influences leakage, signal behavior, thermal performance, and optical loss. It becomes part of the process architecture rather than a generic input.

For the AI datacenter angle, the key product is Photonics-SOI. SOI means silicon-on-insulator.

*Silicon-on-insulator (SOI) structure — thin silicon device layer over buried oxide over silicon handle; foundries pattern waveguides/modulators/splitters in the top silicon.*
![[cruxcapitalgroup-soitec-deep-dive-must-read-005.png]]

The structure is a thin silicon device layer on top, an insulating oxide layer beneath it, and a thicker silicon handle wafer underneath for mechanical support. In silicon photonics, foundries pattern waveguides, modulators, splitters, and couplers in that top silicon layer. The buried oxide helps confine light in the device layer. Small differences in layer thickness, surface quality, or uniformity affect optical performance directly. At volume, that makes the substrate a critical input rather than a commodity.

## The Business Today

*Soitec product taxonomy — RF-SOI, POI, FD-SOI, Photonics-SOI, LNOI, SmartSiC across business segments.*
![[cruxcapitalgroup-soitec-deep-dive-must-read-006.png]]

Soitec is currently being pulled in two directions.

The legacy side of the business is under pressure. In Q3 FY2026, Mobile Communications generated €90 million of revenue, down 37% organically year over year. Automotive and Industrial generated €16 million, down 32% organically. These are the parts of the business weighing on the reported numbers.

The growth side looks very different. Edge and Cloud AI generated €54 million in Q3, up 27% organically year over year, and now represents 34% of quarterly revenue. This is the segment where Photonics-SOI sits, alongside FD-SOI, Soitec’s low-power silicon-on-insulator platform for edge computing, connected devices, wearables, industrial, healthcare, and sensing applications.

That distinction is the entire setup. Soitec is still being suppressed through the lens of a damaged mobile materials business, but the segment tied to AI infrastructure is already moving in the opposite direction.

The reporting does create a problem for me. Edge and Cloud AI is broader than Photonics-SOI, so the segment growth does not tell us exactly how much revenue is coming from photonics wafers alone. That is why the market still has to triangulate the Photonics-SOI story through segment growth, management commentary, Tower’s silicon photonics capacity ramp, and broader industry demand for optical interconnects.

The full-year baseline shows why the recovery could matter. In FY2025, Soitec generated €891 million of revenue, a 33.5% EBITDA margin, a 15.2% current EBIT margin, and €26 million of positive free cash flow. The company has already shown what the model can look like when volumes and factory loading are healthier. FY2026 shows the trough. The upside comes from reloading the existing manufacturing base while the mix shifts toward higher-growth AI-related substrates.

RF-SOI is the damaged legacy profit engine. POI is the healthier mobile offset. Photonics-SOI is the AI growth engine. LNOI is the longer-term optical option. SmartSiC is impaired optionality after the H1 FY2026 write-down.

## The Qualification Moat

Soitec’s core process technology is Smart Cut, which enables precise layer transfer with strong uniformity. The value comes from making high-spec wafers repeatedly, at volume, with enough consistency for foundries to trust them and build qualified process platforms around them.

*Soitec Smart Cut layer-transfer process — the core proprietary tech enabling uniform high-spec wafers at volume.*
![[cruxcapitalgroup-soitec-deep-dive-must-read-007.png]]

Once a silicon photonics foundry qualifies a specific substrate stack, customers design their chips using a process design kit built around that platform. Device behavior, design rules, performance expectations, and yield learning all become tied to that stack. Swapping the substrate supplier then means fresh qualification work, new characterization, customer revalidation, and yield risk.

That is the moat. It is strongest where the substrate is directly tied into a qualified photonics manufacturing flow. Broad SOI and photonics-grade SOI are different conversations. GlobalWafers can be credible in broad SOI while still needing to prove itself in high-volume photonics-grade SOI. Until there is clearer evidence of qualified high-volume photonics-grade supply from another vendor, Soitec appears strongest at the harder end of that market.

## The Mobile Correction

RF-SOI is still the piece weighing on Soitec’s reported numbers.

*RF-SOI inventory destocking dynamics — Soitec under-shipping by 200–300K wafers per quarter to clear customer inventory (2.5M → 2.0M 8-inch-equivalent wafers Sep→Dec).*
![[cruxcapitalgroup-soitec-deep-dive-must-read-008.png]]

During the last cycle, customers built too much inventory across the smartphone RF supply chain. Soitec then chose to ship below end demand so the channel could work down excess wafers instead of carrying the problem forward. Management has put numbers around the reset with customer RF-SOI inventory moved from roughly 2.5 million 8-inch equivalent wafers in September to around 2.0 million by December, with Soitec under-shipping by roughly 200,000 to 300,000 wafers per quarter.

That creates the near-term pain. Lower shipments reduce revenue, and lower volume weakens factory loading. On the Q3 FY2026 call, management quantified fab underloading as roughly a 600 basis point drag on gross margin, with foreign exchange adding another headwind.

This is also why the recovery can be powerful. Soitec has a fixed-cost manufacturing base. When utilization falls, margins compress quickly. When volumes return, the same structure can create strong operating leverage. So RF-SOI is dragging the model today, but inventory normalization can become a margin recovery lever if demand stabilizes.

## POI Gives Mobile a Healthier Second Leg

POI gives Soitec exposure to a different part of the smartphone RF front end.

*POI (piezoelectric-on-insulator) value chain — Soitec's POI wafers feed Skyworks Sky5 filter platforms; filters become more important as RF environment gets denser.*
![[cruxcapitalgroup-soitec-deep-dive-must-read-009.png]]

RF-SOI is tied to switches and other RF front-end functions. POI is tied to filters. That distinction is useful because modern phones are carrying more bands, more carrier aggregation, and denser signal environments. As that RF environment gets more complex, filters become more important because they help separate and manage signals inside tighter device footprints.

That is why the Skyworks agreement is relevant. Soitec signed a multi-year supply agreement for POI wafers for Skyworks’ Sky5 platform, with the announcement highlighting high-volume manufacturing, advanced packaging compatibility, compactness, robustness, and dense signal environments up to 3+ GHz.

POI gives Soitec a healthier mobile offset tied to continued RF complexity. If RF-SOI inventories normalize while POI keeps scaling with a major customer like Skyworks, the mobile segment can recover with a better mix than the headline inventory correction suggests.

## Photonics-SOI and the AI Infrastructure Angle

AI datacenters need more bandwidth, lower power per bit, and better signal integrity as clusters grow larger. Electrical links face hard constraints on distance, heat, and signal integrity at scale. Optics keep moving deeper into the network for that reason.

*Photonics-SOI's role in the AI datacenter optical roadmap — pluggables (1.6T), co-packaged optics (CPO), and longer-term optical I/O.*
![[cruxcapitalgroup-soitec-deep-dive-must-read-010.png]]

Soitec participates at the wafer layer across three drivers. High-speed pluggable optics, especially as the industry moves toward 1.6T, is the current volume opportunity. Co-packaged optics (CPO) moves the optical interface closer to the switch ASIC or compute complex, targeting higher bandwidth density and lower power by shortening the electrical path. Soitec has said CPO chip architectures can integrate photonics for around 30% energy savings versus current transceiver-based solutions, and that CPO components rely specifically on Photonics-SOI substrates. Optical I/O is earlier and more speculative, but it is the larger long-term idea if optical links move all the way to processors and chiplets.

*Silicon photonics integration value chain — Soitec sits at the wafer/substrate layer feeding foundries like Tower.*
![[cruxcapitalgroup-soitec-deep-dive-must-read-011.png]]

Soitec wins if silicon photonics keeps becoming a larger part of the AI networking roadmap. It has no need to pick every optical module winner. Management described Photonics-SOI as “very dynamic” in Q3 FY2026, pointing to continued demand across pluggable transceivers and co-packaged optics. That is directionally consistent with everything happening at the foundry layer.

## Tower

Tower Semiconductor is the most important external datapoint for this thesis. Tower has announced $920 million of silicon photonics and SiGe investments, targeting full qualification by Q4 2026, with capacity expected to exceed five times its Q4 2025 annualized run-rate. More than 70% of that silicon photonics capacity is already reserved or in the process of being reserved through 2028, backed by customer prepayments. Tower has also announced work around 1.6T datacenter optical modules for NVIDIA networking protocols.

Tower captures foundry process value. Soitec captures substrate value. The pull-through depends on supplier share, wafer mix, qualifications, substrate pricing, and actual wafer starts. Tower is a demand signal, a strong one, but still indirect.

When a foundry commits nearly a billion dollars to silicon photonics and reserves most of that capacity before the lines are even built, the direction of the thesis gets a lot easier to hold.

## LNOI: Real Technology, Secondary Priority

Lithium niobate on insulator has attractive optical properties for high-speed modulation. Soitec has positioned it as a complement to silicon photonics for optical applications beyond the 1.6T roadmap.

*LNOI (lithium niobate on insulator) — high-speed modulation optical properties, positioned as complement to silicon photonics beyond the 1.6T roadmap; treated as option value.*
![[cruxcapitalgroup-soitec-deep-dive-must-read-012.png]]

I want clearer evidence of customer traction, qualification progress, and revenue contribution before giving LNOI meaningful weight in the model. Photonics-SOI is the current AI growth engine. LNOI is future option value, and treating it as anything more right now would be getting ahead of the evidence. If the optical roadmap needs additional material platforms to keep pushing bandwidth and power efficiency further out, LNOI could extend the story.

## Risks Worth Taking Seriously

CPO could take longer than expected, pushing the larger volume opportunity further out. Silicon photonics content per optical link at 1.6T could disappoint. Tower could ramp without visible Soitec pull-through, forcing harder questions about supplier share. GlobalWafers could prove itself in high-volume photonics-grade SOI faster than expected. Mobile could stay weak longer. LNOI could remain a roadmap item well past when bulls hope it converts. And the stock has already re-rated, meaning the next leg requires evidence in order to underwrite reasonably.

The China angle is worth watching. Soitec and NSIG extended their licensing framework for 10 years, with no new technology transfer and a joint commitment to strengthen Soitec’s SOI IP rights in China. That supports the IP-control argument, but China localization pressure is a background risk in any strategic semiconductor materials story.
