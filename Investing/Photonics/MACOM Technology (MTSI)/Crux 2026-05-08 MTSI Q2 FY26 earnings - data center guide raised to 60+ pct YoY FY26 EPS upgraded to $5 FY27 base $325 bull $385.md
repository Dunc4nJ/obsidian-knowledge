The subtitle was clear: **Optics, Copper, Defense. MACOM does it all.**

After this quarter, that framing still works. But the main update is that my model was too conservative.

When I wrote the original report, MACOM was guiding for data-center revenue to grow 35% to 40% in FY2026. After this quarter, management raised that to over 60%. That is a serious reset.

Revenue came in at $289M, up 6.4% sequentially and over 22% year over year. Adjusted EPS was $1.09. Adjusted gross margin was 58.5%, up 90 bps sequentially. Book-to-bill was 1.5x, the largest bookings quarter in company history.

The segment numbers were strong across the board too. Industrial and Defense revenue was $120.7M. Data Center revenue was $98.2M. Telecom revenue was $70.1M. All three grew sequentially, but data center was the standout, up 14.5% from Q1.

The Q3 guide was the part that really changed the model. Management guided total revenue to $331M to $339M, adjusted gross margin of 59% to 60%, and adjusted EPS of $1.31 to $1.37. Data center should grow about 35% sequentially in Q3, which means it could be running at roughly $130M+ per quarter next quarter.

That is a major step-up from where I was modeling the company two months ago.

### The Thesis Update

My original MACOM thesis was that the company sits in an attractive part of the AI infrastructure stack, making the analog and photonic components inside high-speed optical links: TIAs, drivers, photodetectors, lasers, and equalizers.

That thesis got stronger this quarter.

Current growth is being driven by 800G and 1.6T PAM4 products, pluggable optical modules, optical cable production, and rising demand for 200G/lane photodetectors. The important part is that this growth is showing up before the bigger CW laser opportunity becomes a model driver. The current business is being carried by products already in production. Real customer demand. Real backlog.

CW lasers remain the longer-term upside layer.

### The CW Laser Timing Correction

In March, I was a bit too aggressive on CW laser timing. MACOM's 75mW-class lasers have strong optical performance, and customers have tested and validated that performance. But reliability work and process qualification are still ongoing. The CEO said he would avoid putting CW lasers into FY2026 models and sounded cautious about FY2027 as well.

I would now treat CW lasers as a FY2028 upside layer. That actually makes this quarter look better. The data-center business is accelerating without needing CW lasers to carry the story. Near-term thesis is cleaner. 1.6T is driving revenue today. and CW lasers remain the call option.

There was also a smaller laser point that I think is worth discussing. As competitors shift more attention toward higher-power CW lasers for silicon photonics, MACOM said this is creating a gap in legacy DFB lasers used in 100G data-center modules. That is less exciting than the CW laser story, but it could become a near-term revenue pocket because MACOM already has those products ready today.

### Photodetectors, Copper, and Coherent Light

MACOM talked about growing demand for 200G/lane photodetectors supporting 800G and 1.6T optical connectivity, with product advantages including low dark current, high sensitivity, self-hermetic chips, integrated lensing, and stacked photodetector plus TIA designs.

So the photodetector receives the light and the TIA converts that signal into something usable. If MACOM can supply both, the company becomes more valuable to module makers. The 1.6T ramp is pulling through MACOM's analog chips and its own photonic devices simultaneously. That is the content expansion I wanted to see.

On copper, MACOM continues to push linear equalizers for copper interconnects at 800G and 1.6T, including applications closer to compute. This is important for portfolio construction. My existing positions are heavily weighted toward pure optical names. MACOM gives optical exposure while also participating if copper gets stretched further inside racks. If more links stay copper, MACOM participates. If more links go optical, MACOM participates there too. I have the same general principle for my investments into SMTC (Semtech) and CRDO (Credo).

On coherent light, management said coherent light belongs in the data-center category, while traditional metro and long-haul DCI sits in telecom. As AI workloads spread across buildings, campuses, and regional clusters, the line between data center and telecom keeps blurring. MACOM is positioning for scale-up, scale-out, and scale-across. Coherent light could become a higher-end scale-across layer over time, especially as hyperscalers push for higher bandwidth and better power efficiency across longer links. Still early, but now part of the roadmap I am tracking.

---

### Defense, LEO, and the IQE Deal

Industrial and Defense revenue was $120.7M, also a record. Management said I&D grew 22% in the first half of FY2026 and now expects full-year I&D growth above 20%. MACOM's defense exposure spans radar, missile defense, drone defense, electronic warfare, secure communications, RF-over-fiber, and space-based sensors. These are long-cycle markets with high qualification barriers, which gives the company a more durable earnings profile than pure AI infrastructure names.

On telecom and LEO, management said they expect momentum from telecom as they enter FY2027 due to LEO space production programs, with exposure across payloads, gateways, terminal opportunities, free-space optics, phased-array antennas, D2D links, and high-frequency satellite backhaul. This will be lumpy. Treat LEO as a FY2027 kicker rather than a FY2026 driver.

The IQE investment is a supply-chain signal worth talking about, especially since a lot of my readers are invested into IQE. MACOM agreed to invest £45M into IQE alongside long-term supply agreements for epitaxial services, with management tying this directly to indium phosphide and silicon carbide supply-chain security. If 1.6T, 3.2T, silicon photonics, photodetectors, CW lasers, and higher-frequency defense systems all keep scaling, InP and SiC supply become even more strategic. MACOM is preparing itself.

---

### Margins and Capacity

Adjusted gross margin was 58.5% in Q2. Q3 guidance is 59% to 60%, and management previously talked about exiting the year around 59%. They now sound closer to 60%. This is about more than demand. It reflects utilization, yield improvement, internal manufacturing, product mix, and operating leverage. Q3 adjusted operating margin is expected around 30%.

On capacity, MACOM is expanding North Carolina wafer capacity by about 30% with roughly $15M to $16M of investment. Management said the company can pursue the next revenue target through incremental expansion inside existing facilities rather than a new greenfield fab. Capacity expansion story, but capital efficient so far.

The cash flow profile also helps explain why the market gives MACOM a premium multiple. Management expects fiscal 2026 cash flow from operations to exceed $300M, and the company ended Q2 with roughly $665M of cash and short-term investments. MACOM also has $202M of deferred tax assets, which should keep the adjusted tax rate low for the next few years.

### Valuation and Pricing Framework

My original MACOM valuation framework was too conservative. I tend to lean conservative with what I can underwrite.

In March, I used roughly $4.40 of FY2026 adjusted EPS as the base case. That supported a NTM base-case range of roughly $200 to $240, with a bull case around $260 to $300.

That framework has to move higher.

MACOM already earned $1.02 in Q1 and $1.09 in Q2. Q3 guidance is $1.31 to $1.37. At the midpoint, MACOM should already be around $3.45 of EPS through the first three quarters of FY2026.

My updated FY2026 EPS estimate is now around $5.00.

My updated FY2027 EPS estimate is around $6.50.

My updated FY2028 EPS estimate is around $8.25.

That gives me a cleaner price framework.

For year-end 2026, I would value MACOM on FY2027 earnings power.

My bear-case scenario is $255. That assumes roughly $6.00 of FY2027 EPS and a lower premium multiple if growth cools.

My base-case scenario is $325. That assumes roughly $6.50 of FY2027 EPS and a premium multiple because data center remains strong, photodetectors scale, defense keeps growing, and margins hold near 60%.

My bull-case scenario is $385. That assumes roughly $7.00 of FY2027 EPS and continued scarcity valuation as 1.6T stays strong, 3.2T design activity begins layering in, coherent light and copper equalizers contribute, and LEO becomes more visible.

That puts my year-end 2026 target framework at:

**Bear: $255**

**Base: $325**

**Bull: $385**

At a current price around $350, MACOM is already trading above my base case and closer to the bull case.

For year-end 2027, I would value MACOM on FY2028 earnings power.

My bear-case scenario is $285. That assumes roughly $7.25 of FY2028 EPS and a lower multiple if data-center growth normalizes.

My base-case scenario is $375. That assumes roughly $8.25 of FY2028 EPS and a sustained premium multiple as MACOM moves closer to a larger revenue base with 60% gross margins and 30%+ operating margins.

My bull-case scenario is $500. That assumes roughly $9.25 of FY2028 EPS and a continued AI optical scarcity multiple if 3.2T, photodetectors, coherent light, LEO, and eventually CW lasers all become visible growth drivers.

That puts my year-end 2027 target framework at:

**Bear: $285**

**Base: $375**

**Bull: $500**

This is the cleanest way I see it now. It will definitely adjust over time.

The business is stronger than my original model. The earnings base moved higher. The margin story improved. The backlog is stronger. Data center growth reset from 35–40% to over 60%.

But the stock also moved aggressively.

At $350, the market is already pricing MACOM like a premium AI optical / compound semiconductor platform. That price can work if the company keeps compounding toward the FY2027 and FY2028 bull-case path. But the easy money from my original framework has been pulled forward.

So my personal updated view:

**Below $300:** attractive entry zone, acknowledging that there still is meaningful downside risk if something happens where these companies stop being rewarded their multiple.

**Around $325:** fair value on my updated base case

**Around $350:** paying for a good part of the bull case

**Above $385:** stock needs a much clearer path toward FY2028 upside

**$500:** bull-case outcome if MACOM keeps executing and the market keeps assigning a scarcity multiple

Please keep in mind these are not price targets, just a way for me to frame how I view this company. And as I noted, I tend to lean more conservative than the market in many ways, so look at this through that lens.
