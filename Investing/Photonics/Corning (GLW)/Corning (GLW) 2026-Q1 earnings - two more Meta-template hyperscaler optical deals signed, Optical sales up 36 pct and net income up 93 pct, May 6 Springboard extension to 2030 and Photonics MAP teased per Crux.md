# Corning (GLW) Q1 2026 earnings — Crux Capital breakdown

The defining update from [[Corning (GLW)]] Q1 2026 is that the Meta optical deal (multiyear, up to $6B) is now visibly a *template* rather than a one-off — Corning signed two additional large, long-term hyperscaler agreements of similar size and duration, with more in process. Crux's read: the story is migrating from "optical products supplier" to "customer-backed AI optical capacity partner," with risk-sharing contracts protecting cash flow and returns through a heavy expansion cycle (2026 capex ~$1.7B). Headline Q1 financials were strong — Core sales $4.35B (+18% YoY), Core EPS $0.70 (+30%), Optical Communications $1.846B (+36%) with Optical net income $387M (+93%). The Q2 guide ($4.6B / EPS $0.73–$0.77) absorbed a ~$30M temporary solar-shutdown expense, which Crux argues spooked a high-bar tape but did not damage the thesis. May 6 framed as the next major catalyst: Springboard extension to 2030, an explicit upgrade, and a new **Photonics Market-Access Platform** aimed at Gen AI OEM customers — with management hinting that the "scale-up" piece (CPO / optical I/O closer to the compute layer) may now be closer than investors assumed.

### The Quarter

Corning grew revenue, expanded margins, improved ROIC, signed two more hyperscaler agreements, and set up May 6 as the next major catalyst.

The headline numbers:

* **Core sales:** $4.35B, up 18% year over year
* **Core EPS:** $0.70, up 30% year over year
* **Core operating margin:** 20.2%, up 220 bps year over year
* **Core gross margin:** 39.1%, up 120 bps year over year
* **ROIC:** 13.5%, up 190 bps year over year

Growth with margin expansion. Since the Q4 2023 Springboard starting point, Corning has grown sales 33%, EPS 79%, operating margin 390 bps, and ROIC 470 bps. Management called the trajectory "powerful." That framing is fair.

---

### Optical Was The Star

Optical Communications delivered the quarter.

* **Q1 Optical sales:** $1.846B, up 36% year over year
* **Q1 Optical net income:** $387M, up 93% year over year
* **Enterprise sales:** up 36% year over year
* **Carrier sales:** up 36% year over year

Growth came from Gen AI products, data-center interconnect, and fiber-to-the-home. Enterprise and Carrier both grew 36%, which matters because it means the AI optical demand is pulling through two separate channels simultaneously. More on the Carrier angle below.

---

### Meta Became The Template

This was the biggest update from the quarter, and it deserves to be understood fully.

Corning already had the Meta agreement, a multiyear, up-to-$6B deal tied to optical fiber, cable, and connectivity for AI data centers. Now Corning has two more, each similar in size and duration. Management also said they are working to conclude additional agreements beyond those two. They also said that they will let the customers disclose their arrangements as they so choose. When the news with Meta came out, it was a great catalyst for the stock, both for visibility and demand signal.

That sequence changes the quality of the story considerably. Meta was a major proof point when it was announced. Now it looks like the first visible example of a broader model that is actively replicating itself across the hyperscaler landscape.

Hyperscalers need optical capacity. Corning has the manufacturing base, product portfolio, and technical capability to deliver it. Corning expands capacity. Customers help de-risk that expansion through long-term commitments, pricing structures, and shared risk mechanisms.

> "I can share that these deals are very significant, and they share the risk and rewards of the required expansions with our strategic customers."

That is a meaningfully different setup than a supplier building capacity ahead of demand and hoping orders arrive. Corning is tying expansion to committed customer demand and using contracts to protect returns.

I like the customer diversification bits we got. Corning wants exposure to the AI buildout broadly rather than any single winner. That is the correct approach for a physical-layer supplier, and it helps explain why the long-term agreement structure keeps repeating.

> "...we are trying to serve all of our customers, and we're trying to get very balanced coverage so that we aren't dependent on any one model maker or any one AI cloud provider."

Corning now has Meta, two additional signed hyperscaler agreements, and more potential agreements still in process.

This looks less like a normal fiber cycle and more like a customer-backed AI infrastructure capacity cycle. Management was precise about what they are trying to achieve with the agreement structures.

> "What we seek to do with these arrangements is to make sure we're appropriately sharing the risk of the required expansions with our customers in a way that assures return to our shareholders."
>
> "Overall, we feel very good about having accelerated cash flow and on really not going through any sort of significant dip due to an investment cycle, largely because of the risk-sharing agreements that we are seeking with our customers..."

Corning is still investing heavily, with 2026 capex around $1.7B, but management expects significantly more free cash flow this year while using customer financial support to help fund the required expansions. The agreement structures are designed to protect cash flow and returns through the expansion rather than sacrifice them.

### Optical Margins

Analysts pushed management on whether Optical profitability could eventually move above Display-like levels.

> "The simple answer is yes... Our goal is to create so much value that, you know, this becomes an all-time star for us as a company."

He tied the upside to three things: the rate of adoption, the value of Corning's innovations, and the size of the moat Corning builds. The CFO added that Optical has a different financial model than Display. It still requires investment but is less capital intensive than pure glass melting and forming, which means the return profile can be very attractive.

The pricing environment is also favorable. Management said capacity is scarce and customers are securing access to Corning's newest optical products rather than buying commodity fiber. When Corning creates value for the customer through products that lower installation cost, increase density, improve performance, and fit more fiber into constrained environments, it can capture part of that value through better margins.

> "If we can sell more solutions, our new innovations, our margin goes up."

That sentence explains the margin story better than anything else on the call.

Corning has historically traded like a cyclical glass and fiber company. Management is making the case that Optical can become one of its best return businesses. Optical is growing faster than the company average, benefiting from AI demand, moving toward higher-value products, and producing strong ROIC.

---

### Carrier - Part Of The AI Story

The Carrier side is quite interesting.

Carrier sales grew 36% year over year, driven by both data-center interconnect and fiber-to-the-home. The DCI piece is the important one. AI data centers are increasingly built across campuses, regions, and linked facilities, which creates demand for data-center interconnect. Some of that demand comes directly through hyperscalers. Some comes through carriers building networks for hyperscaler traffic.

Lumen was a good talking point. Management said Lumen is using a new Gen AI fiber and cable system that allows far more fiber to fit into existing conduit, and that Lumen expanded and extended its agreement to access newer fiber technology. That is exactly the kind of physical-layer constraint AI creates: more optical capacity through existing infrastructure, which is a solvable problem if you have the right product.

Corning now effectively has two AI optical channels running simultaneously. Direct hyperscale enterprise demand on one side, carrier-driven data-center interconnect on the other. That makes the Optical revenue more durable than a single-channel story.

### Guidance:

Q2 guidance is where the market found the pressure point.

* **Q2 core sales:** approximately $4.6B, up about 14% year over year
* **Q2 core EPS:** $0.73 to $0.77, up about 25% year over year
* **Q2 solar expense:** additional $30M versus Q1 from an extended solar wafer facility shutdown

The shutdown includes a transition to a permanent power system and production equipment upgrades. The solar drag also started before Q2. Q1 already included roughly a $0.04 EPS impact from bringing up wafer capacity. Q2 adds another $30M of expense from the shutdown and equipment work. Even absorbing that, Corning is guiding for double-digit revenue growth and about 25% EPS growth. Management described Q2 as "one of the strongest quarters in a string of very strong quarters."

The stock sold off because the market had a high bar. The business is still guiding to a strong quarter while absorbing a temporary cost. The bigger question is whether May 6 resets the long-term growth framework, and based on how often management emphasized the event, I think it will. But time will rell.

### Solar Is Bigger Than I Expected

Solar is secondary to the optics thesis, but it deserves more attention after this quarter.

Solar sales grew 80% year over year. Corning's prior target was $2.5B of revenue by 2028 at profitability above the corporate average. Management now plans to raise that sales plan as part of the May 6 Springboard upgrade.

The business has three pieces:

Polysilicon, where Corning activated idle assets to serve domestic demand and the business performed above the 20% corporate operating margin target in Q1.

Wafers, where Corning built the largest U.S. solar ingot and wafer facility in 18 months and has committed customers for output, currently moving through the Q2 maintenance shutdown.

And modules, where Corning acquired and ramped an Arizona facility that management expects to cross above 20% operating margin in Q2.

Solar is creating near-term earnings noise and a long-term growth platform at the same time. Both can be true. The Q2 expense is real, but management is framing it as a throughput investment tied to committed demand. The long-term solar plan appears larger than I initially thought.

---

### Glass Innovations

Corning restructured its segment reporting, creating a standalone Solar segment and combining Display and Specialty Materials into Glass Innovations, which now includes Electronics Glass and Advanced Optics. The segment changes are part of the story. Corning is separating Solar and grouping advanced glass and optics capabilities in a way that makes the new growth platforms easier for us to underwrite.

Glass Innovations looks steady at a glance with Q1 sales of $1.42B up 1% year over year, net income of $324M up 2%. But the AI angle sits inside Advanced Optics.

Management said it sees short-term and long-term opportunities in semiconductor advanced optics driven by high-performance computing and AI data-center buildouts. They also called out higher expected demand for the company's EUV lithography business as chipmakers ramp production for generative AI.

### May 6 Is The Real Catalyst

Management mentioned the May 6 investor event repeatedly, and the emphasis was deliberate. They talked about it A LOT!

Corning plans to share a significant upgrade to Springboard, extend the framework through 2030, and introduce a new Photonics Market-Access Platform in Optical Communications aimed at Gen AI OEM customers. The January upgrade had already targeted $11B of incremental annualized sales by end of 2028. Now they are preparing another upgrade with a longer time horizon and a new photonics-specific platform on top of it.

> "Now, based on increasing demand for our innovations, we actually plan to upgrade again and extend our plan through 2030 at our investor event in New York City on May 6."
>
> "...this represents a significant enhancement to our financial profile and establishes a new base from which to launch another round of strong, more profitable growth."

The Photonics MAP is the phrase worth paying attention to. It sounds bigger than basic fiber and cable, and the Gen AI OEM customer language raises real questions about how far into the stack Corning is moving. Scale-out and scale-across already underpin the current thesis. Management gave a signal on the call that the scale-up piece may be closer than investors assumed.

> "What has happened is technical progress and a number of very deep dialogues with key customers that has now increased the probability of the scale-up piece of the network making a difference in the near term in our revenue outlook."

If May 6 reveals meaningful scale-up exposure, CPO connectivity, or optical links closer to the system, the growth runway gets more interesting. Who exactly the Gen AI OEM customers are, whether cloud customers, system OEMs, switch OEMs, or AI infrastructure builders, could also change how investors think about the addressable market.

Management gave us enough on this call to confirm the direction. Then they saved the full framework for next week. "Obviously, we have a lot of news to share next week." That was one of the clearest lines on the call.

### Sector Read-Through

This quarter sent a strong signal for the optics sector broadly.

Hyperscalers are securing optical capacity years in advance. AI data centers are becoming more fiber dense. DCI demand is showing up through Carrier channels, not just direct enterprise relationships. High-density optical products are becoming strategic infrastructure rather than commodity inputs. And photonics is moving from a niche technical category into a formal AI infrastructure classification with its own market-access platform at Corning.

The strongest read-throughs are for fiber, cable, high-density connectivity, data-center interconnect, scale-across networks, optical transport, installation infrastructure, and eventually CPO and optical I/O as the stack moves closer to the compute layer.

I would be careful applying this directly to every small-cap photonics name though. This quarter strengthens the sector backdrop and raises the demand signal. Individual companies still need their own customer wins, qualification progress, manufacturing execution, and product-market fit. The tide is rising but that does not lift every boat automatically.

---

### What Could Go Wrong

Valuation is the first risk. Corning had rerated hard the last few months, which means a conservative-looking guide can trigger selling even when the business signal improves.

The second risk is solar execution. The Q2 expense appears temporary, but the wafer ramp is behind plan and Corning needs to show that the shutdown and upgrades improve throughput in later quarters.

The third risk is May 6 expectations. Management teased the event heavily, which creates a higher bar for the announcement.

The fourth risk is capacity execution: these agreements create visibility, but Corning still has to expand fiber, cable, and connectivity operations while protecting returns.

The fifth risk is AI capex timing. The long-term direction looks strong, but quarterly schedules can shift if hyperscalers adjust build plans.

Those risks are real. After this quarter, the core thesis still got stronger in my opinion
