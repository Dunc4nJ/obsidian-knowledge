# Crux Capital 2026-04-14 — Nokia's Third Act starting to come into view

[[Nokia (NOK)]] is earning a different frame. The BofA upgrade explicitly separates Optical + IP from the rest of the portfolio and assigns the segment a richer valuation — consistent with management's deliberate repositioning around AI/cloud connectivity, 800G coherent pluggables, and the Infinera acquisition.

- **March update data:** launched 800G coherent pluggables, components and intra-DC solutions, expanded fab + packaging capabilities, **36% increase in AI/cloud business**, **14% growth in optical business**.

### Why this upgrade makes sense

Justin Hotard (CEO) said 2025 was "a foundational year in repositioning Nokia for long-term value creation." He tied that directly to the Infinera acquisition, the simpler operating model, and a strategy centered on the areas where Nokia sees room to build scale and durable leadership.

*Nokia "What a year it's been" — Infinera acquisition, launched 800G coherent pluggables, components and intra-DC solutions, expanded fab capabilities, increased packaging capabilities. 14% growth in optical business, 36% increase in AI/cloud business, #1 market share in 4Q25 (Global ex. China).*
![[cruxcapitalgroup-nokias-third-act-001.png]]

The more consequential point came when he described where demand is actually showing up.

> "Order intake was solid across both Optical and IP Networks, with a book-to-bill above 1, supported by particularly strong demand from AI and cloud customers."
>
> "Optical networking will become an even more critical part of the infrastructure to support the AI super cycle."

Those lines place AI and cloud demand at the center of the optical and IP story, and the BofA upgrade fits that frame directly. They also help explain why the story feels different today. Nokia is talking about the parts of networking that are getting pulled forward by AI spending rather than simply waiting for a broader telecom cycle to improve.

### Where Nokia sits in the stack

Nokia touches several layers of the network at once. Optical transport, coherent pluggables, IP routing, switching, and the infrastructure carrying traffic between data centers, campuses, metro routes, and long-haul networks all sit inside the picture.

*Nokia Network Infrastructure overview — Optical Networks €3.2bn 2025 revenue, #1 or #2 in optical systems; IP Networks €2.6bn, #1 in edge routing; Fixed Networks €2.0bn, #1 in broadband. Reach: User/On-prem → Edge cloud → Metro → AI factory (inside data centers) → Long-haul → Subsea → AI factory (wide-area network). Theme: driving down cost and power per bit; increasing scale, agility, and resiliency.*
![[cruxcapitalgroup-nokias-third-act-002.png]]

AI traffic does not stay inside one rack or one building. Models are trained across large clusters, inference runs across distributed compute, and a rising volume of traffic has to move through physical networks with real power, fiber, and routing constraints. A business sitting across optical and IP can participate in more than one layer of that shift.

That is part of what makes Nokia more interesting here. Optical Networks, IP Networks, and Fixed Networks stretch across access and distribution, inside data centers, and the wide-area network. Multiple ways to win if AI keeps raising the value of connectivity.

There was also a useful way of framing this at OFC. David Heard pointed out that compute improved roughly 60,000x over the last 20 years while networking improved only about 30x. That gap says a lot about why this layer is getting more attention now. If AI keeps driving compute higher, the network has to catch up.

### The optical business is getting easier to underwrite

The strongest part of the current setup is the optical business.

> "In optical, our 800G ZR and ZR+ pluggable products are shipping, with initial units performing well in the field. We now have multiple design wins and are supplying into scaled deployments."

The March updates sharpen the same picture: launched 800G coherent pluggables, components and intra-data-center solutions, expanded fab and packaging capabilities, a 36% increase in AI and cloud business, and 14% growth in optical business. Those signals connect demand, product progress, and manufacturing preparation in the same slide.

Management's broader framing adds the right context.

> "Connectivity is becoming a critical differentiator in the AI supercycle, not only for communication service providers and hyperscalers, but also for new areas like defense and national security."
>
> "Fundamentally, what I see is a much more mature and larger optical market driven by the AI, the AI infrastructure build-out, than we've seen in the past."

There was another useful detail from OFC here as well. Heard said optical product cycles have compressed from roughly four years to less than two, with 18-month splits in many cases as customers push for lower cost per bit and lower power per bit. That is a good reminder that AI is changing the tempo of optical innovation, not just the size of the market.

### Why hyperscaler demand changes the story

AI infrastructure spending is reshaping the demand profile for networking equipment. The buyers at the center of this cycle care about throughput, reach, fiber density, power efficiency, and operational simplicity. Nokia is showing up in that conversation through optical transport, coherent pluggables, and broader AI and cloud demand inside Network Infrastructure.

*Global AI infrastructure spending — $2.5tn over next 5 years, 44% CAGR (historical 2021-2025 ~$50-260B; projected 2026-2030 ~$370-960B). AI-driven bandwidth growth — 23% global WAN AI traffic CAGR, 65% intra-data-center capacity CAGR. Fiber deployments — 1.2bn kilometers 2026-2030. Optical interfaces (ports per year) — Coherent: 970k (2025) → 3M (2030); Intra-DC: 50M (2025) → 215M (2030). Sources: IDC, IoT Analytics, Gartner, Dell'Oro; Bell Labs, Corning, Mordor Intelligence, The Business Research Company, Light Counting, Cignal AI.*
![[cruxcapitalgroup-nokias-third-act-003.png]]

The BofA note effectively separates the Optical and IP business from the rest of the portfolio and assigns that segment a richer valuation. That framework makes sense if one part of Nokia is gaining relevance to hyperscaler and AI infrastructure budgets while other parts move on a different cadence. Nokia has been setting that view up deliberately.

> "Datacenters will be our number one growth target for the coming years."
>
> "These are AI supercomputers, and AI supercomputers need higher bandwidth, richer connectivity."

That is the key shift. Datacenter and AI spending are pushing Nokia into a different conversation than the one the stock sat in for years.

### Why the IP side matters too

The opportunity extends beyond transport optics. Management talked about data center switching, a new event-driven automation solution, and progress in platforms helping control and manage heavier traffic flows. As traffic spreads across campuses and regions, value accrues both to the business moving the light and to the business helping direct where that traffic goes.

> "Network Infrastructure remains our primary growth engine, particularly Optical and IP Networks, where we see strong structural demand."

Routing, switching, and orchestration sit alongside optical in the same growth conversation. That breadth is part of why Nokia can participate in the AI buildout through more than one product category.

*Network segmentation by reach (with application TAM) — Campus $1.0bn (minimized power for shorter reach, CPO switches); Metro $6.4bn (optimized for hosts with smaller power envelopes, e.g. IPoDWDM); Regional $5.5bn (maximized performance for hosts with larger power envelopes, e.g. thin transponder); Long-haul (no $ shown); Subsea $2.5bn (maximized for spectral efficiency with greatest reach). Network applications: CPO Switches, Switches, Routers, Thin transponders, Full fiber solutions, Embedded transponders.*
![[cruxcapitalgroup-nokias-third-act-004.png]]

One comment from OFC fits especially well here. Heard said optical and switching together are opening up huge opportunities for hyperscalers and for the wide-area network. This shows why the IP side belongs in the same frame as optics rather than sitting off to the side as a separate story.

---

### Why Infinera matters

Infinera is an optical networking company Nokia acquired last year. It brought in deeper optical systems expertise, more coherent technology, in-house photonic and DSP capabilities, and a broader position in the transport and data-center-interconnect market.

The acquisition gives Nokia more optical scale, more product breadth, and a stronger position in the cloud and AI connectivity conversation. It also adds more vertical integration through areas like fab capacity and advanced packaging, which matters in a market where power, density, and speed of execution all carry more weight.

Nokia places Infinera directly alongside 800G coherent pluggables, fab expansion, packaging capability, intra-DC solutions, and AI and cloud growth. That placement is intentional. Infinera is being presented as a foundational piece of the current direction.

### My takeaway

Nokia is becoming more interesting because it is earning a different frame. The business increasingly looks like an optical, IP, and AI-linked network infrastructure story with real product progress, stronger hyperscaler relevance, and a broader role in the layers of the network that grow more valuable as AI scales.

If that frame keeps strengthening, Nokia's third act still has room to develop.
