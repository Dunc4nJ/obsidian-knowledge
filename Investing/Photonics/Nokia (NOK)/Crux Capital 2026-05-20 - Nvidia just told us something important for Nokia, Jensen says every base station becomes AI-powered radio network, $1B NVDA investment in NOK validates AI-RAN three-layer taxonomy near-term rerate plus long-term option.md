# Crux Capital 2026-05-20 — Nvidia just told us something important for Nokia

- **NVDA earnings call put AI-RAN inside the edge-AI / physical-AI architecture, on the record.** Colette Kress grouped "AI RAN-based stations" alongside PCs, gaming consoles, workstations, robotics, and automotive as "Edge Computing devices for agentic and physical AI." Jensen went further: *"In the future, every single base station, every single radio network would become an AI-powered radio network."* He closed the call placing AI-RAN telco base stations alongside robotics, autonomous vehicles, and embedded medical instruments as the next CUDA edge surface.
- **Direct NOK read-through**: [[Nvidia (NVDA)]] and [[Nokia (NOK)]] are partnered on this very layer, and NVDA invested **$1 billion into NOK** to bring it to life. Together they "represent the clearest Western alternative to Huawei-led infrastructure as this architecture evolves into 6G."

  3. **AI ON RAN** — running AI applications at the edge of the wireless network (machine vision/QC at factories, real-time video analytics, drone fleet coordination, industrial automation/private wireless at ports/mines/campuses, defense + sovereign AI with data residency, outdoor robotics coordination). *"Largest long-term upside"*, longer-dated, least proven today — but the version that could "genuinely reprice Nokia over a multi-year horizon."
- **Why NOK, not Ericsson, not just NVDA?** Nokia "touches more of the map" — optical and IP transport as the near-term proven rerate driver (Infinera contributing, 2027 coherent roadmap strong); RAN software and carrier relationships including NVDA partnership and **T-Mobile field evaluation beginning in 2026**; private wireless as a fast-growing industrial infrastructure category (NOK is market leader); defense and sovereign networks (data residency, trust, non-Huawei architecture); licensing cash flows funding the business while longer-dated options mature; **San Jose InP capacity** as a future angle on optical and CPO supply chain.
- **The near-term rerate is already underway and does NOT depend on AI-RAN.** Nokia Q1 2026 results: AI and cloud customer sales **+49%**; ~**€1B of new AI and cloud orders**; Network Infrastructure growing; Optical growing; Infinera integration progressing. "These can move the stock in the next 2–4 quarters and do not require AI-RAN to work at all." ### What even is AI-RAN?

Let's talk about what this actually means and why it matters for Nokia specifically. I dove into it further in the linked articles, so this will be quick.

The traditional RAN handles the radio access network. Radios, cell sites, baseband functions, spectrum management, traffic scheduling, network software. It is the wireless part of the mobile stack. AI-RAN brings accelerated computing and AI into that same layer. There are three ways this plays out, and they have very different timelines and investment implications.

*Crux's three-layer AI-RAN taxonomy diagram — three columns:*
- ***AI FOR RAN** (blue, "Improve the network", Near-term): Traffic optimization · Energy efficiency · Automation · Lower downtime. Label: "Most commercial today".*
- ***AI AND RAN** (blue, "Shared infrastructure", Developing): Radio + AI on one platform · Better site economics · Distributed compute · Higher asset utilization. Label: "Emerging now".*
- ***AI ON RAN** (green, "Edge AI applications", Longer-dated): Machine vision · Robotics · Real-time video · Industrial automation. Label: "Largest long-term upside".*
![[cruxcap-nvda-important-002.png]]

**AI FOR RAN**. The first and most immediate is using AI to improve the network itself. This is the least exciting version on paper but the most real right now. Better traffic optimization, lower energy use, faster anomaly detection, smarter spectrum usage, more automation, fewer human-in-the-loop decisions. Operators already need lower opex and better performance. The business case fits inside the existing network budget without requiring any new revenue model or customer. Nokia benefits here as a RAN software vendor before any of the bigger architectural shifts arrive.

**AI AND RAN.** The second version is running AI and wireless workloads on shared accelerated infrastructure. This is where NVIDIA becomes central to the Nokia story. If the same compute platform can support both radio network functions and AI inference workloads, the telecom site starts being a distributed compute node. The economics only work if utilization stays high, which is the real challenge, but the Nokia and NVIDIA partnership is specifically designed to test and prove this architecture.

**AI ON RAN**. The third and most consequential version is running AI applications at the edge of the wireless network, right where the physical world generates data. This means:

- Machine vision and quality control at factories
- Real-time video analytics for public safety and smart cities
- Drone fleet coordination and inspection workflows
- Industrial automation and private wireless at ports, mines, and campuses
- Defense and sovereign AI applications that require local data residency
- Robotics coordination across large outdoor environments

This is the version of AI-RAN that could genuinely reprice Nokia over a multi-year horizon. It is also the least proven version today. But the direction of travel is clear, and NVIDIA naming it explicitly on an earnings call is not nothing.

### Why Nokia?

Why Nokia and not Ericsson? Why not just own NVIDIA directly and call it a day?

Nokia is the company that touches more of the map, and in a theme as fragmented as AI infrastructure, touching more of the map is actually the point. Here is what Nokia brings to this setup that no other single name does:

**Optical and IP transport** as the near-term proven rerate driver, with Infinera already contributing and the 2027 coherent roadmap looking strong.

**RAN software and carrier relationships** across global operators, including the NVIDIA partnership and T-Mobile field evaluation beginning in 2026

**Private wireless** as one of the fastest growing industrial infrastructure categories, with Nokia already a market leader

**Defense and sovereign networks** where data residency, trust, and non-Huawei architecture requirements create durable demand

**Licensing cash flows** that fund the business while the longer-dated options mature

**San Jose InP capacity** as a future angle on the optical and CPO supply chain.

The market has finally caught up on Nokia. But primarily on the optical and IP recovery. That is the part it understands (at least it does in our FinX bubble). The AI-RAN layer is barely in the price. And today, NVIDIA made that layer significantly more visible to a much larger audience than Nokia's own investor day ever could.

Anduril is another **adjacent** (more private 5G / Tactical edge) proof point that I haven't brought up yet. They recently launched their 5G Comms Sentry Tower in collaboration with Nokia Federal Solutions, combining Anduril's Sentry Tower hardware with Nokia private 5G to deliver deployable connectivity for real-time operations in remote environments. That is the defense version of the same broader theme. Autonomous systems, counter-drone networks, sensors, and tactical AI need secure communications close to the field. Nokia gives those systems a private wireless and mission-critical network layer. This does not make Nokia an Anduril proxy, but it does make the defense and physical AI angle more tangible. And people get hyped about Anduril.

### The Near-Term Rerate vs. The Long-Term Option

The near-term rerate is real and already underway. Nokia's Q1 2026 results showed AI and cloud customer sales up 49% and roughly 1 billion euros of new AI and cloud orders. Network Infrastructure is growing. Optical is growing. The Infinera integration is progressing. These are the things that can move the stock in the next two to four quarters, and they do not require AI-RAN to work at all.

*Crux's "Long-term option" 5-step roadmap visual: AI-RAN trials → Shared compute validation → Carrier deployments → Edge AI workloads → Physical AI use cases → "A larger role in distributed AI" (green destination panel).*

The long-term option is what today's NVIDIA call was really about for NOK. If AI-RAN becomes a meaningful architectural layer of 6G, Nokia is one of the best-positioned Western vendors to participate. That option is not in the near-term model. It is not what I am underwriting in my base case. But it is real, it is growing more visible, and NVIDIA just gave it a platform.

That combination, a near-term rerate that is already in motion and a longer-dated is exactly the kind of setup I look for.
