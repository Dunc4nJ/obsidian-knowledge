### What Nokia actually is right now

The interesting business is Network Infrastructure. Specifically two pieces inside it.

First, Optical Networks. Roughly €3.2 billion of revenue in 2025. This is the business that moves data over fiber. So think long-haul, metro, submarine, data center interconnect. Nokia bought Infinera last year and went from a decent optical player to roughly the number two vendor globally with around 20% market share, based on Nokia's own market positioning disclosures and Dell'Oro data. Number one outside China in Q4 2025, again per their own disclosure.

Second, IP Networks. Roughly €2.6 billion. IP means Internet Protocol networking. Think routing and switching that decides where data goes. FP5 is Nokia's custom silicon here and Nokia says it delivers around 6 terabits per second with significantly better power efficiency than the prior generation. They're going after Cisco and Arista in hyperscale fabrics. Early innings but real.

Combined, those two businesses did about €5.8 billion in 2025. So the main question is, where does it go from here?

Otherwise…

Fixed Networks adds another €2.0 billion. Less exciting as it declined 13% in Q1 partly from portfolio cleanup. I think of it as a stabilizer, not a growth driver. Worth knowing it's a drag on the mix-shift story right now.

Then there's Nokia Technologies which is their patent licensing arm. €1.4 billion a year, 26,000+ patent families, over €800 million contracted and recurring through 2030. Solid margins. We don't really talk about it enough. It basically funds a lot of what the company is trying to do. And it's not static as automotive licensing is growing fast. Nokia now has four Chinese automakers licensed on cellular, Mercedes on wireless, and their expansion areas run-rate is already above €200 million annually. That number keeps going up.

The balance sheet and licensing cash flow are important because Nokia can fund this pivot without needing to act like a speculative optics startup.

### The new CEO

I don't think I touched on this before, but Nokia replaced its CEO on April 1st 2025. Pekka Lundmark out, Justin Hotard in.

Hotard came from Intel's data center and AI division. Before that, HPE high-performance computing. He is not a telecom lifer. This is someone whose entire career has been inside the infrastructure that AI runs on.

When a company that sells to telecoms hires a data center executive as CEO, they're telling you something about where they think the growth is coming from and where they want to shift their priorities.

### The 2026 bridge

Nokia did roughly €19.9 billion of total revenue in 2025. Inside that, Optical and IP did roughly €5.8 billion combined, close to 30% of the company. That is already large enough to matter.

Nokia hasn't given a clean full-year 2026 revenue guide, but management gave enough to build the bridge. Network Infrastructure is now expected to grow 12-14% in 2026. Optical and IP are expected to grow 18-20%. My rough 2026 revenue estimate for the group is €20.7-21.5 billion.

The important part is the mix. Total Nokia may only grow mid-single digits, but Optical and IP should move from roughly €5.8 billion in 2025 to roughly €6.8-7.0 billion in 2026.

That is a good part of the re-rate case.

The whole company is still growing like a mature infrastructure company. The AI-facing pieces are growing much faster underneath the surface.

Margins are the second part. Nokia's full-year operating profit guide implies roughly 9-12% comparable operating margin depending on where revenue lands. The bigger target is inside Network Infrastructure specifically that segment had 9.5% operating margin in 2025 and management is targeting 13-17% by 2028.

So the setup is that revenue mix shifts toward Optical and IP. Network Infrastructure margins move higher. The market starts valuing Nokia less like legacy telecom and more like AI network infrastructure.

### Why AI is actually Nokia's problem to solve

AI is many things. And one of those things is a connectivity story.

An AI data center is one giant machine. When you start connecting clusters across buildings, campuses, cities, regions that's where Nokia lives. We call it scale-across. The layer that links everything together.

Read more here: [CIEN and NOK Bullish](https://cruxcapitalgroup.substack.com/p/cien-and-nok-bullish) (Gaetano, Apr 3).

Management said AI traffic is already around 20% of all network traffic globally. About 80 exabytes a month. And that's mostly still human-to-machine. If agentic AI scales the way the industry expects, machine-to-machine traffic becomes the larger and faster-growing driver. The network has to grow with it.

More traffic means more fiber. More fiber means more coherent optics. More coherent optics means Nokia makes more money.

That's the chain.

### The TAM is genuinely large

Optical transport market ex-China: $13.1 billion in 2025, Nokia sizes it at $24 billion by 2030. That's $11 billion of additional annual market in one category over five years. Just in optical.

800G coherent pluggables, the specific product shipping now, Cignal AI forecast over 200,000 module shipments in 2026, driven by Meta and other hyperscalers. Nokia's pluggables are already shipping to at least one large US customer.

Data center interconnect is a multi-billion dollar market today and analysts broadly expect it to multiply several times over by end of decade as AI cluster connectivity demand scales. Nokia is already top three alongside Ciena and Cisco.

IP routing and switching is a $20 billion market. Private wireless, where Nokia is number one globally per Dell'Oro, grew strongly in 2024, well ahead of expectations, and is still early.

AI-RAN is the longer-dated option. The NVIDIA and Nokia joint announcement cited Omdia framing the opportunity as significant. I treat the specific long-term estimates as directional rather than precise, but the direction is clearly large. Nokia has ten committed operator partners and a $1 billion equity investment from NVIDIA at $6.01 a share (well done Jensen). T-Mobile US is the lead partner. Trials start this year.

I don't model AI-RAN into my base case right now. But it's sitting there as a real option and leaves lots of room for excitement in the future.

I don't need all of these markets to hit perfectly. I need enough of them to keep Optical and IP compounding faster than the rest of Nokia.

### Infinera and the San Jose fab

Nokia paid about $2.3 billion for Infinera. Closed February 2025. The synergy targets are €200 million net operating profit by 2027, over 10% EPS accretion same year.

But let's look at the timing. Nokia bought optical scale right before the market started paying up for optical exposure. That's hard to do and they did it.

What I'm watching now is San Jose. Nokia is building out an indium phosphide fab there, moving from 3-inch to 6-inch wafers, which dramatically increases how many optical components they can produce. Lasers, coherent engines, photonic integrated circuits. The fab starts contributing late 2026 and scales through 2027-2028.

Why does this matter? Because optical demand only converts to revenue if you can actually ship. Supply has been the bottleneck across the whole industry. San Jose is Nokia's answer to that.

The 2027 product roadmap on top of this is real with 1.6T coherent, 2.4T, coherent-lite, double-sided pluggables. Nokia claims 70% lower total cost of ownership across the integrated architecture. Cisco is also moving toward 2.4T coherent optics, which is useful validation that this is an industry cycle, not just a Nokia slide.

### NVIDIA, AI-RAN, and defense

Optical and IP are the proof today. But Nokia has two more options sitting behind them.

The first is AI-RAN. Most AI infrastructure right now is centralized with giant data centers, massive compute clusters, traffic flowing in and out. The next phase pushes compute closer to where data is actually created. Devices, factories, cameras, robots, vehicles, industrial systems. That's the edge. And the edge runs on telecom infrastructure. This is an area I am increasingly wanting to gain exposure to.

Read more here: [I Want To Invest Here - EDGE AI](https://cruxcapitalgroup.substack.com/p/i-want-to-invest-here-edge-ai) (Gaetano, May 2).

Nokia already has the operator relationships, the radio software, the field footprint. NVIDIA brings the accelerated compute layer. Nokia's 5G and 6G software is being ported onto NVIDIA's CUDA platform. NVIDIA's ARC-Pro stack gets embedded into Nokia's AI-RAN product line. Architectural alignment.

If telecom sites become edge AI infrastructure over the next decade, Nokia's physical footprint becomes a lot more valuable than the market is pricing today. That's a real option.

The second is defense. Nokia has created a dedicated Defense unit and partnered with Lockheed Martin. Secure private networks, tactical communications, NATO-aligned infrastructure, trusted connectivity for governments. It's small today. But the geopolitical backdrop is that governments are actively trying to move critical communications infrastructure away from untrusted vendors. Nokia has the wireless, routing, private-network, and mission-critical assets to compete in that world.

So the fuller picture looks like this. Optical and IP connect AI data centers together. AI-RAN could extend AI infrastructure out to the edge. Defense adds a sovereign network angle that's strategically valuable in ways that don't show up in near-term numbers.

Nokia has a lot of ways to win.

### My model

This is not meant to be precise price targets. It is meant to show what has to happen for the valuation framework to change.

The starting point is Optical and IP at roughly €5.8 billion in 2025. The 2026 guide implies roughly €6.8-7.0 billion.

From there:

Bear case: 2026 is the peak growth year. Optical and IP reach roughly €6.8-7.0 billion, then flatten or decline modestly as AI and Cloud orders prove lumpy. Nokia gets credit for a better cycle but the multiple stays telecom.

Base case: 2026 is the breakout year, but it doesn't have to be the peak. Optical and IP grow toward roughly €8 billion by 2028 as 800G, IP design wins, San Jose and InP capacity, and the 2027 coherent roadmap keep the business compounding. The mix shift becomes visible enough for a partial rerate.

Bull case: €9 billion plus. The 1.6T and 2.4T cycles layer on top of 800G. IP accelerates. Hyperscaler design wins compound. Nokia starts getting valued like an AI infrastructure platform. That's when the multiple re-anchors in a meaningful way.

Nokia's own 2028 target is €2.7-3.2 billion of operating profit. If they hit the midpoint and the market applies even a modestly higher multiple to the AI-facing mix, the implied value is well above where the stock sits today.

Ciena is the clean optical comp. It has been rewarded because investors see it as a direct beneficiary of AI-driven bandwidth demand. Nokia is messier, but it has more ways to win: optical transport, IP routing, switching, InP manufacturing, private wireless, AI-RAN, licensing, and defense networks. The point is not that Nokia should trade exactly like Ciena. The point is that the market is willing to pay up for clean AI optical exposure while Nokia is still fighting the legacy telecom label. That gap is the bet.

### How I think about price

These are scenarios, not promises. The stock has already moved (it's at $13.00 right now, ~$73 billion market cap) so each step genuinely needs proof.

At this price, the near-term upside looks modest on paper. But that's exactly the point of a rerate story. The multiple changes as the mix changes, not as a one-time event. I am not buying $13.00 for $16. I am buying it for what it could be worth if the market stops pricing it the way it is.

My 12-month base case is $16-18. That assumes AI and Cloud orders remain strong, Optical keeps growing well above the company average, IP starts converting design wins, and Network Infrastructure margins improve.

My 12-month bull case is $20-22 if Q1 momentum repeats and investors begin giving Optical and IP a clearer AI-networking multiple.

My 18-24 month base case is $22-26. That requires San Jose and InP execution, stronger IP contribution, and growing confidence in the 2027 coherent roadmap.

My 18-24 month bull case is $28-32 if Optical and IP keep compounding and the market starts using a sum-of-the-parts lens.

The next 12 months are about proving the rerate.

### Some things that worry me

RAN share is leaking. According to Omdia data, Nokia's global RAN share fell from 19.5% to 17.6% in 2024. AT&T went to Ericsson — up to $14 billion over five years per AT&T's own announcement. T-Mobile US is now the key anchor in North America and there are reports Ericsson is pushing hard for it. Losing T-Mobile would hurt.

The stock has already moved massively in twelve months.

Coherent pluggable competition is real. Both Ciena and Nokia are pushing higher speeds in their next-generation coherent silicon. Ciena with WaveLogic 6, Nokia with PSE-6s. These head-to-head performance comparisons matter when hyperscalers are evaluating vendors, and Nokia needs to prove it can compete at the highest performance tier. I do think there is plenty to go around right now though.

Customer concentration. One big order pushed out by a hyperscaler and the quarter looks ugly. Ciena investors know this pattern well.

Fixed Networks declining 13% in Q1 is a real drag. It's not the thesis but it muddies the mix-shift story in the near term.

I'm wrong if AI and Cloud orders prove lumpy, IP fails to convert, San Jose slips, and the market treats Optical growth as a cyclical spike rather than a durable mix shift.

Margins. NI operating margin was 9.5% in 2025. Target is 13-17% by 2028. Progress on that is what turns the revenue story into an earnings story.

I'd get more aggressive if AI and Cloud orders repeat, IP growth accelerates, San Jose stays on schedule, and Network Infrastructure margins start moving toward that 13-17% target range.
