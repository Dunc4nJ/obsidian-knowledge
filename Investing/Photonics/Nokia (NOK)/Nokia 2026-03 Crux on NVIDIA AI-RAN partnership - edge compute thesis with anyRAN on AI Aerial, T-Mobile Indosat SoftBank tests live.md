## Original Content

Nokia is one of the companies that I will be covering on my Substack. I will be releasing a full thesis + deep dive report in the coming weeks. In the meantime I want to discuss one cool partnership that exists and introduce you all to a concept you may be unfamiliar with.

So let's talk about Nokia and Nvidia. This partnership pushes AI infrastructure outside the four walls of the datacenter.

Back in October, NVIDIA agreed to invest $1 billion in Nokia, and the two companies said they would work together on AI-RAN for 5G-Advanced and 6G. Since then, Nokia has said the partnership has moved into successful functional tests with operators including T-Mobile, Indosat, and SoftBank.

We often talk about AI infrastructure as giant centralized clusters. Whether thats within a data center or connecting data centers. That is still the main buildout. NVIDIA is also building a second layer. It wants AI compute to live closer to where data is created and where decisions have to happen fast. Telecom networks already have physical locations spread everywhere, with power, transport, software, and connectivity. Nokia already sits inside that network layer, so it gives NVIDIA a real path into that footprint.

Let me introduce AI-RAN. Artificial Intelligence-Radio Access Network integrates AI directly into cellular network infrastructure to enhance 5G/6G performance, improve spectral efficiency, and enable edge computing.

A network site that used to focus on wireless traffic can also support AI workloads. NVIDIA and T-Mobile said cell sites and mobile switching offices can support distributed edge AI workloads while still delivering 5G. So the same general telecom footprint that keeps devices connected can also become part of the compute layer.

This directly ties into the future of AI. A lot of the next wave is tied to the physical world. Cameras, industrial systems, robotics, real-time video, autonomous machines, software that needs to react quickly. Those use cases benefit from compute being close by. If telecom infrastructure can handle some of that work, then the network becomes more valuable than a simple connectivity pipe. It becomes part of the intelligence layer too. NVIDIA's own framing around "physical AI applications" running over AI-RAN-ready infrastructure points directly at that direction.

So now tying this back to Nokia, they already has the radio software, the RAN stack, and the operator relationships. Nokia said it has been running its anyRAN software on NVIDIA's GPU-accelerated AI-RAN platform, and it also pointed to AI-RAN traction with BT, Elisa, NTT DOCOMO, and Vodafone using NVIDIA AI Aerial. So there is actual operator work happening around it.

Now lets tie this back to an investment thesis for Nokia. NVIDIA is trying to make sure AI compute does not stay trapped inside hyperscale campuses. It wants AI in datacenters, inside enterprises, inside robots, and increasingly at the edge of the network. Nokia helps with that because it already controls part of the infrastructure map NVIDIA wants access to. That is why a telecom partnership like this matters.

The original announcement did mention future work around AI networking solutions and the possibility of using Nokia's switching and optical technologies in NVIDIA's future infrastructure. I look forward to hearing more about that but the visible progress so far has centered on AI-RAN, edge AI, and AI-native telecom networks. So today we see edge compute and network modernization first, with broader infrastructure optionality sitting underneath it.

So in addition to Nokia expanding into the laser and transciever market, I also look at Nokia here as an edge AI infrastructure participant. NVIDIA is the company trying to stretch AI compute across every valuable layer of infrastructure it can reach. That does not suddenly turn Nokia into a core hyperscale optics winner tomorrow. What it does do is put Nokia in the path of a real architectural shift where the network itself starts to matter as a compute surface.
