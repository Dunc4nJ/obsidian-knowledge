## Original Content

**What's been going on with Tower lately?**

Tower Semiconductor has become one of the more active names in AI optics over the last few months. Tower has tied itself to the current 1.6T ramp, pushed deeper into 400G-per-lane and 3.2T-era development, expanded its CPO-related toolkit, added optical switching relationships, raised its SiPho and SiGe investment plan to $920 million, and announced a restructuring that would give it direct control of its 300mm Fab 7 asset in Uozu. That is a lot of movement in a short period of time, and it all points in the same direction: Tower is taking a larger role in the manufacturing layer underneath AI optical infrastructure.

---

**Let's start with what Tower actually is**

Tower is a specialty foundry. It makes money by providing process technologies and manufacturing capacity for customers that design chips and photonic integrated circuits on top of its platforms. The key platforms in this story are silicon photonics and silicon germanium. Silicon photonics handles the optical side of the link. Silicon germanium supports high-speed electrical functions like drivers and transimpedance amplifiers. That combination is why Tower keeps surfacing around optical modules, LPO, active cables, and future co-packaged architectures.

---

**The business is getting richer**

The latest earnings call made the internal shift very clear. Management said silicon germanium and silicon photonics reached 27% of 2025 revenue, up from 17% in 2024, while silicon photonics alone reached $228 million. Management also said 48.78% of the revenue growth from Q1 to Q4 2025 dropped to net profit because the incremental photonics revenue carried higher value. Tower is filling more of its fabs with higher-value optical and connectivity products, and the margin structure is improving with that mix.

---

**Why the name suddenly shows up more**

The biggest current-node signal came straight from management. On the February call, Russell Ellwanger (CEO) said 1.6T is the fastest-growing silicon photonics node in the industry and that Tower is "by far the majority supplier" of 1.6T silicon PICs. A few days earlier, Tower had announced that its silicon photonics platform was supporting 1.6T data-center optical modules designed for NVIDIA networking protocols. Tower is already positioned inside the current bandwidth ramp, with a roadmap extending well beyond it.

---

**This is becoming a capacity story too**

Management also told us that customer demand strengthened enough to lift the silicon photonics and silicon germanium investment plan to $920 million, up from the previously announced $650 million. More than 70% of planned silicon photonics capacity is already reserved or in process through 2028, backed by customer prepayments, with customers seeking capacity reservation agreements through that same window. That shifts the story from Tower holding an interesting piece in the supply chain toward Tower building capacity against visible, committed demand.

---

**Why CPO keeps coming up around Tower**

Tower's CPO angle has become more concrete lately. Management said on the earnings call that they see

CPO as a substantial incremental opportunity as optics moves deeper into scale-up interconnects and eventually into XPU-to-high-bandwidth-memory links. Tower pointed to three specific pieces that fit that direction: wafer-to-wafer bonding between silicon photonics ICs and silicon germanium electrical ICs, work on DWDM laser sources, and progress on heterogeneously integrated indium phosphide on silicon for 400G-per-lane and 3.2T-era products. That is a real stack of enabling technologies rather than a generic CPO mention.

The recent announcements fit that same pattern. In February, Tower and Scintil announced heterogeneously integrated DWDM laser sources for AI infrastructure. DWDM lets one fiber carry more data by sending multiple wavelengths of light at once, and the laser source is what generates that light. The importance here is that future CPO systems will need compact, efficient laser solutions that can scale in manufacturing, and Tower said this work is already supporting customer evaluations with a path toward volume production.

In March, Tower and Coherent demonstrated 400Gbps-per-lane transmission in a production-ready silicon photonics process targeting 3.2T optical transceivers. Each lane is one data channel, so 400G per lane is the speed step needed for the next generation of higher-bandwidth optical links. That tells us Tower's platform is being pushed toward the next node, with relevance for both pluggables and CPO.

A separate March development agreement announced by Lightwave Logic targeted integration of 110GHz-plus EO polymer modulator designs into Tower's PH18 silicon photonics PDK for 400G-per-lane applications. A modulator is the device that places data onto the light signal, so the point here is that Tower is also exploring faster materials that could help its platform reach those next speeds.

Put together, Tower is building around the next optical node from several angles at once: laser sources, modulators, and a silicon photonics platform being pushed toward 3.2T and CPO.

---

**The Japan move**

A big part of Tower's recent story is where its future optical manufacturing will sit. The company is using 300mm wafers to scale more of its next-generation silicon photonics work into higher-volume production, so control over 300mm capacity matters a lot here. On the February call, management said Intel expressed an intention not to perform under the Fab 11X agreement, that mediation is underway, and that the affected flows were originally qualified in Fab 7 in Japan and are now being redirected there. Management also said Fab 11X sits outside the updated 2028 financial model.

That is why the March 25 Japan announcement matters so much. Tower said it will take full ownership of the 300mm Fab 7 operation in Uozu through a wholly owned Japanese subsidiary, while Nuvoton will take full ownership of the 200mm Fab 5 operation. The transaction is targeted to close on April 1, 2027, subject to approvals. Tower is also targeting a path to expand Uozu 300mm capacity, contingent on METI subsidy approval, with an option to purchase the existing Fab 7 building and land plus adjacent land for further growth. Tower also said its photonics technologies are already qualified and shipping in volume from Fab 7, which means this is existing optical manufacturing capacity being expanded and brought more directly under Tower's control. That makes the Japan move one of the most important structural developments in the story right now.

---

**Tower is widening its reach beyond modules**

The recent news flow also shows Tower expanding beyond transceivers. In February, Tower and Salience Labs announced a partnership to manufacture photonic integrated circuit optical circuit switches (OCS) for AI infrastructure, with the effort moving from development into pre-production. In March, Tower and Oriole announced work on nanosecond OCS for scale-up and scale-out AI architectures. On the power side, Tower introduced a new Gen3 BCD LDMOS platform aimed at AI processor power delivery and the AI Power Wall. Tower's footprint is widening across the data path and the power path simultaneously.

---

**So to put it all together**

Line it all up and the trajectory becomes clear. Tower is moving from broad foundry participation in optical infrastructure toward a more central manufacturing role in the next optical buildout. It already sits inside the current 1.6T silicon photonics ramp. It is investing heavily against future demand. It is adding building blocks for 3.2T and CPO. It is tightening control over a key 300mm Japan asset. And it is expanding into adjacent areas like optical switching and AI power delivery.
