# ASMPT subsidiary AMICRA holds 40-50pct share in active-alignment die bonders critical to 800G+ optical transceiver assembly

## Key Takeaways

- AMICRA's NANO platform achieves ±200nm placement accuracy at 3 sigma in mass production — 2-5x more precise than competitors MRSI (owned by [[Mycronic (MYCR.ST)]]), Palomar, or the Japanese suppliers, who operate in the 500nm-1μm range. In photonic packaging every additional micrometer of misalignment disproportionately worsens coupling loss; this is the difference between a working transceiver and scrap, not a marketing detail.
- Volume math: every 800G transceiver contains 60-100 active-alignment bonds, every 1.6T module roughly doubles that. ~30M 400G/800G/1.6T modules produced globally in 2025, with the 800G+ segment growing ~30% annually through 2030. AMICRA estimated at 40-50% share of active-alignment-capable bonders, including LPO and external CPO laser sources — making it foundational volume infrastructure beneath the CPO hype.
- The investment problem: AMICRA was acquired by [[ASMPT (0522.HK)]] in 2018 and integrated. Sold under ASMPT's photonics division, which is <10% of ASMPT's HKD 14.5B revenue base. The market has long since absorbed the TCB, HBM, and CPO optimism into ASMPT's price. Per Moody: "technically dominant, industrially mature, indispensable to a structurally growing market, and yet today not really an investment case" — another German hidden champion buried inside a larger conglomerate.
- Machines cost €1.5M-€3M, weigh several tons, sit on granite anti-vibration platforms, and perform real-time active optical alignment. Founded 2001, ~150 employees, "practically zero" visibility in the financial press despite criticality to the global transceiver industry.
- Part of Moody's ongoing series on German hidden champions in photonics ("physical enablers"); the QT references an earlier piece in the same series about a firm whose listed parent is Mycronic. Track this series for further single-name surfacing of European/Japanese subscale equipment makers buried inside conglomerates.

> Imagine having to place a laser diode the size of a grain of salt, roughly 300 micrometers wide, onto a submount with such precision that its optical output aligns perfectly with the core of a glass fiber. The core of that fiber is just nine micrometers wide, about one tenth the width of a human hair.
>
> Shift the diode sideways by a single micrometer and you lose roughly 30% of your optical power. Shift it by three micrometers and the entire module becomes scrap.
>
> And this is not a one off alignment inside a cleanroom lab. It has to be done thousands of times per month in mass production of optical transceivers.
>
> That is exactly what AMICRA was built for. And nobody in the world does it more precisely.
>
> Founded in 2001 and acquired in 2018 by [[ASMPT (0522.HK)|ASMPT]], the company builds what are arguably the world's most precise die bonders for photonic packaging.
>
> The NANO platform places components with up to ±200 nanometer accuracy at 3 sigma, roughly one fiftieth the thickness of a human hair. No other commercially available die bonder achieves these numbers under real production conditions.
>
> Direct competitors, MRSI in Massachusetts, Palomar in California, and a handful of Japanese suppliers, typically operate in the 500 nanometer to 1 micrometer range based on public specifications, two to five times worse.
>
> In this business, every additional micrometer of misalignment disproportionately worsens coupling loss. This is not a marketing detail. It is the difference between a functioning module and scrap.
>
> These machines cost between €1.5M and €3M, weigh several tons, sit on granite based anti vibration platforms, and perform active optical alignment in real time.
>
> In a world where every 800G transceiver contains 60 to 100 such bonds, and every 1.6T module roughly doubles that number, this is no longer a niche business. It is foundational volume infrastructure beneath the CPO hype everyone talks about.
>
> In 2025, roughly 30 million 400G / 800G / 1.6T optical modules were produced globally, with the 800G and above segment expected to grow around 30% annually through 2030.
>
> Even classical pluggable transceivers, LPO modules, and the external laser sources used for CPO itself are assembled on NANO systems.
>
> Industry consensus estimates AMICRA's market share in active alignment capable high precision bonders at roughly 40 to 50%, making it the undisputed global leader in its category.
>
> Unfortunately, AMICRA has been fully integrated into ASMPT since 2018. The systems are sold under ASMPT's photonics division, and the photonics business still represents less than 10% of ASMPT's HKD 14.5B revenue base.
>
> If you want exposure to AMICRA, you buy ASMPT, and the market has long since absorbed the TCB, HBM, and CPO optimism.
>
> AMICRA is technically dominant, industrially mature, indispensable to a structurally growing market, and yet today not really an investment case.
>
