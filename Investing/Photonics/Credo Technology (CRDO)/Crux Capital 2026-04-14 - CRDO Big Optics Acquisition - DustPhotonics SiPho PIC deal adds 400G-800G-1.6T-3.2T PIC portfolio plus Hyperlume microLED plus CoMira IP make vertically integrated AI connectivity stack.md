# CREDO - Big Optics Acquisition

Crux Capital's April 14, 2026 reaction to [[Credo Technology (CRDO)]]'s definitive agreement to acquire DustPhotonics — a silicon photonics PIC developer whose portfolio spans 400G, 800G, 1.6T (with 3.2T roadmap) and includes 800G-2xFR4 / 1.6T-2xFR4 PICs, retimed optics, LPO and LRO. The deal moves Credo from the "around-the-optical-engine" role (SerDes, retimers, optical DSP, AECs) to *inside* the optical module — vertically integrated SerDes + DSP + Silicon Photonics + system stack. CEO Bill Brennan reframes the company as spanning "die-to-die links to chip-to-chip and board-level links, to rack and row scale copper, to mid-reach optical and to resilient facility-wide optical solutions." Combined ZeroFlap + optical DSP + silicon photonics is guided to $500M+ optical revenue in FY27, with significant production ramp beginning Q1 FY27. Move follows Hyperlume (MicroLED short-reach) and CoMira (link-layer / ECC / security IP) — stacking copper, PIC, MicroLED, link-layer toward near-package and CPO architectures. Author lifts entry levels: 1st add ~$110, 2nd $95, 3rd $87.

## Why This Matters

This is the clearest single-event re-rating event for the [[Credo Technology (CRDO)]] thesis since the AEC franchise win — Credo moves from the electrical side of the link *into* the optical engine. The vertical integration claim is now SerDes + retimers + AECs + optical DSPs + Silicon Photonics PICs + ZeroFlap transceiver platform + MicroLED short-reach (Hyperlume) + link-layer/ECC/security IP (CoMira), and the math on $500M+ FY27 optical revenue gives the optical thesis a concrete number to chase. The strategic frame matters as much: in a near-package and CPO-bound world, owning the *PIC* — not just what surrounds it — is where the value migrates. Author's entry-level upgrade ($110 / $95 / $87) is a quantitative tell that the post-deal re-rating raised the floor of accumulation price for the position. ### THE NEWS

"SAN JOSE, Calif.--(BUSINESS WIRE)-- Credo Technology Group Holding Ltd (Credo) (NASDAQ: CRDO), an innovator in providing secure, high-speed connectivity solutions that deliver improved reliability and energy efficiency for the next generation of AI-driven applications, cloud computing, and hyperscale networks, today announced it has entered into a definitive agreement to acquire DustPhotonics, a leading developer of Silicon Photonics Photonic Integrated Circuit (SiPho PIC) technology for optical transceivers. The acquisition will position Credo with a vertically integrated connectivity stack spanning SerDes, Digital Signal Processing (DSP), Silicon Photonics and system integration for scale out and scale up networks — addressing both electrical and optical interconnects across the full AI infrastructure buildout."

Before DustPhotonics entered the picture, Credo already had one of the strongest positions in AI connectivity.

Lot's of the story if centered through AECs, or active electrical cables which are copper cables with chips inside that help data travel farther and more reliably at very high speeds.

In an AI cluster, every unstable link can slow deployment, waste power, and create debugging work across the network. That business alone had already turned Credo into one of the biggest winners in the group.

The bigger question was how far Credo could push beyond the electrical side of the link as bandwidth climbed, optics moved closer to the processor, and hyperscalers asked for more control over reliability across the entire fabric.

The DustPhotonics deal gives a much clearer answer.

Credo agreed to acquire DustPhotonics, a silicon photonics company whose products sit inside high-speed optical transceivers. A silicon photonics PIC, or photonic integrated circuit, is the optical engine that routes and manages light inside the module. If Credo used to be known mainly for what happens around the optical engine, this deal moves it much closer to the engine itself. This is less about adding another product line and much more about changing where Credo sits in the AI interconnect stack.

### Credo before DustPhotonics

Credo built its franchise around high-speed SerDes, AECs, retimers, and optical DSPs.

SerDes converts data between serial and parallel form at very high speeds. Retimers restore signal quality as data travels across a link. DSPs clean up and manage the signal inside an optical module. That set of products gave Credo a strong grip on the electrical side of AI networking and a way to sell something hyperscalers care about deeply which is cluster reliability.

Bill Brennan laid out the ambition on the earnings call:

> "Our purpose-built SerDes and ICs, vertically integrated system model and deep hyperscaler partnerships win at scale. We established leadership in high reliability copper connectivity and built strong position in optical DSPs and retimers. Now, our strategy is to lead in reliability, power efficiency, and signal integrity across the full spectrum of AI and data center connectivity, from die-to-die links to chip-to-chip and board-level links, to rack and row scale copper, to mid-reach optical and to resilient facility-wide optical solutions."
>
> "By extending both inward toward the silicon and outward across the data center, we're positioning Credo to encompass the entire connectivity fabric of AI infrastructure."

DustPhotonics fits directly into a roadmap Credo had already started describing. The company was pushing outward through cables, transceivers, and system products, and inward toward the chips and optical components that shape performance, power, and serviceability inside the link.

### Why management moved now

Timing is the key part of this story.

In Q3 fiscal 2026, Credo reported $407 million of revenue, up 52% sequentially and more than 200% year over year. Non-GAAP gross margin reached 68.6%, and management guided the next quarter to $425 million to $435 million while pointing to more than 50% year-over-year growth in fiscal 2027. Credo is making this move from a position of strength, with real customer momentum and strong margins.

Just as important, the optical roadmap was already accelerating.

*Credo product shot — "ZeroFlap Optical Transceivers" — caption: "Credo's ZeroFlap (ZF) Optical Transceivers are engineered to enhance the management and mitigation of optical link flaps delivering greater network stability and operational efficiency for AI backend fabrics. When used in conjunction with Credo PILOT software and integrated with the ZeroFlap Host SDK for telemetry harvesting and coordination, ZF Optical Transceivers form an integral part of Credo's complete ZeroFlap networking solution."*
![[cruxcapitalgroup-credo-big-optics-acquisition-002.png]]

ZeroFlap optics (Credo's transceiver platform built around link telemetry and software controls designed to catch degrading links before they create service problems) had moved from concept into customer qualification and early production shipments.

> "Based on strong customer traction, we now expect to see a significant production ramp beginning in first quarter fiscal 2027 and continuing throughout the year."

That line gives the DustPhotonics deal much more weight. Credo is buying deeper optical capability while its own optical products are already moving toward a ramp. They also said the combined portfolio of ZeroFlap optical transceivers, optical DSPs, and silicon photonics products is expected to generate more than $500 million of optical revenue in fiscal 2027.

DustPhotonics gives Credo a stronger position inside the optical module itself.

Its portfolio includes silicon photonics PICs across 400G, 800G, and 1.6T, with a roadmap to 3.2T. Products like 800G-2xFR4 and 1.6T-2xFR4 PICs pack four optical wavelengths onto a fiber pair, letting a module carry more bandwidth without a separate fiber for every lane. The portfolio also supports retimed optics, LPO, and LRO which are different ways of balancing signal processing, power, and robustness across the link depending on the deployment context. That is exactly the part of the market Credo has been building toward through its DSP family.

Brennan framed the acquisition directly:

> "Combining forces with DustPhotonics marks a defining step in Credo's strategy to lead across the full spectrum of AI connectivity. We've built a strong position in high-speed electrical solutions, and this move decisively expands that leadership into Silicon Photonics with best-in-class PIC technology that complements our ZeroFlap Optical Transceivers and DSP portfolio.
>
> This combination positions us at an inflection point in optical. As adoption accelerates across hyperscale AI infrastructure, we expect our optical business to scale into a meaningful and rapidly growing contributor by fiscal 2027.
>
> More importantly, we are building a vertically integrated connectivity platform that spans from copper to optical and from chip to cluster—allowing us to solve for the two constraints that matter most at scale: reliability and power efficiency, while deepening our role as a strategic partner to our customers."

### Why this changes the optical roadmap

The most important thing to understand here is that copper versus optics was never the real strategic battle for Credo.

Credo's view is that AI infrastructure will use a mix of technologies, each one serving a different reach, power budget, and reliability profile. As those networks grow, value moves toward companies that can control more of the transition points between layers. Brennan addressed that directly:

> "It's about deploying the right technology at the right reach and the right power envelope. We see the industries evolving even more so to a heterogeneous mix of short reach copper, pluggable optics, near package optics, and eventually CPO."

As the industry moves toward near-package and co-packaged optics, the optical engine becomes more central to the whole architecture. That is where DustPhotonics fits. Credo's Cardinal optical DSP family (a low-power 1.6T DSP with integrated drivers for both EML and silicon photonics) already pointed in this direction. Combining that DSP roadmap with in-house silicon photonics PICs makes the product story considerably more coherent.

### What we should watch next

The first thing to watch is whether Credo can turn the optical revenue target into real mix shift during fiscal 2027. Management has signaled that optical should become a much bigger contributor, and the market will want to see that show up in product ramps, customer count, and revenue composition.

The second is ZeroFlap. That product family now sits much closer to the center of the investment case. If the ramp runs well, DustPhotonics looks like an accelerator. If adoption moves more slowly, the market may take a more patient view of the full optical thesis.

The third is how Credo talks about integration going forward. Whether the company starts to sound more confident about owning the optical engine, guiding the transceiver roadmap, and shaping future architectures around its own DSP-plus-PIC combination.

There is also a broader competitive angle. As hyperscalers and neocloud providers push toward larger clusters, higher speeds, and tighter power budgets, the companies with the strongest claim on reliability and system control may capture a disproportionate share of value. Credo is trying to make that claim across copper and optics simultaneously.

### My takeaway

AI networking is getting harder. Bandwidth keeps rising, cluster sizes keep climbing, optical content keeps growing, and power and reliability keep moving closer to the center of the design conversation. Credo is responding by owning more of the places where those pressures show up like the cable, the DSP, the software and telemetry layer, and now the photonic engine inside the transceiver itself.

This acquisition gives Credo a stronger shot at evolving from a company known mainly for best-in-class electrical connectivity into one of the more important optical infrastructure platforms in AI networking.
