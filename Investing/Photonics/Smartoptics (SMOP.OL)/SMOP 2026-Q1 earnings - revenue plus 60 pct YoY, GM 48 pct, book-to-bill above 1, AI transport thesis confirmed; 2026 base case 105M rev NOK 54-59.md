## Original Content

One of my top European photonics picks recently reported earnings.

The stock dropped.

And it is now back to the level I initially bought in.

I first wrote about this company a few weeks ago. At the time, I framed it as a smaller, overlooked optics business with real revenue growth, real margins, positive EBITDA, positive cash flow, short lead times, and exposure to the AI-driven transport buildout. The stock had already moved, so I sized it as a starter. I wanted earnings to either confirm the thesis or expose a problem.

After this quarter, the thesis is stronger.

The company grew revenue almost 60% year over year. Gross margin held near 48%. EBITDA more than doubled. Book-to-bill was considerably above 1. Management said they could have shipped more if they had more production capacity.

That is the type of problem I want to see in this part of the market.

My read is that the stock sold off because expectations had moved quickly, Q2 has some production-move timing noise, and the company is now being judged like a higher-quality growth asset. But the underlying business looks better than it did when I first wrote about it.

And after a 25% gain, it is now back to where I first bought it.

So, I want more.

*Inside, I am going through the Q1 results, what changed from my original thesis, why the AI transport angle is getting clearer, what I expect from Q2, and my updated 2026 and 2027 financial framework. None of this is financial advice or a recommendation to purchase shares.*

---

The company is Smartoptics, ticker SMOP on Euronext Oslo.

I wrote this piece a few weeks ago:

They went up 25% since and have now retraced to the exact level I initially bought (~48.5).

Smartoptics reported Q1 revenue of $22.9 million, up 59.6% year over year. Gross margin came in at 48.2%. EBITDA was $2.7 million, EBITDA margin was 11.7%, and adjusted EBITDA margin was 13.7% after excluding relocation costs.

That is a strong quarter, but as usual, the bigger update came from management's commentary.

The original thesis was that AI infrastructure demand would eventually show up in a meaningful way in the metro, regional, data-center interconnect, CSP, and enterprise transport layers. This quarter gave us much more direct evidence that this is already happening. Management talked about GPU clusters, data-center interconnect, machine-to-machine traffic, cloud and AI customers, Neo Scalers, CSPs, and enterprises building their own AI infrastructure.

Their framing was that data centers need power, cooling, and connectivity. Smartoptics sells the connectivity layer.

The company sits one layer removed from headline optical component names that I discuss like Lumentum or Coherent. But as AI infrastructure scales, traffic has to move between facilities, across regions, through operator networks, and into enterprise environments. That is where Smartoptics fits, and management is now connecting those dots much more directly.

---

### Demand Is Ahead Of Supply

One of the most important signals in the quarter was book-to-bill, which was considerably higher than 1. Management said they could have delivered more in Q1 if they had a production facility two or three times larger.

That is the type of constraint that we see often in this industry right now. The company is getting more demand than it can currently ship. Smartoptics is moving into a larger consolidated production facility, which should help over time. Q2 may have timing noise because the first few weeks were disrupted by the move, but management was clear that market demand has shown no signs of slowing.

If revenue timing gets pushed around because of the relocation, that is manageable. If demand fades, that would be a much bigger issue. Based on this call, demand still appears strong.

---

### The Share-Gain Story Is Getting More Obvious

The largest optical vendors are focused on the biggest hyperscaler customers. That creates an opening underneath them. Mid-sized operators, regional CSPs, ISPs, financial customers, data-center operators, and newer AI infrastructure customers still need capacity and vendors that can actually ship.

Smartoptics is filling that gap. In the U.S., the company had orders from around 100 accounts in the quarter. Their largest order booker was an unannounced U.S. Tier 2 operator at roughly 15% of quarterly order booking. They also called out regional Tier 2 operators, a California-based ISP, financial and algorithmic trading customers, and their first individual Neo Scaler order.

This is exactly how a smaller optical transport company can compound. Smartoptics wins by serving customers being underserved while the biggest vendors chase the biggest accounts. That opening looks larger today than when I first wrote about the company.

The retention piece is still important too. More than 50% of 2025 revenue came from customers the company already had in 2020, which tells me this is compounding on top of an installed base rather than constantly rebuilding the revenue base from scratch.

The other important detail is that onboarding cycles appear to be compressing. Management said processes that used to take one to three years can now happen in two to four quarters. That is a big deal because it means the supply gap is accelerating customer decisions rather than simply creating theoretical interest.

Europe remains one of the most important upside levers. Management previously described EMEA as looking similar to where the U.S. was before that region broke out. Americas was still the strongest geography in Q1, but EMEA also grew strongly, especially in the Nordics, U.K., and Ireland. If Europe follows even part of the U.S. playbook, the revenue base can keep scaling from here.

---

### The Product Cycle And The 2027 Kicker

Smartoptics is moving further into higher-end optical transport use cases. Management talked about the gray zone between metro and regional networks and more advanced long-haul applications. That is important because the addressable market expands as the product capability improves.

Management cited Cignal AI's optical transport market estimate moving from $16.5 billion in 2025 to $24.7 billion in 2030. The exact number matters less than the direction. The market is expanding, and Smartoptics believes more of it is becoming reachable as their portfolio improves. The 800G cycle, software and services, and higher-capacity transponders and muxponders all fit directly into that.

The open optical approach is a key part of the story. Customers want capacity, flexibility, faster deployment, and less vendor lock-in. That positioning becomes more attractive when demand is accelerating and larger vendors are stretched.

There is one smaller 2027 angle worth keeping in the model. Smartoptics also sells into enterprise storage networks, where demand tends to move in refresh cycles tied to new Fibre Channel switch generations from Broadcom/Brocade. Management said its 64G DWDM response is coming this summer, with qualification work following after that. This points more toward a 2027 contribution than a near-term revenue driver. It is smaller than AI transport, Europe, 800G, and production capacity, but it gives the model one more possible product-cycle tailwind.

---

### Q2 Is The Next Execution Test

Q2 will likely be the quarter that is up for debate. Smartoptics spent the first few weeks consolidating into a new production facility. Management said those weeks were meaningfully below where they would have been without the move but framed the issue as delayed shipments rather than lost demand.

A clean version of Q2 would be revenue flat to up sequentially despite the move, gross margin still near the high 40s, book-to-bill above 1, and a target update that shows higher long-term ambition. A timing-noise version would be revenue below Q1 but still solid year over year, with management clearly explaining that shipments moved across quarters rather than disappeared. A bad version would be weaker book-to-bill, margin pressure, or a target update that feels vague relative to the scale of the opportunity.

The Q2 target update is actually the next real catalyst. Management was careful to say they are improving the framework rather than walking it back. They want to add more KPIs and set tougher goals. The old framework was built before the current demand acceleration became this visible. If the update comes with higher ambition and better disclosure, the market may reward them.

---

### Financial Framework

My model needs to move higher.

Smartoptics did **$75.3 million** of revenue in 2025. Q1 came in at **$22.9 million**, which annualizes to roughly **$91.6 million**. That came during the seasonally weakest quarter, while book-to-bill was still considerably above 1.

For **year-end 2026**, my framework is:

> Bear case: **$92M revenue**, ~11% EBITDA margin, **NOK 33–38**
> Base case: **$105M revenue**, ~13.5% EBITDA margin, **NOK 54–59**
> Bull case: **$118M revenue**, ~15% EBITDA margin, **NOK 72–78**

The bear case assumes Q2 timing noise, component shortages, and slower growth after the Q1 spike. The base case assumes demand stays strong, Europe keeps improving, and larger accounts continue converting. The bull case assumes book-to-bill stays above 1, the new production setup unlocks more shipments, and Neo Scaler demand becomes more visible.

For **year-end 2027**, the upside gets more interesting:

> Bear case: **$105M revenue**, ~12% EBITDA margin, **NOK 38–43**
> Base case: **$135M revenue**, ~15.5% EBITDA margin, **NOK 76–83**
> Bull case: **$165M revenue**, ~18% EBITDA margin, **NOK 109–118**

The 2027 base case is the clean compounding setup: Europe contributes more, 800G becomes meaningful, software and services grow with the installed base, and gross margin stays in the high 40s.

The 2027 bull case is where Smartoptics becomes a much larger small-cap optics story. That would require sustained above-market growth, larger customers, better production capacity, Neo Scaler traction, and stronger operating leverage.

That is why this pullback has my attention.

On my 2026 base case, Smartoptics trades around **4.7x sales**. On my 2026 bull case, it trades closer to **4.2x sales**. If the company moves toward my 2027 base or bull case, today's valuation falls into the **3x–4x sales** range.

For a profitable optical transport company growing revenue almost **60%**, holding gross margin near **48%**, and seeing book-to-bill above 1, I find this attractive for my own risk:reward tolerance.

I do think more downside is possible, so while I am adding some here, I will also be watching the 44 and 40 levels which have shown solid support in the past.

---

### Risks

Q2 could be messy because of the production move.

Component shortages are real, with management specifically calling out laser chips used in pump lasers for EDFAs.

Competition is a risk if larger vendors shorten lead times or push harder into the mid-market.

Valuation is a risk because expectations are higher now and the company needs to keep executing.

And if the broader AI infrastructure cycle slows, optics names across the board could re-rate lower, and Smartoptics would likely get pulled with that group.
