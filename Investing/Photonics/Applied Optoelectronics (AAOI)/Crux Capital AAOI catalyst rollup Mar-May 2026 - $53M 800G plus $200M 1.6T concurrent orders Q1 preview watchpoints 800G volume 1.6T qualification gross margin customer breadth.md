# AAOI catalyst rollup Mar-May 2026

This note consolidates two Crux Capital posts on [[Applied Optoelectronics (AAOI)]]:

1. **2026-03-23** — announcement of a $53M+ initial 800G order from the same hyperscale customer that placed the $200M 1.6T order
2. **2026-05-07** — pre-call note the morning of AAOI's Q1 2026 earnings, framing the call as the point where the past few months of announcements need to start showing up in the P&L

Provenance is preserved inline via `(per Crux <date>)` citations.

## Framing — orders → production

Crux's overarching framing across both posts: the last few months were about *de-risking the dream*. Management gave the roadmap in Q4, then began checking boxes — firmware progress, 800G orders, the $200M+ 1.6T order, a larger Texas footprint, a ~$20M state grant, more laser capacity, more hyperscaler interest (per Crux 2026-05-07).

The next layer is conversion. Crux's causal chain (per Crux 2026-05-07):

> Orders need to become qualified and then become shipments.
> Shipments need to become recognized revenue.
> Revenue needs to improve margin.
> Capacity needs to become actual production.

The Q1 2026 call is where that conversion either starts to land in the financials or doesn't.

## The 2026-03-23 $53M 800G order

On 2026-03-23, AAOI announced a new **initial volume order of $53M+ for 800G single-mode datacenter transceivers** (per Crux 2026-03-23). The buyer is the **same major hyperscale customer** that had recently placed the **$200M 1.6T volume order** referenced in prior Crux pieces.

Why it matters (per Crux 2026-03-23): the dual order resolves a real bear concern — that hyperscalers would skip 800G and force the industry to wait through the 1.6T transition. Crux's read is the opposite: **1.6T does not kill 800G demand**. Hyperscalers are buying both generations *concurrently* to support GPU cluster buildouts, manage power-per-bit, and stage cost curves. This is consistent with what other transceiver vendors — [[Lumentum (LITE)]], [[Coherent (COHR)]], [[Fabrinet (FN)]] — have been describing.

Step-function math (per Crux 2026-03-23):

- Q4 2025 800G revenue was **<$4M**.
- The $53M order ships **Q2 2026 → mid-Q3 2026** — roughly 1.5 quarters.
- That alone is a **>13x sequential step** on the 800G base.

CFO Stefan Murry explicitly framed this as an **"initial" order** and **"first of more to come"** as the customer scales AI infrastructure across multiple regions (per Crux 2026-03-23). The compressed delivery window signals management's confidence in execution. The order also concretely populates the $217M-mo 800G ladder that management framed on the Q4 call (see [[Crux Capital 2026-02-26 - AAOI Q4 call sets $378M-mo transceiver target by mid-2027, tripling Texas laser capacity, 95% AI lasers by EoY]]).

Crux's tone: straightforwardly bullish. *"We like to see this! The more orders, the better. And the tight time frame really shows the belief in management that they can execute"* (Crux 2026-03-23).

Verbatim framing from the 2026-03-23 piece: *"This $53 million order comes from the exact same major hyperscale customer that recently placed a massive $200 million volume order for AOI's next-generation 1.6T transceivers. This dynamic perfectly validates AOI's technological roadmap, proving that the transition to 1.6T does not kill 800G demand. Instead, hyperscalers are actively buying both generations concurrently to support massive GPU cluster buildups and manage their long-term operating costs, power efficiency, and cost per bit."* Crux further notes the delivery timeline is *"extremely compressed"* and that fulfilling a $53M order in a roughly 1.5-quarter window *"represents an explosive step-function increase in their near-term run rate"* (Crux 2026-03-23).

A subsequent update referenced in the Q1 preview disclosed the order was **upsized to $124M** from the same hyperscale customer shortly after the initial announcement (per Crux 2026-05-07).

## Q1 2026 print — the numbers

Q1 guide and recent history (per Crux 2026-05-07):

- **Q1 guide**: $150M to $165M revenue, 29% to 31% adjusted gross margin, –$0.09 to breakeven EPS
- **Q4 actual**: $134.3M revenue, 31.4% adjusted gross margin
- **FY25**: $455.7M revenue, 30.9% adjusted gross margin

The Q1 guide is a step-up in revenue and a slight dip in gross margin vs Q4. Crux's view: the stock reaction will depend more on the Q2 guide and commentary than the Q1 print itself. Management already told the market 800G was supposed to dominate datacenter revenue starting in Q2. Q1 was always intended as a bridge quarter (per Crux 2026-05-07).

At a **~$13B+ market cap on $455.7M trailing revenue**, AAOI is "definitely priced like a transformation." The stock is roughly **3x where it was at the last call**, and expectations are running high. *"This call has all the fixings to be explosive. In both directions"* (Crux 2026-05-07).

Key behavioral note from the Q4 call: management acknowledged the 800G delay, then redirected toward Q2 and the full-year ramp. They may do something similar here if Q1 still has limited 800G revenue. The market may not like that, but **if Q2 language is very specific rather than directional**, that could help (per Crux 2026-05-07).

Crux frames the Q1 print logic explicitly: *"Management already told us 800G was supposed to dominate datacenter revenue starting in Q2. Q1 was always intended as a bridge quarter. If Q1 is fine but Q2 commentary is weak, that is a problem. If Q1 is fine and Q2 confirms the 800G ramp, the story gets stronger"* (Crux 2026-05-07). The Q1 guide is a step-up in revenue and a slight dip in gross margin vs Q4, but the actual stock reaction will *"depend more on the Q2 guide and commentary than the Q1 print, in my opinion"* (Crux 2026-05-07).

## Bull / Fine / Weak framework

Crux's print-day rubric (per Crux 2026-05-07):

- **Bullish**: revenue **>$165M**, adjusted gross margin **>31%**, Q2 guide **clearly reflects 800G volume shipments**
- **Fine**: revenue in the **upper half** of guidance, gross margin **30-31%**, management **confirms 800G ramping in Q2**
- **Weak**: revenue **near $150M**, gross margin **below guide**, **800G ramp pushed deeper into Q2 or Q3**

Important caveat: the Q1 number can be fine and the stock could still disappoint if Q2 doesn't clearly show 800G entering the P&L. *"'On track' may be enough for the thesis, but the stock near term may need more than 'on track' depending on expectations into the print"* (Crux 2026-05-07).

## Nine watchpoints into the print

### 1. 800G volume shipments

The most important near-term proof point. The $53M order on 2026-03-23 (shipments Q2 → mid-Q3, see above) was **upsized to $124M** from the same hyperscale customer (per Crux 2026-05-07). The order book is being built; Crux wants confirmation that shipments have started. The line Crux wants to hear: **"800G volume shipments have started."** If language stays around qualification and ramp preparation, that is a miss. Crux also wants analysts to push for actual 800G revenue contribution, because "ramping" language can hide a wide range of outcomes (per Crux 2026-05-07).

### 2. 1.6T qualification

On **2026-03-09**, AAOI announced a **$200M+ 1.6T volume order** from a major hyperscale customer. Shipments expected to **begin early Q3 2026 and complete in Q4** after qualification. Early Q3 needs to stay early Q3. If language shifts from "early Q3" to broader second-half framing, that is a timing slip worth flagging. The phrase Crux wants: **"Qualification remains on track"** (per Crux 2026-05-07).

### 3. Gross margin

Revenue growth is necessary but insufficient. The biggest re-rate over time comes from proving that 800G ramps carry better utilization, better mix, and better margin conversion *while* also growing revenue. Q4 was 31.4%; the Q1 guide is 29-31%. **If revenue grows while gross margin stalls or falls with no clear bridge higher over time, that is where the quality concern lives.** What Crux wants: management tying margin improvement to utilization, product mix, yields, and internal lasers as 800G scales (per Crux 2026-05-07).

### 4. Customer breadth

The 10-K shows the **top ten customers represented 96.6% of 2025 revenue**, with **Digicomm at 53.1%** and **Microsoft at 28.8%**. Management has said the mid-2027 forecast is based on multiple hyperscale customers reaching **10%+ contribution by end of 2026**. Crux wants to see **hyperscaler #2 moving from interest to orders**, or **hyperscaler #3 becoming more visible** (per Crux 2026-05-07).

### 5. Texas capacity and internal lasers

The physical capacity story has been de-risked materially. AAOI added two adjacent buildings in **Pearland**, bringing the Houston-area footprint toward **~900,000 sq ft** total across **Sugar Land + Pearland** facilities. Management said this supports **up to 700,000 units of 800G and 1.6T transceivers per month** and a **~350% expansion of laser fabrication capacity by end of 2027**. The **$20.85M Texas Semiconductor Innovation Fund grant** adds credibility.

The remaining question: whether **equipment installation, line qualification, final test capacity, and yield ramp** are on schedule. The key distinction is **customer-backed capacity vs forecast-backed capacity**. The first is bullish. The second carries more execution and dilution risk.

On internal lasers specifically: laser supply is a major industry bottleneck for 800G+ shipments. **AAOI manufactures the majority of its own laser chips at Sugar Land**, which is a structural advantage if it holds at scale. The line Crux wants: **"Laser capacity is scaling ahead of module demand"** (per Crux 2026-05-07).

### 6. Firmware and interoperability

Management said at Raymond James that the new firmware had shipped and the interoperability issue was behind them. **This should be confirmed and closed on this call.** If new interop work shows up, that is a real credibility hit given how specifically management addressed it (per Crux 2026-05-07).

### 7. Balance sheet and dilution

As of **year-end 2025** (per Crux 2026-05-07):

- **$216M cash**
- **$244M accounts receivable**
- **$183M inventory**
- **$130M convertible senior notes**

Since then: AAOI disclosed selling **~$250M of stock through its ATM by 2026-03-12** and **expanded the ATM program to $500M**, leaving roughly **$250M available** as of that filing date. Also received the **$20.85M Texas grant** tied to its Sugar Land expansion.

The updated Q1 balance sheet matters because the ramp is capital intensive. **Dilution is acceptable if it is clearly funding customer-backed capacity that converts into revenue.** The question Crux wants analysts to push on: *"How much of the 2026 capacity expansion is backed by customer commitments versus forecasted demand?"*

### 8. CATV

In **FY2025**, AAOI generated **$245.1M from CATV** and **$195.7M from datacenter**. The business is more balanced than people may realize. **CATV funds the ramp and buys time.** If CATV weakens while the datacenter ramp is still building, the financial bridge may get narrower. **CATV staying healthy while datacenter takes over growth is the right outcome** (per Crux 2026-05-07).

### 9. CPO and ELSFP

AAOI's longer-term roadmap includes ELSFP modules for co-packaged and near-package optics, with a stated target of **400,000 ELSFP modules per month by Q4 2027**. Internal high-power laser capability is a real advantage here if it holds. This is a **secondary priority** for the print — 800G conversion and 1.6T qualification come first. **CPO optionality can make the story larger but weak near-term execution would undermine it** (per Crux 2026-05-07).

## Peer backdrop

- [[Lumentum (LITE)]] and [[Coherent (COHR)]] confirmed the **supply-demand gap remains very large** (per Crux 2026-05-07). The companies that can bring supply will be rewarded. Crux wants to hear that customers are taking AAOI seriously and forming **more LTAs**.
- **Lumentum talked about outsourcing CW lasers** — could increase demand for AAOI if AAOI brings the supply (and chooses to ship them out) (per Crux 2026-05-07).
- *"AAOI is in a great position because they are building a massive manufacturing footprint"* (Crux 2026-05-07).
- Many optics names took **-10-15% on print** and then either rebounded or drifted as the market digests multiples. *"The market is pricing in lots of growth. The market needs time to digest them and decide if they command their multiple"* (Crux 2026-05-07).
- The 2026-03-23 piece highlighted the same dynamic — that LITE, COHR, and [[Fabrinet (FN)]] all described concurrent 800G + 1.6T buying — as cross-confirmation of the AAOI signal (per Crux 2026-03-23).
