## Original Content

Once again, the AAOI call was full of fireworks.

If you were not there for the last call a few months ago, AAOI was trading around $4.5 billion with roughly $500 million in full-year revenue. On that call, management laid out a path toward roughly $378 million in monthly transceiver revenue by mid-2027. The market loved this and has since re-rated the company to roughly $12 billion in market cap.

Now, management did more than confirm that target. They increased the framework by almost $100 million per month.

So now we are facing a potential situation where management sees visibility to **$471 million per month** of datacenter transceiver revenue by mid-2027. If you layer in more than $325 million of annual CATV revenue, the total framework approaches $500 million per month, or roughly **$6 billion annualized.**

That is not formal FY2027 guidance. It is a mid-2027 run-rate framework. But it is still massive.

The stock is valued at roughly $12 billion.

This sounds too good to be true, right?

The fact that the market cap sits at only $12 billion tells us that this is far from the whole story. A long list of things still has to go right:

- Capacity needs to expand dramatically and very quickly.
- Capex needs to ramp, and dilution is on the table.
- Margins need to improve materially.
- Orders need to get qualified.
- Qualified orders need to turn into on-time shipments.
- Customers need to diversify.
- Management needs to prove their execution.
- They have to prove they are a crucial player in CPO.
- 800G and 1.6T revenue need to show up in a real way.

This report unpacks all of it: what changed, what still has to happen, how I am updating the financial model, how I think the market will treat the stock, what my biggest concerns are, and how I am thinking about the risk/reward from here.

### My Quick Thoughts

The headline earnings were pretty week. The call was more bullish.

Revenue came in near the low end of guidance. Gross margin was soft. Q2 gross margin guidance stayed soft. If you only looked at the press release, you would definitely expect the stock to get hit.

But the call changed the read. Management raised the full-year 2026 framework. They quantified 800G revenue for the first time. They confirmed 800G is now shipping in volume. They increased the mid-2027 monthly transceiver revenue framework from $378 million to $471 million per month. And the CEO explicitly clarified that the $471 million number is revenue, not capacity.

Q1 proved the ramp has started. The rest of 2026 has to prove scale.

---

### The Actual Print

They reported Q1 revenue of $151.1 million, up 51% year over year and 13% sequentially. That was inside the prior guide of $150 million to $165 million but near the low end. Non-GAAP gross margin was 29.2%, also inside the 29% to 31% guide but again near the low end. Non-GAAP EPS was a loss of $0.07, within the prior range.

For Q2, management guided revenue of $180 million to $198 million, non-GAAP gross margin of 29% to 30%, and non-GAAP EPS of -$0.03 to +$0.03.

Revenue is ramping. Margin leverage has yet to show up. That is why the print and the call have to be read separately.

---

### 800G Finally Crossed From Order Book to Revenue

This was the first thing I needed to see. Before Q1, AAOI had the roadmap and the orders. Now we have the first proof that the order book is becoming revenue.

AAOI completed its first volume shipment of 800G single-mode transceivers to a large hyperscale customer in Q1. Q1 800G revenue was $4.6 million, or 5.6% of datacenter revenue. That is still quite small. Q1 was mostly a 100G and 400G quarter: 41.9% from 100G, 46.7% from 200G and 400G, 5.6% from 800G, and 5.6% from 10G and 40G.

800G has started but the model is still waiting for it to carry its weight.

Management said they expect to ship nearly four times the quantity of 800G in Q2 compared to Q1. That is the handoff. Q1 proved 800G can ship. Q2 needs to prove 800G can scale. The key question has changed from "can they ship 800G?" to "how fast does it scale, and when does it show up in margins?"

---

### The 2026 Ramp Got Bigger, But It Is Back-Half Loaded

Management raised the full-year 2026 framework to more than $1.1 billion of revenue and more than $140 million of non-GAAP operating income, up from more than $1 billion and more than $120 million respectively.

But the path is extremely back-half loaded. Q1 was $151 million. Q2 midpoint is roughly $189 million. The first half is tracking toward roughly $340 million. To exceed $1.1 billion, AAOI needs more than $760 million in the second half. That is a major step up and puts a lot of pressure on the Q3 guidance that will come with the next print.

Management addressed this directly. Q3 could be up 60% to 80% versus Q2, and Q4 should see another large step. Management also said actual demand is closer to $1.4 billion to $1.5 billion. The limiter is capacity, materials, qualification, and timing.

If they hit hiccups, I think something closer to $700 million of revenue becomes the realistic outcome rather than $1 billion-plus. That would be a significant break in the ramp.

---

### The Mid-2027 Framework Is the Real Upside Case

The biggest long-term update was the new mid-2027 revenue framework of approximately $471 million per month of datacenter transceiver revenue. The breakdown: $90 million per month from 100G and 400G, $217 million per month from 800G, and $164 million per month from 1.6T. This is up from the prior $378 million per month framework, an effective $100 million per month upgrade.

The CEO clarified this is revenue rather than capacity. Actual capacity is higher. Actual demand is higher. But $471 million monthly is what management feels comfortable committing to based on orders in hand, minimum customer commitments, qualified equipment, and the capacity ramp.

That distinction is critical. This is a revenue framework backed by orders, a theoretical slide would look very different. Annualized, $471 million per month equals roughly $5.65 billion of datacenter transceiver revenue. Add more than $325 million of annual CATV, and the overall framework approaches $6 billion annualized.

The valuation debate is now simple: how much of that framework do you believe? 100%? 75%? 50%? 25%? That determines the stock.

One important nuance is that this is a mid-2027 run-rate framework, not formal FY2027 revenue guidance. That distinction matters when building a model.

### Capacity, Capex, and Dilution

AAOI exited Q1 with total manufacturing capacity approaching 100,000 units per month of 800G and 1.6T. They expect to approach 150,000 units per month this quarter. By end of 2026, they expect more than 650,000 pieces per month, with about 30% from Texas. By end of 2027, more than 930,000 pieces per month, with more than half from Texas.

The Houston-area footprint has expanded dramatically across Sugar Land, the 210,000-square-foot facility under development that should begin initial production in Q3, and the Pearland buildings coming online in early 2027. The old bear case around physical real estate is weaker. The new risk is execution: installing lines, qualifying equipment, securing materials, completing final test, and converting capacity into revenue on schedule.

Capacity is expensive. AAOI ended Q1 with $449.4 million of cash and equivalents, up from $216 million at Q4 end. But Q1 capex was $68.7 million, mostly for 400G, 800G, and 1.6T capacity. Management said quarterly capex will be above Q1 levels and expects to fund the expansion through cash on hand, operations, equity sales, and additional debt.

The reason management sounds confident is that they view this ramp as replication rather than reinvention. In the Q&A, Stefan Murry said the company has already built significant capacity in its Asian factories over the last few years, and the current expansion is essentially adding increments with the same type of equipment and the same manufacturing process. That does not eliminate execution risk, but it lowers the risk versus building an entirely new process from scratch. He also emphasized that much of the equipment is developed in-house, which reduces exposure to industry-wide equipment bottlenecks, and that automation reduces the need to scale labor manually.

Dilution is still on the table. It is acceptable if it funds customer-backed capacity that converts into revenue. It becomes a problem if the revenue ramp slips while margins stay weak. I would expect another $250-$500m ATM.

---

### Margins Are the Biggest Quality Test

Q1 non-GAAP gross margin was 29.2%. Q2 guide is 29% to 30%. That is soft.

Management explained that Q1 and Q2 are ramp quarters. The 800G and 1.6T processes are still being fine-tuned. Short-term datacenter mix is a headwind. The explanation makes sense. But the improvement needs to show up soon.

Thompson Lin said he believes they can get to around 35% gross margin by end of 2026. The long-term objective remains around 40%. These are the checkpoints that are really important. Revenue growth is the first step. Margin conversion is what makes growth valuable.

If gross margin is still stuck around 29% to 30% after the ramp starts, the market will push back. If it moves into the mid-30s in the second half, the model changes substantially.

### Internal Lasers, InP, and CPO

Management repeatedly emphasized that in-house laser capability is a strategic advantage, and they are right.

If the industry is constrained by laser supply, companies with internal InP laser capacity are in a much stronger position. AAOI says internal manufacturing has helped them avoid shortages affecting other suppliers. Now they are scaling that capability aggressively, targeting a 350% expansion of laser fabrication capacity by the end of 2027.

The transition from 4-inch to 6-inch is another key long term checkpoint. Management said they are currently working with a 4-inch growth process and targeting 6-inch by the end of next year. They also said they have four to five InP substrate suppliers, with four outside China, and believe they have enough inventory for at least one year.

This is where the CPO discussion got more interesting.

AAOI's ELSFP modules are the external light-source products they are building for co-packaged optics. These use the ultra-narrow-linewidth, high-power lasers management announced late last year. Production is very limited today, but management expects to ramp later this year and into 2027, eventually reaching about 400,000 ELSFP modules per month by the end of 2027.

An important detail is how much larger these CPO lasers are. Management said an ELSFP / CPO laser is roughly 300mW to 400mW, compared with around 70mW for 800G and 100mW for 1.6T. They also said the CPO laser die can be 5x to 6x larger. That means CPO consumes far more InP wafer real estate, which makes internal laser capacity even more valuable.

This is also why management is pushing the InP fab expansion so hard. They talked about adding MOCVD, epi, steppers, coaters, and other equipment. They also said InP laser manufacturing capacity is already tight across the industry and could become even more acute as ELSFP ramps.

The customer side also sounded more concrete. Management said they are working with several customers on potential three-year long-term agreements that include lasers and ELSFP. That gives the CPO / external light-source story more substance than a generic roadmap item.

CPO revenue is still early, but the call made the setup more real. If AAOI can scale high-power internal lasers while the industry moves toward external light sources, the company could become more than a transceiver supplier. It could become one of the few U.S.-based suppliers with internal InP capacity, module assembly, and a credible path into the external laser source layer of future AI networks.

---

### CATV and Customer Breadth

CATV continues to be stronger than people really cared about. Q1 CATV revenue was $66.8 million, up 24% sequentially. Q2 guidance is $75 million to $80 million. Full-year expectation is more than $325 million.

CATV is a support layer rather than the re-rating driver. The re-rate comes from AI datacenter optics. But CATV gives AAOI revenue support and bridge funding while the datacenter ramp scales.

Customer concentration is still significant. In Q1, the top ten customers represented 98% of revenue. Three customers exceeded 10%: one CATV customer at 44% and two datacenter customers at 26% and 25%. The quality of the concentration is improving though. Multiple hyperscalers are now tied to 800G, 1.6T, lasers, and potentially ELSFP.

### Updated Financial Model

I am valuing AAOI on a **12-month view using 2027E revenue**, because the stock is already trading on whether management's mid-2027 framework becomes believable.

Management is now talking about **$471 million per month of datacenter transceiver revenue by mid-2027**, plus more than **$325 million of annual CATV revenue**. That points to a roughly **$6 billion annualized run-rate framework** if everything works.

But mid-2027 run-rate and full-year 2027 revenue are different things, so I am still applying haircuts.

#### Bear Case

The bear case assumes the ramp gets messy.

800G grows, but slower than expected. 1.6T takes longer to qualify and ship. Gross margins fail to move meaningfully above 30%. Capex and dilution stay elevated. The market starts questioning how much of the mid-2027 framework is actually deliverable.

Using **$1.7 billion of 2027 revenue**, an **8x revenue multiple**, and roughly **110 million diluted shares**, I get an implied value around **$124 per share**.

---

#### Base Case

The base case assumes AAOI executes, but with real haircuts.

800G ramps. 1.6T contributes. CATV stays healthy. Gross margins begin moving into the mid-30s. The company captures only part of the full mid-2027 framework, but enough for the market to keep valuing it as a scarce AI optics capacity winner.

Using **$2.9 billion of 2027 revenue**, a **10x revenue multiple**, and roughly **115 million diluted shares**, I get an implied value around **$252 per share**.

---

#### Bull Case

The bull case is where the rerate really happens.

AAOI gets much closer to management's mid-2027 framework. 800G scales hard. 1.6T becomes a major line. Internal lasers become a clear strategic edge. Gross margin moves toward the high-30s.

Using **$4.2 billion of 2027 revenue**, an **11x revenue multiple**, and roughly **120 million diluted shares**, I get an implied value around **$385 per share**.

#### Summary

So my updated framework becomes roughly:

Please understand that the base case (and obviously the bull and blue sky) are pricing in a healthy amount of execution success. If the next few quarters do not go the way management is saying, these will be revised down heavily. This is just what I see visible today.

As we get further along and execution (hopefully improves) my model can easily move higher as well. The Q2 print with the guide for Q3 will be huge.

### How the Market Will Treat This, and My Strategy

The stock will stay volatile because both sides have real arguments.

The bulls have: 800G is shipping, Q2 units step up nearly 4x, full-year guide moved higher, demand exceeds capacity through mid-2027, the $471 million monthly number is revenue, internal lasers are increasingly strategic, CATV is stronger than expected, and CPO gives the story a future layer.

The bears have: Q1 revenue was low-end, gross margin was weak, Q2 margin guide is still weak, the second-half ramp is enormous, capex is rising, dilution is likely, and management has to execute a very complicated capacity ramp across multiple products, facilities, and customers.

Both sides have a case. I remain interested because the upside case is now much larger than a few months ago while the company has started checking real boxes. But the risk is real and the execution is hard.

My own strategy has not changed. I am already positioned and do not **need** a larger allocation, so I am in no rush. But at a certain level, I am interested in some swing trades or some adds to my long term position. The levels I am watching are $130, $120 and $110. This is not financial advice. This is also not a prediction.

Q2 800G revenue is first. Management said Q2 units should be nearly 4x Q1. I want to see that in the dollar contribution next quarter.

Q3 guide is the real inflection test. If the story is working, Q3 should be a major step up from Q2.

Gross margin above 30% and moving toward 35% is the quality checkpoint. Q3 and Q4 are the key quarters for margin proof.

1.6T qualification and shipment timing has to stay on track. Early Q3 shipments were the stated plan.

Capex and dilution need to stay tied to customer-backed capacity rather than speculative buildout.

Laser and InP equipment qualification is the 2027 foundation.

The Needham conference next week may give management a chance to clean up the message and give investors more detail after the market digests this call.
