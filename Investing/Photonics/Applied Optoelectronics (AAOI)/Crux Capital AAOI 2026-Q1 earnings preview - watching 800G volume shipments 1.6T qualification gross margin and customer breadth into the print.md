# Crux Capital AAOI 2026-Q1 earnings preview — watching 800G volume shipments, 1.6T qualification, gross margin and customer breadth into the print

Pre-call note (published the morning of [[Applied Optoelectronics (AAOI)]]'s Q1 2026 earnings). Crux frames this as the call where the last few months of announcements — firmware progress, 800G orders, the $200M+ 1.6T order, Texas footprint, $20.85M Texas grant, more laser capacity, more hyperscaler interest — need to start showing up in the P&L. Orders → qualified → shipments → recognized revenue → margin → actual production.

**Guide context**: Q1 $150-165M / adj GM 29-31% / adj EPS –$0.09 to breakeven; Q4 was $134.3M / 31.4% GM; FY25 $455.7M / 30.9% GM. At ~$13B market cap on $455.7M trailing revenue, the stock is already pricing a 2026-27 transformation. Stock is ~3x where it was at the last call. Q1 print can be fine and the stock can still disappoint if Q2 doesn't clearly show 800G entering the P&L.

**Bull/Fine/Weak framework**:
- **Bullish**: revenue >$165M, GM >31%, Q2 guide clearly reflects 800G volume shipments
- **Fine**: revenue upper-half of guide, GM 30-31%, management confirms 800G ramping in Q2
- **Weak**: revenue near $150M, GM below guide, 800G ramp pushed deeper into Q2 or Q3

**What Crux is watching**:
1. **800G volume shipments** — wants exact line: "800G volume shipments have started." $53M order on Mar 23 upsized to $124M from same hyperscaler. "Qualification/ramp preparation" language = miss.
2. **1.6T qualification** — $200M+ order announced Mar 9, shipments "early Q3 2026" → finish Q4. Wants "Qualification remains on track."
3. **Gross margin** — needs tying to utilization, mix, yields, internal lasers as 800G scales. Margin stall while revenue grows = quality concern.
4. **Customer breadth** — top-10 = 96.6% of FY25 rev; Digicomm 53.1%, Microsoft 28.8%. Mid-2027 forecast needs multiple hyperscalers ≥10% by end-2026. Wants hyperscaler #2 moving from interest to orders, #3 becoming visible.
5. **Texas capacity + internal lasers** — Pearland adds bring Houston footprint to ~900K sq ft (Sugar Land + Pearland); supports up to 700K units/month of 800G/1.6T transceivers; ~350% laser fab expansion by end-2027. Wants: "Laser capacity is scaling ahead of module demand." Customer-backed capacity bullish; forecast-backed = dilution risk.
6. **Firmware/interop** — Raymond James commentary said firmware shipped, interop issue behind them. Needs to be confirmed closed.
7. **Balance sheet/dilution** — YE25: $216M cash, $244M AR, $183M inventory, $130M converts. Disclosed ~$250M sold via ATM by Mar 12, ATM expanded to $500M with ~$250M remaining. Plus $20.85M Texas grant tied to Sugar Land. Wants analysts to push on % of 2026 capacity expansion that's customer-backed vs forecast-backed.
8. **CATV** — FY25: $245.1M CATV, $195.7M datacenter (more balanced than people realize). CATV funds the ramp; needs to stay healthy while datacenter takes over growth.
9. **CPO / ELSFP** — 400K ELSFP modules/month target by Q4 2027; secondary priority, optional upside; weak near-term execution would undermine it.

**Peer backdrop**: [[Lumentum (LITE)]] and [[Coherent (COHR)]] confirmed supply-demand gap is large. Lumentum talked about outsourcing CW lasers — could increase demand for AAOI if AAOI brings supply (and chooses to ship them out). Many optics names took -10-15% on print then either rebounded or drifted as market digests multiples.

**Strategy**: Crux holding core into print, has swing-traded around it and taken some profit. Watching $129 and $119 levels for potential adds, decision rides on call tone. Personal-sizing/risk-tolerance disclaimer applies.

## Original Content

The last couple months were about de-risking the dream. AAOI gave us the roadmap, and then started checking boxes.

We saw firmware progress. 800G orders. A $200M+ 1.6T order. A larger Texas footprint. A ~$20M state grant. More laser capacity. More hyperscaler interest.

Now we need the next layer.

Orders need to become qualified and then become shipments.

Shipments need to become recognized revenue.

Revenue needs to improve margin.

Capacity needs to become actual production.

One thing worth keeping in mind going into this call is how management handled the last one. They acknowledged the 800G delay, then quickly redirected us toward Q2 and the full-year ramp. They may do something similar here if Q1 still has limited 800G revenue. The market may not like that, but if Q2 language is very specific rather than directional, that could help.

AAOI already guided Q1 revenue of $150M to $165M, adjusted gross margin of 29% to 31%, and adjusted EPS from a $0.09 loss to breakeven.

Last quarter they did $134.3M of revenue and 31.4% adjusted gross margin, so they already gave us a step-up in the rev guide (and a slight dip in gross margins). But the actual stock reaction will depend more on the Q2 guide and commentary than the Q1 print, in my opinion.

Management already told us 800G was supposed to dominate datacenter revenue starting in Q2. Q1 was always intended as a bridge quarter. If Q1 is fine but Q2 commentary is weak, that is a problem. If Q1 is fine and Q2 confirms the 800G ramp, the story gets stronger.

This call has all the fixings to be explosive. In both directions. We are up around 3x where we were in the last call, and expectations are running high.

***In this post I will dive into exactly what I am watching, what I think would read bullish/bearish, and what my own strategy is. Also, for paid subscribers I will also be doing a live chat during the call, highlighting what I am hearing. None of this is financial advice.***

### The Numbers

Q1 guide: $150M to $165M revenue, 29% to 31% adjusted gross margin, -$0.09 to breakeven EPS.

Q4 actual: $134.3M revenue, 31.4% adjusted gross margin.

FY25: $455.7M revenue, 30.9% adjusted gross margin.

**Bullish:** Revenue above $165M, adjusted gross margin above 31%, Q2 guide clearly reflects 800G volume shipments.

**Fine:** Revenue in the upper half of guidance, gross margin around 30% to 31%, management confirms 800G shipments are ramping in Q2.

**Weak:** Revenue near $150M, gross margin below guide, 800G ramp pushed deeper into Q2 or Q3.

The stock is already starting to reflect a major 2026 to 2027 ramp. At a $13B+ market cap against $455.7M of trailing revenue, this is definitely priced like a transformation. Yes, based forward projections there is still lots of room for upside of course, but it's not the same setup as it was going into earnings last time. The market is now somewhat pricing in the massive ramp that they are forecasting for next year.

For me, the Q1 number can be fine and the stock could still disappoint if Q2 does not clearly show the 800G ramp entering the P&L. That means "on track" may be enough for the thesis, but the stock near term may need more than "on track" depending on expectations into the print.

### What I Am Watching

**800G shipments.** This is a very important near-term proof point. AAOI announced a $53M+ 800G order on March 23 with Q2 shipments expected, then upsized it to $124M from the same hyperscale customer shortly after. The order book is being built. Now I want confirmation that shipments have started. The line I want to hear: "800G volume shipments have started." If the language stays around qualification and ramp preparation, that is a miss relative to expectations. I also want analysts to push for actual 800G revenue contribution, because "ramping" language can hide a wide range of outcomes.

**1.6T qualification.** On March 9, AAOI announced a $200M+ 1.6T volume order from a major hyperscale customer, with shipments expected to begin early Q3 2026 and complete in Q4 after qualification. Early Q3 needs to stay early Q3. If language shifts from "early Q3" to broader second-half framing, that is a timing slip worth flagging. The phrase I want: "Qualification remains on track."

**Gross margin.** Revenue growth is necessary but insufficient. The biggest re-rate over time comes from proving that 800G ramps carry better utilization, better mix, and better margin conversion (while also growing revenue of course). Last quarter was 31.4%. The Q1 guide is 29% to 31%. If revenue grows while gross margin stalls or falls with no clear bridge higher over time, that is where the quality concern lives. What I want to hear: management tying margin improvement to utilization, product mix, yields, and internal lasers as 800G scales.

**Customer breadth.** The 10-K shows the top ten customers represented 96.6% of 2025 revenue, with Digicomm at 53.1% and Microsoft at 28.8%. Management has said the mid-2027 forecast is based on multiple hyperscale customers reaching 10%+ contribution by end of 2026. I want to see hyperscaler two moving from interest to orders, or hyperscaler three becoming more visible.

**Texas capacity and internal lasers.** The physical capacity story has been de-risked materially. AAOI added two adjacent buildings in Pearland, bringing the Houston-area footprint toward 900,000 square feet total across Sugar Land and Pearland facilities. Management said this supports up to 700,000 units of 800G and 1.6T transceivers per month and a roughly 350% expansion of laser fabrication capacity by end of 2027. The $20.85M Texas Semiconductor Innovation Fund grant adds credibility. The remaining question is whether equipment installation, line qualification, final test capacity, and yield ramp are on schedule. The key distinction is customer-backed capacity versus capacity built on forecasts. The first is bullish. The second carries more execution and dilution risk. On internal lasers specifically, we know that laser supply is a major industry bottleneck for 800G+ shipments. AAOI manufactures the majority of its own laser chips at Sugar Land, which is a structural advantage if it holds at scale. The line I want: "Laser capacity is scaling ahead of module demand."

**Firmware and interoperability.** Management said at Raymond James that the new firmware had shipped and the interoperability issue was behind them. This should be confirmed and closed on this call. If new interoperability work shows up, that is a real credibility hit given how specifically management addressed it.

**Balance sheet and dilution.** As of year-end 2025, AAOI had $216M of cash, $244M of accounts receivable, $183M of inventory, and $130M of convertible senior notes. Since then, the company disclosed that it sold roughly $250M of stock through its ATM by March 12 and expanded the program to $500M, leaving roughly $250M available as of that filing date. AAOI also received a $20.85M Texas grant tied to its Sugar Land expansion. The updated Q1 balance sheet will be important because this ramp is capital intensive. Dilution is acceptable if it is clearly funding customer-backed capacity that converts into revenue. The question I want analysts to push on: "How much of the 2026 capacity expansion is backed by customer commitments versus forecasted demand?"

**CATV.** In FY2025, AAOI generated $245.1M from CATV and $195.7M from datacenter. The recent business is more balanced than people may realize. CATV funds the ramp and buys time. If CATV weakens while the datacenter ramp is still building, the financial bridge may get narrower. CATV staying healthy while datacenter takes over growth is the right outcome.

**CPO and ELSFP.** AAOI's longer-term roadmap includes ELSFP modules for co-packaged and near-package optics, with a stated target of 400,000 ELSFP modules per month by Q4 2027. Internal high-power laser capability is a real advantage here if it holds. This is a secondary priority for this report. 800G conversion and 1.6T qualification come first. CPO optionality can make the story larger but weak near-term execution would undermine it.

### Peer Backdrop

We already know that the supply demand gap remains very large after hearing from Lumentum and Coherent. That will not be a surprise here. The companies that can bring the supply will be rewarded. I want to hear that customers are taking AAOI seriously and are wanting to form more LTA's.

AAOI is in a great position because they are building a massive manufacturing footprint. Also, Lumentum talked about outsourcing CW lasers so that could even increase demand for AAOI if they could bring the supply (if they even want to ship them out).

We also saw how lots of our stocks reacted. -10-15% on the print and the some go up on the call and some have just consolidated or have steadily drifted. The market is pricing in lots of growth. The market needs time to digest them and decide if they command their multiple.

---

### What I Care About Most

Show that the announcements of the last few months are starting to show up in the financials, or will soon.

800G conversion is the first test. 1.6T qualification holding is the second. Gross margin direction is the third. Customer breadth is the fourth.

If those four things move in the right direction on this call, AAOI will become an execution investment more than a story investment.

There are lots of people that have been bearish AAOI since it was in the 60's and are ready to tell you how right they were. If AAOI is showing that management is a concern like people have been highlighting (based on prior shortcomings), and that they are all talk and no execution, then the sentiment could quickly shift in that direction again. On the flip side, if management is executing stronger than people give them credit for, the company can regain a lot of that lost faith.

I like the growth potential and that's why I want to hold my core. But I also recognize that the market is pricing in a lot of future growth and execution here and I want proof that execution is happening before becoming over sized again on my swing trade.

Like I said, my AAOI position is already significant. These levels would represent a significant drop in share price and this is not a projection or an expectation of what is to come.

This reflects my own personal sizing and risk tolerance, everyone should execute their own strategy.
