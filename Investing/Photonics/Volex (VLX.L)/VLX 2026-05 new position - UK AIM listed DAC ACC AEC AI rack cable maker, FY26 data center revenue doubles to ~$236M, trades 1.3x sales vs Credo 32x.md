#### The 1.6T angle

The 1.6T opportunity is really a **mix and margin-quality story**.

[Image — Volex CMD 2026 "Innovation is not just what we make – it's how we make it" slide; transcribed verbatim below]

> **Innovation is not just what we make – it's how we make it**
>
> In this market, product innovation alone is not enough. Creating the optimal manufacturing process provides significant value.
>
> **Process innovation**
> Every cable tracked. Every cable tested. Quality systems built from the ground up to scale rapidly and fulfil demand, even at unprecedented speed.
>
> **+**
>
> **Product innovation**
> Ready before our customers need us. Certified products at 200Gbps, 400Gbps, 800Gbps and 1.6Tbps. Each generation demonstrates capability and delivers return on investment.
>
> **The ability to manufacture rapidly at scale, without compromising quality, that is where lasting advantage is created. We invest in both.**

Volex is marketing 1.6T passive DAC and ACC solutions for very short-reach AI rack deployments. The key is that every speed migration creates a new qualification cycle. Volex says the move from 400G to 800G to 1.6T requires entirely new cables, which creates a recurring upgrade cycle rather than a one-time cable sale.

That should mean higher ASPs. A 1.6T cable is harder to engineer, harder to test, and more valuable to the customer than a lower-speed commodity cable. Volex's CMD deck says every cable is tracked, every cable is tested, and products are certified across 200G, 400G, 800G, and 1.6T.

So 1.6T should improve **margin quality**: more complexity, more qualification, more testing, more customer lock-in, and more pricing resilience.

If 1.6T becomes a production cycle, Volex gets a shot at higher ASPs, better mix, and more wallet share inside the same AI rack customers. If the economics mostly flow to the semiconductor layer or competition compresses pricing, the upside is smaller.

#### Does copper survive in a photonics world?

I am assuming I will get this pushback and I just want to discuss it right away. As most of you know, I own Credo and Semtech and Macom even though they have meaningful copper exposure. My really simple view on this is that boh copper and optics will continue to grow meaningfully for years.

Copper and optics solve different distance problems.

NVIDIA's own documentation says copper DAC is the lowest-cost, lowest-latency, lowest-power way to connect high-speed systems over short distances, mainly inside racks. That is the exact zone where Volex plays: short-reach AI rack connectivity. NVIDIA also says linear active copper can extend that copper reach from roughly 3 meters to 5 meters, which supports the idea that copper still has a role near the rack as speeds rise.

Credo, Semtech, and Marvell are saying the same thing through their own product roadmaps. Credo sells copper AECs up to 1.6T. Semtech is building 800G/1.6T active copper silicon for AI data centers. Marvell launched a 1.6T AEC DSP specifically for short-reach copper inside accelerated compute racks.

Optics wins as reach extends. That is where my broader photonics thesis sits: row-level, cluster-level, building-level, campus-level, and data-center-to-data-center connectivity. NVIDIA's CPO roadmap supports that view. Spectrum-X Photonics is about scaling AI factories to much larger fabrics with better power efficiency, signal integrity, and resiliency.

So the real question is: **how much of the AI rack stays copper as speeds move from 800G to 1.6T and beyond?**

The bull case for Volex: copper keeps a durable role inside dense AI racks because it is cheaper, lower power, lower latency, and easier to deploy across very short links.

The bear case: optics moves closer to compute faster than expected, shrinking copper's window after the FY2026 step-change.

My view: bullish on photonics over distance. Willing to own copper where copper still wins.

When scale-up CPO becomes deeply embedded around GPUs and accelerators, the thesis will need to evolve. But broad replacement of copper inside the rack still looks like a multi-year transition, especially with NVIDIA itself framing copper as more reliable for GPU links today and industry voices pointing toward later-decade mass adoption for optical GPU connectivity.

#### The acquisition track record

Twelve acquisitions since FY2019 for just under $400m aggregate. Their playbook is to find well-managed founder-led businesses with blue-chip OEM customers, acquire at 5–7x EBITDA, integrate, cross-sell. Revenue went from $443m in FY2021 to $1.087bn in FY2025.

The deals that have made the most impact are Ta Hsing (2019, vertical integration in cable extrusion), Servatron (2019, PCBA/EMS capability), and Murat Ticaret (2023, the big one, added Off-Highway as a fifth end market with customers across 26+ countries). The new medium-term plan includes another ~$300m of M&A, so execution discipline remains central to the thesis.

My 12–18 month math:

Volex currently has an enterprise value around **$1.8bn**. Against FY2026 revenue guidance of at least **$1.22bn**, the stock trades around **1.5x EV/FY2026 sales**.

The near-term rerating case is simpler than the full $2bn plan: if FY2026 data-center revenue of roughly **$236m** becomes a base, and FY2027 commentary points toward **$300m+** of data-center revenue, investors may start valuing Volex closer to an AI infrastructure manufacturing supplier than a legacy cable company. And that's a big swing.

On a rough FY2027 revenue setup of **$1.3bn–$1.4bn**, a move to **1.8x–2.2x EV/sales** would imply about **$2.3bn–$3.1bn** of enterprise value.

So the 12–18 month base-bull case is roughly **30%–70% upside in enterprise value**, driven by a modest multiple rerating and proof that the data-center step-up can hold.

The full $2bn revenue / $240m operating profit target remains the longer-term upside case. Management's bridge is roughly **$500m of organic growth**, **$300m from M&A**, and **200 bps of margin expansion**, but the next 12–18 months will be decided by FY2027 data-center guidance and cash conversion.

#### Risks

FY2027 normalization. Management said the accelerated customer-requirements dynamic in FY2026 may not repeat. If data center goes from $236m back toward $200m, the rerating stalls.

Customer concentration. No hyperscaler names disclosed. Concentration risk is real and unquantifiable from public filings.

Working capital. Hub model ties up cash. If H1's FCF pattern persists, the market keeps applying a discount.

Optics moving faster than expected. Copper's edge is inside and near the rack. If that boundary shifts sooner, growth slows.
