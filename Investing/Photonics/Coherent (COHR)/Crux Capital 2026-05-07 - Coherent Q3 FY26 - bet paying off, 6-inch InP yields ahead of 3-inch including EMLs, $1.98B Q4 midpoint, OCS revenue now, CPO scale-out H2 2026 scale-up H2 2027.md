Well, the stock held up a lot better this time than last.

### Why the call was stronger than the print

The market already knew demand was strong. The real question was whether Coherent could show execution behind that demand. This call helped answer that.

Management said the June quarter represents an inflection point in revenue growth. They also said fiscal 2027 growth should be above fiscal 2026 growth. That is a big statement because it moves the discussion beyond one strong quarter. They are talking about an acceleration window that extends into next year.

Management also said demand is exceptional. Bookings stepped up substantially in the prior quarter, and orders now extend into calendar 2028. That gives them a level of visibility optical suppliers historically rarely had.

Older optical cycles were much more boom-bust. This cycle is being driven by real physical constraints like bandwidth, power, reach, density, and the shift from electrical links to optical links across more parts of the AI network. The market will still debate margins and execution, but demand is not the core debate.

### Datacenter & Communications is becoming the company

Datacenter & Communications revenue went from $1.208B in Q2 to $1.362B in Q3. Industrial revenue went from $478M to $444M. Total revenue still grew to $1.806B.

That tells us the AI/datacenter/communications side is doing the heavy lifting.

Datacenter and Communications is the clear growth driver while Industrial shrinks in absolute terms.*

This is a major part of the thesis. Coherent is moving from a broad photonics and materials company with datacenter exposure into one of the core AI optical infrastructure suppliers. This has a re-rate in itself. You can think Coherent is expensive, but I actually think their multiple can expand further as they become more concentrated in the higher growth segment. **Lumentum's** **total revenue** for this quarter was **$808m**. **Coherent's** **datacenter and comms** business did **$1.3B**. Not apples to apples and there is a lot of nuance here, but they trade at the same mkt cap. Just food for thought.

The product stack is wide. They've got 800G and 1.6T transceivers, EMLs, CW lasers, silicon photonics, photodiodes, ZR and ZR+ DCI transceivers, transport and transmission optics, pump lasers and amplifiers, OCS, CPO/NPO, external laser sources, and thermal materials.

That breadth is why Coherent is more complicated than Lumentum. It is also why Coherent has many ways to win if the AI optical stack keeps broadening.

### The 6-inch InP bear case took a hit

A common bear case has been that Coherent's 6-inch InP ramp is weaker than advertised. Another version of that bear case is that Coherent is doing easier products on 6-inch, like CW lasers, while the real EML bottleneck remains elsewhere.

Management pushed back directly.

They said 6-inch yields are better than 3-inch across the product lines, including EMLs. The deck says the same thing. Coherent's 6-inch platform is producing EMLs, CW lasers, and photodiodes, with higher yields than 3-inch lines.

*Coherent "Building the Next-Gen AI Datacenter" slide — Meeting customer demands for scalable, high-performance and efficient infrastructure. Three pillars: SCALING CAPACITY — Ramping Datacenter and Communications capacity end-to-end, from materials to systems; on track to double internal InP output by year-end and more than double again by 2027; 6-inch platform producing EMLs, CW lasers, and photodiodes, with higher yields than 3-inch lines. ACCELERATING PERFORMANCE — Multiple CPO architectures, Silicon Photonics, InP and VCSEL enable flexible networks; 400G/lane links delivering higher bandwidth and faster data transfer; advanced multi-rail optical transport delivering 4X fiber capacity. ENHANCING EFFICIENCY — Advanced thermal and cooling solutions for dense, high-performance AI environments; improved power efficiency per bit across optical systems; materials innovation reducing cost and energy consumption.*
![[cruxcapitalgroup-coherent-is-the-bet-paying-off-002.png]]

That is a big deal.

This does leave room for technical follow-up. It does not tell us the exact power level, lane speed, band, or customer-specific qualification details. You can still ask whether these are the highest-performance 200G EML parts, which band they serve, and how they compare to Lumentum's best products.

Those are fair questions.

But the broad bear case changed tonight. The debate for me is not really whether Coherent has real 6-inch EML progress. The debate is how quickly that 6-inch EML progress becomes large enough in the shipment mix to push margins toward 42%+.

That is a much better debate for Coherent bulls.

Also, keep in mind. If the 6-inch bet wasn't worth it then why would they not just isolated it to one facility? They originally built out capacity in their flagship center. They now expanded it into a second facility and are running 6-inch. And they have plans to expand into a third facility in the beginning of 2027. Why put so much investment into this capacity if it wasn't economically worth it? It's one thing to listen to management's words (which is decisively bullish on 6-inch). It's another to full the money. And the money says that 6-inch is worth the investment.

### The 6-inch benefit is still ahead

This is where the margin debate needs more nuance.

Another common pushback is that if Coherent's 6-inch InP yields are so good, margins should already look much higher. I understand the argument. Margins are the tell over time. But the timing is important.

Coherent never said all InP output is already on 6-inch. They said they are on track to double internal InP output by year-end and more than double again by 2027. On the call, they also indicated that the doubling goal is now expected to happen one quarter earlier than previously planned.

That means the mix shift is still ramping. Wafer starts need to become shipped components, those components need to become shipped transceivers, and then that volume needs to become large enough to affect consolidated gross margin.

So yes, margins are the proof point. But they should lag the yield improvement.

The important point from the call is that the company says the 6-inch ramp is real, it is across EMLs, CW lasers, and photodiodes, the yields are ahead of 3-inch, and capacity is being added across multiple sites. That is exactly what I wanted to hear.

### 1.6T is becoming a current driver

The 1.6T commentary was really strong. Management said 800G continues to grow, while 1.6T is ramping faster. They also indicated that 1.6T should ramp rapidly through this calendar year and into next year.

That is exactly what I wanted to hear.

We have already heard about demos and sampling. What moves the stock from here is production language. Coherent is positioned across both EML-based and silicon-photonics-based 1.6T, and it is also bringing a VCSEL-based 1.6T product for shorter-reach use cases. That gives them more flexibility than a supplier tied to one architecture.

### CPO is more than lasers for Coherent

This was another important call point.

Some people think about CPO exposure as "who supplies the laser?" For Coherent, the story is broader.

Management emphasized that Coherent can bring multiple pieces into CPO: high-power CW lasers, external laser sources, isolators, fiber attach, optical content, and module-level assemblies. That means Coherent is positioning itself as a broader CPO content supplier, rather than only a laser supplier.

The deck gives more timing now. CPO/NPO new revenue is expected in H2 2026. From the call, scale-out CPO revenue is expected to ramp in the second half of calendar 2026, while scale-up CPO is expected in the second half of calendar 2027.

*Coherent "Entering a New Phase of Accelerated Growth" slide — New Growth Engines (Adding $20B+ Incremental SAM): Optical Circuit Switch (New Revenue Now), CPO/NPO (H2 2026), Multi-rail (H1 2027), Thermal Solutions (H2 2027). Existing Growth Engines ($50B+ SAM): Transceivers (1.6T, 3.2T, 6.4T), DCI Transceivers (ZR/ZR+), Transport/Transmission, Optical Components.*
![[cruxcapitalgroup-coherent-is-the-bet-paying-off-003.png]]

That is very soon for something many investors still talk about as a far-off architecture shift.

The NVIDIA deal fits here directly. I still think the right way to frame NVIDIA is customer-backed capacity. The headline is the $2B investment but the real point is that a major AI customer is helping secure future optical supply and capacity.

### CPO scale-up is the bigger prize

Coherent's view is that pluggable transceivers keep growing through the rest of the decade in scale-out, scale-across, and DCI. That means CPO does not need to kill the pluggable thesis.

The bigger CPO opportunity is scale-up.

Scale-up links are electrical today. If those links move optical, that creates new optical content. That is why CPO can be additive rather than a simple format shift.

Management discussed CPO as more than a scale-out opportunity. They see scale-up coming in the second half of calendar 2027. This is one of the bigger reasons 2027 looks interesting.

---

### OCS

OCS was something I really wanted to hear more about.

The deck now labels Optical Circuit Switch as "new revenue now." That is great.

OCS is no longer only a future idea. It is a current revenue contributor with expanding customer demand. Management also sounded confident in Coherent's differentiated liquid crystal approach, pushing the reliability angle because it avoids mechanical moving parts.

They also sounded confident versus Lumentum.

From the call, the market opportunity appears larger than the prior framework. The deck says OCS, CPO/NPO, Multi-rail, and Thermal Solutions together add more than $20B of incremental SAM. On the call, management also discussed OCS as a larger opportunity than they had previously sized, using the $4B figure as a SAM.

The question from here is revenue size. Can OCS become a $100M-per-quarter business? That is what I want to understand over the next few calls.

---

### DCI and Multi-rail

I think DCI and scale-across deserve real attention.

Coherent is seeing strong demand across DCI. They sell ZR and ZR+ coherent transceivers, transport/transmission products, optical components, pump lasers, amplifiers, and systems.

This ties directly into the broader AI infrastructure theme. AI workloads are spreading across facilities. Power constraints are pushing compute into distributed campuses and multiple data centers. That creates more need to move data between facilities.

Coherent's Multi-rail product adds another layer to this. The deck lists Multi-rail as a new revenue contributor in H1 2027.

This is another 2027 growth vector. The COHR story is broader than 800G and 1.6T inside one building. DCI, Multi-rail, and scale-across create another optical demand layer between buildings.

That aligns directly with the broader thesis I have been writing about across Ciena, Nokia, Corning, Lumentum, and the rest of the optical stack.

---

### Thermal is the underappreciated 2027 layer

The deck lists Thermal Solutions as a new growth engine beginning in H2 2027. It also highlights advanced thermal and cooling solutions for dense AI environments, improved power efficiency per bit across optical systems, and materials innovation aimed at reducing cost and energy consumption.

The next wave is about feeding GPUs with enough power, moving enough data between them, and pulling enough heat away from them so the systems can keep scaling.

Coherent is trying to attach itself to that physical bottleneck.

They have talked about advanced materials that can improve heat transfer in dense AI systems. That is where products like diamond-based thermal materials, SiC-related substrates, and thermoelectric materials could become more relevant over time. The idea is that as racks get hotter and power density rises, the materials around the chip and optical system become more important.

I would still treat this as later-dated optionality, rather than the main reason to own the stock today.

But thermal adds another way for Coherent to participate in AI infrastructure beyond transceivers and lasers.

---

### LTAs explain why Coherent sounds less stressed on supply

Coherent definitely seems supply constrained, but it sounds more comfortable about the path to adding capacity.

That likely comes from a few things.

First, Coherent has its 6-inch InP ramp. Second, they are adding multiple sites. Third, it has customer-backed LTAs.

Management talked about additional LTAs being signed and more discussions underway. The structure generally seems to include a customer demand commitment, a Coherent supply commitment, and some form of customer financial support or upfront funding.

That is important because it means Coherent is spending aggressively, but the spending is tied to visibility. The company also has more cash now after the NVIDIA investment. Cash is around $3B, versus roughly $1.5B last quarter.

Coherent is basically saying: demand is here, customers are backing the capacity, and we are going to spend to capture the growth.

That is the right answer if the demand visibility is real.

### Why the stock can still be choppy

Even though I think the report and call were bullish, the stock can still trade choppy.

Expectations were high. Gross margin is still around 40%. OpEx is stepping higher. Capex will remain elevated. The Lumentum margin comparison will keep coming up. There will also be technical debates around exactly which 6-inch EMLs Coherent is yielding, what speeds, what bands, what power levels, and how comparable they are to Lumentum's best parts.

Those are fair debatesbut they also do not change the main takeaway.

The call strengthened the thesis. Coherent confirmed demand, accelerated the forward guide, defended the 6-inch InP ramp, reinforced that EMLs are on 6-inch, gave clearer CPO timing, showed OCS as revenue now, and added more 2027 growth layers.

I think 2027 is going to be a major year. Everything will be converging.
