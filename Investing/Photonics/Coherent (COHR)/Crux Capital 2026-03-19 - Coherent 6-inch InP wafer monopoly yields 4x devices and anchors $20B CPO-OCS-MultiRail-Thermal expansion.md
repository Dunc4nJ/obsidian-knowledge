## Key Takeaways

- [[Coherent (COHR)]] is the only company running InP on **6-inch wafers in volume production** — yielding 4× more devices per wafer at <50% of the legacy 3-inch cost. Every new 6-inch line equals 4× scaling vs putting in a 3-inch line, per CEO Jim Anderson. This is the structural moat funding the entire growth-engine story.
- Hyperscalers are signing **Long-Term Agreements backed by upfront CapEx commitments** to lock in InP capacity. NVIDIA's $2B equity check is the marquee example, with management explicitly directing a "good portion" to InP capacity expansion. Customer-funded CapEx + LTA-backed offtake removes the financing risk from the ramp.
- Four new growth engines layer **$20B+ incremental TAM by 2030** on top of the $50B+ pluggables core: Co-Packaged Optics (CPO), Optical Circuit Switches (OCS), Multi-Rail transport systems (4× traffic in the same fiber footprint), and Thermal Solutions (Thermadite material + thermoelectric energy harvesting). Management says scale-up CPO alone will "dwarf the opportunity in scale-out".
- Pluggable transceivers remain durable — Crux pushes back on the persistent market narrative that CPO will cannibalize pluggables imminently. Scale-out networks between racks still need pluggables, and Coherent is unleashing a wave of new formats (100G ZR BiDi, MultiRail) outside the data center walls.
- OCS adoption is an **underappreciated catalyst** for the pluggable business — over 10 customer engagements, already shipping, and the topology shift drives demand for more (not fewer) optical interconnects. Materials science (Thermadite, energy harvesting) provides a defensible second moat.

> ## Indium Phosphide Bottleneck
>
> To truly grasp Coherent’s competitive standing, we have to understand how they are navigating the most critical bottleneck in the optical networking industry right now which the structural shortage of InP components. This advantage is built on two distinct pillars. First is securing the raw material and second is rapidly expanding the manufacturing capacity to process it.
>
> ![[cruxcapitalgroup-coherent-the-6-inch-bet-004.png]]
>
> First, Coherent is proactively locking down the raw substrate supply. During the OFC Q&A session, they revealed that they aren’t just buying these raw wafers on the spot market. CEO Jim Anderson explicitly detailed how they have insulated themselves from material shortages:
>
> *“We actually have a very diversified supply chain for indium phosphide substrates... we have over 5 different substrate suppliers. Today. And we work with those suppliers not just in the next... 12 to 24 months. We work on the next... like 3 to 5 years of capacity that we’re going to need.”*
>
> However, having the raw substrates is only half the battle as the true industry constraint is the actual fabrication capacity required to process those substrates into high-speed EMLs and high-power CW lasers. Management projects that the industry’s supply-demand imbalance for InP laser capacity could persist for years.
>
> ![[cruxcapitalgroup-coherent-the-6-inch-bet-005.png]]
>
> This is where Coherent’s true alpha lies. They are aggressively monetizing this capacity shortage. Because hyperscalers are desperate to ensure their AI infrastructure timelines aren’t derailed by a lack of components, they are signing Long-Term Agreements (LTAs) backed by upfront financial commitments. Customers are actively providing direct CapEx investments to fund the expansion of Coherent’s manufacturing lines. NVIDIA’s $2 billion equity check is the loudest example of this “skin in the game” dynamic, with Coherent explicitly stating that a good portion of that cash will go directly toward InP capacity expansion.
>
> Coherent is deploying this capital with a solid structural advantage. They are the first and only company in the world running InP on six-inch wafers in volume production. The economics here are staggering where every six-inch wafer yields over four times as many chips at less than half the total cost of legacy three-inch wafers. As Jim Anderson pointed out during the Q&A:
>
> *“The other way to think about it, because you’re getting 4 times as many devices on the 6-inch versus the 3-inch... Every new 6-inch line we put in place is like us putting 4 3-inch lines, right? So we’re scaling at 4 times the rate of if we’re doing it on 3-inch lines.”*
>
> By aggressively hyper-scaling this highly margin-accretive six-inch capacity across their fabs to double total production by the end of this year, Coherent is demonstrating that they want to capture a massive slice of the demand pie. They are using customer-funded CapEx to build their ramp and drive durable gross margin expansion.

> ## Scale-Out, Scale-Across, Pluggables, DCI
>
> There is a persistent, misguided narrative in the market that CPO will immediately cannibalize the traditional pluggable transceiver market. Coherent’s OFC presentation completely shattered that bear thesis.
>
> ![[cruxcapitalgroup-coherent-the-6-inch-bet-006.png]]
>
> For the scale-out networks that connect racks of servers together, standard pluggable transceivers will not be replaced anytime soon. Instead, they are evolving into higher-margin, higher-speed variants. Coherent is currently seeing an accelerating ramp in their next-generation 1.6T transceivers, using both Indium Phosphide EMLs and Silicon Photonics. Because these new 1.6T modules command higher Average Selling Prices (ASPs) and structurally better gross margins than the legacy 800G generation, this transition is highly accretive to Coherent’s bottom line.
>
> *"We see very strong growth in those pluggable formats... pluggable transceivers will remain the dominant form factor in, certainly in scale-across and in the scale-out networks, through at least the rest of this decade."*
>
> ![[cruxcapitalgroup-coherent-the-6-inch-bet-007.png]]
>
> Another exciting opportunity in this segment lies just outside the four walls of the data center in the scale-across network. Because modern AI training clusters require so much electricity, they are fundamentally outgrowing the power limits of single facilities. Operators are being forced to distribute single AI workloads across multiple geographically separated buildings. This creates a massive, structural bottleneck for Data Center Interconnects (DCI) which is the long-haul fiber links that tie these buildings together.
>
> ![[cruxcapitalgroup-coherent-the-6-inch-bet-008.png]]
>
> Coherent is leveraging this exact constraint to unleash a frenzy of new transport innovations that maximize the utilization of existing fiber.
>
> They recently ramped a 100G ZR BiDi (Bidirectional) transceiver that transmits and receives on the exact same fiber strand, and introduced an industry-first L-band 800G transceiver to open up entirely new wavelengths. Both innovations effectively double the capacity of a customer’s existing physical fiber plant.
>
> To address the physical space constraints inside data center telecom huts, Coherent introduced a groundbreaking transport system called Multi-Rail. By utilizing incredible microscopic density, like an uncooled pump packing four chips into one package, they have collapsed massive optical line systems into a fraction of the space
>
> ![[cruxcapitalgroup-coherent-the-6-inch-bet-009.png]]
>
> *“What MultiRail does is it allows you to process 4 times the traffic within the same space and the sublinear scaling of power. It’s truly a groundbreaking innovation.”*
>
> By providing the critical components that allow hyperscalers to link their fragmented compute clusters together without having to lay millions of dollars of new physical fiber, they are capturing immense value in a segment the broader market is largely ignoring.

> ## Scale-Up: The $15 Billion CPO Revolution
>
> ![[cruxcapitalgroup-coherent-the-6-inch-bet-010.png]]
>
> While pluggable transceivers rule the flexible scale-out network connecting racks together, the real architectural revolution is happening inside the rack in the scale-up domain. Currently, this scale-up network connects AI accelerators together to act as a single compute node using purely copper electrical links. As data rates and bandwidth density climb, physics demands that these electrical links convert to optical connections. Because this segment uses zero optics today, transitioning to CPO represents a completely untapped and purely incremental market expansion. Coherent recently upgraded their estimates and projects this CPO market will reach a staggering $15 billion by 2030.
>
> *"We believe the scale up CPO opportunity will dwarf the opportunity in scale out. It will be orders of magnitude larger. And so we view that as all incremental TAM for the optical industry in general, and certainly for us as well. Since that network is 100% electrical today, all of that optical content and scale up is incremental TAM."*
>
> ![[cruxcapitalgroup-coherent-the-6-inch-bet-011.png]]
>
> Coherent possesses a massive competitive advantage in this space through its deep vertical technology stack. They do not just supply the base continuous wave lasers to partners. They design and manufacture every single critical component of the CPO ecosystem in-house, including the Silicon Photonics integrated circuits, External Laser Sources, isolators, thermoelectric coolers, and highly specialized passive optics like polarization maintaining fiber and prism micro lens arrays. A perfect example of this microscopic vertical integration is their photodetector business, where they recently celebrated shipping over 1 billion devices. They differentiate these photodetectors by custom designing them with integrated backside micro lenses to significantly reduce packaging costs.
>
> During the OFC presentation, management showcased three distinct breakthroughs in CPO architectures that prove they are innovating across all fronts:
>
> Coherent recently co-founded a new industry standard called OpenCPX to champion socketed CPO designs. This allows hyperscalers to easily service and swap optical engines just like a standard pluggable module. On the show floor, they demonstrated a massive 6.4 Terabit per second socketed CPO engine that fits on the size of a thumb, complete with their own internally designed fiber attach unit and external laser sources.
>
> While Silicon Photonics gets the most media attention, Coherent is aggressively developing 2D VCSEL arrays for short reach CPO and Near-Packaged Optics. This VCSEL technology offers a clear path to operating at just 1 picojoule per bit, which effectively consumes four to five times less power than standard Silicon Photonics.
>
> Proving their strong positioning across all substrate materials, Coherent demonstrated the industry’s first 400G Indium Phosphide CPO engine. This breakthrough physically integrates Semiconductor Optical Amplifiers directly into the chip, which are highly valuable components for closing complex 400G link budgets.
>

> ## Optical Circuit Switches (OCS)
>
> ![[cruxcapitalgroup-coherent-the-6-inch-bet-012.png]]
>
> As data center topologies evolve, Coherent is seeing massive demand for its Optical Circuit Switches (OCS), prompting management to double their Addressable Market estimate from $2 billion to $4 billion by 2030. This explosive growth is driven by expanding use cases.
>
> *"being able to almost on a job level optimize the topology for the workflow for the maximum utilization of those expensive GPUs is very, very valuable. And I think as our customers started to realize this and think of all the different ways they can use it... that's how we're seeing the use cases expand."*
>
> While initially used in the spine network, hyperscalers are now deploying OCS across scale-up, scale-out, and scale-across environments, as well as using them for rapid fail-over to ensure maximum system uptime. With an OCS, data center operators can send a software command to instantly reconfigure fiber connections. This allows them to physically optimize the network topology on the fly to maximize the utilization of incredibly expensive GPUs for specific AI training models.
>
> ![[cruxcapitalgroup-coherent-the-6-inch-bet-013.png]]
>
> Coherent has established over ten customer engagements and is already shipping these systems into active production deployments, protected by a dual hardware and software moat. On the hardware side, their OCS relies on proprietary Liquid Crystal technology. Unlike competitors who use Microelectromechanical Systems (MEMS) mirror arrays, Coherent’s switches operate with absolutely zero mechanical moving parts and no high-voltage components. This architecture provides strong reliability, which is a critical selling point for hyperscalers. Also Coherent writes a highly praised, proprietary software stack that controls the OCS and integrates seamlessly into the customers’ own network management systems, adding tremendous stickiness to their product.
>
> The widespread adoption of OCS acts as an overlooked catalyst for Coherent’s laser business. Inserting an OCS naturally introduces a slight amount of additional link loss into the network. To compensate for this loss, the transceivers passing data through the switch require significantly higher link margins. Coherent solves this problem as the first company in the world to introduce Differential EML technology, which provides the vastly superior signal integrity and modulation efficiency required for these networks. So as OCS adoption scales, it acts as a direct catalyst driving demand for Coherent’s most advanced, high-margin transceivers.

> ## “Free Energy” and Advanced Thermal Solutions
>
> ![[cruxcapitalgroup-coherent-the-6-inch-bet-014.png]]
>
> As AI data centers scale to unprecedented sizes, operators are running into a massive physical wall. These facilities are fundamentally constrained by two intertwined factors of power delivery and heat. To solve this, Coherent is unleashing a completely new product category that targets a massive $2 billion Addressable Market by 2030. They are taking highly guarded, proprietary materials originally developed for their industrial segment and deploying them directly into the AI data center.
>
> The first major breakthrough is a proprietary material called Thermadite. Thermadite is a specialized diamond silicon carbide ceramic that offers vastly superior heat transfer characteristics compared to traditional materials. By replacing standard copper cold plates and heat sinks with Thermadite, data center operators can successfully drop the junction temperature of their incredibly expensive GPUs by 5 to 10 degrees. This temperature drop is a massive competitive advantage because it allows hyperscalers to safely run their processors harder and faster, effectively allowing them to generate more output and print more AI tokens per chip.
>
> *“Using the thermal materials, we can reduce the junction temperature of the XPU by between 5 and 10 degrees... what actually I think our customers are going to do is... they're going to get more output. So the output of those is their revenue stream.”*
>
> Even more interesting is Coherent’s development of thermoelectric energy harvesting. Today, the immense heat generated by AI training is simply blown away by massive air conditioning units. Coherent is reversing its thermoelectric cooling technology to capture that exact waste heat and convert it directly back into electricity. This recycled electricity can then be funneled right back into the data center’s power supply. Management described this capability as creating “free energy” for their customers.
>
> *"If I can take heat out and recycle it back into power, and that's just like free energy, right? And so that's very compelling to every data center owner, even if it were just, you know, small percentages of recovery of heat.”*
>
> Materials science is notoriously defensible, and competitors cannot simply reverse engineer Coherent’s proprietary ceramic recipes. Also Coherent pairs this unique materials science with decades of deep data center expertise, giving them a unique advantage over standard materials companies trying to enter the space. Management noted that customer engagement is incredibly strong and they expect this segment to start generating significant revenue in the second half of next year.

> ## Closing Thoughts
>
> ![[cruxcapitalgroup-coherent-the-6-inch-bet-015.png]]
>
> While the massive $20 billion TAM expansion from these new growth engines is a main focus this week, a major underlying story is the structural de-risking of Coherent’s profitability. In addition to growing its top-line revenue, Coherent is aggressively expanding its margins. Management noted that they have already improved their gross margin by nearly 500 basis points recently, driven by a new corporate culture where general managers are held strictly accountable for daily margin improvements.
>
> This margin expansion is heavily supported by a ruthless approach to financial discipline and portfolio optimization. Over the past six quarters, Coherent has decisively exited or sold 33 underperforming or non-strategic sites, including the recent sale of a materials processing division in Munich that operated well below the corporate gross margin average. The company has used the proceeds from these divestitures to aggressively pay down its most expensive debt, successfully dropping its leverage ratio from over 3x down to a very comfortable 1.7x.
>
> Another hidden driver of their profitability is a deliberate strategic decision regarding Digital Signal Processors (DSPs). Management revealed that Coherent intentionally outsources all of its DSPs to third-party merchant suppliers for both datacom and telecom products. By completely avoiding silicon DSP development, Coherent is able to concentrate 100% of its R&D dollars purely on optics and photonics, the exact area where their true competitive differentiation lies.
>
> Finally, while the AI data center narrative captures all the attention, Coherent has a massive, stealthy tailwind building in the background. Management is seeing a tangible pickup in their highly profitable Industrial business, specifically led by strong orders from Semicap customers. As semicap vendors like ASML and Applied Materials see a recovery, they are ordering more of Coherent’s industrial lasers. This market recovery provides a fantastic underlying boost that will drive sequential revenue growth in the second half of the year.
