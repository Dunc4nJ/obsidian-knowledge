### The Numbers to Beat

Coherent guided fiscal Q3 revenue to $1.70 billion to $1.84 billion.

Non-GAAP gross margin was guided to 38.5% to 40.5%.

Non-GAAP operating expenses were guided to $320 million to $340 million.

Non-GAAP EPS was guided to $1.28 to $1.48.

The midpoint is roughly $1.77 billion of revenue and $1.38 of EPS.

A clean beat means revenue above roughly **$1.82 billion**, gross margin above **40.5%**, **EPS above $1.45**, and a June-quarter guide that shows another clear sequential step.

A weaker print is revenue near the low end, gross margin below 39%, EPS below the midpoint, and cautious June-quarter language.

The forward guide is the bigger swing factor of course. A high-end Q3 with a soft June guide could feel mixed. A mid-range Q3 with strong June guidance and better 1.6T, OCS, and 6-inch InP detail could still work.

---

### The Demand Signal

Last quarter, Coherent said data center revenue grew 14% sequentially and 36% year over year. The data center book-to-bill ratio was above 4x. Management also said most of calendar 2026 was already booked, calendar 2027 was filling quickly, and large customers were providing detailed forecasts into calendar 2028.

800G is still ramping. 1.6T is starting to ramp. DCI and scale-across are rising because AI workloads are spreading across facilities. CPO is moving from architecture debate to capacity planning.

Tonight's question is whether that demand is turning into shipments, gross margin, and free cash flow.

---

### 6-Inch InP Is the Center of the Thesis

The most important company-specific lever is 6-inch indium phosphide.

InP is used for critical optical devices including EMLs, CW lasers, photodiodes, and photonic integrated circuits. The industry is constrained, and Coherent's answer is larger wafers. Management has said a 6-inch wafer produces more than four times as many chips as a 3-inch wafer at less than half the cost. Coherent is ramping 6-inch InP in Sherman, Texas and Jarfalla, Sweden, with added capacity in Switzerland. Management has also said 6-inch yields already exceed 3-inch yields, which means a supply advantage and a cost advantage in one of the tightest areas of the AI optical supply chain.

The key detail is conversion. Management previously said wafer start to shipped transceiver takes roughly six months. We should listen for whether the September and December wafer starts are flowing into March and June shipments.

Shipped transceivers create the revenue. Lower-cost shipped transceivers create the margin expansion. Gross margin above 40.5% would suggest the 6-inch ramp is beginning to hit the model. A strong demand print with weak gross margin would feel incomplete.

---

### 1.6T

800G is still the larger base and 1.6T is the faster-growing driver.

Management has said both grew last quarter, and that 1.6T demand accelerated across multiple customers. The first phase of the 1.6T ramp is driven by EML-based transceivers and silicon photonics. Coherent also plans a 1.6T VCSEL-based transceiver in the second half of the year. VCSELs run on gallium arsenide rather than indium phosphide, which could help offload short-reach demand while preserving scarce InP capacity for higher-value applications.

The market has already heard about demos and sampling. Tonight I want production language like 1.6T generating revenue across multiple customers, orders extending into 2027, and the product gross-margin accretive. Heavy use of qualification, sampling, or evaluation language would be the weaker setup.

---

### CPO

The biggest CPO opportunity is scale-up. Scale-up links are largely electrical today. When those links move to optical, Coherent gets new content rather than a format shift.

This is why the NVIDIA agreement has real strategic value. If NVIDIA is helping pull optical into scale-up, Coherent is exposed to a new layer of AI cluster content.

I would love to hear on tonight's that CPO scale-up revenue begins earlier than investors expect.

We will definitely hear about scale-out CPO, which is great and a step in the right direction. But will be listening for any scale-up insight.

---

### OCS

OCS is a massive upside layer. We heard how bullish Lumentum's CEO was yesterday.

Coherent's approach uses liquid crystal switching technology, with fewer mechanical failure points and a software stack for control. Management has said Coherent has more than 10 OCS customer engagements and multiple real data center deployments. Demand appears strong, and the limiting factor is manufacturing capacity.

The key question is whether OCS can become a $100 million-per-quarter business and when. If management starts giving revenue milestones, OCS becomes an estimate driver. If it stays in engagement and backlog language, we will continue to treat it as optionality.

---

### DCI and Scale-Across

DCI and scale-across deserve more attention. Again, Lumentum highlighted how strong this market was yesterday.

Coherent's communications business grew 44% year over year last quarter, with DCI the fastest-growing area inside communications.

They sell ZR and ZR+ coherent transceivers, lasers, components, pump lasers, amplifiers, line cards, and systems. That gives Coherent multiple ways to benefit as AI workloads spread across facilities. If management confirms DCI growth remains faster than overall communications growth, the scale-across thesis gets another data point.

---

### Margin

Coherent's long-term gross margin target is above 42%. Last quarter, non-GAAP gross margin was 39%. The Q3 guide midpoint is 39.5%.

Management has said gross margin improved by roughly 470 basis points since FY24 through cost reduction and pricing optimization. Cost reduction includes lower input costs, better yields, higher throughput, shorter cycle times, and 6-inch InP. Pricing optimization means charging closer to the value of scarce products.

Lumentum puts pressure on this story. Lumentum is printing much higher margins in the same AI optical cycle. Coherent has a broader mix, so the comparison is imperfect, but investors will still make it. The bridge for Coherent has to be operating leverage. As 6-inch InP, 1.6T, OCS, and CPO scale, margins need to move toward 42%.

---

### Capex

Capacity requires capex, and that is acceptable as long as customers are backing the spending through LTAs, purchase commitments, or direct financial support.

Coherent has said capacity expansion is the top capital-allocation priority. The NVIDIA deal adds fuel to that, especially in Sherman. The balance sheet is cleaner than a year ago. Management said leverage was 1.7x last quarter, below the company's under-2x target and down materially from prior levels.

Now we need the next layer. Like how much capex is required for NVIDIA, 1.6T, OCS, DCI, and 6-inch InP, how much is backed by firm commitments, and when free cash flow improves. The market will reward growth capex tied to customer-backed demand and be much less forgiving toward open-ended capacity spending.

---

### Bull, Base, Bear

The bull case is a high-end Q3, gross margin above 40.5%, a strong June guide, clear 1.6T production language, evidence that 6-inch InP is helping margins, more NVIDIA model detail, OCS revenue growth, and continued DCI strength. That would push investors to raise FY27 estimates.

The base case is a solid Q3 around the midpoint, gross margin near 39.5%, demand still strong, but limited upside detail. The thesis holds while the market waits for more proof.

The bear case is a low-end print, gross margin below 39%, cautious June guidance, vague 1.6T language, delayed 6-inch cost benefit, slow OCS revenue, and higher capex with limited free-cash-flow clarity. That raises concerns that the demand opportunity is moving faster than the model.
