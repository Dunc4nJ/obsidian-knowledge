# LITE 2026-03-02 — NVIDIA $2B strategic investment plus multi-billion purchase commitment and future capacity access rights for advanced laser components

On 2026-03-02, [[Nvidia (NVDA)]] announced multi-year strategic agreements with [[Lumentum (LITE)]] (8-K Exhibit 99.1, filed under LITE's CIK 0001633978): a **$2 billion equity investment** in Lumentum, plus a **non-exclusive multi-billion purchase commitment** and **future capacity access rights for advanced laser components**. The same day, NVIDIA disclosed a parallel ~$2B arrangement with [[Coherent (COHR)]] (8-K Exhibit 99.1, separate filing — referenced inline below). The two deals together read as NVIDIA hard-committing to the pluggable optical supply chain and moving to control its chokepoint: the laser.

## Key Takeaways

- **$2B investment + multi-billion purchase commitment + future capacity access rights — NVIDIA is locking the EML/laser bottleneck.** The agreement language is precise: *"The non-exclusive agreement includes an NVIDIA multi-billion purchase commitment and future capacity access rights for advanced laser components. In addition, NVIDIA is investing $2 billion in Lumentum to support R&D, future capacity and operations as the company builds out its U.S.-based manufacturing capabilities in a new fab."* Capital plus a binding offtake plus pre-emptive capacity rights is exactly what a buyer does when the constrained input — InP/EML lasers — is the thing it most needs to secure. This is consistent with LITE's standing thesis that InP/EML laser supply is the binding constraint (see [[Crux Capital 2025-12-30 - Lumentum supply-constrained AI photonics thesis, InP 25-30 pct demand-supply gap, 40 pct unit capacity lift, OCS $100M target Dec 2026]] and [[Crux Capital LITE demand-signal compilation Q2-Q3 FY26 - EML 25-30pct supply gap escalates to 30pct, $400M OCS backlog ramps to $1B by CY27, fiscal Q4-Q2 $480-665M with 15-25pct margin, Hurlston sold out through 2027, Greensboro InP mid-2028 unlock]]).

- **Parallel same-day ~$2B Coherent deal confirms it is a supply-chain strategy, not a single-vendor bet.** NVIDIA struck an analogous ~$2B arrangement with [[Coherent (COHR)]] the same day (8-K ex-99.1: <https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000820318/000119312526084366/d42735dex991.htm>). Backing both of the two largest Western InP/EML laser houses simultaneously signals NVIDIA is securing the entire laser-supply layer of pluggable optics, not picking a winner — and is willing to fund the capex (new U.S. fabs) to do it.

- **Implication: lasers, not DSPs, remain the binding constraint on optical-module output — and the dominant buyer is extending the pluggable window while controlling the chokepoint.** Jensen Huang frames the partnership as *"advancing the world's most sophisticated silicon photonics to build the next generation of gigawatt-scale AI factories,"* but the mechanism NVIDIA is funding is laser capacity. If module output is laser-gated rather than DSP-gated, then (a) DSP/retimer content keeps shipping for as long as pluggables ship in volume — supportive of the near-term DSP window that the FRO-first 1.6T ramp in [[SMTC Q1 FY27 earnings 2026-05-26 - hyperscalers gain conviction on 1.6T LRO-LPO but initial ramp is FRO, linear hits ~25 pct transceiver mix in a year or 2]] also implies — and (b) the laser layer captures durable scarcity value, with NVIDIA pre-buying it.

- **The $2B funds a new U.S. fab — capacity, not just capital.** Both Huang and LITE CEO Michael Hurlston tie the investment to capacity expansion: Hurlston: *"In support of this collaboration, we are also investing in a new fabrication facility to increase capacity and accelerate innovation."* The investment "enables Lumentum to scale its manufacturing capacity and R&D to meet the needs of future AI data centers." This is consistent with LITE's Greensboro InP buildout narrative (mid-2028 unlock per the Crux compilation) — NVIDIA is underwriting the multi-year capacity ramp on the binding input.

> Exhibit 99.1

> **NVIDIA Announces Strategic Partnership with Lumentum to Develop State-of-the-Art Optics Technology**
>
> *NVIDIA to invest $2B in Lumentum to grow capacity, advance U.S.-based manufacturing and deepen R&D collaboration in data center optics*
>
> **SANTA CLARA and SAN JOSE, Calif.** — March 2, 2026 — NVIDIA today announced multi-year strategic agreements with Lumentum Holdings Inc. (NASDAQ: LITE) to accelerate innovation in advanced optics technologies, including research and development to enable next-generation AI infrastructure and systems designs.
>
> The non-exclusive agreement includes an NVIDIA multi-billion purchase commitment and future capacity access rights for advanced laser components. In addition, NVIDIA is investing $2 billion in Lumentum to support R&D, future capacity and operations as the company builds out its U.S.-based manufacturing capabilities in a new fab.
>
> Optical interconnect technology and package integration are critical for the continued scaling of AI factories, improving the energy efficiency and resiliency of large-scale AI networks. This expanded collaboration will draw on the strengths of NVIDIA's leadership in AI, accelerated computing and networking and Lumentum's leadership in optics and advanced manufacturing. The investment enables Lumentum to scale its manufacturing capacity and R&D to meet the needs of future AI data centers.
>
> "AI has reinvented computing and is driving the largest computing infrastructure buildout in history," said Jensen Huang, founder and CEO of NVIDIA. "Together with Lumentum, NVIDIA is advancing the world's most sophisticated silicon photonics to build the next generation of gigawatt-scale AI factories."
>
> "This multi-year strategic agreement reflects our shared commitment to advancing the optics technologies that will power the next generation of AI infrastructure," said Michael Hurlston, CEO of Lumentum. "In support of this collaboration, we are also investing in a new fabrication facility to increase capacity and accelerate innovation. We're excited to work together to expand what's possible for the AI optical architectures of tomorrow."
