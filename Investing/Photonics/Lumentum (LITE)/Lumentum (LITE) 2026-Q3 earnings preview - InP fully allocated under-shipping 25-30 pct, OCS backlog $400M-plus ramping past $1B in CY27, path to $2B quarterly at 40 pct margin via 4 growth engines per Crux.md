## Original Content

Lumentum ($LITE) reports after the close today, and this is one of the highest-signal optics calls of the quarter.

Lumentum is now tied to almost every major theme I care about in photonics: cloud transceivers, 1.6T, EML lasers, indium phosphide capacity, optical circuit switches (OCS), co-packaged optics (CPO), external light sources (ELS), and optical scale-up.

This call is about whether Lumentum can earn it's multiple, expand it's capacity enough to meet demand, and capture the massive TAM that is infront of it.

**I will be listening to this call live and providing notes real time for paid subscribers.**

At the end of this article I also lay out **my strategy** going in to the call and what **levels** I am watching for a **potential add**.

And at the end of the the day today I will post a full write-up on the call.

---

### Where the Story Stands

Last quarter was already a major inflection point.

Lumentum reported $665.5M of revenue, up more than 65% year over year, and guided the next quarter to $780M–$830M. The midpoint of that guide is $805M, which would be another company revenue record. Non-GAAP operating margin was 25.2% in Q2, and management guided Q3 operating margin to 30%–31%.

We already know demand is going to be strong, so the question now is conversion: shipments, backlog, pricing power, margins, and guidance.

The challenge is that we already know the March quarter should be strong as well. The stock reaction will likely depend more on Q4 guidance, backlog conversion, and whether management reaffirms the $2B quarterly revenue model.

---

### The Supply Constraint Is the Thesis

The most important part of the Lumentum story is that they are selling into markets where demand is moving faster than supply. Fabrinet reaffirmed yesterday that there is still a major supply constraint, especially in lasers.

Management said indium phosphide capacity remains fully allocated, and even after capacity additions, the company is still under-shipping customer demand by roughly 25%–30%. EML capacity is effectively spoken for through calendar 2027 under long-term agreements.

When a company has scarce capacity, long-term agreements, pricing leverage, and customers asking for more than contracted volumes, the model can change very quickly. That is exactly what happened last quarter. Gross margin moved to 42.5%, up 1,020 basis points year over year. Operating margin expanded by 1,730 basis points year over year.

That is why Lumentum has started to trade more like a strategic capacity bottleneck.

---

### The Four Growth Engines

Management has been clear that the future of the company is built around four drivers, each with a different timing profile.

---

#### 1. Cloud Transceivers

This is the current revenue bridge.

Lumentum acquired Cloud Light to get more direct exposure to cloud transceivers, and the early execution was messy. Management has been candid about that. But the tone has changed. Last quarter, they said the transceiver business had reached a "turning point." They are now in the lead pack as customers move toward 1.6T, and 1.6T demand came in stronger than they expected 90 days earlier.

The important detail is that 1.6T carries a better margin profile than 800G. Transceivers can grow while becoming less of a drag on overall company margins.

The key question is whether 1.6T shipments remain on track for this summer, and whether customer demand has strengthened again.

---

#### 2. Optical Circuit Switches

OCS is a major near-term upside lever.

Lumentum originally expected its first $10M OCS quarter in fiscal Q3. They hit that level one quarter early. OCS backlog is already well above $400M, with most of that expected to ship in the second half of calendar 2026. At the Nokia/OFC event, management said they had signed a new multi-year, multi-billion-dollar agreement with a large OCS customer and expect OCS to ramp beyond $1B in calendar 2027.

OCS felt far away a couple quarters ago. Lumentum is now talking about backlog, customer expansion, and a billion-dollar revenue run rate. The key question is whether the $400M-plus backlog still converts in 2H CY26, whether OCS ramping beyond $1B in calendar 2027 stays intact, and whether additional customers are engaging. Any increase in backlog or customer count would be very bullish.

---

#### 3. Scale-Out CPO

Scale-out CPO is the first CPO revenue layer, mainly tied to ultra-high-power lasers and external light source modules.

Lumentum said it secured an incremental multi-hundred-million-dollar purchase order for UHP lasers supporting optical scale-out, with shipments expected in the first half of calendar 2027. At OFC, management said they expect the first $100M scale-out CPO / UHP laser revenue quarter around late calendar 2026, followed by delivery on the multi-hundred-million-dollar commitment in early 2027.

CPO has been discussed for years. Lumentum is now talking about specific orders, shipment windows, and capacity planning. That is a meaningful shift. We are hearing this across the stack too. CPO is already beginning in early stages.

The other piece I am watching is ELS, or external light source. Rather than selling only a laser chip, Lumentum can sell a more complete external light source module. Management said this creates roughly 2x to 2.5x the revenue content of standalone lasers while preserving strong margins. ELS could let Lumentum move up the value chain while helping customers that lack deep internal optical engineering teams.

---

#### 4. Scale-Up CPO

Scale-up is the real 2027-plus option, and this is where the optical story gets much larger.

Today, copper still dominates short scale-up links inside racks and clusters. But copper becomes harder as bandwidth rises and cable distances increase. Lumentum expects first scale-up CPO shipments in the second half of calendar 2027. Management has said the first scale-up opportunity could be 3x–4x larger than initial scale-out CPO, and later in-rack optical adoption could create an even larger lane opportunity.

If AI architectures move from rack-scale to multi-rack scale-up domains, the optical content per system can move much higher. For Lumentum, that means demand for UHP lasers, external light sources, InP capacity, OCS, and high-speed EMLs. They are adding capacity now for demand that could show up in 2027, 2028, and beyond.

---

### The Capacity Plan

The company is working on several layers at once.

First, Lumentum is squeezing more output from its existing indium phosphide footprint. EML output has increased 8x since FY23, and management expects indium phosphide output to increase another 50% from Q4 CY25 to Q4 CY26. On the Q2 call, management also said the earlier 40% capacity expansion plan was front-loaded, with more than half of that already delivered in the December quarter, and additional capacity expected through Sagamihara, Caswell in the UK, and Takao in Japan.

Second, Lumentum is shifting more capacity toward ultra-high-power lasers for CPO. San Jose is the first UHP ramp. Caswell is becoming the second UHP capacity block. Greensboro is the third, larger step. Management originally viewed Caswell as EML overflow, but the UHP opportunity became large enough that they are now leaning more of that site toward scale-out and scale-up CPO demand.

Third, Lumentum closed on the former Qorvo fab in Greensboro, North Carolina. Management described this as its fifth indium phosphide fab, primarily focused on UHP lasers, with 4-inch and 6-inch compatibility. Shipments are expected in 2028. This fab is important because it supports the next leg of scale-out and scale-up CPO demand, but it sits outside the current $2B quarterly revenue model.

Fourth, backend manufacturing is also part of the capacity story. Lumentum has been expanding through Thailand and leaning more heavily on contract manufacturers. Management said factory capacity is also tight, which means the bottleneck is wider than front-end InP wafers alone. Even with strong wafer output, assembly and manufacturing execution still need to keep pace.

Fifth, the NVIDIA investment gives Lumentum more flexibility to fund strategic CapEx, working capital, and potential vertical integration. Management also said additional capacity through existing fabs or new capacity by acquisition remains an active discussion.

The path to $1.25B quarterly revenue and then $2B quarterly revenue is based on existing and near-term capacity. Greensboro is the next layer of optionality for 2028 and beyond. That means the current model already embeds a major capacity ramp, while the newer U.S. fab supports the next phase of UHP laser and scale-up CPO demand.

---

### The $2B Quarterly Revenue Model

At OFC, Lumentum laid out a path to $1.25B quarterly revenue in 9–12 months, $2B quarterly revenue 9–12 months after that, and 40% non-GAAP operating margin at the $2B level.

The current quarter midpoint is $805M. If management is right, Lumentum could move from an $800M quarterly revenue base to a $2B quarterly run rate within roughly 18–24 months. That is a very aggressive target. The key question is whether management still has the same confidence in that path.

### Earnings Watchlist

#### 1. Q3 Revenue vs. Guide

Guide: $780M–$830M. A beat is good, but Q4 guidance will carry more weight. The market already knows the March quarter was supposed to be strong.

#### 2. Q4 Guidance

The company needs to show a path from the $805M Q3 midpoint toward the $1.25B quarterly revenue target.

#### 3. OCS Backlog Conversion

I want reaffirmation of the $400M-plus backlog, 2H CY26 shipment timing, OCS ramping beyond $1B in calendar 2027, three current customers, and any expansion of that customer count. Any increase here would be very bullish.

#### 4. CPO Order Visibility

More color on UHP laser demand, scale-out CPO shipments, the multi-hundred-million-dollar order, ELS adoption, the first $100M scale-out CPO / UHP laser revenue quarter timing, and scale-up CPO in 2H CY27. The more specific, the stronger the setup.

#### 5. InP Capacity

The best outcome: capacity is rising, demand is still higher. That would support the pricing and margin story.

#### 6. 1.6T Execution

Are 1.6T shipments still starting this summer? Does Lumentum remain in the lead pack? If 1.6T demand is accelerating again, that supports near-term revenue and helps the transceiver margin story.

#### 7. LTAs and Pricing

Last quarter, management said customers asking for incremental volume were opening the door for incremental pricing discussions. I want to hear if that dynamic continues.

#### 8. Backend Capacity

This is one risk I am watching closely. Fab capacity is one constraint, but factory capacity is also tight, and the company is leaning on contract manufacturers to accelerate ramps. Lumentum can have demand and still disappoint if backend execution lags.

---

### What Would Be Bullish

Revenue beats, Q4 guide is strong, OCS backlog conversion stays on track, CPO order language strengthens, 1.6T starts on schedule, InP capacity expands with demand still above supply, and the $2B / 40% model gets reaffirmed. That keeps Lumentum at the center of the AI optics trade.

### What Would Make Me Cautious

Q4 guide below the pace needed for $1.25B, OCS timing slips, CPO commentary becomes vague, 1.6T production issues, ELS timing pushes out, factory capacity creates shipment friction, or pricing language softens. The bar is high. This stock has already been rewarded for the AI optics story. Today needs to show that the story is accelerating.
