## Original Content

*Verbatim from <https://cruxcapitalgroup.substack.com/p/lumentum-demand-signals>*

Lumentum's CEO has been building up a demand story for months. The latest interview with Bloomberg sharpened the picture. Demand is still outrunning supply, the capacity bottleneck looks more durable than many expected, and the next leg of the optics opportunity is coming from optics moving deeper inside the machine rather than staying confined to traditional pluggables, faster than the market anticipated.

### The clearest message yet from Lumentum

Michael Hurlston's latest comments pull several strands of the Lumentum story into one place. Hyperscaler demand continues to run ahead of what Lumentum can physically manufacture, even as the company adds capacity across multiple geographies.

In the most recent interview with Bloomberg, Hurlston said products were sold out through 2027. This was a reaffirmation from their latest earnings call whee he said the same. But he went even further and said Lumentum could be sold out through all of 2028 within two quarters. That is an aggressive statement, and it suggests the constraint story is still getting tighter rather than easing.

What stands out most is that he was describing a current operating reality. The company is adding output in Japan, it has manufacturing in Thailand, it has fabs in the United States and Europe, and the message still comes through the same way. Hyperscaler demand is outrunning all of it. That is the language of a real bottleneck.

### This story has been building for quarters

The progression in Hurlston's commentary over the last few quarters is very clean and consistent.

Let's take a trip down memory lane.

In August 2025, Lumentum reported fiscal Q4 revenue of $480.7 million, up sharply year over year, and Hurlston said they were seeing robust demand across its AI data-center cloud portfolio.

*Aug 2025 fiscal Q4 commentary slide*
![[cruxcapitalgroup-lumentum-demand-signals-001.png]]

He specifically called out strength in EML chips, pump lasers, narrow-linewidth laser assemblies for data-center interconnect, and 800G modules. At that point, management was already confident enough to say it could surpass $600 million in quarterly revenue by June 2026 or earlier.

---

By November 2025, the story had broadened. Revenue rose again to $533.8 million, and Hurlston said Lumentum had strong momentum across data center, data-center interconnect, and long-haul.

*Nov 2025 fiscal Q1 commentary slide*
![[cruxcapitalgroup-lumentum-demand-signals-002.png]]

More importantly, he framed the business as having multiple growth engines rather than a single 800G ramp. He said optical circuit switches (OCS) and co-packaged optics (CPO) had yet to make meaningful contributions, while cloud transceivers were moving onto what he called a "sustainable upward path." That was a subtle but important shift. LITE was no longer describing only today's optics demand. It was also laying out the next layers forming behind it.

---

Then in February 2026, the tone sharpened further. Lumentum reported fiscal second-quarter revenue of $665.5 million and said its forward guidance implied more than 85% year-over-year growth.

*Feb 2026 fiscal Q2 commentary slide*
![[cruxcapitalgroup-lumentum-demand-signals-003.png]]

In that same release, Hurlston said they were at the "starting line" for two substantial opportunities: OCS and CPO. He disclosed that OCS backlog was already well beyond $400 million and that Lumentum had received an incremental multi-hundred-million-dollar CPO order deliverable in the first half of calendar 2027. That is a very different setup from a company riding a single upgrade cycle.

---

OFC added one more layer of visibility here. Lumentum laid out the whole machine more clearly: four growth engines, 1.6T transceiver volume shipments starting this summer, 50%+ EML capacity growth by the end of calendar 2026, OCS ramping toward a greater-than-$1 billion 2027 run rate, and first scale-up CPO shipments expected by late 2027.

*OFC 2026 four-growth-engines slide*
![[cruxcapitalgroup-lumentum-demand-signals-004.png]]

That was the point where the story started looking like a stacked multi-layer optical buildout, all leaning on the same constrained InP manufacturing base.

So the April interview did not invent the thesis. It extended it. The key new piece is duration. Hurlston is now signaling that the shortage can run through 2028, while the next major capacity expansion still takes time to arrive.

### The manufacturing bottleneck is story

This is the part that gets and deserves the most attention.

High-end optical manufacturing has become a bottleneck in its own right. If Lumentum is telling investors that it is falling further behind demand while simultaneously expanding capacity, then the gating factor extends beyond end demand. Think about that for a second. As they are aggressively expanding, they are falling FURTHER behind because demand is rising FASTER than they can grow.

The industry also has to physically build enough optical components, fast enough, at the right performance level.

That is why the brownfield discussion is very much worth talking about. In the interview, Hurlston said brownfield expansion cuts time roughly in half versus building a fab from scratch. Even then, the timeline still stretches out. Lumentum's March 26 announcement on the Greensboro, North Carolina site makes that explicit.

*Greensboro, NC InP facility announcement*
![[cruxcapitalgroup-lumentum-demand-signals-005.png]]

They said the facility, acquired from Qorvo, will significantly expand capacity using 6-inch indium phosphide, or InP, wafers, but it is only expected to ramp production in mid-2028. Even the faster path still leaves the market waiting.

That should shape how we think about this whole group. The key question extends beyond whether AI demand stays healthy. The key question is whether enough optical capacity can come online before the next wave of demand hits. Right now, Hurlston is signaling that capacity still trails that demand curve.

### Why the NVIDIA relationship is key

The NVIDIA piece turns this into a strategic-capacity story.

*March 2 2026 NVIDIA strategic agreement headline*
![[cruxcapitalgroup-lumentum-demand-signals-006.png]]

On March 2, NVIDIA and Lumentum announced multiyear strategic agreements, a multibillion purchase commitment, future capacity access rights for advanced laser components, and a $2 billion NVIDIA investment in Lumentum. That is far beyond standard vendor behavior. A large customer is effectively helping secure supply in a constrained market.

That partnership also helps explain the urgency behind Lumentum's manufacturing moves. In the Greensboro announcement, Lumentum said NVIDIA will be a customer of that facility. So this is more than abstract future optionality. Lumentum is building physical capacity with named strategic demand already attached to it.

So I think a good way to think about it is that NVIDIA appears to view advanced optical capacity as important enough to lock up early. That says a great deal about where the bottlenecks are moving.

### Copper still matters, of course.

One of the most useful parts of Hurlston's latest comments is that he framed the future as a hybrid environment. Copper still plays a major role. At the same time, optics is gaining the opportunity to move closer to the switching and compute fabric over time. That is a much more believable framing than an instant optical takeover that some people suggest, or believe is necessary for these companies to be valuable. It also lines up with what Lumentum has already disclosed around CPO orders for first-half 2027 delivery.

*Optics-deepening-in-system commentary*
![[cruxcapitalgroup-lumentum-demand-signals-007.png]]

This changes how large the opportunity can become. Traditional pluggables already benefit from AI scale-out. OCS adds another layer. CPO potentially moves optics deeper into the system architecture. If Hurlston is right that this transition starts becoming visible in the middle to second half of 2027, then the next leg of optical demand may come from inside the cluster rather than only between racks and buildings. The exact timing still needs close tracking, but the direction of travel is becoming much clearer.

### The numbers are already telling the story

The financial progression backs up the narrative.

Lumentum's revenue climbed from $480.7 million in fiscal Q4 2025 to $533.8 million in fiscal Q1 2026 and then to $665.5 million in fiscal Q2 2026. Over that same stretch, non-GAAP operating margin improved from 15.0% to 18.7% to 25.2%. So this is a case where the income statement is already reflecting the scarcity story. Revenue is accelerating, margins are expanding, and management is still saying supply cannot keep up. That is usually what a real capacity bottleneck looks like.

*Financial progression (revenue + margin) chart*
![[cruxcapitalgroup-lumentum-demand-signals-008.png]]

That is also why the "starting line" comment from February carries so much weight. If Lumentum is already putting up this kind of revenue and margin performance before OCS and CPO are fully contributing, then the market still may be underestimating how many different optical layers are beginning to ramp at once.

### What is actually changing here

The market still tends to think about AI optics as solely a strong demand story. Hurlston is increasingly framing it as a constrained supply story as well.

*Constrained-supply commentary*
![[cruxcapitalgroup-lumentum-demand-signals-009.png]]

Strong demand stories can wobble if orders pause. Constrained supply stories can stay tight for longer, especially when lead times to add capacity are measured in years rather than quarters. The Greensboro fab is a perfect example. It is strategically important, it strengthens U.S. manufacturing, and it will expand InP capacity. It also arrives on a mid-2028 timeline. So it solves a future shortage more than today's one.

So when Hurlston says demand is pulling further ahead even as Lumentum expands, we really need to think about this. This is about securing enough high-end optical manufacturing capacity before the next architectural shift arrives.

### Final takeaway

*Final takeaway visual*
![[cruxcapitalgroup-lumentum-demand-signals-010.png]]

Demand is still running hot. Supply is still tight. NVIDIA is helping lock in future capacity. OCS and CPO are moving from concept toward revenue. And the next meaningful manufacturing relief valve does not show up until mid-2028. That is a strong setup for Lumentum specifically, and it is also a strong signal that the broader high-end optics complex remains in a scarcity phase rather than a digestion phase.
