# Crux Capital 2025-12-30 — Lumentum supply-constrained AI photonics thesis

[[Lumentum (LITE)]] has transitioned from a cyclical optics name (telecom + iPhone sensing seasonality) into a **capacity-constrained AI infrastructure supplier**. Crux argues the re-rating is structural, not transient, on three stackable growth vectors: higher-speed lasers, higher-speed transceivers, and OCS plus CPO adjacency.

## Key Takeaways

- **Lumentum sells components + systems.** Components (laser chips, subassemblies, optical plumbing) drive the story and profitability. Systems (transceivers, OCS) are the acceleration lever if execution stays tight.
- **Physics is the why.** At 800G → 1.6T, copper becomes a power and signal-integrity problem across AI data-center distances. Photons carry the load better. Management ties demand directly to cloud + AI infrastructure, broad-based across data center optics AND campus/regional links.
- **The real edge is supply.** InP wafer fab fully allocated. Roughly **40% more unit capacity** over the next few quarters from yield + throughput work. Demand running **25-30% above supply** — translates to allocation decisions, customer selection, long-term commitments, targeted pricing power.
- **Yield acts as a moat.** In the 200G-per-lane era, bottleneck = producing high-performance InP devices at scale yields. Lumentum gatekeeps the highest-value transition in the AI optics roadmap.
- **Product read-through:** record EML shipment levels, 200G contribution increasing through calendar 2026, continuous-wave (CW) lasers shipping with an internal transceiver path. Lumentum hedged across both EML and CW-powered routes.
- **Cloud Light (transceivers):** historical fits-and-starts framed as fading as Thailand capacity comes online. Path to meaningful quarterly transceiver run-rate while selective on margin-rich opportunities. **1.6T transceivers anchored as mid-2026 shipping milestone**, with management explicitly suggesting 1.6T margin structure improves vs 800G — the next growth leg can carry profit expansion rather than dilute it.
- **OCS = architecture winner.** Eliminates repeated O-E-O conversion by steering light directly fiber-to-fiber. Power + latency unlock that hyperscalers care about now. Target: **~$100M quarterly OCS revenue by December 2026 quarter** with multiple customers running systems in labs, software integration as the gating workstream.
- **CPO is the third vector.** Ultra-high-power laser capacity investments hedge against pluggable form-factor evolution. Even if pluggables shift, high-quality photons still need to come from somewhere.
- **Sleeper angle — cluster sprawl.** Large AI clusters increasingly campus-scale. Power, cooling, real estate push deployments outward → DCI and transport demand. Lumentum's narrow-linewidth/coherent-adjacent strength wins both inside fabric AND between fabrics. Double dip underappreciated.
- **Risks live in execution + concentration:** InP unit capacity lift delivery, yield/quality during mix shift, Thailand scaling, OCS software-gate timing.

Watch over 12-18 months: (1) InP output rising visibly, (2) 200G becoming a real mix driver, (3) transceivers growing smoothly (not lurching), (4) OCS converting lab presence to repeatable deployments. If these hit, Lumentum becomes a scarce-node AI buildout supplier with an OCS call option and a CPO hedge.

## Original Content

The last six months have made one thing very clear. The future of AI scaling is becoming a connectivity problem. The cluster is the computer now, and the network is the constraint. That is the lens I use for Lumentum. The company sits in the photon supply chain that keeps AI fabrics alive, from the laser chips that generate the light, to the subsystems that condition it, to the system-level products that increasingly shape how hyperscalers wire up tens of thousands of accelerators. This looks like a different Lumentum than the market used to know back when the narrative centered on telecom cycles, industrial softness, and iPhone sensing seasonality.

Lumentum sells two things - components and systems. Components are the building blocks like laser chips, laser subassemblies, and the optical plumbing that makes high-speed links behave across real distances. Systems are complete products like optical transceivers and optical circuit switching (OCS). The last quarter made the hierarchy obvious. Components are driving the story and the profitability. Systems are the acceleration lever if execution stays tight.

The reason Lumentum exists in this moment is physics. As the industry pushes deeper into 800G and approaches 1.6T, copper becomes a power problem and a signal-integrity problem across the distances that matter inside modern AI data centers. The photons carry the load better. Look at what management is saying. They are tying the current demand surge directly to cloud and AI infrastructure, and they are framing this as broad based across data center optics plus the networks that connect campuses and regions. The company is increasingly behaving like an AI infrastructure supplier because the network is now part of the compute stack.

The recent quarter and guide put a clean marker down. Revenue stepped up sharply year over year, and management guided the next quarter to another record range. The main take away is that the growth inflection is happening and was pulled forward. Everything else is downstream of whether supply can keep up.

That brings us to the real edge in Lumentum. Supply. Management said the indium phosphide (InP) wafer fab is fully allocated, and they see a path to roughly 40% more unit capacity over the next few quarters from yield and throughput work. They also quantified the gap, describing demand still running roughly 25 to 30% above supply. This turns into allocation decisions, tighter customer selection, long-term commitments, and targeted pricing power. When the scarce node of the stack gets tight, the margin structure changes with it.

This is where yield starts acting like a moat. In the 200G-per-lane era, the bottleneck is producing high-performance InP devices at yields that scale. If Lumentum keeps lifting output while holding quality, they are doing more than shipping more units. They are gatekeeping the highest-value transition inside the AI optics roadmap for a period of time. That is why the capacity commentary matters so much. It is a statement about who gets to participate in the early innings of the 1.6T cycle in real volume.

The product read-through supports that view. Management talked about record EML shipment levels and increasing 200G contribution, with 200G becoming more meaningful through calendar 2026. They also emphasized continuous-wave lasers as a real program, with shipments underway and an internal transceiver path. You do not need to be deep in the weeds here. The takeaway I want to provide is that Lumentum is trying to stay relevant across both major routes the industry can take whether it's higher-performance EML-based paths or CW-powered paths that plug into other integration stacks.

On the systems side, Cloud Light is still the strategic move to capture more dollars per port. The story has always hinged on execution because transceivers are manufacturing-intense and qualification-heavy. The important shift in the most recent commentary is tone. Management described the historical fits and starts as something they expect to fade as Thailand capacity comes online, and they framed a path to a meaningful quarterly transceiver run-rate while being selective and prioritizing margin-rich opportunities. They also anchored 1.6T transceivers as a mid-2026 shipping milestone and explicitly suggested 1.6T margin structure improves versus 800G. That's crucial because it sets up the next growth leg to carry profit expansion rather than dilute it.

OCS is the piece that can change the narrative from them being a great supplier to being an architecture winner. OCS removes repeated optical-electrical-optical conversion at every hop by steering light directly from input fiber to output fiber for circuit-style connectivity. That is a power and latency unlock, and hyperscalers care about both now. Management put a very tangible marker on this with a target of reaching roughly $100 million of quarterly OCS revenue by the December 2026 quarter, with multiple customers already running systems in labs and software integration as the gating workstream. Hardware gates matter, but software gates define the slope of the ramp. When OCS moves from lab validation into repeatable deployment, the revenue quality changes because it starts behaving like a fabric standard rather than a one-off project.

CPO is the third vector they keep returning to, and it is the logical extension of the same theme. Form factors will evolve, integration will move closer to the switch and the package, and thermal budgets will keep tightening. Yet the photon socket remains. Lumentum has been investing into ultra-high-power laser capacity with CPO-style architectures in mind. That is the right hedge because even in a world where pluggables eventually shrink or shift, high-quality photons still need to come from somewhere, and the market keeps pulling that requirement forward.

There is also a sleeper angle that I think people tend to underweight: cluster sprawl. I hear so much fixation on inside-the-data-hall optics because it is easier to visualize and model. The reality is that large AI clusters increasingly behave like campus-scale infrastructure. Power density, cooling, and real estate constraints push deployments outward, and that forces demand for DCI and transport layers that link buildings and campuses together. Lumentum's strength in narrow-linewidth and coherent-adjacent components puts them in position to win both inside the fabric and between fabrics as clusters sprawl. That double dip is an underappreciated driver when the market is primarily watching the front panel.

As always I do want to highlight some risks. Demand reads strong. The risk lives in execution and concentration. Can they actually deliver the InP unit capacity lift they are pointing to. Can they keep yields and quality intact while shifting mix toward next-gen devices. Can Thailand scale without stutters as systems volume grows. Can OCS clear software gates on schedule so revenue turns from promise into repetition. Over a multi-year horizon, architecture choices can move margin pools between chips, modules, and systems, so the company's hedges matter, but the near-term driver remains supply-side execution.

TLDR; Lumentum has moved from a cyclical optics company to a capacity-constrained AI infrastructure supplier with three growth vectors that can stack: higher-speed lasers, higher-speed transceivers, and OCS plus CPO adjacency. The market's job is to price whether this is a transient spike or a structural re-rating. The company is arguing structural, and the data that I am seeing is starting to line up with that argument.

Over the next 12 to 18 months watch whether InP output rises in a visible way. Watch whether 200G becomes a real mix driver as planned. Watch whether transceivers grow smoothly instead of lurching quarter to quarter. Watch whether OCS converts from lab presence into repeatable deployments with software integration clearing gates. If those checkpoints hit, the story tightens further and Lumentum becomes a scarce-node supplier to the AI buildout, with a call option on OCS and a hedge through CPO.
