# LITE demand-signal compilation Q2-Q3 FY26

Consolidates three Crux Capital LITE commentary posts: the **Feb 4 2026 tweet** reacting to the Q2 FY26 earnings call (Gaetano @crux_capital_), the **Mar 17 2026 substack** confirming the OCS slide at Nokia OFC plus the Marvell co-demo, and the **Apr 12 2026 substack** stitching Hurlston's demand-signal progression across three quarters into a single "sold out through 2027, trending toward 2028" narrative. Provenance preserved inline per fact via `(per Crux <date>)` citations; source URLs and image attribution at the bottom. Companion Coherent ([[Coherent (COHR)]]) note was promised for the day after Feb 4; downstream reads on [[Applied Optoelectronics (AAOI)]], POET, ALMU were also flagged for follow-up.

## Key Takeaways

- **EML supply gap is structural and tightening, not transient**: 25-30% short of demand even after pulled-forward throughput and yield work as of Feb 4 (per Crux Feb 4). All EML capacity LTA-locked through CY2027, with marginal units triggering fresh pricing conversations — textbook setup for sustained pricing power (per Crux Feb 4). By April, Hurlston was telling Bloomberg products are sold out through 2027 and "could be sold out through all of 2028 within two quarters" (per Crux Apr 12). The constraint is concentrating value upstream in [[Lumentum (LITE)]]'s InP-based light sources — the same layer [[Sancet 2026 - Goldman optical cheat sheet omits substrate epi equipment and laser layers (IQE Soitec Tower Aixtron AEHR LPKF SIVE)]] argues sell-side cheat sheets miss (per Crux Feb 4).

- **Revenue and margin are accelerating together** — three-quarter progression: $480.7M / 15.0% non-GAAP OM (Aug 2025 fiscal Q4 25) → $533.8M / 18.7% (Nov 2025 fiscal Q1 26) → $665.5M / 25.2% (Feb 2026 fiscal Q2 26), with Q3 guide ~$805M (~21% sequential step-up) and forward guide implying >85% YoY revenue growth (per Crux Feb 4 + Apr 12). Crux's framing: "Revenue is accelerating, margins are expanding, and management is still saying supply cannot keep up. That is usually what a real capacity bottleneck looks like" (per Crux Apr 12).

- **OCS is now contracted, not slideware**: a Lumentum slide at the Nokia Optical Fiber Communication Conference titled **"OCS Ramping to >$1B 2027 Run Rate"** carries a March 2026 update — "New multi-year, multi-billion-dollar OCS agreement reached" with order backlog $400M+ to be fulfilled in 2H CY26 (per Crux Mar 17). Hurlston at Morgan Stanley revised the prior aspirational "$100M OCS quarter late CY26" UP to "closer to $400M across the last two quarters of CY26" (per Crux Mar 17). Q1 FY26 was the first $10M OCS quarter — three months earlier than planned (per Crux Mar 17). The prior [[Crux Capital 2025-12-30 - Lumentum supply-constrained AI photonics thesis, InP 25-30 pct demand-supply gap, 40 pct unit capacity lift, OCS $100M target Dec 2026]] $100M target is now positioned as the *low* end vs Hurlston's $400M-across-two-quarters revision (per Crux Mar 17).

- **CPO and ultra-high-power lasers are second/third revenue legs with hard backlog**: another multi-hundred-million-dollar CPO+UHP order is shipping 1H CY2027, plus a material UHP shipment inflection later in 2026 (per Crux Feb 4). The Feb release disclosed an incremental multi-hundred-million-dollar CPO order deliverable 1H CY2027 (per Crux Apr 12). CPO booking timing aligns with the supply-chain map in [[Sancet ENA bull case - Enablence in-house PLC fab in Fremont makes them irreplaceable Sivers partner in AI CPO supply chain]].

- **Four growth engines layout at OFC 2026**: (1) cloud transceivers / 1.6T volume shipments starting summer 2026, (2) **50%+ EML capacity growth by end CY26**, (3) OCS ramping toward **>$1B 2027 run rate**, (4) first scale-up CPO shipments expected by late 2027 (per Crux Apr 12). Mgmt frames OCS + CPO as still at "the starting line" despite the Q2 print already at $665.5M (per Crux Feb 4).

- **Management is now thinking like a constrained manufacturer**: open evaluation of additional fab capacity *including acquisitions*, plus faster pivot to contract manufacturing for backend assembly because build rates are being gated outside the wafer fab too (per Crux Feb 4). Capacity-add language signals demand visibility strong enough to justify structural decisions. Backend gating means assembly throughput is its own constraint — same story one node downstream as the III-V-foundry-as-bottleneck thesis in [[Ren Aramb thesis - Q1 2026 EPS up 30x YoY makes Win Semi the III-V foundry bottleneck for the photonics and LEO satellite ramps]].

- **NVIDIA relationship turns this into a strategic-capacity story** (March 2, 2026 announcement): multiyear strategic agreements, multibillion purchase commitment, future capacity access rights for advanced laser components, **$2B NVIDIA investment in Lumentum**, and NVIDIA named as customer of the Greensboro facility — physical capacity built with named strategic demand already attached (per Crux Apr 12).

- **Greensboro NC InP fab — the next manufacturing relief valve, but it's mid-2028**: announced March 26 2026, acquired from Qorvo, will significantly expand capacity using **6-inch indium phosphide (InP) wafers**, but only ramps mid-2028 (per Crux Apr 12). Hurlston: brownfield expansion cuts time roughly in half vs greenfield, but timeline still stretches out. Even the faster path leaves the market waiting.

- **OCS customer concentration is narrow**: three customers driving OCS demand, two of them taking most of the volume (per Crux Mar 17).

- **ELS (external light source) modules are a strategic wildcard** — moving up the stack from chips to packaged ELS modules expands content per deployment and broadens the addressable customer set; direct read-through to POET's thesis with a separate ELS-focused post promised (per Crux Feb 4).

- **Scarcity phase vs digestion phase**: "Demand is still running hot. Supply is still tight. NVIDIA is helping lock in future capacity. OCS and CPO are moving from concept toward revenue. And the next meaningful manufacturing relief valve does not show up until mid-2028. That is a strong setup for Lumentum specifically, and it is also a strong signal that the broader high-end optics complex remains in a scarcity phase rather than a digestion phase" (per Crux Apr 12).

## OCS arc

- **First $10M OCS quarter in Q1 FY26 — three months earlier than planned** (per Crux Mar 17).
- **OCS backlog past $400M** as of Feb print, across multiple customers, weighted to 2H CY26 and early FY27 (per Crux Feb 4 + Apr 12).
- **Hurlston Morgan Stanley revision**: aspirational "$100M OCS quarter late CY26" → "closer to $400M across the last two quarters of CY26" (per Crux Mar 17).
- **Lumentum OFC slide — "OCS Ramping to >$1B 2027 Run Rate"** — bullets:
  - March 2026 update: New multi-year, multi-billion-dollar OCS agreement reached
  - Order backlog $400M+ to be fulfilled in 2H CY26
  - Field-proven reliability
  - Elegant design lends itself to high-volume manufacturing
  - Future-proofed via wavelength-agnostic architecture and native high-radix scalability
  (per Crux Mar 17).
- **Four OCS use cases**: (1) spine-switch replacement, (2) optical scale-up across very large compute systems, (3) network protection / redundancy / failover, (4) scale-across and data-center interconnect. Spine replacement is highest-leverage — keeps more of the network in optics and offsets the power burden of scaling giant AI fabrics (per Crux Mar 17).
- **OCS architecture**: opens a direct light path between two network points rather than constantly round-tripping electrical↔optical conversions. MEMS-based platforms — **R300 for large-scale deployments**, **R64 for smaller AI-DC applications**. Pitched as lower latency, better signal integrity, lower power (per Crux Mar 17).
- **Customer concentration**: three customers driving OCS demand, two of them taking most of the volume (per Crux Mar 17).
- **Marvell co-demo role** at the Lumentum/Marvell OFC demonstration: connectivity silicon + longer-reach optical-link technology + management/telemetry software around the OCS fabric. Marvell mgmt on its last call: 1.6T entered production fiscal Q4 2026 with very strong bookings from multiple Tier 1 customers; portfolio spans **scale-out, scale-across, scale-up** (per Crux Mar 17).
- **Asymmetric benefit**: [[Lumentum (LITE)]] benefits if OCS itself becomes a real network layer (selling the switch); [[Marvell Technology (MRVL)]] benefits from the broader trend — every step the AI network takes toward "more optical, more complex, more software-controlled" expands its DSP / pluggable / telemetry surface area (per Crux Mar 17).
- **OCS thesis re-anchor**: the prior [[Crux Capital 2025-12-30 - Lumentum supply-constrained AI photonics thesis, InP 25-30 pct demand-supply gap, 40 pct unit capacity lift, OCS $100M target Dec 2026]] $100M-quarter target is now positioned as the *low end* vs Hurlston's $400M-across-last-two-quarters revision (per Crux Mar 17).
- **Confirmation of OCS as booked multi-customer revenue stream with timing visibility** — reads as confirmation for the broader OCS thesis embedded in the [[Celestial Photonic Fabric Platform for AI Accelerators - arXiv 2507.14000 specs 33TB unified memory and 115Tbps all-to-all switching across 16 XPUs (Ding Diep 2025)]] photonic-fabric narrative (per Crux Feb 4).

## Frameworks

- **Scarcity phase vs digestion phase**: AI optics is no longer a pure demand story; Hurlston is increasingly framing it as a constrained supply story. Strong demand stories can wobble if orders pause; constrained supply stories stay tight longer when lead times to add capacity are measured in years (per Crux Apr 12).
- **Four growth engines** (OFC 2026): cloud transceivers/1.6T, EML capacity expansion, OCS, CPO (per Crux Apr 12).
- **Four OCS use cases**: spine-switch replacement, optical scale-up, network protection/redundancy, scale-across/DCI (per Crux Mar 17).
- **Scale-out / scale-across / scale-up** taxonomy — used by Marvell to describe its connectivity portfolio that wraps the OCS fabric (per Crux Mar 17).
- **Copper hybrid framing** — copper still plays a major role; optics gains opportunity to move closer to the switching and compute fabric over time. More believable than instant optical takeover; lines up with disclosed CPO orders for 1H 2027 delivery (per Crux Apr 12).
- **Choke-point thesis** — Lumentum sits at the InP-based light-source choke point while simultaneously building real second and third legs (OCS and UHP lasers) into booked backlog (per Crux Feb 4).
- **LTAs as baseline + marginal-pricing dynamic** — LTAs set the baseline price; any incremental unit becomes a new negotiation in a tight regime (per Crux Feb 4).
- **Manufacturing-as-bottleneck**: high-end optical manufacturing is itself a bottleneck — the industry has to physically build enough optical components, fast enough, at the right performance level. As LITE expands aggressively, it falls FURTHER behind because demand rises faster than capacity grows (per Crux Apr 12).
- **ELS modules as category creation** — moving up the stack from chips toward packaged external-light-source modules expands content per deployment, broadens the customer set, and becomes a category-creation moment for how people model the photonics TAM (per Crux Feb 4).

## Expanded Crux narrative (verbatim or near-verbatim passages preserved)

### Feb 4 tweet — closing TLDR and constrained-manufacturer language

Crux Capital framing (Gaetano, Feb 4 quote-tweet thread): *"This quarter was Lumentum proving they sit at a choke point (InP-based light sources), while simultaneously building real second and third legs (OCS and UHP lasers) that start to look like repeatable revenue streams with backlog and shipment timing."* Mgmt-mindset language: *"They are thinking like a constrained manufacturer now. They openly discussed evaluating additional fab capacity, including acquisitions. They described a faster pivot toward contract manufacturing for backend assembly because build rates are being gated outside the wafer fab too. This is what you say when demand visibility is strong enough to justify structural capacity decisions."*

Per-wafer economics: *"They are pushing harder into 200G lane lasers. They even quantified how the early mix is already punching above its weight in revenue dollars. If they keep marching toward higher-speed content, you get the double benefit of more revenue per wafer plus better margins."*

ELS framing: *"ELS modules is the strategic wildcard … They talked about moving up the stack from chips toward more packaged external light source modules. If that gets real, it expands content per deployment and broadens who they can sell to. It also becomes a category creation moment for how people model their photonics TAM."*

### Mar 17 substack — OCS architecture and why-spine-replacement narrative

Why OCS matters: *"Moving data around the system is becoming one of the main limits on performance. Marvell has been saying that connectivity is now a primary bottleneck in AI infrastructure, and Lumentum is pushing OCS as one answer to that problem. Optical circuit switching, or OCS, creates a direct light path from one point in the network to another. Instead of constantly pushing traffic through multiple layers of electronic switching and repeated optical-to-electrical conversions, the network can open a direct optical path and let the data travel more cleanly."*

Why spine-replacement is the leverage point: *"As more of the data center moves into optics, companies have started asking a simple question. Why keep bouncing back and forth between electrical and optical domains if more of the traffic can stay optical? … If operators can keep more of the network in optics, that can help offset some of the power burden that comes with scaling giant AI fabrics. That is one reason OCS has moved so quickly from niche idea to serious product category."*

Marvell asymmetric-benefit framing: *"Lumentum benefits if OCS itself becomes a real new layer in AI networking. Marvell benefits if AI networks keep becoming more optical, more complex, and more software-controlled, because that creates more need for the chips, DSPs, pluggables, and telemetry that sit around the optical fabric."*

### Apr 12 substack — manufacturing-bottleneck thesis and constrained-supply pivot

The bottleneck dynamic: *"High-end optical manufacturing has become a bottleneck in its own right. If Lumentum is telling investors that it is falling further behind demand while simultaneously expanding capacity, then the gating factor extends beyond end demand. Think about that for a second. As they are aggressively expanding, they are falling FURTHER behind because demand is rising FASTER than they can grow. The industry also has to physically build enough optical components, fast enough, at the right performance level."*

Why-NVIDIA-relationship-is-key framing: *"On March 2, NVIDIA and Lumentum announced multiyear strategic agreements, a multibillion purchase commitment, future capacity access rights for advanced laser components, and a $2 billion NVIDIA investment in Lumentum. That is far beyond standard vendor behavior. A large customer is effectively helping secure supply in a constrained market. … Lumentum is building physical capacity with named strategic demand already attached to it. … NVIDIA appears to view advanced optical capacity as important enough to lock up early. That says a great deal about where the bottlenecks are moving."*

What is actually changing: *"The market still tends to think about AI optics as solely a strong demand story. Hurlston is increasingly framing it as a constrained supply story as well. Strong demand stories can wobble if orders pause. Constrained supply stories can stay tight for longer, especially when lead times to add capacity are measured in years rather than quarters. The Greensboro fab is a perfect example. It is strategically important, it strengthens U.S. manufacturing, and it will expand InP capacity. It also arrives on a mid-2028 timeline. So it solves a future shortage more than today's one."*

Starting-line framing in context: *"If Lumentum is already putting up this kind of revenue and margin performance before OCS and CPO are fully contributing, then the market still may be underestimating how many different optical layers are beginning to ramp at once."*
