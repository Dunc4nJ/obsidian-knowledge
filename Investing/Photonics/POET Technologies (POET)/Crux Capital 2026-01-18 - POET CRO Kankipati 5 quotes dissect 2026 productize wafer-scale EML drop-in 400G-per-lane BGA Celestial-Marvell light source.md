## Original Content

On Friday, the CRO of POET Technologies, Raju Kankipati, was interviewed on Picmagazine and he dropped some really important crumbs that are worth dissecting. In this post I will call out to what I believe are the most significant, dissect them, and create a 'pulse' check on the company going into 2026.

2026 is a show me year for POET, and they are starting off swinging.

Let's jump right in.

Here are the 5 quotes that I want do discuss:

1. *"This year is our year to productize, meaning to get into production and start shipping volume of these engines."*
2. *"The poet solution enables wafer scale assembly of EMLs on our silicon platform, so without any active alignments."*
3. *"One of the huge benefit of this point terralight family is the same engines can be used for two different module types... it saves a lot of engineering resources and makes manufacturing less capex intensive."*
4. *"At that speed [3.2T], our engine will just sit like a BGA package on a module... it eliminates wire bonds throughout the solution... 2026 will be good year for us to demonstrate 400 gig per lane."*
5. *"We've been working with celestial AI, which recently got acquired by Marvel... we're also actively working on engaging a few other large customers."*

---

*"This year is our year to productize, meaning to get into production and start shipping volume of these engines."*

If you have been following my content you know how often I speak about the significance of 2026. This is the year for POET to show that all the technological foundation and partnerships that have been laid can actually be 1) productized and 2) commercialized.

They are claiming to be really busy across almost a dozen active customers. While I am patiently awaiting the $5m production order to be fulfilled, I am also believing that the company is building a repeatable, high-yield manufacturing hub that can service the entire hyerpscaler market. With such a strong cash position, focus is on execution.

---

*"The poet solution enables wafer scale assembly of EMLs on our silicon platform, so without any active alignments."*

This is the lego concept/analogy that I and others speak of often. It is the core of the investment thesis for POET. In traditional optics, manufacturing is a challenge. You power on every laser using a high-precision robot to wiggle it by microns until the light hits the fiber. Traditionally relatively slow, capital-intensive and could result in low throughput.

POET's solution is that they use microscopic guide rails printed directly onto the silicon interposer. They drop the EML lasers into these slots and they just place in there accurately. No powering on the laser. I don't need to go to in depth here as I have covered it in depth before and if you want to understand it deeper it would be best to look at their explanations of it with a video.

Management explicitly confirmed that they are doing this at wafer scale. This means they can process hundreds of engines at once on a single wafer. That is how you hit the margins needed to compete with the giants. If they can maintain the yields on this process during the H2 26 ramp, the cost advantages over traditional SiPh could be massive.

---

*"One of the huge benefit of this point terralight family is the same engines can be used for two different module types... it saves a lot of engineering resources and makes manufacturing less capex intensive."*

Here we are talking about efficiency and stickiness. Usually if a customer wants a 500m reach (DR8) and a 2km (2xFR4) then they have to design two entirely different modules. This is expensive, potentially twice the engineering talent, and it significantly increases capex.

POET is basically saying "use our one Teralight engine for both". Think about the implications of this for a customer like Luxshare or Foxconn. They can design on moterhboard, one manufacturing process, and one testing sweet. Then they could just plug in the POET engine to give their hyperscaler customers the reach they need.

This could save months of R&D. The vision is that once a customer builds their massive roadmap around POET's common engine, it would become a massive hurdle for them to switch. This makes POET sticky and potentially the standard heart of the transceiver market.

*"At that speed [3.2T], our engine will just sit like a BGA package on a module... it eliminates wire bonds throughout the solution... 2026 will be good year for us to demonstrate 400 gig per lane."*

Ok fair enough, I could see some people looking at this statement and glossing over. So much technicality. So let's break it down.

800G refers to the single fiber connection moving 800 Gigabits of data per second. This is the current widely used state. The industry is moving to 1.6T (1,600 Gigabits) and they are already focusing in on 3.2T (3,200 Gigabits). I asked ChatGPT to put this into perspective that people could conceptualize and it said that **3.2T is roughly enough bandwidth to stream 120,000 4K movies at the exact same time through a cable the size of your thumb.** I thought that was a fun and mind-blowing fact. Please don't shoot the messenger if it's not exactly 120,000, just understand that the amount of data being moved, and the speed at which it is moving, is immense.

To hit these speeds the goal is to increase the lane speed. Think of a highway. You can either add more lanes (which makes the chip larger and more expensive) or you can make the cars go faster. The industry wants to make the data travel faster. So moving from 100G to 200G to 400G.

There are some physics problems that get introduced here. In a traditional chip there is communication through tiny gold wires called wire bonds. At lower speeds, these wires are fine. But at 400G per lane, electricity is vibrating so fast that those wires leak energy, pick up noise from other parts of the chip, and create massive heat. This inductance essentially crashes the signal.

POET's goal is to kill the wire bond entirely. They are moving to a semicon package style that looks more like a high-end processor than a traditional optical part. Through TSV (through-silicon vias), signals travel vertically through the chip rather than around the edge, which is a significantly shorter path and results in way less signal loss. And with BGA packaging (ball grid array), the entire optical engine sits flat on the circuit board. It connects using tiny solder bumps underneath which is the same way a NVIDIA GPU or an Apple M3 chip connects to a motherboard.

Management will be demonstrating this 400G-per-lane performance in 2026. This would be a massive upgrade in physics. If wire bonds become a bottleneck, POET could prove to be the socket that is the solution.

*"We've been working with celestial AI, which recently got acquired by Marvel... we're also actively working on engaging a few other large customers."*

Ah, is this the validation we have been waiting for? Ever since the Marvell deal, we have been operating under the assumption that the POET and Celestial AI partnership is still ongoing. While we obviously don't quite know what Marvell will do with all the components of the supply chain long term, I do believe this is a massive signal.

I believe it is safe to assume that Marvell didn't spend $3.25 billion on Celestial AI without a deep audit. And this audit would show that the POET Starlight is a light source for that architecture. IMO ou don't buy the photonic fabric if you aren't comfortable with the the engine. In fact, Celestial's CEO David Lazovsky (who was POET's former Chairman) has been very vocal about the "co-designed" nature of this stack.

The ultimate validation will be any specifics tied to this, whether its official press releases, design-ins, or the big one: volume production orders. If Marvell is aiming for $500M+ in revenue from this fabric by 2028, they need a light source that is already qualified and manufacturable today. POET is currently the only one currently sitting in that seat. Technically speaking, Marvell COULD shake up the supply chain, but I don't see why they would want to. But that is why it will be a big deal to see any further confirmation.

Also, if we get more updates or orders from these other 'large customers' that could be significant upside for the stock. We're already seeing hints of this with the NTT 100G bidi chip and the Luxshare 800G ramp. I would like to see some updates on these engagements throughout 2026 to prove that POET is becoming the quote "Switzerland of Photonics" for more than just one giant player.
