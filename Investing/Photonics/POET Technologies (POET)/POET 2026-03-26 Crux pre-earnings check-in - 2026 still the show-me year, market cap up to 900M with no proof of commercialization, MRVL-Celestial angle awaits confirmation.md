# POET 2026-03-26 — Crux pre-earnings check-in: 2026 is still the "show-me" year, market cap doubled to ~$900M without commercialization proof, MRVL-Celestial angle still needs confirmation

Crux Capital Group's pre-earnings check-in on [[POET Technologies (POET)]] roughly seven months after taking an initial position around a $400M market cap and a $6 share price. Stock has roughly doubled to a ~$900M market cap without yet delivering proof of commercial conversion — so the framework hasn't really changed (it is still "very interesting photonics company that needs to prove it can commercialize"), but the stakes have because dilution-adjusted upside is narrower and downside risk is more real at the higher valuation.

## Key Takeaways

- **Same core question, higher stakes**: framework when Crux first bought (~$6 / ~$400M cap) was "exciting story, cool tech, solid partnerships, needs to prove it can commercialize." That framework is essentially unchanged seven months in, but [[POET Technologies (POET)]] now trades at ~$900M cap. The conversion-to-revenue bridge still has not been crossed; the cost of missing it is higher.
- **Management voice is more commercial, less science-project**: CRO has become noticeably more public and operator-like in messaging. Recent interviews emphasize finishing qualification, getting production lines ready, shipping units, converting engagements into committed development programs, building toward recurring revenue. That tone shift is what the market needs from a name labeled a "science project."
- **2026 is still the "show-me" year, but with more structure**: CRO has said POET has a "healthy number of units" scheduled to ship across the next 3-4 quarters and that near-term focus is finishing qualification + production-line readiness. The timeline is becoming tangible rather than abstract — but risk is not removed, just sharpened. If 2026 exits with nothing materially changed, Crux says the thesis needs revisiting.
- **Partnership list broader but partnerships ≠ revenue**: named partners now include Lessengers, QCi, LITEON, ADTRAN, and the older Celestial AI relationship. Broader interest is real and signals platform credibility — but partnership announcements are not the same as conversion into development programs / production orders / recurring shipments. That conversion is the bridge POET still has to cross.
- **MRVL acquisition of Celestial AI is interesting, not yet confirmed exposure**: [[Marvell Technology (MRVL)]]'s acquisition of Celestial AI lifts the strategic value of the prior POET-Celestial relationship and is a key piece of the bull case, but it does not automatically guarantee POET multi-year direct revenue exposure. Need confirmation that POET is "designed in" before treating the angle as locked.
- **Earnings expectations modest**: Crux expects little signal from the numbers themselves (still largely pre-revenue, as far as is known). Cash balance disclosure would be nice; what would matter most is direct CEO commentary on a call, and no call appeared scheduled as of writing. Focus is on future possibility, not current EPS.
- **Cash position ~$400M**: Crux reads the cash as prep for the commercial inflection. Allocation is sized for execution risk; building further only on incremental proof.
- **Self-discipline note**: position is one of only two in Crux's book currently in the red (modestly, due to dilution) despite the run-up, because Crux entered very early. This is framed as a story that still needs to prove itself, not one whose proof has arrived.

## Original Content

$POET is slated to report earnings soon.

Some sources say today after hours. Most seem to point to March 30th or 31st.

Either way, I'll cover it once it drops.

But before that happens, I want to talk through POET a bit and lay out some expectations for this earnings report and for 2026 in general.

POET has become a very commonly discussed stock on X.

It was one of my first photonics positions, and funny enough, it is still 1 of only 2 names I own that is in the red. Only slightly. Darn dilution.

I first bought in when the market cap was around ~$400M and the stock was around $6.

Now we are closer to a ~$900M market cap.

So naturally, a few questions come up:

Has my conviction changed?

How much has the story actually changed?

Has meaningful progress really been made?

And what should we expect over the coming quarters?

That's what I want to answer here.

So let's dig in.

---

When I first bought into the company, my framing was pretty simple: Exciting story. Really cool tech. Solid partnerships. Still needs to prove it can actually commercialize.

Fast forward around 7 months, and honestly… that core framing is still mostly the same.

Now, to be fair, progress has absolutely been made.

Quite a bit, actually. I think the story today is stronger than it was when I first bought it.

But I also think the most important question has not *really* changed at all:

Can POET move from "very interesting photonics company" to a real production business with recurring revenue?

That is still the whole game.

So what *has* changed?

---

### 1 - The company is clearly further along commercially than it was

This is the biggest thing. Back when many people first looked at POET, it still felt like a company that mostly had promising technology, interesting slides, and a long list of things that could happen.

Today, it feels more grounded than that.

We have heard much more directly from management about production, qualification, customer shipments, and actual near-term priorities.

The CRO has become much more public and much more clear in how he is framing the business.

That matters to me for sure and i've covered his interviews in depth on X.

For a company that gets labeled a "science project," investors need to see management acting like operators and not just engineers.

And that is what the recent interviews have started to show at least.

The message has become much more commercial.

They are working on finishing qualification, getting production lines ready, shipping product, converting engagements into committed development programs, and building toward recurring revenue.

That is a much better place to be.

---

### 2 - 2026 is still the "show me" year

I have said this before, and I still think it is the right framing. The difference now is that management has started putting more real structure around that idea.

The CRO recently said they have a healthy number of units scheduled to ship over the next 3 to 4 quarters, and that the near-term focus is on finishing qualification and getting production lines ready.

That is important because now we are no longer talking in broad, abstract terms about someday commercialization.

We are talking about specific products that are supposed to move through qualification and into production over a fairly defined window.

That does not mean the risk is gone though. It just means the timeline is becoming more tangible. And that is exactly what the market needs from them.

We also have harder metrics to track. If we exit 2026 and nothign as changed here, we may need to revisit the thesis.

---

### 3 - The partnership list is stronger, but that still does not equal scale

This is where I need to stay grounded and not let my feet run ahead of me.

Yes, the partnership and customer list has expanded.

Lessengers.
QCi.
LITEON.
ADTRAN.
And of course the older Celestial AI connection that we still need to watch closely.

That all matters. It shows interest is real, that POET is not operating in a vacuum and that the platform is getting attention from real players. And if they do successfully scale, they have more breadth.

But partnership announcements are not the same thing as meaningful revenue.

That distinction is really key.

This company does not have a problem attracting interest.

The I still need to see as an investor is conversion.

Conversion into development programs, production orders, recurring shipments, and actual revenue that builds quarter after quarter.

That is the bridge POET still has to cross.

---

### 4 - The Marvell / Celestial angle is exciting… but still needs confirmation

Celestial AI being acquired by Marvell is definitely interesting and a very key part of the current bull case.

It raises the strategic value of that prior relationship, no question.

But this does not automatically give POET direct, guaranteed revenue exposure for years to come.

Maybe it does become meaningful. I am hoping so, and I do believe it will.

Maybe POET is indeed designed in and it becomes a real part of the story.

But we still need confirmation of that.

---

### What do I expect from earnings?

Honestly, not too much. I would like to hear from the CEO directly during a call. I don't see a call scheduled (if anyone does, please comment). This is where most of the insight will lie.

The numbers are fairly irrelevant unless they show some kind of surprise. Seeing their cash balance would be nice. But the focus isn't on their current earnings situation. It's on the future possibility. Keep that in mind. They are still largely pre-revenue (as far as we know).

---

So to summarize, the stakes for POET have become higher then when I first got in because they have gone up so much in valuation without proof of commercialization or repeat orders.

They have a lot of cash (somewhere around $400m). My read on this is that they are preparing for the commercial inflection.

But it always comes back to this…

They need to prove that they can deliver. And that their tech actually makes a meaningful difference in the real world, at scale.

Until we have that proof, POET will continue to have execution risk. And at the valuation it's trading at, that means the downside risk is real and significant. Please understand this if you invest here or are interested in investing.

I am very hopeful that that inflection point comes in 2026. But I do understand the risk and what it means for my position if it doesn't, and I am allocated accordingly. I am awaiting further progress to continue to build my position.
