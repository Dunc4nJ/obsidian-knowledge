# POET FY25-Q4 earnings — Crux Capital analysis

Crux Capital's first earnings note on [[POET Technologies (POET)]] frames Q4 FY25 as a setup-and-roadmap quarter, not a proof-of-scale quarter. The biggest deltas vs. the prior story are (a) a $430M cash balance that defangs the long-standing financing-risk overhang on a ~$850M market cap, (b) the first explicit annual unit shipment target (30,000+ optical engines in 2026), and (c) a more concrete 2026 production cadence — Malaysia light sources in Q2, 800G optical engines in Q3. Strategy framing also extended beyond standard transceiver engines into ELSFP, high-power, CPO, and GPU-to-GPU / GPU-to-memory optical interconnect ("wink wink Celestial/[[Marvell Technology (MRVL)]]"). Reported Q4 revenue was just $341,202 (vs. $298,434 in Q3); the commercial story still has to migrate from promise into reported numbers, with Malaysia ramp, Q3 800G recognition, and realized ASPs as the next checkpoints.

## Key Takeaways

- **Cash balance: ~$430M** — material for a ~$850M market cap company, removes near-term capital-raise pressure and funds manufacturing/hiring/M&A. Crux notes prior cycles where POET still raised more after large balances, so caveat earned.
- **First explicit unit target: 30,000+ optical engines in 2026.** Earlier disclosures only covered capacity, initial production orders, and a revenue ramp — no clean public annual unit figure until now.
- **2026 production cadence with checkpoints:** Malaysia light-source production begins Q2 2026; high-speed 800G optical engines begin Q3 2026. Concrete enough to track quarter-by-quarter.
- **Strategic broadening beyond standard transceiver engines** — ELSFP, high-power applications, CPO, and GPU-to-GPU / GPU-to-memory optical interconnects. The MRVL/Celestial wink positions POET against the silicon-photonics-for-scale-up narrative.
- **Reported Q4 revenue: $341,202** (vs. $298,434 in Q3). The commercial base remains trivially small relative to the story.
- **Headline net loss overstated** by non-cash items: $30.6M derivative warrant adjustment and a $6.85M non-cash loss tied to the SPX interest acquisition. The income statement doesn't yet inform on real demand.
- **M&A language sharpened** — management is evaluating deals to add talent and secure differentiated component supply, framed as part of a broader module strategy.
- **What's NOT new**: the >$5M production order and the large financing rounds were pre-announced; this quarter repackaged them.
- **Open questions to monitor**: Malaysia Q2 actually starting on time; Q3 800G recognition showing up in reported sales (not just commentary); 30k-unit shipment target translating into legible revenue (pricing/mix not yet public); whether AI-interconnect framing earns customer-level validation (named wins, larger orders, visible module revenue).

## Original Content

### What improved

The biggest improvement was the balance sheet. POET said it now has roughly $430 million in cash, which materially changes the setup versus the old story where financing risk sat over everything. That gives them room to scale manufacturing, hire, and pursue acquisitions without the same near-term capital pressure. Hopefully. I have felt this in the past as well and they raised more. But $430m is a lot of cash for a $850m mkt cap company.

The second improvement was execution visibility. Management laid out a more explicit 2026 production cadence with light-source products in Malaysia beginning in Q2 2026, followed by high-speed 800G optical engines in Q3 2026. That is more concrete than the older broad commercialization language.

The third improvement was strategic framing. POET leaned harder into the idea that its opportunity extends beyond standard transceiver engines, putting more emphasis on ELSFP, high-power applications, CPO, and even GPU-to-GPU and GPU-to-memory optical interconnects (wink wink Celestial/Marvell)

---

### What was genuinely new

The most important new item for me was the 30,000+ optical engine shipment target for 2026. POET had not previously put a public annual shipment number like that on the record. Earlier disclosures covered capacity, initial production orders, and a revenue ramp, but this appears to be the first clean unit target.

The second genuinely new piece was the sharper 2026 ramp sequence. Management did not just say it is scaling, it went as for as attaching that scaling to a timeline: Q2 for light sources, Q3 for 800G engines. That gives us actual checkpoints to track across the next few quarters.

There was also more direct language around module partnerships contributing revenue this year. That does not guarantee meaningful revenue, but it is a more concrete commercial marker than generic customer-engagement commentary.

Also more explicit strategic language around acquisitions. Management said it is evaluating deals to add talent and secure differentiated component supply, tying that directly to a broader module strategy.

---

### What did not really change

This was still not a proof-of-scale revenue quarter obviously. Reported Q4 revenue was $341,202 versus $298,434 in Q3, so the actual reported commercial base remains very small.

The headline net loss also overstates the underlying deterioration because it included large non-cash items, particularly the $30.6 million derivative warrant adjustment and the $6.85 million non-cash loss related to the SPX interest acquisition. The income statement still does not tell you much about real demand inflection.

Some of the headline narrative points were also not new. The greater than $5 million production order and the large financing rounds had already been announced before this release. This quarter repackaged them into a stronger execution narrative rather than introducing them fresh.

---

### What still needs proving

The central remaining question is straightforward. Revenue conversion. POET now has the cash, the partnerships, and the production timeline. What it does not have is a reported revenue base that matches the ambition of the story.

So let's keep an eye on:

* Whether Malaysia actually begins light-source production in Q2
* Whether 800G engine production starts in Q3 and begins showing up in reported sales rather than only in commentary
* Whether the 30,000-unit shipment target translates into legible revenue. POET has not provided enough public pricing detail to do that cleanly, so we still need visibility into mix, realized ASPs, and the split between high-speed and high-power volume
* Whether the broader AI interconnect framing earns customer-level validation. The language around CPO, GPU-to-GPU, and GPU-to-memory is directionally meaningful, but the market will eventually want harder evidence in the form of named wins, larger orders, or visible module revenue

---

### Closing Thoughts

This was a positive thesis quarter, but primarily because it improved the setup and the roadmap rather than proving the model. The biggest positives were the $430 million cash balance, the clearer 2026 production roadmap, and the first explicit 30,000-unit shipment target. The biggest remaining gap is that reported revenue is still very small, which means the commercial story still has to move from promise into numbers.
