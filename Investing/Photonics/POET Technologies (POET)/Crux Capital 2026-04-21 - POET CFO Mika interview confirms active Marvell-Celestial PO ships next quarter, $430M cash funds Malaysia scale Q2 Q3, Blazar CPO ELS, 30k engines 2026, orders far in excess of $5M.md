### 1. The Marvell / Celestial AI Connection

In my previous deep dives, I noted that Celestial AI (now owned by Marvell) was the core anchor customer for POET's external light source (ELS) products. The relationship dates back to early 2022, but we needed confirmation that it would translate into actual, post-acquisition revenue. Mika provided the hardest evidence of this to date.

Mika explicitly clarified POET's position in the value chain, stating, "We're a supplier to Marvell now that they've acquired Celestial AI". He confirmed that POET is supplying the high-bandwidth, multi-frequency, high-power light sources needed to "light up the photonic fabric" for GPU-to-GPU and GPU-to-memory communication.

What is new is that Mika revealed that POET currently holds an active invoice and a purchase order from Celestial AI. Even more importantly, he provided a firm timeline: POET intends to ship product against this invoice "this year. In fact, I believe some of it will be shipped next quarter".

The product being shipped is Starlight, POET's packaged light source engine built on the Optical Interposer. Marvell already has a massive internal optical portfolio (silicon photonics, DSPs, TIAs). The fact that they are moving forward with POET's external light source rather than redesigning the system entirely around in-house tech is a massive validation of POET's wafer-scale passive alignment approach.

---

### 2. The $430M, M&A, and the EML Constraint

We know POET secured roughly $375 million in recent financing, pushing their total cash balance to a massive $430 million. During the interview, CFO Thomas Mika outlined exactly how this capital transforms their strategic positioning with Tier-1 hyperscalers, describing the cash as providing the "Three C's": Credibility, Capacity, and Capability.

1 - Credibility: Hyperscalers will not formally commit to a technology unless they are confident the supplier has the financial runway to survive long-term. POET is currently under NDAs with suppliers to these hyperscalers, and a $430 million bank account proves to these giants that POET is a permanent fixture rather than a fragile micro-cap.

2 - Capacity: Mika noted POET currently has the baseline capacity to assemble 1 million units, but they must demonstrate to hyperscalers that they can physically scale that to 5 to 10 million units. The cash funds this massive manufacturing expansion.

3 - Capability (and the EML Constraint): Mika stated that POET intends to use its capital or its stock to expand its talent base and "own components" to prevent margin stacking.

By publicly hinting at acquiring component manufacturers, POET is signaling a strategic move to vertically integrate its supply chain.

---

### 3. Ending Margin Stacking Through Wafer-Scale Manufacturing

The core thesis has always been that POET is fundamentally a *manufacturing* argument. Mika validated this by explaining the unit economics of the Optical Interposer.

Traditional module makers stack margins by buying external components, manually assembling them with expensive active alignment, and taking a hit at every layer. Mika noted that by bringing components in-house, POET can avoid this. More importantly, he explained that POET builds its devices "500 at a time, not one at a time," using automated, semiconductor capital equipment. This eliminates manual alignment and positions POET to achieve semiconductor-like gross margins.

To execute this, POET is heavily relying on its manufacturing scale-up in Malaysia (partnering with Globetronics and NationGate). The transition timeline is firm with light-source production in Malaysia targets Q2 2026, followed by 800G optical engine production in Q3.

---

### 4. Blazar and the InP Shift at OFC 2026

Mika shared a great anecdote regarding their Blazar external light source (ELS) prototype. When they showed it behind closed doors at OFC 2025, Mika admitted "nobody got it". Fast forward to OFC 2026 in Los Angeles right now, and the reception has flipped. Mika noted they were visited by several laser companies who now understand they desperately need an approach to laser development similar to POET's.

Blazar is explicitly positioned as a replacement for traditional DFB-laser-based solutions for Co-Packaged Optics (CPO). Its wafer-level chip-scale technology is designed to significantly increase the effective supply of highly constrained Indium Phosphide. Laser companies are faced with the fact that they they need a packaging platform that maximizes material efficiency, which is exactly what the Optical Interposer aims to achieve.

---

### 5. Bridging the Revenue Gap

While the roadmap and the cash pile are great, we must remain grounded in the financials. POET's reported Q4 2025 revenue was still tiny at just $341,202.

However, the inflection point is visible. Management expects to ship more than 30,000 optical engines in 2026. Last year they disclosed >$5 million order for Infinity engines (which will ship to a non-Chinese Asian systems integrator in H2 2026) is the start of this ramp. But Mika explicitly told us to look higher, stating that the company expects new purchase orders this year that are "far in excess of that 5 million".
