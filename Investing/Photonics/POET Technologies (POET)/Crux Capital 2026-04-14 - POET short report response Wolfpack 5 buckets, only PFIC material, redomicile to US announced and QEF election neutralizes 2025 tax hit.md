# Crux Capital 2026-04-14 - POET response to Wolfpack short report

A point-by-point response from Crux to the Wolfpack Research short report on [[POET Technologies (POET)]]. Crux divides the report into 5 buckets, ranks each by what's actually material, and updates with a key fact pattern: POET subsequently addressed the PFIC issue by announcing US redomicile plans + 2025 QEF election support.

The 5 buckets:

1. **PFIC / tax / IRS** — the *only* bucket Crux thinks investors should examine. Per POET's 2025 20-F, the company "believed" it was not a PFIC for 2024 and didn't expect to be for 2025. But the **January 2026 prospectus supplement** softened to "no determination" with PFIC status determinable only year-end. The bucket flipped from theoretical to practical the day this post was edited: POET committed to providing QEF election info for 2025, said the 2025 net loss means a timely QEF election is **not expected to create current income inclusions**, and the **board approved intention to redomicile to the US** with shareholder vote slated for **June 26, 2026**. That kills the PFIC overhang prospectively once redomicile completes. Crux: not a thesis-breaker for him personally; long-term holders should still talk to a tax pro.
2. **Stock promotion / paid influencers** — pre-existing bear case. Crux: promotion ≠ falsehood; what matters is whether the information pushed is false, capital misused, or business is not progressing. Sponsored coverage should be discounted vs independent third-party.
3. **Dilution / financing / cash raises** — also pre-existing. The real question is what the cash buys (production, manufacturing readiness, customer programs). 2026 is the line in the sand for commercial conversion.
4. **Serial pivots / missed promises / moving goalposts** — backward-looking for Crux. Owns POET on the *current* optical-interposer + AI-connectivity opportunity, not the decade-old pitch. Would reassess if that current opportunity changed.
5. **Value chain / hyperscalers / what POET actually is** — Crux finds this "strangest" part of the report. POET is a component/platform company, not a direct-to-Nvidia or finished-system shop. That's obvious to anyone who's spent time on the stock.

Crux's takeaway: **none of these claims are novel except PFIC**, and PFIC has now been addressed structurally (QEF + redomicile). The real question remains *can POET convert cash, partnerships, product work, and positioning into real commercial traction?*

**The PFIC issue was real enough that POET had to address it, but management just made it much more manageable for shareholders.**

More specifically, the announcement says three things.

Second, because POET reported a **net loss for 2025**, the company says a timely QEF election for that year is **not expected to create current income inclusions for 2025** and is expected to result in **no negative U.S. federal income tax consequences** for U.S. shareholders who continued to hold their shares. Third, POET says its board has approved the intention to **redomicile to the U.S.**, which would eliminate the possibility of the company being classified as a PFIC in future years once that happens. If shareholder approval is required, the matter is expected to go to the June 26, 2026 meeting.

---

Hey everybody, quick update on the short report that came out today.

If you haven't read it, and you want to, here's the link:

[Short Report](https://www.wolfpackresearch.com/items/we-believe-poet-is-a-obvious-stock-promote%2C-has-created-an-irs-nightmare%3A-us-holders-have-until-april-15th-to-act)

These reports can absolutely move price in the short term, and that is part of the point. Whoever publishes them usually has a financial incentive for the stock to go down, just like longs have an incentive for the stock to go up. So I always think it makes sense to read these with a clear head, separate the claims out, and deal with them one by one.

It's also important to understand that the author here is a generalist. They are not an industry expert in optics. Their shorts cover a vast array of industries. I believe that is quite important. This is not an industry that is very easy to understand if you do not spend time here often.

For me, this report breaks down into five buckets.

**1) PFIC / tax / IRS**

This is the one bucket I actually think is worth investors looking into for themselves.

I am not a tax expert, so I am not going to give tax advice here. If you think this issue is relevant to you, especially if you hold in a taxable U.S. account, then you should do your own work on it and talk to a tax professional if needed.

What I do think is worth pointing out is what the company has and has not said.

In POET's 2025 20-F, the company says it believes it was not a PFIC for the year ended December 31, 2024 and does not expect to be classified as a PFIC for the year ending December 31, 2025. At the same time, it also says PFIC status can only be determined at the end of each taxable year and depends on factors such as the composition of its income, the value and nature of its assets, and its market capitalization, so there can be no assurance it will not be classified as a PFIC in the current or any future tax year.

In POET's January 2026 prospectus supplement, the company uses more cautious language, stating that it had not made any determination regarding its PFIC status for the year ended December 31, 2025, the current taxable year, or any other year. That filing also says PFIC status can only be determined after year-end, that there can be no assurance the company will not be a PFIC for 2025 or future years, and that neither the company nor its U.S. counsel expresses any opinion regarding its PFIC status or expectations. The same filing also states that a U.S. holder of a PFIC is required to file IRS Form 8621 and should consult a tax advisor.

So I think the fair takeaway is that this is a real tax/compliance topic that investors should look into for themselves, but I am not going to sit here and act like I am qualified to tell everyone exactly how it applies to their personal situation.

Personally, this is not a thesis breaker for me. If I believe in the stock over the long term, a filing headache or a more complicated tax situation does not automatically make me walk away. But that is my view, and everyone should make that call for themselves and consult a tax professional if desired.

---

**2) Stock promotion / paid influencers**

This has been used as a bear case for a while, and I have talked about it before.

My view is pretty simple. There is a difference between promotion and falsehood.

If a company is paying for visibility, trying to get in front of more investors, and trying to improve access to capital markets, I do not automatically have an issue with that. Small companies do this all the time. What would matter to me is whether the information being pushed is false, whether the capital being raised is being misused, or whether the business underneath it is not moving forward.

That is the real line for me.

The same goes for paid research coverage. I have already said before that when coverage is sponsored, it should be read with that in mind. It is useful, but it is not independent in the same way fully third-party coverage would be.

So no, I do not think "they tried to get visibility" is some devastating bear case on its own. I would care if the underlying business was not progressing. Based on what I have seen from partnerships, product activity, and what management has discussed publicly, I of course do not think that is the right conclusion today.

---

**3) Dilution / financing / cash raises**

This is another one I have been very clear about in the past.

Of course I would prefer less dilution. Every shareholder would.

But again, the real question is what the money is being used for.

If the capital is being used to move the business forward, support production, fund development, expand manufacturing readiness, support customer programs, or strengthen the company strategically, then I can live with dilution. If the company keeps raising and there is no commercial progress to match it, that is when I would start to care a lot more. 2026 is a big line in the sand for me ehre.

So yes, dilution is real. The better question is whether management turns that cash into actual commercial progress. That is what matters from here.

---

**4) Serial pivots / missed promises / moving goalposts**

A lot of people have brought this up over time.

Personally, I do not care much about what POET was trying to do ten years ago. I care about what it is trying to do now, why the opportunity is attractive now, and whether it can execute now.

If a company sees a better application for its technology and pivots toward it, I do not view that as bearish. In a lot of cases, I would rather see management move toward the biggest opportunity than stay stuck chasing something that is clearly less important.

If I had owned stock in this company for a decade, maybe I would feel differently. I understand why some legacy holders would be frustrated by past delays or changing narratives. But that is not how I am looking at it. I own it because of the current optical interposer and AI connectivity opportunity. If that opportunity changed, I would reassess.

So for me, this bucket is mostly backward-looking. I am focused much more on execution from here.

---

**5) Value chain / hyperscalers / what POET actually is**

This was honestly one of the stranger parts of the report to me.

Yes, POET is a component and platform company. It is not just "selling directly to Nvidia" or dropping finished systems straight into hyperscalers. That is obvious to anyone who has spent real time understanding the stock.

That is not some hidden bear case. That is just describing where the company sits in the stack.

---

So overall, my view is pretty straightforward.

The one part of this report I think investors should actually examine for themselves is the PFIC/tax issue.

The rest of it mostly comes down to questions we already knew mattered: promotion, dilution, commercialization, execution, and where POET fits in the value chain.

For me, none of that changes the core question.

Can POET convert its cash, partnerships, product work, and industry positioning into real commercial traction?

That is still the question that matters most, and that is what I will keep watching.

At the end of the day, so much of this is just your own personal view on these factors. If they concern you, then please address them as you see fit. None of what was said here is novel or new aside from the PFIC/tax issue.
