### The Thesis

Every AI system runs on data. If the data layer underneath is slow, fragmented, poorly organized, or hard to access, the AI system is going to underperform no matter how good the model is.

Everpure, formerly known as Pure Storage, ticker $P, sits in that data layer. They help enterprises and hyperscalers store, access, protect, manage, and govern large amounts of data.

AI changes how important that layer is.

---

### Why Storage Suddenly Matters More

Think about what actually has to happen when a company deploys AI internally.

Say a company builds an internal AI assistant. Employees ask it questions. It searches through contracts, customer records, support tickets, internal policies, product documentation, financial data. It tries to give a useful answer.

For that to work, all of that data has to be somewhere accessible. It has to be fast to retrieve. It has to be organized well enough that the AI can actually use it. And it has to be governed, meaning the AI should be able to access what it is supposed to access and nothing more.

If the data is scattered across old systems, duplicated in three places, slow to retrieve, or sitting in infrastructure that was never designed for this kind of workload, the AI assistant is going to be slow, unreliable, or just wrong.

That is the problem Everpure helps solve.

---

### What They Do

Everpure makes all-flash storage systems. Flash storage is the same technology inside the SSD in your laptop, just built for enterprise scale with a lot of software, reliability, and management tools layered on top.

So the idea is that you take NAND flash memory, build enterprise-grade hardware around it, and then wrap it in software that handles performance, data protection, compression, access controls, upgrades, and management.

NAND is just the raw ingredient. The value Everpure adds is the full system that makes that storage actually useful at enterprise and hyperscale levels.

For this thesis, the products that matter most are:

**FlashBlade** handles unstructured data, meaning all the messy stuff that does not live in a clean database. Documents, logs, images, contracts, code, support tickets. That is the product most relevant to AI workloads because most enterprise AI has to deal with this kind of data.

**FlashBlade//EXA** is built specifically for AI and high-performance computing. The idea is that we know GPU clusters are expensive and if they are waiting on storage to deliver data, you are wasting compute. EXA is designed to feed those clusters fast enough that the compute stays busy.

**Fusion** is the management layer. Instead of managing storage system by system, Fusion lets a customer apply policies and automate across all their storage environments at once. As companies scale AI across more infrastructure, having a central control layer becomes more useful.

**Evergreen** is their subscription and lifecycle model. Traditional enterprise storage has always involved painful upgrade cycles where you eventually have to rip out old equipment and replace it. Evergreen is designed to let customers upgrade over time without that disruption. That means more recurring revenue and longer customer relationships.

They also announced a definitive agreement to acquire a company called 1touch, which would add the ability to help customers discover, classify, and govern their data. That means helping customers understand what data they have, where it lives, who can access it, and how it is supposed to be protected. That becomes much more important when AI agents are allowed to interact with internal systems and enterprise data.

The way I think about the customer motion is that Everpure gets in the door by solving hard storage problems: performance, reliability, uptime, painful upgrades, and faster data access. But once they are close to the customer's mission-critical data, the opportunity expands. Storage performance is the first sale and data management is the platform expansion.

In their last call management said more customers are having enterprise-scale conversations about Everpure becoming a strategic storage partner across a broader part of their infrastructure. Deals over $5 million grew 80% year over year in Q4. That suggests the company is moving beyond one-off storage wins and into larger platform relationships. That is a meaningfully different kind of business.

### The Agentic AI Part

This is the part that made me really interested.

Right now most enterprise AI is still pretty passive. You ask it something, it answers. Great.

But the direction the industry is actively moving is toward agents. Agents do not just answer questions. They take actions, they can read a document, check a database, update a record, open a ticket, route information between systems, and execute multi-step workflows without someone clicking through each step. That creates a much bigger data problem.

A passive chatbot needs to retrieve some information. An agent needs to interact with data constantly, across multiple systems, in real time, with the right permissions at every step.

The more agents an enterprise deploys, the more pressure that puts on the infrastructure that stores, organizes, and governs their data.

To be clear Everpure is not building the agents, it's obviously not selling the CPUs that run them. But it sits near the layer that all of that activity depends on.

More agentic AI means more data retrieval, more orchestration, more governance requirements. That is the chain that eventually points back toward a company like this.

---

### The Hyperscale Part, And Why It Changes The Economics

Everpure has one confirmed design win with a major hyperscaler and is in active testing with several more of them.

What makes this interesting is not only the size of the opportunity, it is also the business model.

In Everpure's normal enterprise business, when they sell a storage system, they buy the NAND flash inside it. NAND prices go up and down. When NAND gets expensive, their margins can get squeezed.

In the hyperscaler model, the hyperscaler sources the NAND themselves. Everpure provides the architecture, software, controllers, and integration layer around it.

So the hyperscaler buys the raw materials and Everpure provides the engineering and design that makes those materials into something actually useful at scale.

Management says that model could produce gross margins of 75% to 85% on the hyperscale business. That is above the company's typical product gross-margin range and would be accretive to the overall margin profile. And it removes a big chunk of the commodity risk.

So Everpure potentially gets exposure to AI data-center infrastructure without carrying the full NAND commodity burden. That is the part I think the market has may be under appreciating.

### They're Already In Business

One thing I like about this one compared to a lot of AI-adjacent ideas is that there is already a real business underneath the thesis.

Fiscal 2026 revenue was $3.66 billion, up 16% year over year. Q4 was their first quarter above $1 billion in revenue. Subscription annual recurring revenue hit $1.92 billion, up 16%. Remaining performance obligations (RPOs), which is basically contracted future revenue, reached $3.67 billion, up 40%. Free cash flow was $616 million. And the company guided fiscal 2027 revenue to $4.3 billion to $4.4 billion.

Over 14,500 customers. Around 64% penetration of the Fortune 500. A net promoter score of 84, which is genuinely high for enterprise infrastructure.

This is a profitable, growing business where the AI layer is an added dimension on top of something already working.

### The Market Is Moving In This Direction

I would never treat third-party market estimates as a direct revenue map for one company but they are useful for understanding whether the wind is at your back or in your face.

Grand View Research estimates the AI-powered storage market can grow from roughly $31 billion in 2024 to $118 billion by 2030, a roughly 26% annual growth rate. It also estimates AI data management can grow from roughly $26 billion in 2023 to $104 billion by 2030, a roughly 23% annual growth rate.

If Everpure was to directly be placed into one of these, it fits most cleanly into AI-powered storage today. The AI data-management angle is more of a developing adjacency through Fusion and the pending 1touch deal. But the direction is clear in that AI is making storage and data management more strategic, and the market is expanding to reflect that.

### The Concerns

NAND prices are a near-term headwind. The company already flagged that Q1 product gross margins are expected to be at the lower end of their normal range because component costs moved up faster than customer pricing caught up. That should recover, but it is a watch item.

Hyperscale is still mostly promise. One design win and active testing is encouraging. Multiple production deployments is what actually matters.

And EXA, the AI storage product, has had a strong early proof point but needs to keep converting. One customer test-buy-reorder cycle is a good sign. Dozens more is what makes it a real business.

### Why I Bought

I am buying it because I think AI is going to make the data layer underneath it more strategic than the market currently prices in. And I wanted to own that idea in a company that already has a profitable base business, real customers, real recurring revenue, and multiple ways for the AI thesis to show up in the numbers.

I started small. I may add gradually if proof points arrive. If hyperscale converts, if EXA keeps winning, if margins recover, if the 1touch deal closes and adds real product capability, then the position deserves to grow.
