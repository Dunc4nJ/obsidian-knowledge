# VICR powers Cerebras wafer-scale engines and is set up for visibility as CBRS IPO surfaces founder-led $1.5b fab with 20pct CEO stake

Joe ([@joedab12](https://x.com/joedab12)) front-runs the [[Cerebras (CBRS)]] IPO with a [[Vicor (VICR)]] thesis: Vicor's vertical power delivery (VPD) is a key component making Cerebras wafer-scale engines work, and the IPO will draw new eyes to a rare founder-led ($5b+ market cap, 79-year-old CEO at 20%+ stake) name with a defensible IP moat, ramping royalty ARR, and capacity-constrained product revenue.

## Key Takeaways

- **VPD is structural to wafer-scale compute, not a Cerebras-only story.** Vicor's vertical power delivery is described as a "key component" of [[Cerebras (CBRS)]] wafer-scale engines — but Cerebras is *not* what fills the $1.5b fab. A hyperscaler and OEM (Joe's "high probability" guess: [[Alphabet (GOOGL)]] and [[Advanced Micro Devices (AMD)]]) are also ramping VPD, and [[Nvidia (NVDA)]] is implied as the likely third-party licensee. The thesis: VPD is becoming the default architecture for high-density AI compute power delivery, and Vicor sits on the IP.

- **Founder-led with unusually high insider alignment.** The CEO (Patrizio Vinciarelli) is 79 and owns over 20% of shares outstanding. Joe claims he can't find another >$5b market-cap name with a founder-CEO stake that large — flagged as a structural quality signal alongside the operational thesis.

- **$90m royalty ARR is the IP moat in financial form.** After winning "a huge judgement" against large companies last year, Vicor is now collecting $90m in royalty ARR and is on track to roughly double that in 18 months. This is recurring high-margin revenue layered on top of the product business — and creates the pricing umbrella that supports the licensing deal pipeline.

- **Capacity is sold out: fab1 at $1.5b full-capacity by year-end, fab2 doesn't open until 2028, license deal bridges the gap.** Fab1 just expanded to $1.5b annual revenue capacity and will hit full utilization by year-end FY26. Fab2 (target 2028) is being built to handle excess demand, but the two-year gap means Vicor is structuring a third-party license deal with someone who has spare manufacturing capacity — likely [[Nvidia (NVDA)]] per Joe's read. A license deal would convert capacity constraint into royalty stream years before fab2 opens.

- **$500-700 share price target benchmarks 2027 numbers against [[Monolithic Power (MPWR)]].** Joe's price target derives from lining up Vicor's projected 2027 P&L next to MPWR (the closest comp). The implication: Vicor at scale should command an MPWR-style multiple on much higher absolute power-density value-add — the comp is doing the valuation work, not a DCF.
