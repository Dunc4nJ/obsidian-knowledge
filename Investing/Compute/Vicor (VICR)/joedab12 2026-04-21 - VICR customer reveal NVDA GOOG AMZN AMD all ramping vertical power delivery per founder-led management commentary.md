# joedab12 2026-04-21 — VICR customer reveal: NVDA, GOOG, AMZN, AMD all ramping vertical power delivery

## Key Takeaways

- **Founder-CEO commentary in the Q1 2026 press release reframes [[Vicor (VICR)]] from a small-cap power module vendor into a multi-hyperscaler-pull story.** Joe's read of "the big customers like [[Nvidia (NVDA)]] [[Alphabet (GOOGL)]] [[Amazon (AMZN)]] [[Advanced Micro Devices (AMD)]] are coming" maps directly onto Vinciarelli's "OEMs and Hyperscalers" language — the second CHiP fab and an alternate-source 2nd-gen VPD line are being built explicitly so those names can get **redundant** supply access. Redundancy language is the tell: hyperscalers do not ask for second-sourcing unless they are sizing the spend.
- **70 pct sequential backlog growth into Q1 is the quantitative leg of the thesis** — that is a step-function, not a trend-line, and it is paired with management's explicit "setting the stage for revenue growth" framing. The backlog is now the leading indicator that the AI-compute power-density ramp is finally landing on Vicor's order book after a multi-year wait.
- **The IP-litigation overhang is converting from risk to moat.** Vinciarelli's "Precluding unlawful importation of computing systems infringing Vicor IP is having an effect" + "the need for a license to avoid disruption of supply from copycat power system manufacturers" frames the ITC actions as actively forcing OEMs to pay-or-design-around. That is the founder-led posture you want from a small IP-rich incumbent facing an industry-wide demand pull.
- **Capacity is the binding constraint, not demand.** A second fab plus an alternate-source 2nd-gen VPD line is a multi-year capex commit that gates upside — bull case is execution-on-capacity, bear case is delays gift share to copycats while ITC drags. The fab footprint is the variable to watch from here.
