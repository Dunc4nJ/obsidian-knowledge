# joedab12 2026-04-21 - VICR fab1 capacity expansion uses nearby satellite facility for simpler steps lifting capacity 50pct ahead of fab2 and third-party license deal

## Key Takeaways

- [[Vicor (VICR)]] uncorked a third capacity lever that was not in any sell-side model: shifting the **simpler production steps** of its power-module flow to a nearby satellite facility, which lifts effective fab1 throughput by **50pct**. This is incremental capex-light expansion against the existing fab1 footprint — not a new clean-room build.
- Critically, this is **distinct from "fab2"** (still under construction, targeted for 2028) **and distinct from the third-party manufacturing/license deal** (still being negotiated). Three independent capacity initiatives running in parallel — each can ship volume on its own timeline, and the upside cases stack rather than substitute.
- The fab1 50pct lift implies the company is willing to fragment process flow across geographies to chase demand, which is unusual for a power-module manufacturer where IP-heavy back-end steps usually stay co-located. The simpler steps (likely package assembly, test, or lower-IP front-end work) moving offsite signals management has identified the back-end yield-critical chokepoints and is parallelizing everything else.
- Language is operationally specific ("patchwork solution") and demand-side definitive ("insatiable") — author added to a largest position on the disclosure. Reads as a confirmation that the demand picture has tightened materially since the last earnings update, not as soft guidance.
- Investment implication: any model that treats fab2 (2028) as the binary capacity catalyst is mismeasuring near-term revenue trajectory. Fab1 + satellite = near-term step-function, third-party license = mid-term option, fab2 = long-term ramp. All three contribute to the $1.5b annual run-rate path that anchors the [[Vicor (VICR)]] thesis.
