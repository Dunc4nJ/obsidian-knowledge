# joedab12 2026-05-12 - VICR vs MPWR comp at 14.3b vs 82b market cap implies $600 fair value on fab1 alone with $800-1000 path via fab2 plus third-party license

## Key Takeaways

- **Direct mcap-to-2027-revenue comp says VICR is mispriced even on fab1 alone.** $VICR at $14.3B mcap vs [[Monolithic Power (MPWR)]] at $82B, with 2027 revenue of $1.6B vs $4.5B respectively — proportional valuation puts VICR at 35% of MPWR mcap = $29B = >$600/share. That ignores fab2 and any licensing optionality.
- **The bull case is structural, not just multiple expansion.** Joe stacks four hard-to-replicate advantages MPWR doesn't have: (a) owned fab with zero debt, (b) 100% US-made power modules vs MPWR's fully Chinese/Korean/Taiwanese outsourced supply chain, (c) ~$100-120m of 100% gross margin recurring royalty revenue layered on top of the $1.5B product line by 2027, (d) a second fab coming in 2028 with no announced demand cap.
- **Fab1 customer roster confirms the AI compute thesis.** The 1.5B annual capacity is being filled by [[Cerebras (CBRS)]], one hyperscaler, and one OEM — Joe's high-conviction read is [[Alphabet (GOOGL)]] and [[Advanced Micro Devices (AMD)]] (cross-references his earlier posts). Fab1 ramp completes by year-end.
- **The third-party licensing deal is the path from $600 to $800-1000.** Demand exceeds what fab1+fab2 can absorb before 2028, so Vicor is putting a manufacturing/licensing arrangement in place with an external partner (Joe floats Delta as a plausible counterparty). The embedded source quote confirms Vicor management's own language: "enabling an alternate source of high current density 2nd Gen VPD modules to give licensed OEMs and Hyper-scalers access to best-in-class power system technology from more than one source."
- **Position signal — author is not selling.** Despite the move, Joe explicitly states he hasn't sold a single share and won't for a long time; the math is the answer to "is it too late?"
