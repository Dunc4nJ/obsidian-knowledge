# Jim Cramer to discuss VICR on Mad Money as GOOG and AMD ramp while NVDA awaits third-party license deal with demand exceeding capacity

## Key Takeaways

- **Cramer mention as sentiment catalyst, not thesis driver** — Joe pre-empts a Jim Cramer segment on [[Vicor (VICR)]] by separating headline-driven price action from the underlying ramp. The "Cramer curse" framing concedes the segment may trigger a near-term pullback, but Joe treats any such dip as an add point — i.e. the thesis isn't the segment, the thesis is what's happening at the hyperscaler customers.

- **GOOG and AMD already ramping; NVDA gated on a third-party license deal** — Joe asserts [[Alphabet (GOOGL)]] and [[Advanced Micro Devices (AMD)]] are in ramp on VICR's power-delivery architecture today, while [[Nvidia (NVDA)]] adoption is conditional on closing a third-party license agreement (consistent with the Factorized Power Architecture IP licensing path VICR has signaled). This frames NVDA as the next leg of demand, not the foundation of current revenue.

- **Demand-versus-capacity gap is "clear"** — The closing line ("they have way more demand than capacity") is the operative claim. If true, near-term prints are supply-constrained rather than demand-constrained, which inverts the usual cyclical-semis risk: the binding question becomes fab2 ramp pace and any incremental license/royalty stream, not order-book softness.

- **High-conviction-long posture, indifferent to short-term tape** — In the reply thread Joe explicitly states he is "a long term thinker on my highest conviction plays like $VICR" and doesn't try to time short-term dips. Useful as a stylistic marker — this is a holder reacting to a media event, not a trader positioning around it.
