# MPWR Q1 silence on vertical power delivery vs Q4 hype confirms VICR is winning the VPD market and taking share at hyperscalers

## Key Takeaways

- **MPWR's Q4-to-Q1 narrative shift is the loudest tell**: [[Monolithic Power (MPWR)]] talked up vertical power delivery (VPD) extensively on the Q4 call, then said *absolutely nothing* about VPD on the Q1 call. In a market where MPWR was positioning itself as a credible challenger to VICR's incumbent VPD franchise, going dark within one quarter is the kind of silence that only makes sense if the design-win bake-offs at hyperscalers and accelerator OEMs went against them.
- **VICR's customer meeting cadence pre-dates and explains the capacity bump**: $VICR met "very recently" with **both** AI accelerator OEMs (AMD + NVDA) AND multiple hyperscalers (GOOG, AMZN, MSFT, META — all developing custom AI silicon) to demo VPG gen 2 with 5th-gen multiplication. The Andover MA fab1 expansion from $1B to $1.5B annual capacity was announced shortly after and explicitly attributed to those meetings — translating commercial interest into committed silicon spend.
- **Fab1 customer slate is already accounted for — and excludes the largest one**: Fab1 is being filled by **GOOG + AMD + Cerebras** (Cerebras IPO pending). NVDA is structurally too large for fab1 alone — a previously disclosed "1 customer who can fill 2 fabs on their own" is being routed through a **third-party license deal** because internal supply can't cover them. The math means VICR is now sourcing AI-accelerator power for the top-3 merchant accelerator vendors (NVDA, AMD, plus the hyperscaler ASICs) without a credible second-source threat from MPWR.
- **The implication for the VPD TAM split**: if MPWR has effectively conceded the leading-edge VPD slots at this generation of hyperscaler/accelerator builds, the next several quarters of VICR's revenue trajectory should reflect a step-function in book-to-bill driven by fab1 fill + the third-party license royalty stream — and MPWR's analog power-management story re-anchors to its legacy enterprise/automotive/industrial businesses rather than the AI-accelerator power TAM it pitched in Q4.
