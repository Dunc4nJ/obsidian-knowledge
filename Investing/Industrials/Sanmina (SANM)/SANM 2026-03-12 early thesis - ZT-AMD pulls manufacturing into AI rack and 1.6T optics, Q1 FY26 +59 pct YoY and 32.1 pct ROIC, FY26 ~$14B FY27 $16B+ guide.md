## Original Content

*Verbatim from <https://cruxcapitalgroup.substack.com/p/sanmina-sanm-an-early-thesis>*

I spend a lot of time looking for companies the market still sees one way, even as the business is starting to change underneath the surface.

Sanmina is starting to look like one of those names.

It's easy to see Sanmina as a manufacturing company. And that is fair. The company helps customers build complex hardware across communications, cloud, defense, industrial, and other markets.

But what is getting more interesting is where that manufacturing exposure is shifting.

Sanmina is becoming more tied to the physical buildout of AI infrastructure.

That includes AI racks, systems integration, optical hardware, liquid cooling, communications gear, and now even power equipment.

That mix shift is the reason I am paying attention, especially the optics angle which we will get to.

The biggest catalyst here is the ZT Systems deal.

That acquisition pushed Sanmina much deeper into hyperscaler and AI data center infrastructure. It gave the company more exposure to the physical layer of AI deployment like the racks, the systems, the cooling, the networking, and the broader hardware stack needed to actually stand these clusters up.

Management has said the business has a very good chance of reaching roughly $14 billion of revenue in 2026 and that AI opportunities are on track to deliver $16+ billion in calendar 2027 (currently ~$7B mkt cap)

That is a very different growth profile than what most people probably still associate with Sanmina.

The other thing that pulled me in is the optics angle as you would guess.

Sanmina has explicitly said it is growing and expanding its optical advanced packaging business. Management talked about high-performance network systems across 400G, 800G, and 1.6T, and said they are starting to ship 1.6T.

To be clear, Sanmina doesn't suddenly becomes an optics pure play, but it gives the company real exposure to the network plumbing of AI infrastructure.

The way I think about it is this. Sanmina has optical exposure in the sense that it participates in packaging, integration, and manufacturing around optical systems. A company like Fabrinet is much more directly centered on optical manufacturing. Sanmina is broader. But if Sanmina starts winning a bigger share of higher-value optical and communications content, that can matter a lot for how the market values the business (potential re-rate).

There are a few other pieces here that make the setup more interesting.

Sanmina has an explicit AMD relationship through the ZT transaction, where it became a preferred U.S.-based manufacturing partner for AMD cloud rack and cluster-scale AI solutions.

It also has ecosystem relevance in photonics through OpenLight, which has identified Sanmina as one of its packaging partners for laser-integrated silicon photonics.

On the cooling side, Sanmina has a partnership with DeepCoolAI around liquid cooling and high-density AI deployments.

And on the power side, the company is building out a Houston transformer facility and working with Končar on medium-voltage transformer design.

That starts to paint a much bigger picture.

This is a company that is getting pulled into several of the most important physical bottlenecks in AI infrastructure like compute deployment, networking, cooling, and power.

That is all really interesting.

The financial profile also helps.

In Q1 FY26, Sanmina reported $3.19 billion of revenue, up 59% year over year, with 6.0% non-GAAP operating margin and $179 million of operating cash flow.

That 6% margin may not sound exciting if you are used to software or semis.

But for a manufacturing and systems integration business, it is pretty solid.

More importantly, the company is growing quickly while still holding margins and generating cash. That is what gives the story credibility.

The ROIC number stood out to me too. Sanmina reported 32.1% non-GAAP pre-tax ROIC in Q1, up from 23.5% a year ago.

That is a strong signal that this is not just top-line growth being bought at any cost. There is real return discipline underneath it.

There is also a supply chain angle that I think could matter more over time, and we talk about this in our on-shoring plays.

Sanmina has a meaningful U.S. and Mexico footprint, plus defense exposure and domestic infrastructure relevance. In a world where hyperscalers and governments care more about manufacturing resilience and geographic diversification, that can become more valuable.

So TDLR;

Sanmina still wears the label of a legacy manufacturer.

But the business is becoming more important to the actual buildout of AI infrastructure.

That does not automatically mean the stock deserves a huge premium tomorrow.

It does mean the market may eventually need to look at the company through a different lens if this mix shift keeps building. Need to watch how the product mix progresses and where revenue accrues.

This is just my initial thesis and it gets placed on my 'one to watch' list with an initial tracker position.
