# SANM 2026-Q2 earnings: $4.01B revenue 2x guide, ZT $1.88B on AMD accelerated compute, mix 69 pct Cloud and AI, FY27 $16B+ guide

[[Sanmina (SANM)]] reported a Q2 FY26 blowout that Crux argues moves the re-rate thesis from "potential" to "active execution story." Revenue of $4.01B more than doubled YoY and beat guidance of $3.1-3.4B; non-GAAP EPS of $3.16 vs $2.25-2.55 guide. The ZT Systems segment delivered $1.88B, with management confirming the upside was new platform business — specifically rack-scale AI systems built on [[Advanced Micro Devices (AMD)]] accelerated compute technology — not legacy ZT revenue rolling through. Optics exposure was confirmed across 400G/800G/1.6T including shipments of 1.6T switches with 1.6T pluggables in development (a [[Fabrinet (FN)]]-style manufacturing role, not optical component ownership). The Communications Networks + Cloud/AI Infrastructure bucket reached $2.77B (69% of revenue, up from 62% in Q1). FY26 guide formalized at $13.7-14.3B revenue / 6.3-6.6% margin / $10.75-11.35 EPS, with FY27 confidence "increasingly" at $16B+ — bridge implies core Sanmina ~$10B + ZT $6-7B. Author position: long, building from ~3% toward 5% allocation.

#### Overview

Sanmina delivered the first real proof point that the re-rate thesis might just work.

This was a beat across every line that we care about, while the call directly confirmed the exact parts of the thesis I cared about most which was the ZT, accelerated compute, optics, power, and the FY27 revenue bridge. A one-quarter beat is nice but a one-quarter beat that confirms the direction of the business is something else entirely.

The numbers:

* **Revenue: $4.01B** vs guidance of $3.1B to $3.4B
* **Non-GAAP EPS: $3.16** vs guidance of $2.25 to $2.55
* **Non-GAAP operating margin: 6.4%** vs guidance of 5.7% to 6.2%
* **Operating cash flow: $399M**
* **Free cash flow: $342M**
* **Non-GAAP pre-tax ROIC: 34.7%**

Revenue more than doubled year over year. Margins expanded. Cash flow was excellent. For a manufacturing and systems integration business, that is the exact combination worth paying attention to. The margin percentage by itself misses the point. Sanmina is scaling revenue, holding 6%+ operating margins, generating cash, and earning strong returns on capital.

---

#### ZT

The biggest piece of the thesis was always ZT, and it delivered above plan.

ZT revenue came in at $1.88B, driven by strong execution and customer demand, with some accelerated compute shipments originally expected in the second half pulling into Q2. This is important to understand. A customer wanted product earlier than scheduled. Sanmina secured the components, built the systems, and shipped them. That tells me customers trust Sanmina to deliver when timing gets pulled forward.

It also explains why Q3 ZT revenue is guided lower at $1.0B to $1.2B. I would avoid overreacting to that step-down. ZT is in a transition year and this business will be nonlinear while customer schedules move around. The right way to judge ZT is the annual contribution and the FY27 production schedule, not a clean Q2-to-Q3 line. Management still expects ZT to finish FY26 well within the original $5B to $6B annualized range.

That is what I care about.

---

#### The Accelerated Compute

One of the most important questions heading into the call was whether the ZT upside came from future-facing business or old ZT revenue rolling through.

Management answered it directly. Q2 accelerated compute strength was new platform business.

In this context, accelerated compute means rack-scale AI systems built around GPUs and similar AI accelerators, the systems hyperscalers use for training and inference at scale. That is exactly why AMD wanted ZT. AMD's AI opportunity is increasingly about delivering full rack-scale systems, and ZT brought the systems expertise while Sanmina now owns the manufacturing side.

The call also clarified that the accelerated compute strength was based on AMD technology. That makes the AMD/ZT/Sanmina thesis considerably cleaner.

---

#### What the Quarter Actually Confirmed

Three parts of the original thesis moved from thesis to evidence this quarter.

On **optics**, the Q2 deck listed optical systems and pluggables at 400G, 800G, and 1.6T. Management then added on the call that Sanmina is shipping 1.6T switches, with 1.6T pluggables in development. They also said Sanmina builds some of the most advanced switches for its partners. This is a manufacturing and integration function in the optical supply chain, closer to a Fabrinet-style role than optical component ownership. But the exposure is real and it is now confirmed.

*Sanmina Q2 FY26 end-market mix: Communications Networks + Cloud & AI Infrastructure = $2,771M (up from $733M Q2 FY25); Industrial & Energy/Medical/Defense & Aerospace/Auto = $1,242M. Total $4,013M. Comms/Cloud-AI now 69% of revenue.*
![[cruxcapitalgroup-sanm-earnings-update-005.png]]

On **CPU and inference infrastructure**, Slide 17 explicitly listed accelerated compute (GPU), general purpose compute (CPU), and storage under Cloud and AI Infrastructure. Inference demand creates more physical system volume. Sanmina helps turn that volume into deployable infrastructure. The language is now in the company's own materials.

*Sanmina Communications Networks / Cloud & AI Infrastructure slide: High Performance Comms Networks (IP switching & routing; optical systems & pluggables 400G/800G/1.6T; broadband access; 5G); Cloud & AI Infrastructure (accelerated compute GPU; general purpose compute CPU; storage). Key takeaway: "AI is driving growth for the entire end-market."*
![[cruxcapitalgroup-sanm-earnings-update-006.png]]

On **mix**, Communications Networks and Cloud and AI Infrastructure reached $2.77B in Q2, or roughly 69% of revenue, up from 62% in Q1. Core Sanmina contributed $891M to that segment, up 22% year over year, while ZT delivered $1.88B. The AI and cloud networking bucket is becoming the center of the company. That is the shift I was waiting to see show up in reported numbers. The more this segment becomes the company, the harder it is to value Sanmina through the old manufacturing lens.

Book-to-bill was also above 1.1, which supports the idea that demand is still building rather than fading.

#### ZT Integration Risk Is Fading

*ZT Systems phased execution slide: Step 1 (Largely Complete) — integration of ZT Systems, incremental capex in power/liquid cooling/test cell. Step 2 (Current Priority) — won and shipped new accelerated compute, won next-gen accelerated compute with key hyperscale/OEM customers, building/shipping pre-production for next-gen, finalizing customer production schedules. Step 3 (Next Phase) — driving growth/expansion, vertical integration synergies, expanding on existing Sanmina engineering capabilities. "Executing to the Plan."*
![[cruxcapitalgroup-sanm-earnings-update-007.png]]

Sanmina said the first phase of integration is now largely complete, including incremental capital investments in power, liquid cooling, and test cell capacity. The company has already won and shipped new accelerated compute business, won next-generation accelerated compute business with key hyperscale and OEM customers, started building and shipping pre-production systems, and begun finalizing customer production schedules.

That moves the ZT story from integration risk to execution risk. That is a better place to be. The next watch item is production schedules for next-generation accelerated compute locking in. If those schedules firm up, the FY27 case gets considerably stronger.

---

#### Power Is Still 2027, But It Looks More Real

The power angle got more concrete this quarter. The deck highlighted distribution and instrument transformers, switchgear, battery controllers, monitoring equipment, busbars, rectifiers, and power storage and management. Management talked about expanding the energy business from engineering and design into full system integration with vertical integration.

*Sanmina Industrial & Energy slide: Power Generation & Distribution (SOFC equipment, distribution & instrument transformers, switchgear); Semiconductor Capital Equipment (lithography, wafer deposition & handling); Power Storage & Management (battery controllers & enclosures, monitoring, enclosures/busbars/rectifiers); Safety & Surveillance Equipment. Key takeaways: doing well, growth to accelerate H2; expanding energy business for AI data centers; engineering & design → full system with vertical integration.*
![[cruxcapitalgroup-sanm-earnings-update-008.png]]

Power is behind ZT as the current revenue driver. But it is becoming a real strategic leg for 2027, and the timing fits the broader backdrop. Power has become one of the central bottlenecks in AI data center expansion. This also fits the broader policy backdrop, with Washington increasingly pushing AI and data center developers to bring their own power solutions and fund the infrastructure required to support new load growth. Sanmina is positioning itself inside that constraint.

#### The FY27 Case Got Stronger

Before this quarter, the $14B FY26 revenue idea was management commentary. Now it is official guidance.

*Sanmina FY'26 Outlook slide: Revenue $13.7B - $14.3B; Non-GAAP Operating Margin 6.3% - 6.6%; Non-GAAP Diluted EPS $10.75 - $11.35. Core Sanmina expected to grow high single digits. ZT Systems will be well within the $5-6B annualized estimate. Caption: "Positioned to Deliver $16B+ in FY'27."*
![[cruxcapitalgroup-sanm-earnings-update-009.png]]

**FY26 guidance:**

* Revenue: $13.7B to $14.3B
* Non-GAAP operating margin: 6.3% to 6.6%
* Non-GAAP EPS: $10.75 to $11.35

More importantly, management said they are increasingly confident in $16B+ revenue in FY27. Honestly I think they are sandbagging this a bit too based on how they were talking.

The call also gave a rough bridge, core Sanmina moving toward roughly $10B if it continues growing high single digits, with ZT contributing roughly $6B to $7B.

That is the number I care about most. Q2 was a strong quarter. FY27 is the real underwriting point, and after this call it looks more credible than it did before.

---

#### Supply Constraints: Risk And Bull Signal At The Same Time

Management flagged material shortages around memory, custom ASICs, and other components. They also said core Sanmina communications and cloud infrastructure has grown 20%+ year over year for the last six quarters and could have grown more with better component availability.

That is a risk and a demand signal in the same sentence. Some of the revenue ceiling appears to be held back by components rather than weak demand. That makes the shortages frustrating in the near term and somewhat reassuring about the underlying demand picture.

---

#### Balance Sheet And Capital Return

Sanmina generated $399M of operating cash flow and $342M of free cash flow in the quarter. The company ended with $1.58B in cash, $3.7B of liquidity, and 0.56x net leverage. The board authorized an additional $600M for share repurchases, and management said directly that they view the stock as a great long-term investment.

The company is investing for growth and returning capital at the same time. That combination is something I like.

---

#### What Still Needs Watching

Q2 had accelerated compute pull-forward from the second half, which makes the ZT revenue path nonlinear through the rest of the year. Q3 ZT steps down to $1.0B to $1.2B because of that timing shift, and next-generation accelerated compute production schedules are still being finalized. Component shortages around memory and custom ASICs could persist through 2026 and into 2027. Working capital will likely rise as Sanmina rebuilds inventory for future accelerated compute ramps.

The next proof point is production schedule visibility for FY27 converting into real revenue commitments.

#### My Current Position

This quarter materially strengthened the thesis. ZT beat plan on new platform business. Accelerated compute demand showed up in the right place. The AI and cloud mix rose to 69% of revenue. Optics got confirmed across 400G, 800G, and 1.6T. Power remains a real 2027 leg. Margins held above 6%. FY26 guidance is now $13.7B to $14.3B. FY27 confidence increased.

The thesis has moved from potential re-rate candidate to active execution story.

I am holding my position and plan to build gradually, moving from roughly 3% toward a 5% allocation. That puts Sanmina in the middle range of my optics exposed portfolio sizing, below my largest positions at around 12% but above the smaller positions I use to track early ideas or are allocated in higher spec plays. I have no strong view on what the stock does in the short term, which is exactly why I prefer building slowly.

After this call, I am more confident in the long-term setup than I was before it.
