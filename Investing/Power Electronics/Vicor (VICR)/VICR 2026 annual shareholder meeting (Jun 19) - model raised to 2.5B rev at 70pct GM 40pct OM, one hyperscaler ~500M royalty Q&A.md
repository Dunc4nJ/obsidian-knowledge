# VICR 2026 annual shareholder meeting (Jun 19) — model raised to $2.5B rev at 70% GM / 40% OM, one hyperscaler ~$500M royalty Q&A

Full recorded webcast of Vicor's [2026 Annual Meeting of Stockholders](https://vimeo.com/1202920258/b8750092eb), held Friday June 19, 2026 at Goodwin Procter LLP, Boston (1:42:14). Structure: formal business (CFO **Jim Schmitt**) → strategy review (**Phil Davies**) → open Q&A (CEO **Patrizio Vinciarelli**), the last of which is the highest-signal section. The meeting was in Vicor's Q2-2026 quiet period, so no current-quarter financials were discussed. This is the primary source for the raised long-term model and the licensing/royalty commentary.

## Key Takeaways

- **Long-term model stepped up across the board.** Phil Davies opened the strategy review by raising Vicor's aspirational objectives from the ones set three years ago: revenue **$1B → $2.5B**, gross margin **65% → 70%**, operating income **35% → 40%**. He framed this as a *result of progress already made* plus the growing power-module opportunity across the "top 100 customers" in four segments and the IP-licensing practice — not a demand forecast. Asked by Needham's analyst about timing, Vinciarelli declined to guide but said "I see scenarios where that could happen remarkably quickly," enabled by "a combination of our licensing model, partnerships... fabs that could be jointly owned, and our own existing fab and a new fab."

- **IP licensing is the near-100%-margin flywheel — and enforcement is the catalyst.** The model: an AI-processor OEM or hyperscaler licenses specific or *all* Vicor IP, is then free to source infringing products from any supplier, and pays a royalty scaled to the IP licensed, *discounted by* module purchases from Vicor. Established structure to date: the "largest OEM in AI" holds **two** licenses, the "nearest competitor" ([[Monolithic Power (MPWR)]]) **one**, and "one large hyperscaler" **one**. Deals are catalyzed by ITC action — Vicor asserted patents in its first ITC case (exclusion order obtained), then used Customs/Border Patrol follow-up to force a supplier to stop shipping infringing components, "which immediately catalyzed action because... tens if not hundreds of billions worth of revenues suddenly were on the line." Two ITC cases done, a second in progress, "there's going to be more." The most recent licensee negotiated an all-inclusive license in ~3-4 weeks (~5 near-daily calls); its royalty rate this year is *half* of next year's locked-in rate.

- **The $500M-royalty Q&A (the anchor exchange).** Individual investor **Joe Debabne (@joedab12)** — who attended — asked whether the Oct-2025 licensing figure ($90M run-rate with line of sight to ~double, i.e. ~$180M) had accelerated. Vinciarelli: *"the $200, $300 million contribution from licensing is a conservative number... could get a lot higher. Just one hyperscaler. Just one might lead to that number."* Earlier in the same exchange he named [[Alphabet (GOOGL)]] as "the one hyperscaler that took the lead" on first-generation VPD (paying for expensive, hard-to-assemble, less-reliable competitive VPD to power its TPUs). Joe, who asked the question, publicly characterized the answer as a single hyperscaler (Google) potentially worth **~$500M/yr in royalty income** ("crazy numbers, but that's what was said") — the precise $500M figure is Joe's read of "a lot higher than $200-300M... just one hyperscaler," not a number Vinciarelli stated verbatim.

- **VPD is the moat; Vicor argues the industry is "running in the wrong direction."** Vicor's current-multiplier technology (current gain × current density "off the charts") beats VRs/IVRs and the 800V→6V architecture pushed by [[Nvidia (NVDA)]] and the Open Compute Consortium; 48V vertical power delivery with high current gain saves kilowatts on-board, tens of kW per rack, and megawatts at the data-center level vs a 6V intermediate bus (Ohm's law, I²R). [[Cerebras (CBRS)]] (customer since 2017) is the lead customer: gen-2 VPD development is "done" (~9 months later than last year's timeline due to a signal-integrity/grounding fix requiring an isolated current multiplier); current density steps 3→5 A/mm² (a 47% step, targeted Q1-2027, may be pulled forward) and the multiplier steps 40:1 → 56:1. NVIDIA "has not yet deployed a system with VPD" but hit power/GPU-performance constraints for lack of it; [[Advanced Micro Devices (AMD)]] and all OEMs/hyperscalers "are all going to use VPD... an AI system without VPD just doesn't work." VPD is ~15-20% of 2026 revenue, jumping to a "substantially larger" share in 2027.

- **Capacity is the binding constraint; governance uneventful.** Fab 1 (Andover) run-rate goal raised from $1B to **$1.5B** (aided by ~2x revenue-per-panel on gen-2 VPD and selective product mix — Vicor may cede commoditized NBM volume to licensees and keep capacity for single-source customers). A **second fab** is being pursued: "about to make another offer" on a ~350,000 sq ft building with room to add 600-700k sq ft, within a 20-mile radius of Fab 1; separately, second-source manufacturing-partner talks continue (a first candidate deprioritized; a larger company to be met in early July). Formal business: all 11 directors re-elected (≥94.5% of votes cast), advisory say-on-pay passed 99.8%. Vinciarelli controls **79% of total voting power** (26.7% of common stock, 94.1% of Class B), making Vicor a Nasdaq "controlled company."
