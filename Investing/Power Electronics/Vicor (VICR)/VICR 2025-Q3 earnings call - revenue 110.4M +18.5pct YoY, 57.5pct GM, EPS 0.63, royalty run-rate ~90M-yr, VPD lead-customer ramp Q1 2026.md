## Key Takeaways

- **Licensing is becoming the second engine.** Royalty revenue hit $21.7M in Q3 (up 62% YoY), a run-rate of **~$90M/year**, and management expects licensing income to grow **~50% per year** with **line of sight to roughly doubling within a couple of years**. In Q3 one existing licensee signed a **two-year expansion agreement including catch-up payments**. Royalties are structured as recurring, escalating, or fixed — **no upfront license fees** — and, critically, **only power-module patents are being monetized so far; the VPD IP has not yet been asserted or licensed**, implying a large untapped monetization layer. This is the "record licensing rate" CEO Vinciarelli flagged in the release.

- **The ITC exclusion order is the flywheel behind licensing.** Vicor frames a **seven-stage enforcement ladder** (complaints → exclusion order → customs enforcement) where royalty rates are **more attractive to licensees who settle at an earlier stage**. The first exclusion order runs **for the life of the patents** and reaches **any OEM or hyperscaler dependent on infringing power modules / contract manufacturers**. Vinciarelli said Vicor is "pursuing additional actions to curtail access to infringing computing systems," and management expects **additional exclusion orders and additional licensing deals** to follow — i.e., litigation converts into recurring royalties.

- **Vertical Power Delivery (VPD) lead-customer production launches Q1 2026.** Gen 2 VPD delivers up to **5 A/mm² peak current density** in a **1.5mm-thin thermally-adept package**, and 5th-gen ChiP "current multipliers" target **~24x higher current gain than VRs/IVRs**. Vicor says its lead customer has **met target specifications**, with **qualification engagements open with major OEMs and a hyperscaler**, positioning VPD as (per management) the "only solution that can meet their processor requirements." A higher-current Gen 5 ("133% solution") is in development with **initial samples targeted for January**. VPD sits on the critical path of AI accelerator power delivery — competing against multi-phase VRs / integrated voltage regulators like those from [[Monolithic Power (MPWR)]], and aimed at [[Nvidia (NVDA)]]-class and hyperscaler accelerator platforms.

- **Fab 1 (Andover) is a built-out ~$1B ChiP foundry running at record yield but under-absorbed.** Final-test yields reached **98%** on a mass-produced module (a record), with capacity **upwards of 100,000 modules/month** and short cycle times. But **fab utilization stayed low**, so **under-absorption suppressed product gross margin** — the flip side is significant operating leverage as utilization climbs, with management stating that filling the fab alone could push **product revenue past $1 billion/year**. Management also floated a **Fab 2 / capacity-replication** path (fabs that could be built with shared ownership, ~1-year lead time) tied to the licensing model.

- **Bookings soft near-term, guidance withheld.** Q3 book-to-bill was **0.98**, one-year backlog $152.8M (-1.5% QoQ), while shipments to stocking distributors jumped **+39% QoQ / +46% YoY** (channel restock). Management **declined to give quarterly guidance given uncertainty in the timing of additional license deals**, but reiterated **2025 will be a record year for revenue, profitability, and EPS**; Q4 tax rate expected in the **low single digits**.

## Call substance — prepared remarks and Q&A (factual synthesis)

The following is a synthesis of what management disclosed on the live call, organized by the topics of interest. It is written in my own words; figures are the company's.

**Vertical Power Delivery (VPD).** VPD is Vicor's lateral-to-vertical power-delivery architecture that mounts current-multiplier modules directly beneath the processor to cut PDN (power distribution network) losses at very high current. Management described second-generation VPD reaching **up to 5 A/mm² peak current density** in a **1.5mm-thin package**, and fifth-generation ChiP current multipliers targeting **~24x the current gain of conventional voltage regulators (VRs) and integrated VRs (IVRs)** — the same "handicapped multi-phase VRs / IVRs" the CEO disparaged in the press release. The **lead customer has met target specs**, with a **production launch targeted for Q1 2026**. Beyond the lead customer, **qualification engagements are open with major OEMs and a hyperscaler**, and management positioned VPD as the only approach that can meet next-gen processor current/thermal requirements. A higher-current Gen 5 variant (referred to as the "133% solution") is in development, with **first samples targeted for January**.

**Licensing / royalty deals.** Royalty run-rate is **~$90M/year** (Q3 royalty revenue $21.7M). Management guided to **~50%/year licensing growth** and **line of sight to doubling within ~2 years**. Deal structures are **recurring, escalating, or fixed royalties with no upfront fee**. In Q3, **one existing licensee expanded its agreement for two years and made catch-up payments**. Importantly, **only power-module patents are currently monetized — VPD-specific IP has not yet been asserted or licensed**, which management framed as future upside. The licensing thesis: as high-density power (NBMs and VPD) sits on the critical path of high-performance computing, **OEMs and hyperscalers must license, renew, or expand scope**.

**Fab 1 capacity.** The first ChiP foundry (Andover) is described as an effectively **~$1B-capacity** facility. **Final-test yields hit 98%** on a high-volume module (a record), enabling **>100,000 units/month** at short cycle times. Utilization remained **low**, driving **under-absorption** that held down product margin; conversely, **filling the fab could take product revenue past $1B/year**, implying large latent margin leverage.

**Fab 2 plans.** Management discussed a **capacity-replication / Fab 2 concept tied to the licensing model** — additional fabs that could be built with **shared ownership**, with a replication **lead time of roughly a year**. This positions licensing + shared-ownership fabs as a capital-efficient way to scale supply for licensees.

**Hyperscaler / OEM customer hints.** No customers were named. Management repeatedly referenced a **lead customer** (VPD, launching Q1 2026) plus **selected engagements comprising a hyperscaler and multiple OEMs**, and acknowledged customers' stated **need for multi-sourcing** — a dynamic Vicor's licensing model can serve (licensed second sources) while still collecting royalties.

**ITC / patent litigation.** The **first ITC exclusion order** is in force **for the life of the patents** and reaches **any OEM or hyperscaler dependent on infringing power products** sourced through infringing contract manufacturers / module makers. Vicor described a **seven-stage enforcement ladder** (from complaints through customs enforcement) with **more attractive royalty rates for earlier settlers**, and signaled **additional exclusion orders and additional licensing deals ahead**. The Q2 2025 results had included a **$45.0M patent-litigation settlement** for prior infringement (booked in Q2, absent in Q3), which is why Q3 revenue and gross margin fell sequentially despite operational strength.

**Bookings / backlog / margins / guidance.** Q3 **book-to-bill 0.98**; one-year backlog **$152.8M** (-1.5% QoQ, +1.5% YoY). Shipments to stocking distributors up **39% QoQ / 46% YoY**. Gross margin **57.5%** (down 780 bps QoQ purely because Q2 carried the settlement; up 840 bps YoY), with **fab under-absorption** cited as the margin drag. Management **withheld quarterly guidance** (license-deal timing uncertainty) but reaffirmed **2025 as a record year for revenue, profitability, and EPS**, with a **low-single-digit Q4 tax rate**.

> Andover, Mass., October 21, 2025 (GLOBE NEWSWIRE) — Vicor Corporation (NASDAQ: VICR) today reported financial results for the third quarter ended September 30, 2025. >
> Product revenues and licensing income for the third quarter ended September 30, 2025 totaled $110.4 million, an 18.5% increase from $93.2 million for the corresponding period a year ago, and a 21.7% sequential decrease from $141.0 million in the second quarter of 2025, which included a $45.0 million patent litigation settlement.

>
> Cash flow from operations totaled $38.5 million for the third quarter, compared to cash flow from operations of $22.6 million for the corresponding period a year ago, and cash flow from operations of $65.2 million in the second quarter of 2025. Capital expenditures for the third quarter totaled $4.0 million, compared to $8.4 million for the corresponding period a year ago and $6.2 million for the second quarter of 2025. Stock repurchases for the third quarter totaled $15.6 million, compared with $17.5 million for the second quarter of 2025. Cash and cash equivalents as of September 30, 2025 increased 7.0% sequentially to approximately $362.4 million compared to approximately $338.5 million as of June 30, 2025. Backlog for the third quarter ended September 30, 2025 totaled $152.8 million, a 1.5% increase from $150.6 million for the corresponding period a year ago, and 1.5% sequential decrease from $155.2 million at the end of the second quarter of 2025.
>
> Commenting on third quarter performance, Chief Executive Officer Dr. Patrizio Vinciarelli stated: "Licensing revenue reached a record rate in Q3 following the litigation settlement for prior infringement recorded in Q2. As high density power systems pioneered by Vicor, including NBMs and VPD, are on the critical path of high performance computing, I expect Vicor's IP licensing practice to grow substantially. OEMs and hyper-scalers need a license, or need to renew their license or expand its scope. Vicor is pursuing additional actions to curtail access to infringing computing systems sourced from infringing contract manufacturers relying on infringing power module makers."
>
> "At the core of Vicor's IP licensing practice, Vicor's high density power module business leverages its first ChiP foundry with its distinct growth opportunity. With foundry processes delivering 98% final test yields for mass produced modules, higher margins await greater absorption of foundry capacity, exceeding $1 Billion per year. The current density of 5th generation ChiPs and 2nd Generation VPD, surpassing handicapped multi-phase VRs, or IVRs and their inefficient power distribution networks, will bring about greater foundry capacity utilization."
