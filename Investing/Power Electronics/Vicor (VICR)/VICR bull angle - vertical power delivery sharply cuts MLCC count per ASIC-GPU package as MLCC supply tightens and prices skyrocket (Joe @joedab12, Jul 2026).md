# VICR bull angle - vertical power delivery sharply cuts MLCC count per ASIC-GPU package as MLCC supply tightens and prices skyrocket

Concentrated [[Vicor (VICR)]] bull Joe (@joedab12) surfaces an MLCC-reduction angle he says he hadn't covered in 300 prior posts: MLCCs (multilayer ceramic capacitors) are a supply constraint right now with skyrocketing prices, and switching an ASIC/GPU package to vertical power delivery (VPD) sharply cuts how many MLCCs that package needs — a second-order tailwind for Vicor's VPD pitch.

## Key Takeaways

- **The bull argument is second-order: VPD reduces the MLCC bill of materials, and MLCCs are themselves a scarce, price-spiking input.** With the [[Vicor (VICR)]] converter's output sitting millimeters from the die bumps, loop inductance collapses and the regulator's effective response reaches higher into the frequency spectrum — so the module absorbs much of the mid-band decoupling job the land-side "MLCC army" used to do. Fewer MLCCs per [[Nvidia (NVDA)]] / [[Advanced Micro Devices (AMD)]] / [[Broadcom (AVGO)]] / [[Alphabet (GOOGL)]] accelerator package means less exposure to a constrained, inflating component.
- **VPD wins on two mechanisms simultaneously: very low output impedance from Vicor's current multipliers, plus integrated capacitance carried on the modules.** Both let the package shed discrete land-side ceramics rather than just relocate them.
- **Fewer MLCCs also improves reliability and effective capacitance, not just cost.** A class II MLCC can lose half or more of its rated capacitance under DC-bias derating at operating voltage; cutting the count removes both derating headaches and failure points per board.
- **The author is separately long Murata ([[Murata (MRAAY)]]) as an MLCC supplier, but far smaller than his VICR position** — he frames the MLCC squeeze as net-bullish for Vicor even though it would also benefit passives makers like Murata and [[Vishay (VSH)]].
- **On the skeptic's "engineers only reach for VPD when the BOM forces it" objection, the price/supply spike is exactly the forcing function** that moves VPD from secondary option to design-in — reinforcing the thesis rather than undercutting it.
