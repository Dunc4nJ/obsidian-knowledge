## The thesis arc

**The moat thesis (March).** Joe's foundational post (Mar 21) is a physics explainer: as AI accelerators draw 600–1,000+A at sub-1V, PDN resistance losses scale with I²R, so the "last inch" between regulator and die is the binding constraint. Vicor's vertical power delivery (VPD) places current multipliers directly under the processor, claiming ~20x lower PDN resistance — "that's Vicor's entire moat." He ties it to [[Cerebras (CBRS)]] (23kW wafer-scale engines, a confirmed VICR customer, later "our lead customer") and repeatedly baits Cerebras CEO Andrew Feldman to explain why they chose Vicor.

**Earnings run-up and the capacity raise (April).** Ahead of the Apr 21 premarket Q1 call, Joe posts a 7-point checklist (Apr 9): gen2 VPD demos, full fab utilization (he spotted 3rd-shift job postings), who's filling Fab 1, a 3rd-party manufacturing/licensing deal (which he says takes the stock to $300+), Fab 2 site selection, ITC progress, and book-to-bill. He "added a ton at 156." The call delivers the scoop: Fab 1 capacity raised from ~$1B to $1.5B via an adjacent building (not Fab 2). His core deduction crystallizes — Cerebras + one hyperscaler + one OEM fill the $1.5B fab; management said one customer "can fill 2 fabs" alone (=[[Nvidia (NVDA)]], ~$2B/yr), so the Fab 1 pair "must be" [[Alphabet (GOOGL)]] and [[Advanced Micro Devices (AMD)]]. He flags the CEO's "patent portfolio is a landmine" quote and adds "sub 260."

**Rotation and valuation framework (May).** Joe repeatedly rotates memory profits into VICR — trimming [[Micron (MU)]] (held from sub-$100 in 2024), selling MXL, MTSI and ONTO, and putting "a big chunk of the MU cash raise" into VICR inside a tax-free IRA. His May 12 [[Monolithic Power (MPWR)]] comp becomes the valuation backbone: MPWR ~$75–82B cap doing ~$3.5–4B at 55% GM with zero IP/licensing, versus VICR ~$12–15B doing $1.5B product + ~$100–120M of 100%-GM royalty at 70% GM, owning its own debt-free US fab — so "Fab 1 alone" justifies ~$600/share and a 2nd-source deal implies $1,000–1,500. He distills it (May 13) to "only 3 things": a smooth Fab 1 ramp to $1.5B by year-end, a 2nd ITC win, and a licensing deal (the signal NVDA is coming). The stock ran to ~$294–316 then pulled back ~50% of the 137→316 move; he'd "buy a shit ton of August calls" at 230 and adds near sub-235 (May 19). The Cerebras IPO is his catalyst for "new eyes."

**Licenses, the ASM, and royalties (late May–June).** May 26: Vicor updates Q2 guidance ($126M → $142M) with additional royalties (Joe reads it as more "admissions of IP theft") and signs an all-inclusive license — an OEM proactively securing "multi-source access to all… innovations pioneered and patented by Vicor" (Jun 7 scoop, earnings-release screenshots). The centerpiece is the Jun 19 annual shareholder meeting (recap Jun 21), where he got 15 minutes with Phil Davies: new long-term goal of $2.5B at 70% GM (up from $1B at 60% a year earlier); the license took 3 weeks and lets the licensee keep buying from known infringers short-term; one hyperscaler + one OEM ramping gen2 VPD next year; a 2nd-source partner being chosen (another candidate "early July"); an offer on a Fab 2 building with room for +650k sq ft (~4x footprint); royalty ARR of $90M (Oct 2025) reaching $180M "sooner" with a $300–400M+ ceiling; and a possible fab partnership with a scale customer (his guess: NVDA). He adds premarket at 337 (Jun 22), citing the CEO's "I see scenarios where this can happen remarkably quickly," and Needham publishes a supportive note.
