> Every AI chip needs to be fed. Behind the $5 trillion GPU market caps and the race to build gigawatt-scale data centers sits a less glamorous but equally critical bottleneck: power delivery.
>
> A single AI server rack now draws 40-100kW, ten times what a traditional rack consumed five years ago. Nvidia's GB200 NVL72 system alone requires liquid-cooled power stages converting hundreds of amps at sub-1V tolerances. The silicon, silicon carbide, and gallium nitride devices that make this possible, converting AC to DC, stepping voltages down, managing thermals, protecting circuits, are power semiconductors.
>
> What follows is a structured walkthrough of every investable name worth knowing.
>
> ---
>
> **LAYER 1: SUBSTRATE & WAFER MATERIALS**
>
> These companies produce the foundational wafers, silicon, silicon carbide (SiC), and gallium nitride (GaN) substrates, on which all power devices are built. This is the bottleneck layer: whoever controls substrate supply controls the pace of the SiC/GaN transition.
>
> **Wolfspeed ($WOLF)** — The only publicly traded pure-play SiC substrate and epitaxial wafer supplier at scale. Its wafer business is the strategic crown jewel. The company emerged from a prepackaged Chapter 11 in September 2025 after reducing debt ~70% (legacy equity was heavily diluted). It is now scaling its 200mm platform, anchored by the Mohawk Valley device fab in New York and supported by wafer production in Siler City, North Carolina.
> Financials: Market cap ~$2.7B. TTM revenue ~$713M but still deeply unprofitable. Post-restructuring cash of ~$1.2B+ provides runway and a much stronger balance sheet. Stock has seen strong post-emergence momentum. High-risk, high-reward name centered on successful 200mm execution and a path to sustained profitability.
>
> **Coherent Corp ($COHR)** — Formed from the II-VI / Coherent merger, this is a diversified materials company with a significant SiC substrate division. Coherent supplies 150mm and 200mm SiC substrates to multiple device makers, making it one of Wolfspeed's few true competitors in the Western world.
> Financials: Market cap ~$16B. Revenue ~$5.5B (diversified across photonics, lasers, and compound semiconductors). The SiC substrate business is a fraction of total revenue but growing rapidly. Profitable at the consolidated level with positive operating income. A safer way to play SiC substrates with diversification.
>
> **Soitec ($SOI.PA)** — A French company specializing in engineered substrates, including Smart SiC and Smart Cut technology for power and RF applications. Soitec's technology allows thinner, higher-quality substrates that reduce waste.
> Financials: Market cap ~€6B. Revenue ~€900M. Gross margins in the ~30% range. The power/automotive substrate segment is the fastest-growing part of the business. Listed in Paris, not on U.S. exchanges.
>
> **AXT Inc ($AXTI)** — A smaller compound semiconductor substrate maker producing indium phosphide, gallium arsenide, and germanium substrates. Peripheral to the core power semi market but relevant for GaN-on-GaAs and adjacent applications.
> Financials: Micro-cap (~$150M). Revenue below $100M. Marginally profitable or break-even. A niche, speculative play.
>

>
> **LAYER 2: INTEGRATED DEVICE MANUFACTURERS (IDMs)**
>
> These are the large companies that design, fabricate, and sell power semiconductor devices (MOSFETs, IGBTs, diodes, thyristors, SiC/GaN transistors). They dominate the market by revenue and R&D spend.
>
> **Infineon Technologies ($IFX.DE)** — The undisputed global #1 in power semiconductors. Infineon holds the largest market share in automotive power, industrial power, and is the leader in both Si-IGBT and SiC MOSFET revenue. Its acquisition of GaN Systems in 2023 gave it a leading GaN position as well. This is the most comprehensive power semiconductor platform in the world.
> Financials: Market cap €75B ($82B). Annual revenue ~€16B. Power & Sensor Systems segment alone does ~€4.5B. Gross margins ~45%, operating margins ~25%. Strong balance sheet, consistent free cash flow. The benchmark stock for the space.
>
> **STMicroelectronics ($STM)** — Europe's second power semiconductor titan. STM was an early mover in SiC, signing a landmark long-term supply deal with Tesla. Strong in automotive (EV traction inverters, onboard chargers) and industrial. Operates its own SiC wafer fab in Catania, Italy, with vertical integration back to raw SiC crystal growth.
> Financials: Market cap ~$30B. Annual revenue ~$13B. Gross margins ~40%. Recently navigated a cyclical downturn that compressed margins, but the long-term SiC pipeline remains robust. Trades at a discount to Infineon on valuation multiples.
>
> **ON Semiconductor / onsemi ($ON)** — The most dramatic turnaround story in power semi. Under CEO Hassane El-Khoury, onsemi exited commodity product lines, pivoted hard into SiC for EVs and intelligent sensing, and transformed margins. Now aggressively targeting AI data center power delivery as the next growth vector. Stock is up ~138% YTD in 2026.
> Financials: Market cap ~$51B. Annual revenue ~$7B. Non-GAAP gross margins pushed above 45%. Q4 free cash flow grew 12% even as revenue fell 11% during the cycle trough, validating the restructuring. Recently raised $1.3B via convertible notes at 0% coupon, a sign of market confidence. One of the strongest profitability improvements in the entire semiconductor industry over the past three years.

> Financials: Market cap ~$278B. Annual revenue ~$16.5B. Gross margins ~60%, operating margins ~38%. Industry-best free cash flow per share track record. Dividend aristocrat. The defensive, high-quality pick in power semi.

> **My Rankings for the Top 5 Power Semiconductor Stocks**
>
> This ranking optimizes for asymmetric return from current levels, where the gap between today's price and what the stock could be worth in a bull case is widest. More upside always means more risk.
>
> **1. Wolfspeed (WOLF) — $2.7B market cap** — The most asymmetric setup in the entire space. Wolfspeed controls the SiC substrate bottleneck, the foundational material every other SiC device maker needs. Post-Chapter 11, the balance sheet is cleaned up, the 200mm SiC fab (the world's first at that scale) is still being built out, and the stock re-listed at depressed levels. If the fab reaches target utilization, revenue could 3-5x from the current ~$713M run rate, and gross margins would inflect from negative to 40%+. At $2.7B, the market is pricing in heavy skepticism. A successful execution would make this a $10B+ company. The downside: execution risk is extreme, the company is burning cash, and SiC fab ramps have a history of delays. This is the highest-ceiling, lowest-floor name on the list.
>
> **2. Navitas Semiconductor (NVTS) — $6B market cap** — Revenue is $41M. If Navitas captures even 2-3% of the AI data center power conversion TAM over the next three years, that's a $500M+ revenue business on a fabless cost structure with 40%+ gross margins. The GaN IC technology is proven, the GeneSiC SiC portfolio adds a second vector, and the 59% revenue concentration in one AI-infrastructure distributor tells you where the growth is coming from. The stock is already up 251% YTD, so timing matters, but from a 3-year view, the revenue base could be 10x larger than today. The risk is that GaN remains niche in data centers and Infineon/TI absorb the opportunity with scale advantages.
>
> **3. Aehr Test Systems (AEHR) — ~$1.5B market cap** — The purest picks-and-shovels play on SiC scaling. Every SiC wafer that Wolfspeed, onsemi, STM, Infineon, or Rohm produces needs burn-in testing, and Aehr's FOX-XP platform is the standard tool. Revenue is ~$100M and growing rapidly, but SiC production volumes are projected to 5-10x over the next five years. Aehr's revenue is directly levered to that ramp with high incremental margins. A $100M revenue equipment company serving a market that's about to quintuple has obvious math. The risk: customer concentration and the possibility that larger equipment players enter the market.
>
> **4. Vicor Corporation (VICR) — ~$5B market cap** — Vicor's factorized power architecture and power-on-package modules solve a physics problem that gets worse with every GPU generation: delivering 1,000+ amps at sub-1V to a processor die without unacceptable voltage droop and heat. If this architecture becomes standard in AI accelerator designs, Vicor's content per server could be $500-1,000+. Revenue is ~$400M today. In a bull case where 2-3 major AI chip platforms adopt Vicor's approach, this is a $2B+ revenue company at 50%+ gross margins, a $25B+ market cap. The risk: Nvidia and AMD's power delivery teams could design around Vicor's IP, or MPWR's integrated approach wins instead.
>
> **5. Alpha & Omega Semiconductor (AOSL) — ~$1.5B market cap** — The most overlooked name in power semi. AOSL makes power MOSFETs and PMICs for computing, server, and industrial applications. At $1.5B market cap and ~$650M revenue, it trades at ~2.3x sales, a fraction of MPWR's 37x. The company is modestly profitable, has real products shipping into server power applications, and would benefit enormously from any broadening of AI power demand beyond the top-tier suppliers. This is the deep value pick: if the AI power theme lifts all boats, AOSL re-rates from 2x to 5x sales and the stock doubles without heroic assumptions. The risk: commodity-ish margins, no SiC/GaN differentiation, and limited pricing power versus Infineon and onsemi.
>
> **The pattern**: The highest-upside names are the smallest ones operating at the extremes, either controlling a bottleneck (WOLF on substrates, AEHR on test), holding a differentiated technology position (VICR, NVTS), or trading at a valuation disconnect (AOSL). The large-caps (Infineon, TXN, MPWR) are better businesses but the upside is more linear. If you're sizing positions by conviction-weighted upside, the answer is small positions in 1-3 and larger positions in the top-5 quality names from the earlier rankings.
