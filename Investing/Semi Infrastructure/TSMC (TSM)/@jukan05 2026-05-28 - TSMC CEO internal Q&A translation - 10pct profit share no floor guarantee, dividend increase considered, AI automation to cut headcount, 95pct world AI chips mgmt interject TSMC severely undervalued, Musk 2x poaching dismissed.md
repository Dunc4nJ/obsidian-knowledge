## Key Takeaways

- **The "95% of the world's AI chips are made at TSMC" + "stock severely undervalued" interject is the load-bearing investor signal**. Quoted verbatim from the management aside during the growth-sustainability Q&A: *"Other companies' stocks pumping for no good reason, and that TSMC's stock is severely undervalued. 95% of the world's AI chips are made at TSMC — please keep up the good work, everyone."* — this is the C-suite, in an internal employee setting, explicitly framing the stock as mispriced against the chip-share dominance. @NHLobstah flagged this and the dividend signal as the two highest-signal extracts.
- **Dividend is the chosen capital-return lever** — *"We're considering continuing to increase the dividend"* — buyback, charity donations, employee redistribution all explicitly rejected in adjacent Q&A. Implies forward-cash-return path is rising-dividend, not buyback. Material for total-return modeling of [[TSMC (TSM)]].
- **AI automation as headcount-reduction strategy is admitted**: Q "Since you're developing AI, can we use fewer people and pay out more in bonuses?" → A: *"I'm working on it."* TSMC management actively pursuing internal AI automation to reduce labor cost share. The "I'm working on it" is unusually candid for an internal-relations setting where the more defensive answer would deny headcount-reduction intent.
- **Structural compensation regime shift visible**:
  - Profit-sharing **dropped to 10%** with **no guarantee of staying at 10%** (only the 1% articles-of-incorporation floor is structural)
  - **Engineers bearing the cut** (smaller bonuses) while **VPs+ get stock grants** (effective raise) — CEO commits only to "look into the relationship" not to fix
  - Future raises explicitly larger at lower grades, smaller at higher — suggests management is anticipating an engineer-retention problem and pre-targeting compensation re-bracketing
  - Samsung peer comparison rejected on grounds that "DRAM industry earns in one year and lives off it for ten — different industry, not comparable" — distinguishes TSMC's annuity-like advanced-node business from memory-cycle peer [[Samsung Electronics (005930.KS)]]
- **Talent flight risk acknowledged but dismissed**: Elon Musk offering 2x salary to poach TSMC people. CEO: *"First, I'm not worried. Second, do you really believe Musk? Do you believe you can smoke cigars like he does?"* — cultural cigar-reference dismissal. Reader @kpb458 surfaces the obvious counter: *"Ripe for poaching..."* — implies xAI / SpaceX / Tesla-adjacent talent demand could materially erode TSMC's engineering bench if compensation continues compressing.
- **Recession contingency NOT modeled but soft-acknowledged**: *"That's not a scenario we're assuming. But if the global economy really did collapse, management would take care of its people."* — management uses paternal "take care of people" language, not contractual / structural commitment. Standard Taiwanese-employer framing but worth noting as cyclical-protection signal.
- **Unions explicitly discouraged**: *"If a union is formed, management has to spend a lot of energy dealing with it, so I don't encourage it. You're already getting this much in bonuses — why would you want a union?"* — confirms the management posture that the 10% profit-share IS the structural worker-share mechanism, and unions would be a parallel/redundant structure that adds management overhead. Implies if the 10% level falls further, union-organization pressure may follow.
- **Growth-sustainability Q & "no red flags"**: Q acknowledges CEO can't keep sustaining 1.3x growth, asks if operational red flags exist. A: *"There are no red flags."* + immediately followed by the management "severely undervalued" interject. The implication is that the growth deceleration is structural (you can't 30% YoY forever from this base) but not a deterioration signal — the company sees nothing breaking, and the moderation is geometric not strategic.

**@jukan05 (Jukan) — 2026-05-28 01:36:20 GMT:**

> TSMC CEO's remarks to TSMC employees regarding bonuses
>
> Q: Why are engineers taking a pay cut (smaller bonus) while VPs and above are getting stock — effectively a raise? A: I'll have to go back and look into the relationship between the two.
> Q: The articles of incorporation stipulate that profit-sharing must be no less than 1%. If the company keeps cutting it down toward 1%, can the articles be amended to protect employee rights? A: No.
>
> Q: Last time, the compensation restructuring was "cut the bonus, raise base salary." This time you're cutting the bonus again — will base salaries go up? A: Future raises will be larger for lower job grades and smaller for higher ones.
> Q: Is there a ceiling on compensation? A: There is no ceiling.
>
> Q: (The questioner's logic was unclear. The gist was questioning whether overexpansion of fabs and headcount is diluting the bonus pool.)
> Q: The bonus isn't increasing with EPS, but you mentioned earlier that the employee birth rate is 5x Taiwan's average. That doesn't add up. Shouldn't the company follow the government's lead and have the company raise the kids? A: Not possible.
>
> Q: Rather than putting that money into green energy investments, why not pay it out to employees, or at least restrict it to charitable donations? A: Not possible.
> Q: Even in downturns, the money doesn't get returned to employees. Wouldn't it be better to spend it on improving company benefits? A: The money doesn't get redistributed that way.
>
> Q: Since you're developing AI, can we use fewer people and pay out more in bonuses? A: I'm working on it.
>
> Q: Can we spend more on software and hardware equipment? A: Yes.
> Q: Will ETE (equipment-related roles) get raises? A: I'll go back and check. They should be increasing.
>
> Q: You said you can't keep sustaining 1.3x growth — is that because you're seeing some operational red flags down the road? A: There are no red flags.
> **(Management interjects) Complains that other companies' stocks are pumping for no good reason, and that TSMC's stock is severely undervalued. 95% of the world's AI chips are made at TSMC — please keep up the good work, everyone.**
>
> Q: Elon Musk is offering 2x salary to poach our people. Aren't you worried? A: First, I'm not worried. Second, do you really believe Musk? Do you believe you can smoke cigars like he does?
> Q: Anything on the financials going forward? A: **We're considering continuing to increase the dividend.**
>
> Q: Looking at the Samsung strike, TSMC's profit-sharing ratio has already dropped to 10%. Can you guarantee that at least 10% will continue to be distributed to employees going forward? A: No. The DRAM industry "earns in one year and lives off it for ten" — it's a different industry, not comparable.
> Q: Do you support employees forming a union? A: If a union is formed, management has to spend a lot of energy dealing with it, so I don't encourage it. You're already getting this much in bonuses — why would you want a union?
>
> Q: What if business turns south in the future? A: That's not a scenario we're assuming. But if the global economy really did collapse, management would take care of its people.
