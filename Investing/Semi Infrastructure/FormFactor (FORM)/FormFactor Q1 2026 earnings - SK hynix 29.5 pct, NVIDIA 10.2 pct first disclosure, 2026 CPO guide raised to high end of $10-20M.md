# FormFactor Q1 2026 earnings - SK hynix 29.5 pct, NVIDIA 10.2 pct first disclosure, 2026 CPO guide raised to high end of $10-20M

PhotonCap's [[FormFactor (FORM)]] Q1 2026 earnings teardown isolates three independent demand signals that aligned in a single quarter: HBM (memory cycle), CPO (silicon photonics cycle), and AI networking (compute cycle). Wafer test is the chokepoint where all three converge.

## Key Takeaways

- **SK hynix at 29.5% of revenue (up from 23.3% YoY) is the direct read on HBM4 ramp** — Korea revenue nearly doubled ($43.2M → $80.6M, now 35.6% of total) and DRAM probe card revenue grew +70% YoY to $82.9M. This is HBM4 priority allocation in numbers, not generic memory cycle. It is also a dual-edged structure: single-customer + single-country concentration risk is now explicit. The exposure to [[SK Hynix (000660.KS)]] is the cleanest pure-play HBM4 derivative beta available on a US-listed probe card name.

- **First-ever 10%+ customer disclosure of [[Nvidia (NVDA)]] at 10.2% reframes [[FormFactor (FORM)]] as an AI networking probe play, not just a memory probe play** — Foundry & Logic probe card revenue $111.2M, +30% YoY, with networking probe card growth cited as the driver. The question for the next 12 months is whether 10.2% is a one-time networking ASIC order or the start of share expansion to other AI silicon programs.

- **2026 CPO revenue guidance raised to the high end of the $10-20M range (~$20M) signals Triton Insertion 1 is production-ready** — co-developed with [[Advantest (6857.T)]] and Tokyo Electron, with Q4 2025 Keystone Photonics (Germany) acquisition integrated. The unconfirmed but material variable is whether Triton material stacks match TSMC's COUPE production line — a customer relationship that would dwarf the current $20M guide. Material alignment is visible; customer disclosure is not.

- **Wafer test sits structurally upstream of bonding & packaging, so probe capacity must scale with bonding capacity** — [[BE Semiconductor Industries (BESI.AS)]], [[Applied Materials (AMAT)]], [[ASMPT (0522.HK)]], Hanmi, EVG all expanding bonding capacity for HBM4 + CPO simultaneously creates derivative demand for FORM, which is the only comprehensive wafer test player exposed to HBM probe, CPO probe (vs ficonTEC), and Foundry & Logic probe at once. EIC test belongs to [[Advantest (6857.T)]] / [[Teradyne (TER)]]; PIC probing belongs to FORM + ficonTEC. The Triton platform spans both via the co-development structure.

- **Stock returned +361% over one year vs SMH +134% (~2.7x the semi index)** — the market priced this thesis for a year before Q1 2026 confirmed it in print. Earnings released April 29, 2026. Q2 2026 guide $240M ± $5M, GM 49.5% ± 1.5%, EPS $0.61 ± $0.04. The narrative has caught up to the print; the next move depends on whether the three axes diverge or compound. PhotonCap's prior write-ups on [[Aehr Test Systems (AEHR)]] / FORM / [[Keysight (KEYS)]] (the three pillars of SiPh wafer test) and the 7-company bonding cycle frame this as a multi-quarter structural ramp.

> ## 3. Why wafer test sits at the chokepoint of the cycle
>
> Semiconductor wafer test is the step where logic, DRAM, and Flash die are electrically validated at the wafer level before moving to back-end packaging. It sits immediately upstream of bonding and packaging. When bonding capacity scales, probe test capacity must scale with it. This structure was covered in a previous PhotonCap article comparing three wafer test equipment companies (AEHR, FORM, KEYS).[7]
>
> ## [The Three Pillars of SiPh Wafer Test: What AEHR, FORM, and KEYS Actually Do](https://photoncap.net/p/the-three-pillars-of-siph-wafer-test)
>
> In silicon photonics (SiPh), wafer test is more complex. Standard logic die only need electrical probes touching the wafer, but photonic integrated circuits (PICs) require optical signal measurement alongside electrical. Optical coupling must be precisely controlled at the wafer level, both edge coupling and grating coupling must be supported, and measurement throughput requires automation compatibility (OHT, Overhead Hoist Transport).[11]
>
> CPO (co-packaged optics) adds another layer of difficulty. EIC (electrical IC) and PIC are integrated in the same package, but EIC test equipment (Advantest, Teradyne) and PIC probing equipment (FormFactor, ficonTEC) cover different domains. On a production line, a single platform must handle both.[8] FormFactor's Triton platform handles PIC probing, with Advantest providing electrical test integration and Tokyo Electron providing wafer handling and production automation in a co-development structure.[3]
>
> The bonding cycle accelerating simultaneously in HBM4 and CPO was covered in a previous PhotonCap article analyzing seven bonding equipment companies including BESI.[9] As bonding capacity scales, probe test capacity must follow. Within that layer, FormFactor as a comprehensive wafer test player is exposed to HBM probe, CPO probe, and Foundry & Logic probe simultaneously.

> ## 4. Where the real difference begins
>
> Everything up to this point is visible from press releases and IR pages. Record quarter, Q2 guidance at $240M, 2026 CPO guidance at $20M, SK hynix at 29.5%, NVIDIA at 10.2% for the first time. That is the surface of publicly available data.

> What matters is that the three numbers are not moving independently. They point to the same supply chain bottleneck. Whether HBM concentration is a direct signal of SK hynix HBM4 ramp rather than just a generic memory cycle, whether the 2026 CPO revenue high-end guide is not just first revenue but positions Triton as a potential production test platform candidate for TSMC's COUPE production line, and whether NVIDIA's 10.2% is a one-time networking ASIC order or the starting point of a 12-month share expansion. Those are the questions visible beyond a single quarter's earnings.
