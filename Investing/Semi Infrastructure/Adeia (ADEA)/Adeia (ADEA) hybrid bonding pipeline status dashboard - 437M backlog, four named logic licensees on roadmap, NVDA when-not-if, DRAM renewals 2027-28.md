# Adeia (ADEA) hybrid bonding pipeline status dashboard — $437M backlog, four named logic licensees on roadmap, NVDA when-not-if, DRAM renewals 2027–28

Follow-up tweet from Pennycheck two days after the [[Adeia (ADEA) hybrid bonding catalyst brief - AMD license sets logic precedent, NAND in production, DRAM renewals 2027-28 unlock unit royalties|12-page Adeia catalyst brief]], compressing the entire licensing thesis into a single dashboard image plus a re-share of the page from Roth Capital's April 14 Tech Talk takeaway that anchored the framework. The dashboard adds new disclosed data points (60% adj EBITDA margin, $437M Q1 contracted backlog, 1,100+ HB patent portfolio, zero capex) and pulls the "RapidCool DTC" direct-to-chip cooling product into the pipeline as a 2028E commercialization catalyst not emphasized in the prior brief.

## Key Takeaways

- **The "tollbooth for compute" framing is the operative thesis** — [[Adeia (ADEA)]] is a sub-$4B market cap (~$3.7B per the brief) name whose price embeds only legacy media-IP + Pay-TV economics, while the semis-IP pipeline above represents 14 named counterparties (across logic, hyperscaler-direct, memory, and adjacent product families) priced at zero today. Pennycheck: "Mkt thinks it's a boring compounding media IP company but if you play closely attn they are the tollbooth for the future of compute with a endless line of catalysts over the next 2 years." The single-image scorecard format makes the asymmetry legible at a glance — every dollar that flips a "ROADMAP" cell to "LICENSED" prints royalty revenue that is not in the model.

- **The $437M Q1 2026 contracted backlog is a hard floor.** First time this number has surfaced in vault notes — Q1 disclosure of $437M contracted backlog (vs 2026E semis revenue of $40M and the long-term $500M annual licensing target referenced in the [[Adeia (ADEA) hybrid bonding catalyst brief - AMD license sets logic precedent, NAND in production, DRAM renewals 2027-28 unlock unit royalties|catalyst brief]]) means roughly 11 years of current run-rate semis revenue is already booked under contract. Combined with 60% adj EBITDA margin and "ZERO capex" framing, this is a pure-IP business model with operating leverage — incremental licensing revenue drops to the bottom line.

- **Logic ecosystem ladder is fully mapped with hard ROADMAP dates.** Beyond the [[Advanced Micro Devices (AMD)]] license (settled March 2026, pre-Markman), the dashboard lists [[Broadcom (AVGO)]] (3.5D XDSiP/TPU/MTIA/OpenAI ASIC) with "Roth 2026 lead" and "2026–27 catalyst" — confirming Roth Capital's view that AVGO is the next probable license. [[Intel (INTC)]] (Foveros Direct on Clearwater Forest) and [[Marvell Technology (MRVL)]] (Trainium/Maia ASIC stack) follow with 2026–27 and 2027+ catalyst timing respectively. Three names on the roadmap, AMD signed — the licensing flywheel for logic now has named-and-dated counterparties for the next 24 months.

- **Hyperscaler-direct row bypasses the ASIC partners — Roth flags "actively engaged."** [[Amazon (AMZN)]] (Trainium, Graviton), [[Alphabet (GOOGL)]] (TPU custom silicon), and [[Meta Platforms (META)]] (MTIA accelerators) are all tagged "ACTIVELY ENG" — direct license with no ASIC intermediary — with 2026–28 catalyst windows. This is the asymmetric upside leg: hyperscalers don't usually license patents directly (they push that responsibility to their merchant-silicon partners), so direct engagement implies internally-designed silicon is in play and Adeia is collecting on both sides of the ASIC partnership boundary.

- **The memory renewal cycle 2027/28 is now explicitly framed as a "structural step-up."** Three-row block: [[SK Hynix (000660.KS)]] (HBM4e+/HBM5 16–20+ layers, 2027 renewal), [[Samsung Electronics (005930.KS)]] (3D NAND/HBM/DRAM, 2027/28 renewal), [[Micron (MU)]] (HBM/DRAM, 2027/28 renewal) — all currently "LICENSED" on fixed-fee terms flipping to **per-unit at renewal**. [[Western Digital (WDC)|WDC]] / [[SanDisk (SNDK)]] 3D NAND already deployed online Q2/Q3 driving "revenue inflects next Q." [[Kioxia (285A.T)]] BiCS NAND in production today. The dashboard's "30%+ power savings" line is the recurring physics argument backing the renewal upgrade.

- **[[Nvidia (NVDA)]] tagged "WHEN, NOT IF" — and RapidCool DTC is a second product line.** The dashboard's "Optionality — Priced at Zero Today" footer makes two calls: NVDA ("Future Rubin / Vera-gen HBM stack" — "largest un-modeled vector, Post-2027") and RapidCool DTC, an Adeia-developed direct-to-chip data-center liquid cooling product positioned as "Entirely separate product, TAM ~$5B by 2030, 2028E+ commercialization." The brief covered cooling-cost dynamics tangentially; this dashboard elevates RapidCool to a distinct product/revenue line, not just a sidecar to HB.

- **Thread reactions surface the operational risks**: the CEO transition is the most-cited near-term concern ("Davis stepping down after bad diagnosis / sick" — replies note "leadership changes at IP-licensing companies (where deal-making relationships are everything) is a real risk"). Comparison anchors from the replies: $CEVA (another IP licensor that 8x'd in a year) and the fact that current company guidance is only $100M IP revenue this year — implying the dashboard's pipeline is upside vs guide, not what's in the model.

> **All roads lead to Hybrid Bonding (HB):** Last week we hosted a "Tech Talk" with Dr. Laura Markirimi, SVP of Engineering at Adeia around Hybrid Bonding (HB) and Direct-to-chip (DTC) cooling solutions (RapidCool). HB dates back to the 2015 acquisition of Ziptronics ($39M by Tessera), and today stands at a portfolio of over 1,100+ patents around methods, process, chemistries, etc. that deliver improved performance (speed, latency), power, thermal, cost and form factor benefits over existing thermal energy interconnect and packaging solutions. We reiterate power consumption savings in excess of 30%, which are meaningful when considering Data Centers were estimated to consume 4%+ of electricity in the US and could ramp to 12%+ by 2028. We highlight the following:
>
> - **Logic chiplets:** **The Advanced Micro Devices (AMD-Buy; Desilva) deal was a seminal event as it underscored the technical validation, adoption trends, and monetization aspects of HB for deployments of logic (processors) in data centers and AI applications. We believe this is a precursor to other logic deals as products commercialize in 2026 led by Broadcom (AVGO-NC), as well as Intel (INTC–Neutral, DeSilva), Marvell (MRVL–Buy, DeSilva) and others. Additionally, we believe that as ADEA is actively engaged with vertically integrated hyperscalers (Amazon (AMZN–Buy, Kulkarni), Alphabet (GOOGL; Kulkarni), Meta Platforms (META–Buy, Kulkarni, etc.), and that Nvidia (NVDA–NC) could benefit from the adoption of HB. Finally, HB is expected to expand outside the data center to edge processing solutions (AI accelerators and inference processors), led by autonomous applications (auto, etc.).**
>
> - **High density memory (3D NAND & HBM DRAM):** Within high density memory, HB is finding adoption in NAND (at 200+ payers) and HBM DRAM (HBM4e/5 at 16/20+ layers). Today, within 3D NAND, Kioxia and Sandisk (SNDK–NC) utilize HB, which appears to be driving better performance (including 30%+ power reduction) and market share gains. Meanwhile, HBM adoption is expected to coincide with HBM4e commercialization in 2027, which parallels expected renewal timelines for the "big 3" memory vendors; SK Hynix (2027), Samsung and Micron (MU–NC). We believe that the recent JEDEC potential relaxation of height standards is a red herring and misses the point that HB provides significant power and thermal benefits. The company believes this is likely to translate to a bifurcation of performance-led, HB-enabled memory for data centers and lower-end memory (thermal bonding) for less demanding applications.
>
> - **Direct-to-chip…when, not if:** Adeia's Direct-to-Chip cooling technology, RapidCool, has remained on an accelerated development timeline (initiated 20xx) and could commercialize in as little as 3+ years (2028+). Leveraging efficient cold-plate microfluidics with a redirected internal flow design optimized to remove hot spots from the chips, the technology delivers efficient on-chip cooling, suitable for high-performance applications.
