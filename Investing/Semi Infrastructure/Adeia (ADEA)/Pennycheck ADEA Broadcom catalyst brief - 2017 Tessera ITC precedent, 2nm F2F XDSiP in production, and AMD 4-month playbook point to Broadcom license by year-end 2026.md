# Pennycheck ADEA Broadcom catalyst brief - 2017 Tessera ITC precedent, 2nm F2F XDSiP in production, and AMD 4-month playbook point to Broadcom license by year-end 2026

Pennycheck's May 14, 2026 catalyst brief on [[Adeia (ADEA)]] builds a documentary case that a multi-year [[Broadcom (AVGO)]] semiconductor IP license is the next near-term shoe to drop, modeled on the four-month [[Advanced Micro Devices (AMD)]] file-to-settle cadence and the 2017 Tessera v. Broadcom ITC playbook.

## Key Takeaways

- **The 2017 Tessera v. Broadcom ITC playbook removes counterparty risk on the litigation strategy.** Predecessor entity Tessera won an ALJ Initial Determination on US Patent 6,849,946 in June 2017 (Investigation 337-TA-1010) and converted it into a multi-year license with upfront payment plus recurring quarterly royalties by December 2017. CEO Paul Davis explicitly defines "multi-year" as ≤5 years (Roth conference, March 2026), so the 2017 license expired ~2022 and never covered 3.5D XDSiP hybrid bonding because that platform did not exist - Broadcom only announced XDSiP December 5, 2024. Chief Semiconductor Officer Craig Mitchell rejoined January 2026 after 30 years at Tessera/Invensas, giving Adeia institutional muscle memory for re-running the play.

- **Broadcom moved from roadmap to production on February 26, 2026, creating the operational forcing function.** Broadcom is shipping 2nm Face-to-Face hybrid copper-bonded silicon to Fujitsu now, with broader customer XPU shipments confirmed for 2H 2026 (Meta MTIA, Google TPU, OpenAI XPU). The bonding chemistry is a TSMC SoIC-X derivative - the same hybrid-bonding family Adeia just settled with AMD over - so the claim construction Adeia advanced in W.D. Texas applies without modification. Every additional XPU that ships without a license is incremental Adeia negotiating leverage.

- **Adeia named Broadcom on its own corporate blog two days after suing AMD (November 5, 2025), and Roth Capital placed Broadcom *first* in the next-logic-license sequence (April 14, 2026).** Davis reinforced the priority ordering at Roth (March 23, 2026) by naming Broadcom ahead of Intel and Marvell on the logic-roadmap list. [[BE Semiconductor Industries (BESI.AS)]]'s Q3 2025 earnings call independently corroborated Broadcom's role in the hybrid-bonding demand stream - an outside channel check from an equipment vendor with no stake in Adeia's licensing narrative.

- **Royalty math: a Broadcom license is structurally larger than AMD on silicon-area and customer-breadth bases.** Roth anchors the AMD royalty at ~$2M/Q ramping to $5-10M+/Q. Broadcom's lead XDSiP product bonds four compute dies + one I/O die + six HBM modules with F2F HCB throughout; Broadcom has "more than five 3.5D products in development" with [[Meta Platforms (META)]] MTIA, [[Alphabet (GOOGL)]] TPU, OpenAI XPU, and Fujitsu Monaka named publicly. CFO Keith Jones guided recurring revenue to ~$90M quarterly by year-end 2026 from $66.3M in Q1 2026 - a Broadcom license is the single largest line item supporting that step-function ramp toward Adeia's $100M semiconductor / $500M total licensing targets.

- **Risk: TSMC indemnification or PTAB IPR challenge could stretch the timeline beyond the AMD four-month cadence.** AMD settled before Markman; Broadcom is a more sophisticated litigant historically and could deflect via inter partes review or foreign-jurisdiction settlement. CEO transition (Davis stepping down Q4 2026 for health reasons) adds soft uncertainty, partially offset by Mitchell's operational presence. Even a slower 2017-style 18-24-month cadence implies a 2027 settlement - still near-term against the $100M semi target.

> ## Why This Matters - The Compressed Catalyst Cycle
>
> The Adeia thesis to date has rested on two semiconductor-licensee events: Kioxia/SanDisk (NAND, already in production but Q1 2026 zero-revenue print due to contract structuring) and AMD (logic, settled March 9, 2026). Broadcom is not just one of several - Broadcom is the catalyst with the most evidence of imminence on every available axis: a documented litigation playbook history, a chip in production right now using exactly the bonding chemistry Adeia just settled with AMD over, public naming by Adeia itself, sell-side specificity, equipment-vendor confirmation, and hyperscaler customer commitment that converts to royalty units.

The thesis is not that Broadcom 'might' license. The thesis is that Adeia has both the legal mechanism and the operational urgency to force the conversation in 2026, on a cadence compressed by the AMD precedent of four months from filing to settlement.

> ## The Playbook Precedent: Tessera v. Broadcom (2017)

> ## Phase 1 - The ITC Investigation and ALJ Initial Determination
>
> 'Administrative Law Judge Sandra Dee Lord issued a notice of initial determination ('ID') in Certain Semiconductor Devices, Semiconductor Device Packages, And Products Containing Same, Investigation No. 337-TA-1010. … ALJ Lord found a violation of Section 337 of the Tariff Act due to infringement of U.S. Patent No. 6,849,946 by Broadcom and its named customers. … The ALJ also recommended that the ITC issue its standard remedies that would bar Broadcom and its customers Arista Networks, ARRIS/Pace, ASUSTeK/ASUS, Comcast Cable, HTC, NETGEAR, and Technicolor from importing, selling, and engaging in a variety of related domestic activity in connection with infringing imported products.'
>
> - Tessera Technologies press release, July 5, 2017. Source: investor.xperi.com
>
> Note the structural similarity to the AMD case Adeia just won: file at the ITC, name the customers downstream so the import ban radius widens, force the conversation.

> ## Phase 2 - Settlement With Multi-Year License
>
> 'Tessera Technologies and Broadcom have reached a deal to resolve Tessera's pending case at the U.S. International Trade Commission accusing Broadcom of infringing its semiconductor patents, as well as other U.S. and international litigation. … In conjunction with the settlement, Broadcom entered into a new multi-year patent license agreement with Tessera. > ## Phase 3 - The 2017 License Does Not Cover Hybrid Bonding XDSiP
>
> Two structural reasons the 2017 license does not bar new litigation:
>
> - (1) Term expiry. Davis stated at the Roth 30th Annual Conference (March 23, 2026) that Adeia characterizes 'multi-year' as five years or less, distinguishing it from 'long-term' agreements (greater than five years). On that math, the 2017 Broadcom multi-year license expired around 2022.
> - (2) Product scope. The 2017 case asserted U.S. Patent No. 6,849,946, described by Xperi as 'a key patent on a fundamental manufacturing process technology that is not only very broadly infringed across all of Broadcom's significant product lines.' The infringing Broadcom products at the time were standard semiconductor chips across networking, set-top boxes, modems, and switch ASICs - manufactured at 16nm, 20nm, 28nm, 45/40nm, 65/60/55nm, 90nm, and 130nm process nodes. Broadcom's 3.5D XDSiP hybrid-bonded XPU platform was announced December 5, 2024 and entered production February 26, 2026. The platform did not exist when the 2017 license was negotiated, and the hybrid bonding patent family asserted against AMD in November 2025 was not in scope.

> ## The bonding chemistry is what Adeia just sued AMD over
>
> AMD's 3D V-Cache uses TSMC's SoIC-X die-to-wafer hybrid bonding. Broadcom's 3.5D XDSiP uses TSMC's CoWoS-L plus a Face-to-Face hybrid copper bonding implementation that industry analysis consistently characterizes as a TSMC SoIC-X derivative.
>
> 'However, 3.5D XDSiP's F2F HCB is likely a proprietary implementation of TSMC's bumpless SoIC-X stacking technology, albeit with Broadcom's proprietary design and automation flow (which is something indirectly confirmed by Kevin Zhang from TSMC).'

> - Anton Shilov, Tom's Hardware, December 7, 2024.

>
> The claim construction Adeia advanced in W.D. Texas against AMD's use of SoIC-X-based hybrid bonding does not require modification to apply to Broadcom's SoIC-X-derivative F2F implementation. This is why AMD settled in four months rather than litigate - and it is why Broadcom faces the same risk calculus.

> ## SECTION 8

> ## Royalty Math: What a Broadcom License Is Worth
>
> Adeia's long-term target is $100M in recurring semiconductor revenue, scaling toward a $500M total annual licensing target. Davis confirmed semiconductor revenue was $26M in 2025 (up from $18M in 2024). The AMD license is anchored by Roth at a low-end of ~$2M/Q ramping to $5-10M+/Q ($20-40M+ annualized) at maturity. A Broadcom license sized comparably or larger is the most concrete near-term path to the $100M semiconductor revenue target.
>
> ## Why Broadcom is plausibly larger than AMD on a royalty basis
>
> - Silicon area weighting. AMD's hybrid bonded surface area is concentrated in X3D consumer SKUs and EPYC-X server SKUs (cache-on-compute). Broadcom's lead XDSiP product integrates four compute dies + one I/O die + six HBM modules with F2F HCB throughout the entire die-to-die interconnect fabric. Royalty bases that scale with bonded silicon area or interconnect count favor Broadcom.
> - Customer breadth. Broadcom acknowledged 'more than five 3.5D products in development' (Broadcom press release, December 5, 2024). Public reporting names Google TPU, Meta MTIA, OpenAI XPU, and Fujitsu Monaka. Each program ramps independently, compounding the royalty base.
> - Hyperscaler license collapse. Roth (April 14, 2026) flags vertically-integrated hyperscalers (Amazon, Alphabet, Meta) as part of the engagement pipeline. In ASIC arrangements, the hyperscaler typically pushes patent obligations onto the chip designer. If Broadcom takes the license, several hyperscaler engagements collapse into that single deal - the dollar size rises commensurately.
>
> On Roth's AMD framework applied to Broadcom: even at parity ($2-10M/Q), a Broadcom license alone would contribute $8-40M annualized of pure incremental revenue toward the $100M semiconductor target. A bigger sizing is defensible given the area and customer breadth differential.

> ## SECTION 10

> ## Risk Factors Specific to the Broadcom Thesis
>
> Risks to the Broadcom catalyst, in descending order of materiality:
>
> ## (1) TSMC indemnification or claim-construction defense
>
> If Broadcom or TSMC argues that Adeia's bonding chemistry IP runs through TSMC's SoIC-X process rather than around Broadcom's product, the negotiation gets messier than AMD. AMD settled before Markman; Broadcom could pursue inter partes review through the PTAB to challenge the asserted claims. The 2017 Tessera precedent showed Broadcom does engage at the ITC and does settle - but the prior history is process-IP, not bonding chemistry. Counter-argument: Adeia chose not to sue TSMC in the AMD case, indicating their litigation theory targets product manufacturers, not foundries. The same posture applies to Broadcom.
>
> ## (2) CEO transition uncertainty
>
> Davis announced his intent to step down later in 2026 for health and personal reasons. The succession process is underway with a target Q4 selection. Litigation strategy decisions of this magnitude could be deferred or modified during the transition. Counter-argument: Davis remains CEO through the transition; chief semiconductor officer Craig Mitchell returned January 2026 with 30 years of institutional history at predecessor entities and is positioned to drive the playbook.
>
> ## (3) Confidential financial terms compressing market reaction
>
> The AMD deal financials were not disclosed. A Broadcom deal will almost certainly be the same. Investors will need to take Roth's $2-10M/Q framework on faith until the first quarterly royalty print arrives.
>
> Counter-argument: even without disclosed dollars, an 8-K announcement of a multi-year Broadcom license is interpretable in context of the AMD precedent and would likely drive a re-rating on the recurring-revenue trajectory alone.
>
> ## (4) Broadcom delay tactics via foreign licensing or settlement deflection
>
> Broadcom has historically been a sophisticated patent licensee and litigant. A Broadcom defense team could deflect the dispute through procedural delay or settlement in lower-leverage jurisdictions. Counter-argument: the 2017 ITC case ran from initial filing through settlement in approximately 18-24 months. Even on a slower cadence than AMD, a Q2-Q3 2026 filing implies a 2027 settlement - still near-term against ADEA's $100M semi target.
