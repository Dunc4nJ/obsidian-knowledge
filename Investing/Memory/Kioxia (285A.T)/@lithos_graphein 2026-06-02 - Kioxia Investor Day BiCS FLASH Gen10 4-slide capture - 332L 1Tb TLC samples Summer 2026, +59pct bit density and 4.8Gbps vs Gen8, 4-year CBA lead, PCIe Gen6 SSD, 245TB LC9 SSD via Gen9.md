## Key Takeaways

- **Gen10 = 332 layers, 1Tb TLC, sample shipments targeted Summer 2026**. Kioxia's contrarian bet vs industry's >400L pursuit. Kioxia's curve-optimization argument: 332L is the cost-curve sweet spot — vs >400L peers, Kioxia projects **~<23% fewer layers, ~<10% lower GB cost, ~>10% better power efficiency, ~>35% better cell reliability**. The bet: industry's chase to >400L is past the GB-cost minimum on the U-shaped curve (wafer cost rises faster than GB density past optimum) — Kioxia stays at the floor.
- **Gen10 vs Gen8 step-function improvements** (slide 003, all percentages relative to Gen8 baseline):
  - **Bit Density +59%**
  - **Interface Speed +33%** (3.6Gbps → 4.8Gbps)
  - Read Throughput **>15% better**
  - Write Throughput **>30% better**
  - Read Power Efficiency **>40% better**
  - Write Power Efficiency **>30% better**
  - = **High Bandwidth TLC-SSD supporting PCIe Gen.6** in the KIOXIA CM Enterprise NVMe SSD form factor
- **CBA (CMOS directly Bonded to Array) = 4-year lead over industry** (the load-bearing technical-leadership claim). Kioxia adopted CBA in 2024 with Gen8 at 3.6Gbps; Gen9/10 hit **4.8Gbps from 2027**. Industry (US + South Korea manufacturers excluding JV partners) doesn't adopt CBA until **2028** and reaches 4.8Gbps only at Gen10 in **2029**. **CY29 GB output ratio**: Kioxia majority at 4.8Gbps Gen9/10; Industry majority still at ≤3.6Gbps with only the Gen10 slice at 4.8Gbps. *"By early adoption of CBA technology, Kioxia will be able to supply high-performance products superior to those rest of the industry in the future."*
- **BiCS FLASH dual-axis roadmap** (slide 004 — High Performance vertical × Large Capacity horizontal):
  - Gen5 → Gen6 (pre-CBA) → **Gen8 (CBA introduction)** = 2Tb QLC enabling **8TB per package via 32-die stacking**
  - Gen9 = LC9 enterprise SSD form factor, **245TB Large Capacity SSD**
  - Gen9 → Gen10 → **Gen.Y** (high-performance branch, 4.8Gbps+ ceiling)
  - Gen10 → **Gen.11** (large-capacity branch, continuous process migration via CBA-leveraged optimized capex)
  - **Strategy framing**: "Through optimized Capex, Kioxia will maximize GB output to meet diverse market demands"
- **Implication for memory-wall / HBF context**: Kioxia is the **NAND chokepoint** counterpart to SK Hynix's DRAM/HBM chokepoint position. The 332L vs >400L cost optimization + 4-year CBA lead + PCIe Gen.6 throughput collectively position Kioxia to capture incremental AI-server NAND capacity demand (KV cache / model weights / checkpoint storage) at superior unit economics — relevant to the broader memory-wall framing in [[PENG SK Hynix HBF memory tier plus Celestial photonics at 1x sales 2x memory revenue - ThematicTrader bull thesis]] and [[PENG Optical Memory Appliance unlocks 1000TB cluster pool vs 11TB copper KV cache, 70pct cost-per-query reduction - BryzonX thesis]]. The 245TB LC9 single-SSD form factor enables the "fewer, denser, lower-power" enterprise storage pattern hyperscalers want for AI infrastructure.
- **Memory sector peer set** (Kioxia's competitive surface — all hub-linked): [[Samsung Electronics (005930.KS)]] (NAND + DRAM + HBM, Kioxia's biggest scale competitor), [[SK Hynix (000660.KS)]] (NAND + HBM dominance), [[Micron (MU)]] (NAND + HBM), [[SanDisk (SNDK)]] (NAND, former Kioxia JV partner via Western Digital spin), [[Western Digital (WDC)]] (NAND-spin parent), [[Macronix (2337.TW)]] (specialty NAND/NOR), [[Winbond (2344.TW)]] (specialty memory), [[GigaDevice (603986.SS)]] (Chinese NAND/NOR). Kioxia is pure-play NAND, the only Japanese-listed NAND major.

### Slide 1 of 4 — "BiCS FLASH™ generation 10: Technological Leadership (Layer Count)" (Kioxia deck page 33)

*Custom-built Kioxia investor-day slide. Header: "Kioxia achieves cost competitiveness and improved power consumption by optimizing the layer count to balance chip size, investment costs, and power efficiency". Mixed text + visual chart + comparison illustration + spec table.*

*Left panel — "GB cost vs. Layer Count in BiCS FLASH generation 10" — U-shaped GB-cost curve plotted against Layer Count (x-axis 200-500). The curve descends as **GB density increases** through lateral shrink (left side), reaches minimum at **generation 10 (332L)** marked with green dot, then ascends past 400L as **wafer cost increases** dominates the GB density gains. "Industry average >400L" callout positioned on the rising right shoulder of the curve. Spec: 1Tb TLC.*

*Right panel — "Advantages of Choosing 332 Layers" — side-by-side memory-stack diagrams. Left stack ">400 Layers" depicts taller memory cell stack with many memory layers between memory-layer and memory-cell endpoints with electron-flow (e-) annotations. Right stack "332 Layers" shows shorter, more compact stack with fewer layers and visibly less electron-travel distance — implies shorter electron paths = better power efficiency + better cell reliability.*

*Comparison table — **332L vs >400L** (Kioxia's estimation):*

| Metric | Layer Count | GB Cost | Power Efficiency | Cell Reliability |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **332L vs >400L** | About <23% | About <10% | About >10% | About >35% |

*Reading: 332L has ~<23% fewer layers, ~<10% lower GB cost, ~>10% better power efficiency, ~>35% better cell reliability than >400L industry approach.*

### Slide 4 of 4 — "BiCS FLASH™ Road Map" (Kioxia deck page 32)

*Custom-built Kioxia investor-day slide.
