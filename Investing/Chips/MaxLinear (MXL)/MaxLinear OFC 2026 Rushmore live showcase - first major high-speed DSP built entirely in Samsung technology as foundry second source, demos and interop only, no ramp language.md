# MaxLinear OFC 2026 Rushmore live showcase — first major high-speed DSP built entirely in Samsung technology as foundry second source, demos and interop only, no ramp language

Official [[MaxLinear (MXL)]] press release (2026-03-12, ahead of OFC 2026, March 17–19) announcing live demonstrations of the Rushmore 1.6T 200G/lane PAM4 DSP and Washington 200G/lane TIA. One year after [[MaxLinear unveils Rushmore 1.6T PAM4 DSP at OFC 2025 - sampling and commercial availability on Samsung leading-edge CMOS no node named, sub-25W DR-FR module target|the OFC 2025 unveil]], this is the second primary source behind the Rushmore/Samsung supply-moat pillar evaluated in [[MXL 2026-06-10 briefing - optical DSP re-rating thesis, Q1 FY26 infrastructure up 136 pct, Rushmore 1.6T Samsung ramp, five-pillar bull case amid CPO delay debate]].

## Key Takeaways

- **The foundry second-source claim, verbatim**: *"Rushmore is the first major high-speed DSP to be built entirely in Samsung technology, providing customers with a foundry second source and additional supply flexibility."* This is the company's own framing of the supply-moat argument — a deliberate alternative to the [[TSMC (TSM)]]-centric supply chains in which [[Marvell Technology (MRVL)]], [[Broadcom (AVGO)]], and (at lower power tiers) [[Credo Technology (CRDO)]] DSPs are built, and a notable design win for [[Samsung Electronics (005930.KS)]] Foundry in high-speed networking silicon.
- **Fab language still names NO node**: the portfolio bullet says only *"built on Samsung's leading-edge CMOS process"* — identical in substance to the 2025 release's "Samsung's leading-edge CMOS technology". Across both company press releases there is **no mention of 4nm, 3nm, or GAA anywhere**. Circulating bull threads asserting a specific Samsung node are not supported by either PR — exactly the "exact Rushmore node" open question flagged in the [[MXL 2026-06-10 briefing - optical DSP re-rating thesis, Q1 FY26 infrastructure up 136 pct, Rushmore 1.6T Samsung ramp, five-pillar bull case amid CPO delay debate|MXL briefing]].
- **No production-ramp, volume, or ship-date language for Rushmore — anywhere in the release**: the content is demos and interoperability only — an end-to-end optical link demo transmitting *"224Gb/s-per-lane"* data (Rushmore DSP + Washington TIA + partner optics), an electrical-performance demo, the Ethernet Alliance 224G interop demonstration, and the OIF 224G CEI Very Short Reach (VSR) interop. The only volume/shipment language in the release belongs to the prior-generation Keystone 400G/800G DSPs (*"millions of units shipped"*). A year after "sampling and commercial availability," the company is still demonstrating readiness, not announcing a ramp.
- **Sub-25W remains enablement language; headline features are IFEC, ILT, MPI localization, and driver swing**: portfolio bullet verbatim — *"Rushmore: 1.6T PAM‑4 DSP for sub‑25W optical modules, built on Samsung's leading‑edge CMOS process, featuring the industry's highest swing integrated driver and advanced capabilities such as IFEC, ILT, and MPI localization."* "For sub-25W optical modules" describes what customer modules the DSP targets, not a measured module power. IFEC and ILT are new branding versus the 2025 feature list (which disclosed MPI detection/localization, 8x8 crossbar, pre-FEC BER monitor, automated DPD).
- **Positioning spans both scale-up and scale-out AI networking**, with demonstrated reach *"from short‑reach multimode links to reaches of up to 2 km"* and a four-product chipset stack (Rushmore DSP, Washington 224G TIA, Keystone 400G/800G DSPs, Topanga 100G/lane TIAs) covering fully retimed optical transceivers, LRO, and AEC applications — the both-sides-of-the-architecture-fight posture described in the briefing note.

> **CARLSBAD, CA** – (BUSINESS WIRE)– March 12, 2026 – MaxLinear, Inc. (Nasdaq: MXL), a leading provider of high‑speed interconnect ICs for data center, metro, and wireless transport networks, today announced it will showcase its next‑generation 1.6T PHY chipset at OFC 2026, March 17–19, in South Hall, Booth 1049\. The portfolio includes the Rushmore 200G/lane PAM‑4 DSP family and the Washington 200G/lane TIA.

> Rushmore is the first major high-speed DSP to be built entirely in Samsung technology, providing customers with a foundry second source and additional supply flexibility.

> At the MaxLinear booth, two live demonstrations will highlight Rushmore’s performance, signal integrity, and diagnostics capabilities for next‑generation optical and electrical interfaces. One demonstration will show end‑to‑end optical link performance using the Rushmore 1.6T DSP with the Washington TIA and partner optics to transmit and receive 224Gb/s‑per‑lane data. A second demonstration will highlight Rushmore’s electrical performance, underscoring robust operation in demanding system environments. The demonstrated performance supports deployment across a wide range of data center use cases, from short‑reach multimode links to reaches of up to 2 km.

> Rushmore will also be showcased in industry interoperability demonstrations at OFC. These include the Ethernet Alliance interoperability demonstration, showcasing compliance with emerging 224G Ethernet standards, and the OIF 224G CEI Very Short Reach (VSR) interoperability demonstration. Together, these demonstrations validate Rushmore’s readiness for next‑generation electrical interfaces and its compatibility across switches, optical modules, and high‑speed cabling solutions.

> In addition to Rushmore demos, MaxLinear will also showcase customer optical modules featuring its Keystone 400G and 800G DSPs, as well as Rushmore DSPs, for fully retimed optical transceiver, LRO and AEC applications. Keystone DSPs have been widely adopted by module vendors and hyperscale data center customers worldwide, with millions of units shipped.

> MaxLinear’s portfolio addresses both scale‑out and scale‑up networking architectures required for AI data center growth. The company’s roadmap spans optical and copper interconnects, delivering solutions optimized for power, reach, and system flexibility to support today’s high‑volume 400G and 800G deployments while enabling future 1.6T systems and beyond.
