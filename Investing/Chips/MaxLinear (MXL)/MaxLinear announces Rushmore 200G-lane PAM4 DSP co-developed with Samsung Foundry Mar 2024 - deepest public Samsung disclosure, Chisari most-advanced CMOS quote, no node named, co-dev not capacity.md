# MaxLinear announces Rushmore 200G/lane PAM4 DSP co-developed with Samsung Foundry (Mar 2024) — deepest public Samsung disclosure, Chisari most-advanced-CMOS quote, no node named, co-dev not capacity

Official [[MaxLinear (MXL)]] press release dated **2024-03-26** (OFC 2024 in San Diego, booth 4501), announcing the *development* of **Rushmore**, MXL's fourth-generation 200G/lane PAM4 SerDes + DSP family. This is the **origin source** for the Rushmore product line and — critically — the **single deepest public disclosure of the [[Samsung Electronics (005930.KS)]] relationship that exists**. The later launch releases drop the explicit "Samsung Foundry" framing in favor of vaguer "Samsung's leading-edge CMOS" language: see the launch one year later, [[MaxLinear unveils Rushmore 1.6T PAM4 DSP at OFC 2025 - sampling and commercial availability on Samsung leading-edge CMOS no node named, sub-25W DR-FR module target]], and the OFC 2026 follow-up [[MaxLinear OFC 2026 Rushmore live showcase - first major high-speed DSP built entirely in Samsung technology as foundry second source, demos and interop only, no ramp language]]. The Rushmore-Samsung pillar of the bull case is examined in [[MXL 2026-06-10 briefing - optical DSP re-rating thesis, Q1 FY26 infrastructure up 136 pct, Rushmore 1.6T Samsung ramp, five-pillar bull case amid CPO delay debate]].

## Key Takeaways

- **The Samsung quote, verbatim — names a person, names "Samsung Foundry," names NO node.** Marco Chisari, **Executive Vice President and Head of US Foundry Business, Samsung Electronics**, is quoted directly: *"We are excited to collaborate closely with MaxLinear on this industry-leading achievement. Their proven expertise in high-speed interconnect solutions, combined with their strong development approach, made them an ideal partner for developing this next generation PAM4 SERDES IP and DSP using **our most advanced manufacturing CMOS process technology**."* The exact process language is **"our most advanced manufacturing CMOS process technology"** — deliberately node-free. No 4nm, no 3nm, no GAA, no SF4/SF3 anywhere in the release. This is the most specific the Samsung relationship ever gets in public, and it still refuses to name silicon. Bull threads attributing a specific Samsung node to Rushmore are NOT sourced from any MaxLinear press release.
- **"Samsung Foundry" appears explicitly here — and essentially nowhere else.** The sub-headline and body both use the exact phrase *"Samsung Foundry leading-edge-CMOS-based"* / *"This new Samsung Foundry leading-edge-CMOS-based family."* The OFC 2025 launch release and the OFC 2026 follow-up both downgrade this to *"Samsung's leading-edge CMOS technology"* (no "Foundry," no Samsung executive quote). So the deepest public Samsung disclosure is the *oldest* one — the relationship got *less* specific in public over time, not more.
- **What "joint development" actually commits: co-development of IP/product, NOT capacity.** The release frames this as *"developed in conjunction with [MaxLinear's] homegrown co-optimized SERDES"* and Chisari calls MXL *"an ideal partner for developing this next generation PAM4 SERDES IP and DSP."* This is a **co-development / IP collaboration announcement** — Samsung as the leading-edge foundry building MXL-designed silicon, with MXL contributing its own co-optimized SERDES. There is **no wafer-capacity agreement, no take-or-pay, no volume commitment, no minimum-purchase language** anywhere. Co-development ≠ capacity agreement. Corroborating the absence of any binding supply contract: an **EDGAR full-text search for "Samsung Foundry" across all MaxLinear SEC filings returns zero hits as of 2026-06** — the Samsung relationship has never been disclosed as a material contractual obligation in an MXL filing, only via marketing press releases.
- **Node ceiling cross-check against MXL's own filings.** MXL's FY25 10-K (filed Feb 2026) caps its most-advanced node disclosure at *"down to 4 nanometer."* That 10-K figure is the closest the company comes to a node number — and it's stated generically, not tied to Rushmore or Samsung. Combined with Chisari's node-free "most advanced manufacturing CMOS" and Samsung's leading-edge SF4-class processes circa 2024, "4nm-class Samsung" is the most a reader can responsibly infer — and even that is inference, not disclosure. **Neither MaxLinear nor Samsung has ever publicly confirmed the Rushmore process node.**
- **Timeline announced here vs. what actually happened.** This March 2024 release is a *development* announcement — Rushmore is described as in development, with the Keystone 5nm 100G/lane family (already shipping) as the predecessor. It commits to **no sampling date and no ship date**. What actually followed: **sampling + commercial availability announced March 2025** (one year later, at OFC 2025 — see the OFC 2025 note), and **production ramp guided to late 2026** per the Q1 FY26 commentary. So from this development reveal to guided revenue ramp is roughly **~2.5 years (Mar 2024 → late 2026)** — a long arc that matters for the time-gated nature of the Rushmore bull thesis.
- **Power/performance claims — all qualitative, all comparative, none quantified.** The release promises *"a significant leap in performance, power efficiency and density"* and *"overall best-in-class power and performance"* from the co-optimized SERDES, but gives **no watts, no pJ/bit, no measured numbers**. The only hard spec is the architecture: 200G/lane PAM4, doubling Keystone's 100G/lane rate. The sub-25W module target that appears in the 2025 launch is NOT in this 2024 development release.
- **Product scope (gen-1 disclosure).** The Rushmore family spans **fully re-timed optical modules, half re-timed optics, and active electrical cables (AECs)**, plus **8x200G-to-8x200G re-timers** and **8x100G-to-4x200G gearbox variants** — a deliberately broad SKU map across optics, copper, and gearbox use cases. Built on the Keystone 5nm 100G/lane DSP heritage.
- **Third-party TAM frame.** LightCounting's Vlad Kozlov is quoted projecting **PAM4 DSP shipments growing at a 50% CAGR 2023–2027**, AI/ML-driven — the standard analyst tailwind cited to position the Rushmore launch into a rising market.

> CARLSBAD, Calif.--(BUSINESS WIRE)-- MaxLinear, Inc., a leading provider of high-speed interconnect ICs enabling data center, metro, and wireless transport networks, today announced the development of its fourth generation 200G/lane PAM4 SERDES (Serializer/Deserializer) and DSP family, Rushmore. This new Samsung Foundry leading-edge-CMOS-based family delivers a significant leap in performance, power efficiency and density, enabling data center operators and cloud providers to address rapidly increasing data traffic demand, the transition to higher speed networks, and the critical power efficiency needs of AI/ML networks.
>
> The Rushmore family encompasses a diverse array of variants tailored to meet the evolving needs of modern data centers and telecommunications networks. This includes support for fully re-timed optical modules, half re-timed optics, and active electrical cables (AECs). Moreover, MaxLinear is introducing both 8x200G-to-8x200G re-timers and 8x100G-to-4x200G gearbox variants, providing customers with a comprehensive suite of solutions to address their specific requirements.
>
> “The AI/ML revolution has accelerated the pace of innovation across the industry, and that is also the case at MaxLinear,” said Drew Guckenberger, Vice President of High Speed Interconnect at MaxLinear. “Starting from our highly successful Keystone 5nm 100G/lane DSP family, the amount of additional innovation that has gone into the new Rushmore 200G/lane DSP family is incredible. The impacts on power and performance our partners will see are expected to be significant for the industry as a whole as we scale into the new AI/ML world.”
>
> Building on the technical and commercial success of the Keystone family of 5nm 100G/lane DSPs, Rushmore doubles the data rates and significantly reduces latency for Big Data and AI/ML applications, while simultaneously adding significant additional functionality and performance extensions. The Rushmore family of DSP has been developed in conjunction with homegrown co-optimized SERDES to achieve overall best-in-class power and performance.
>
> "We are excited to collaborate closely with MaxLinear on this industry-leading achievement. Their proven expertise in high-speed interconnect solutions, combined with their strong development approach, made them an ideal partner for developing this next generation PAM4 SERDES IP and DSP using our most advanced manufacturing CMOS process technology,” said Marco Chisari, Executive Vice President and Head of US Foundry Business, Samsung Electronics. “This collaboration signifies our commitment to delivering cutting-edge solutions that empower our customers to build next-generation data center and high-performance computing applications."
>
> AI and ML have emerged as key drivers for the growth of the global high-performance computing market, which is directly linked to the need for faster data processing and transfer for AI workloads. Training AI models can be computationally expensive and time-consuming. Higher data rates, enabled by technologies like 200G/lane SERDES and DSPs, allow for faster data movement between processing units and memory, accelerating the training process. Similarly, faster inference speeds are crucial for real-time applications like autonomous vehicles or image recognition, requiring efficient data transfer through high-bandwidth connections.
>
> “Shipments of PAM4 DSPs will grow at a CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate) of 50% between 2023 and 2027, primarily driven by AI/ML applications,” said Vlad Kozlov at LightCounting. "MaxLinear, with its strong track record in high-speed interconnect solutions, is well-positioned to capitalize on this trend by delivering innovative 200G/lane PAM4 SERDES and DSP solutions that address the specific needs of the AI/ML market, such as high performance, low power consumption, and low latency."
