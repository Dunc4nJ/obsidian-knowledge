# MXL FY2025 10-K excerpts - node ceiling REGRESSES to 4nm (was 3nm in FY24 10-K), Samsung named only as Pay-TV OEM not foundry, restricted cash jumps 25K to 27.2M for a purchase-obligation guarantee

MaxLinear, Inc. Annual Report on Form 10-K for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2025, filed with the SEC on 2026-01-29 (accession 0001288469-26-000011). Captured as standalone verbatim excerpts of the segment financials, the process-node disclosure, foundry language, customer/geographic concentration, the restricted-cash jump, and the liquidity-sufficiency statement — the primary-source counterpart to the bull-case threads in [[MXL 2026-06-10 briefing - optical DSP re-rating thesis, Q1 FY26 infrastructure up 136 pct, Rushmore 1.6T Samsung ramp, five-pillar bull case amid CPO delay debate]] and the quarterly evidence in [[MXL 2026-Q1 10-Q - inventory purchase obligations 180.3M not 210M, WIP up 25 pct QoQ on data center wafer prepay, infrastructure now 46 pct of revenue]].

## Key Takeaways

- **THE NODE SENTENCE — the ceiling REGRESSED to 4nm in this filing.** The FY2025 10-K states MaxLinear uses process nodes "ranging from 0.25µ **down to 4 nanometer**" (verbatim, Item 1, transcribed below). This matters because of the trajectory across filings: the **FY23 10-K said "down to 5 nanometer," the FY24 10-K said "down to 3 nanometer," and this FY25 10-K REGRESSED to "down to 4 nanometer."** This is the **first primary-source ceiling on Rushmore's node** that is internally consistent with what MaxLinear can actually claim. A 4nm finest-node disclosure implies a **Samsung SF4-class** process — **one full node behind** the 3nm coherent/PAM4 DSPs from peers ([[Marvell (MRVL)]] Ara at TSMC N3, [[Broadcom (AVGO)]] Sian3 at 3nm). The retail/press "3nm GAA" folklore around Rushmore most likely traces back to the **retracted FY24 10-K sentence** ("down to 3 nanometer"), which this filing has now walked back. If the finest node MaxLinear is willing to disclose is 4nm, then the headline 1.6T DSP is plausibly on a 4nm-class Samsung process, not 3nm GAA.
- **Samsung appears EXACTLY ONCE in the entire 10-K — and only as a Pay-TV OEM customer, never as a foundry.** A case-insensitive scan of the full document returns a single "Samsung" hit: in the Pay-TV market discussion, listing "Vantiva SA, Hitron Technologies Inc., Compal Broadband Networks, Inc., Humax Co., Ltd., and **Samsung Electronics Co., Ltd.**" as OEMs MaxLinear sells *into*. The named **foundry partners are TSMC and UMC** (both in the manufacturing disclosure transcribed below), with certain products supplied on a turnkey basis by [[Intel (INTC)]]. There is **no Samsung Foundry agreement, capacity commitment, or take-or-pay supply contract disclosed anywhere in the annual report.** The "Rushmore built on Samsung leading-edge CMOS" narrative (from OFC marketing — see [[MaxLinear OFC 2026 Rushmore live showcase - first major high-speed DSP built entirely in Samsung technology as foundry second source, demos and interop only, no ramp language]] and [[MaxLinear unveils Rushmore 1.6T PAM4 DSP at OFC 2025 - sampling and commercial availability on Samsung leading-edge CMOS no node named, sub-25W DR-FR module target]]) has **zero corroboration in the audited 10-K's foundry section.**
- **FY25 returned to growth but stayed deeply loss-making.** Total net revenue **$467.6M, +30% YoY** (from $360.5M). Segment mix: **Broadband $204.4M (+75%)**, **Connectivity $78.0M (+40%)**, **Infrastructure $148.2M (+30%)**, **Industrial & multi-market $37.1M (-50%)**. Gross margin **57%** (gross profit $265.8M). But the company posted a **loss from operations of $(126.9)M** and a **net loss of $(136.7)M** — heavy R&D ($208.6M) and SG&A ($159.6M) plus $24.5M of restructuring charges kept operating expenses at $392.7M, 84% of revenue. The loss narrowed sharply from FY24's $(245.2)M net loss but the business is nowhere near operating breakeven.
- **Customer concentration jumped in FY25.** Two customers accounted for **28% of net revenue** (Customer A **16%**, Customer B **12%**) and the **top-ten customers were 65%** — up from one-customer-at-12% / top-ten-60% in FY24, and one-customer-at-10% / top-ten-54% in FY23. Concentration is rising as the infrastructure ramp pulls revenue toward a small set of data-center/optical buyers. Geographically, **49% of net revenue shipped to Hong Kong** (ship-to destination), 82% to Asia overall, 12% to Vietnam.
- **Restricted-cash anomaly: long-term restricted cash jumped from $25K to $27.2M for "a guarantee in connection with a purchase obligation" — counterparty unnamed.** Long-term restricted cash went from **$25 thousand (Dec 31, 2024) to $27,187 thousand (Dec 31, 2025)**; total restricted cash rose from $1.0M to $28.6M. The filing's only explanation: "The increase in restricted cash from $1.0 million to $28.6 million is due to **a guarantee in connection with a purchase obligation.**" The 10-K does **not name the counterparty.** **FLAG — UNKNOWN:** a ~$27M cash guarantee tied to a purchase obligation is consistent with **capacity-securing collateral** (e.g., a foundry/substrate prepayment or take-or-pay backstop tied to the data-center ramp), but the filing does not say so. Read alongside the Q1 FY26 10-Q's explicit "**prepayment for wafers supporting rising demand for certain data center products**" (see [[MXL 2026-Q1 10-Q - inventory purchase obligations 180.3M not 210M, WIP up 25 pct QoQ on data center wafer prepay, infrastructure now 46 pct of revenue]]), this looks like a supply-chain investment — but that is inference, not disclosure.
- **No going-concern language; liquidity declared sufficient.** Management states the **$101.4M of cash, cash equivalents, and restricted cash** at year-end "will be sufficient to fund our projected operating requirements for at least the next twelve months." Indebtedness is the **$125.0M Initial Term Loan** (seven-year term, maturing June 2028), and the **$100.0M Revolving Facility remained undrawn.** No going-concern qualifier appears in the filing. (Caveat: the $101.4M total includes $28.6M of *restricted* cash; unrestricted cash and equivalents were $72.8M. And this 10-K's contingency disclosures — unaccrued — include the Silicon Motion / SIAC arbitration; see the addendum in the Q1 10-Q note for the magnitude of that off-balance-sheet tail.)

### 2. THE NODE SENTENCE — process technology / foundry disclosure (verbatim, Item 1)

> We utilize an increasing range of process technologies to manufacture our products, from standard CMOS to more exotic processes including SiGe and GaAs. Within this range of processes, we use a variety of process technology nodes ranging from 0.25µ **down to 4 nanometer**. We depend on independent silicon foundry manufacturers to support our wafer fabrication requirements. Our key foundry partners include Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Corporation, or TSMC, in Taiwan, and United Microelectronics Corporation, or UMC, in Taiwan and Singapore. We generally do not depend on a single source for the supply of our materials. Additionally, certain products are supplied to us by Intel Corporation, or Intel, on a turnkey basis.

[Emphasis on "down to 4 nanometer" added; all other text verbatim. **Node trajectory across filings** (not in this excerpt — from the prior 10-Ks): FY23 10-K = "down to 5 nanometer"; FY24 10-K = "down to 3 nanometer"; FY25 10-K (this filing) = "down to 4 nanometer." The finest disclosed node REGRESSED one node versus the FY24 annual report. **Named foundries: TSMC and UMC only.** No Samsung Foundry. Certain products turnkey from Intel.]

### 4. Customer concentration + geographic concentration (verbatim)

> A significant portion of our net revenue has historically been generated by a limited number of customers through sales of our products. > As of December 31, 2025, we had cash and cash equivalents of $72.8 million, restricted cash of $28.6 million and net accounts receivable of $46.1 million. Additionally, as of December 31, 2025, our working capital, which we define as current assets less current liabilities, was $62.8 million.

> We believe that our **$101.4 million of cash, cash equivalents, and restricted cash at December 31, 2025 will be sufficient to fund our projected operating requirements for at least the next twelve months.** As of December 31, 2025, our indebtedness totaled **$125.0 million**, which consists of outstanding principal under the Initial Term Loan under the June 23, 2021 Credit Agreement.
