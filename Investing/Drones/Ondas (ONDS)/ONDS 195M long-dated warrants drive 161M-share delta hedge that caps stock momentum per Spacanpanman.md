# ONDS warrant overhang creates ~161M-share delta hedge that caps stock momentum

This thread starts with @SteveUrkelDude posting a [[Ondas (ONDS)]] daily chart showing repeated failed breakouts and asking why the stock can't catch momentum despite a textbook setup. @spacanpanman replies with a structural answer: 195.5M long-dated warrants outstanding force warrant holders to delta-hedge by shorting common stock, and at current prices that hedge equals ~161M shares — close enough to the real-time short interest of 131M to explain the persistent overhead supply.

## Key Takeaways

- **The chart problem is structural, not technical** — Spacanpanman's mechanical explanation reframes Urkel's "why can't this break out" question: the failed breakouts aren't tape-reading noise, they're the predictable behavior of a stock with ~40% of fully-diluted shares locked up in 7-year warrants whose holders short common to hedge gamma. Technical analysis on a name with this kind of overhang gives misleading signals.
- **Delta hedge sizing puts a soft cap above current price** — at ~$10 the warrant deltas imply ~161M short shares; at $13 they rise to ~180M. Each push higher mechanically forces ~9-10M additional shares of fresh supply per dollar, dwarfing typical daily volume. This is the "wall" that turns breakouts into failed breakouts.
- **Long-dated warrants barely re-hedge** — because the warrants are 7-year ($20 and $28 strikes), their gamma is low at $10. The deltas grind rather than whip, which means the overhang acts as a slow drip rather than a violent squeeze-and-release. The stock can grind higher only as fundamentals catch up to the strike prices, not as a momentum trade.
- **Short interest ≈ delta** — real-time SI of 131M vs. 161M implied delta means most of the recorded short interest is hedged, not directional. Squeeze setups that work on stocks with high *unhedged* short interest don't work here, because covering one side forces the warrant holder to re-short the other.
- **Implication for sizing the bull case** — even if [[Ondas (ONDS)]]'s $375M+ 2026 revenue guide and $1.5B pro-forma cash justify the underlying business value, the path from $10 to $20 (Street average PT) has to drag the warrant deltas with it. Rallies will be slower and more grinding than fundamentals alone would suggest. This is more a vehicle for patient compounding (or a covered-call income overlay) than a fast-money breakout trade.
