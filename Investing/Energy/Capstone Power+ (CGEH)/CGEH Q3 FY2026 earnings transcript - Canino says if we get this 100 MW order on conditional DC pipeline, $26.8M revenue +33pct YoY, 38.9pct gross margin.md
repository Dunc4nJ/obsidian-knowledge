# CGEH Q3 FY2026 earnings transcript - Canino says "if we get this 100 MW order" on conditional DC pipeline

The first earnings call from [[Capstone Power+ (CGEH)]] since the longview thesis. Headline financials are strong — record $26.8M revenue (+33% YoY), 38.9% gross margin (+14 pts), 7th consecutive quarter of positive Adjusted EBITDA — but the data-center thesis remains aspirational. Canino's language is conditional throughout ("if we get this 100 MW order, what does this look like"); no named DC customer; no DC orders in backlog. Meanwhile rental fleet revenue is down due to <$60 oil. This is the primary-source ground truth that future bull/bear theses need to be measured against.

## Key Takeaways

- **The DC pipeline is still narrative, not signed orders.** Canino's exact words on factory capacity prep — *"we just recently had a dry run session with some of our key suppliers to basically say, 'Okay, **if we get this 100 megawatt order**, what does this look like, and how fast can we ramp up?'"* — frame the pipeline as conditional. He confirms zero named DC customers ("hopefully" we see first DC orders "in the coming few quarters") and never quantifies signed DC backlog. The C&I pipeline is real and growing; the DC pipeline is *"magnitudes, magnitudes above what the C&I pipeline is"* but explicitly less qualified ("the team is getting really good in qualifying and making sure they're not chasing everything"). Same comp dynamic [[Bloom Energy (BE)]] used pre-2024 — ramp is real once first marquee DC contract closes, but until that happens this is a distributed-generation business with DC optionality, not a DC business.
- **Factory capacity math: 350 MW single-shift / 700 MW two-shift, gated by supply chain.** *"7 megawatts on the floor at any given time. And since it only takes us about a week to build a C1000, if you've got 7 megawatts in one shift per week, you multiply that by 50 weeks, you're at 350 megawatts. And then if you go to two shifts, you're at 700 megawatts."* This is the bull-case bound on revenue ramp — at the $3.5M/MW engineered-package ASP Canino cited, 350 MW = $1.225B max single-shift product revenue. The constraint isn't the floor, it's the supply chain (the dry-run was specifically to find choke points). Mobile manufacturing is offered as a release valve ("a customer who wants a mobile manufacturing facility, because they've got a five-year build-out for their data center").
- **The $3.5M/MW ASP pencils only with the full Engineered Equipment Package — microturbine + absorption chiller + dry cooler + battery storage** — not the microturbine alone. An analyst pressed on the gap (historical product revenue has run <$2M/MW) and Canino conceded *"we did a poor job on this one. We didn't clearly explain what's in that $3.5 million per megawatt."* This is the key disclosure: the bull-case unit economics depend on selling the bundle ("one throat to choke"), which is unproven at scale and isn't what showed up in any of the four quarters of trailing data. The thesis math should be re-stress-tested at $2M/MW until bundled revenue is demonstrated.
- **Rental revenue is structurally exposed to oil — confirmed weak this quarter.** *"It's primarily oil and gas, and... as oil goes below $60, you know, these folks start to move units off."* Rental dropped to $3.9M, dragging total revenue. Mitigation is the Interconnect Flexibility Program (utility customers needing 1-3 year bridge power while waiting for service) — but that's pipeline, not revenue. Anyone modeling CGEH should treat rental fleet income as oil-price beta, not steady recurring.
- **Gross margin 38.9% is partly one-time, not pure mix.** John Miller (CFO) explicitly declined to call it a trend: *"we had a pretty intense mix of part sales in the quarter, and we also had... some good news as we found some liabilities that were on the books for FPP program liabilities that we didn't need, so that... fell straight to the bottom line."* Translation: a chunk of the 14-point margin expansion is a one-time FPP liability release. The underlying margin trend is still upward (DFMA cost-out, Cal Microturbine acquisition, parts mix shift) but the print isn't the new run rate.
- **Goldman's 37.5% preferred stake doesn't block uplisting.** Miller confirmed *"it's not necessary for us to either redeem or purchase that instrument for us to relist or uplist."* The Nasdaq/NYSE American move is on the table for after first profitable year — material catalyst, no Goldman renegotiation required.
