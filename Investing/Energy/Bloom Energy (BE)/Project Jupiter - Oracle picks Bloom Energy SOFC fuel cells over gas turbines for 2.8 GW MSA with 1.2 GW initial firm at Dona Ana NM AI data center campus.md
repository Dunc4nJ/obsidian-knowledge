# Project Jupiter: Oracle picks Bloom Energy SOFC over gas turbines for 2.8 GW MSA

This is the canonical AI-DC behind-the-meter (BTM) proof point of 2026 for [[Bloom Energy (BE)]]. Two coordinated press releases dated April 13 and April 27, 2026 announce: (1) an Oracle/Bloom master services agreement for **up to 2.8 GW** of solid oxide fuel cell (SOFC) systems, with an **initial 1.2 GW firm contracted** and deployment underway; (2) a hyperscale instantiation of that MSA at Oracle's **Project Jupiter** campus in Doña Ana County, New Mexico, where Bloom will install **up to 2.45 GW** as the **sole power source — explicitly replacing previously-planned gas turbines and diesel generators** to consolidate the campus into a single fuel-cell microgrid.

For the BTM-power thesis, Oracle's stated rationale is the load-bearing detail: **~92% NOₓ reduction vs the gas turbine baseline, negligible water use, faster time-to-power**. This is a customer publicly choosing combustion-free generation OVER gas turbines/diesel for a hyperscale AI campus — a direct negative read-across to combustion-based BTM vendors competing for the same socket.

## Key Takeaways

- **Largest commercial SOFC deal on record, structured as a multi-year MSA.** [[Oracle (ORCL)]] has contracted up to 2.8 GW from [[Bloom Energy (BE)]] under a master services agreement, with **1.2 GW firm initial** and deployment "underway and continuing into next year." Project Jupiter alone consumes 2.45 GW of that envelope. The MSA structure converts what would have been one-off project wins into a multi-year drawdown — the kind of recurring backlog the long thesis on Bloom required to be priced as anything other than a project-shop.

- **Oracle SWAPPED OUT previously-planned gas turbines and diesel for fuel cells** at a hyperscale AI campus. The Oracle/BorderPlex release is unambiguous: "This updated power design will replace Project Jupiter's previously planned gas turbines and diesel generators and consolidate the facility into one single microgrid campus." This is the negative datapoint for combustion-based BTM vendors — including microturbine names like [[Capstone Power+ (CGEH)]] and the larger frame/aeroderivative gas turbine OEMs ([[GE Vernova (GEV)]] and peers). At the very least it punctures the assumption that hyperscale AI campuses are a captive gas-turbine TAM; at most it sets a precedent permitting authorities and ESG-mandated capital can cite for the next 50 sites.

- **Rationale is permitting-friction, not price.** Oracle cites three reasons in its own words: ~92% lower NOₓ emissions, near-zero water consumption, and faster time-to-power. None of those are about LCOE. Read against the broader regime shift from "capital scarce / permission abundant" (2024–25) to "capital abundant / permission scarce" (2026+), this is exactly the trade buyers are now willing to make: pay more in $/kW to get power that can actually be permitted on schedule in water-stressed and NOₓ-limit-constrained jurisdictions like Doña Ana County. Combustion vendors can't win this trade on price alone if the buyer's binding constraint is permission, not capital.

- **Time-to-power is a measurable Bloom advantage now, not a marketing claim.** The press release cites a specific delivery: Bloom delivered an Oracle fuel cell system in **55 days** vs the **90-day anticipated schedule** — a real, dated proof point in a market where peer vendors are quoting multi-year lead times (GEV is "fully sold out through 2030" per the Jason's Chips thesis). Time-to-power is not just a tiebreaker anymore; it's the gating factor on which AI revenue gets captured this cycle vs deferred to next.

- **The 800 V DC reference is the architecture moat.** Bloom calls out that its systems are "aligned to emerging standards such as 800 V dc." Native DC output skips the AC inversion and step-down losses that combustion gensets impose on a DC-native AI rack. This is the technical edge cited in the prior Bloom long thesis (see [[Jasons Chips Bloom Energy (BE) thesis - fuel cells displace gas turbines for AI data centers via native 800V DC, modularity, 90-day deployment, and unconstrained TAM]]) and the press release effectively endorses it: Oracle's hyperscale deployment is being built around the architecture, not in spite of it.

- **BorderPlex is the regional/political channel.** The Oracle release prominently features BorderPlex Digital Assets as the campus developer, with $50M for water systems, $360M for schools/infrastructure, and $6.9M for workforce + community programs — plus 4,000 construction jobs and 1,500 ongoing positions. This is the playbook for greasing permitting in mid-tier U.S. jurisdictions: pair the hyperscaler tenant + clean-power vendor + local growth-infrastructure developer + visible community giving. Expect to see this template replicated; it makes the next-five-Project-Jupiters easier to permit, and it's a tailwind for any "Growth Infrastructure Campus" developer that can repeat the formula.

## Read-across to other BTM names

- **[[Capstone Power+ (CGEH)]]** — microturbine vendor pitched as "Bloom-but-cheaper" for tier-2/3 sites (see [[Longview thesis - Capstone Power+ (CGEH) is a 14x like Bloom Energy with microturbine plus ESP for AI data center BTM power]]). The Bloom/Oracle precedent doesn't kill the CGEH thesis on its own — microturbines and SOFCs aren't always competing for the same socket — but it removes one of the clearer bull paths (combustion BTM as the default at hyperscale AI sites) and reinforces the bear concern flagged in [[Everyone Hates Poetry exits CGEH at 11.50 in Dec 2025 warning of retail pumps mis-stating cap structure and hyperscaler vs tier-2-3 framing]] that hyperscaler-grade buyers want non-combustion power. Watch whether CGEH wins any hyperscaler MSAs in 2026; if it can't, the tier-2/3 base case has to carry the entire thesis.
- **[[GE Vernova (GEV)]]** — broader gas-turbine context. GEV is sold out through 2030, so any single project swap is not material to backlog. But Oracle's stated rationale (NOₓ + water + time-to-power, not price) is the more uncomfortable read: it suggests the marginal hyperscale AI buyer is willing to pay up to skip combustion entirely. If that pattern holds across the next ten campuses, the marginal turbine buyer becomes utility/grid, not hyperscaler — a different demand mix than what's currently embedded in GEV multiples.

Last year, Bloom Energy delivered a fully operational fuel cell system to Oracle in just 55 days—more than a month ahead of the anticipated 90-day deployment schedule.

> # Oracle, BorderPlex, and Bloom Energy to Power Project Jupiter with Cleaner, Water-Efficient Fuel Cell Technology
>
> AI data center campus in Doña Ana County will preserve electricity rates for residents, dramatically reduce water use, and protect local air quality
>
> **Austin, Texas—Apr 27, 2026**
>
> *Project Jupiter rendering, embedded above as `oracle-bloom-jupiter-001.jpg`.*
>
> Oracle and BorderPlex Digital Assets today announced Project Jupiter will utilize Bloom Energy fuel cells to fully power the AI data center campus in Doña Ana County, New Mexico. As part of Oracle and Bloom's previously announced expanded partnership, the data center will be supported by up to 2.45 GW of installed Bloom fuel cell capacity. This updated power design will replace Project Jupiter's previously planned gas turbines and diesel generators and consolidate the facility into one single microgrid campus.
>
> Fuel cells generate electricity without combustion, meaning the Bloom microgrid is highly efficient with low emissions and water use. Compared to its previously planned gas turbines, Project Jupiter with the Bloom microgrid will reduce NOₓ emissions by approximately 92% and will use a negligible amount of water.
>
> "We are excited to move forward with this updated energy solution, which reflects our commitment to both the latest innovation and community priorities as we advance the next generation of AI infrastructure," said Mahesh Thiagarajan, executive vice president, Oracle Cloud Infrastructure. "Bloom's fuel cell technology enables us to deliver highly reliable on-site power with a lower environmental footprint—supporting the project's performance needs while contributing to stronger environmental outcomes."
>
> For BorderPlex, the announcement marks an important milestone in a vision that began with a simple conviction: that industrial land in the southern New Mexico desert could become a national platform for advanced computing, cleaner energy, and long-term economic growth. BorderPlex will continue supporting Project Jupiter's success in New Mexico while advancing additional growth infrastructure opportunities and other clean-energy initiatives in the state and beyond, including solar, storage and geothermal opportunities. BorderPlex and Oracle will continue working together on future initiatives that expand access to the infrastructure needed to support these priorities.
>
> "Project Jupiter started with a belief that Doña Ana County could become a Tier 1 industrial engine for New Mexico," said Lanham Napier, chairman, BorderPlex Digital Assets. "We said we could help bring cleaner energy, stronger infrastructure, more jobs and new investment to southern New Mexico, and that vision is becoming reality. With this announcement, Project Jupiter is becoming a platform for better jobs, stronger infrastructure, and generational opportunity in a region with the talent, work ethic, and ambition to help lead New Mexico's next chapter of growth."
>
> "Bloom has rapidly become the platform of choice for powering AI data centers responsibly," said Aman Joshi, chief commercial officer, Bloom Energy. "Bloom's fuel cell technology will power what is expected to be one of the largest data center microgrids operating in the United States at the time of completion. Our energy solution not only dramatically reduces water use, it is also cleaner, quieter, and helps protect electricity rates for local residents. This is a model that can be replicated across America—it is digital power for the digital age."
>
> Oracle will continue to bear all energy costs for Project Jupiter, ensuring the initiative has no impact on residents' electricity rates or grid stability. Together with the data center's closed-loop, non-evaporative cooling systems designed to minimize day-to-day water use, the fuel cell solution addresses community priorities around air quality and water stewardship.
>
> Project Jupiter has also committed $50 million to help repair, upgrade, and improve local water systems; $360 million in direct support for schools, infrastructure, and local services; and $6.9 million to fund workforce development, the Boys and Girls Club of Las Cruces, clean drinking water, the community college, and habitat restoration.
>
> Construction continues to move forward on schedule. Over the life of the project, Oracle expects Project Jupiter to create 4,000 construction jobs and support 1,500 ongoing positions on-site and in the surrounding community, prioritizing local hiring, opportunities for New Mexico's skilled trades and union workforce, local suppliers, local contractors, and workforce partnerships that help New Mexicans access these opportunities.
