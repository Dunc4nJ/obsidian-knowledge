# Crux Capital opens AMBA position as #1 edge AI pick with $90-95 base case on CV72 CV75 CV7 N1 product cycle

## Key Takeaways

- Crux Capital opened a starter position in [[Ambarella (AMBA)]] — narrowing the prior [[Crux Capital 2026 edge AI shortlist - Ambarella CEVA and Ouster as vision chip IP and lidar plays|three-name shortlist]] to one pick on the back of clean financials (<$5B market cap, FY26 revenue +37% YoY, free-cash-flow positive, zero debt) plus production proof (~80% of FY26 revenue from edge AI, ~$1B cumulative edge AI revenue, 370+ customer AI products in production across security cameras, automotive, drones, video conferencing, and industrial automation).
- The product-cycle bridge is the multiple-rerating lever: 5nm CV72 and CV75 already hit a high-single-digit % of Q4 FY26 revenue with ASPs running $10–$75 vs. legacy single-digit ASPs and triple-digit potential for the most advanced parts; CV7 (first 4nm chip) has sampled with revenue starting Q4 FY27; N1 targets edge-AI infrastructure and warehouse perception hubs. The thesis is unit growth *plus* higher content per device — not unit growth alone.
- Management-ranked growth drivers: robotics/drones/warehouse automation has the clearest near-term proof point — an unnamed e-commerce customer deployed N1-655-based perception hubs across large-scale warehouses, and Q4 was the first full quarter of aerial-drone production revenue. Automotive is the slower, larger lane (~$13B FY27–FY32 invited-bid pipeline; CV3-AD plus radar/perception from the VisLab and Oculii acquisitions). Edge infrastructure (N1 in vision boxes / on-prem AI servers) is the most abstract but most asymmetric.
- Scenario framing: bear ~$50, base $90–$95, bull $125–$135 against ~$3.1B market cap and roughly 6.4x FY27 EV/sales on the guide midpoint. FY27 guidance of 10–15% growth is a transition year; the bull case requires FY28 reacceleration via CV/N1 plus visible robotics, drone, and edge-infrastructure revenue. Author plans to start small around the 8-day EMA / ~$64 area and add on execution.
- Concentration risk is the most underappreciated downside — WT Microelectronics (a Taiwan fulfillment partner shipping to Asian customers) was 73.1% of Q4 and 69.7% of FY26 revenue, layering supply-chain and geopolitical fragility on top of ~8x FY26 sales valuation and intensifying competition from larger semis across security, auto, drones, and edge AI.

> ## The Company: Ambarella
>
> The company is Ambarella, ticker $AMBA.
>
> *Ambarella company introduction — branding and overview*
> ![[cruxcapitalgroup-i-took-a-new-position-edge-ai-003.png]]
>
> Ambarella is my top edge AI candidate at this time because it has a really clean combination of real revenue, direct physical AI exposure, customer deployments, and product-cycle upside. This is a device-level edge AI company. Ambarella sells low-power vision AI SoCs (System on Chip) that sit inside cameras, drones, robots, vehicles, security systems, and industrial devices. The chip processes visual data locally, runs AI inference, and sends useful outputs instead of raw data.
>
> *Ambarella thesis pillars — real revenue, physical AI exposure, customer deployments, product-cycle upside*
> ![[cruxcapitalgroup-i-took-a-new-position-edge-ai-004.png]]
>
> So think of the flow like this: a security camera captures video. Ambarella's chip processes the image, runs AI locally, and sends an alert. So maybe A person near a restricted gate. A vehicle stopped in a lane. A worker entering an unsafe area. A drone identifying an object. A robot detecting an obstacle. Whatever it may be, Ambarella helps devices see and understand locally.
>
> *On-device AI inference flow — capture, process locally, send only the alert*
> ![[cruxcapitalgroup-i-took-a-new-position-edge-ai-005.png]]
>
> This is a different problem from centralized AI training. Edge devices have especially tight power, heat, cost, and latency constraints. They need enough AI performance to make a real-time decision while fitting inside a constrained physical product with a practical bill of materials.
>
> *Edge device constraints — power, heat, cost, latency, BOM*
> ![[cruxcapitalgroup-i-took-a-new-position-edge-ai-006.png]]
>

The IoT side includes enterprise security, home security, portable video, robotics, AI vision boxes, developer AI workstations, and on-prem AI servers. The auto side includes safety, telematics, ADAS, and longer-term autonomy. That mix tells you where the business lives today and where the growth is coming from.

> ## From Video Chips To Physical AI
>
> Ambarella started as a video-processing company, and that is important as video is still the foundation of the thesis.
>
> *Video chips → physical AI evolution timeline*
> ![[cruxcapitalgroup-i-took-a-new-position-edge-ai-007.png]]
>
> They were founded around the idea that video is a unique type of data requiring an optimized SoC architecture. Over time that architecture evolved from human-viewing video processors into AI perception chips for the physical world. The evolution runs across three stages.
>
> The first generation was legacy video processors with single-digit ASPs (Average Selling Price). These were mostly designed for human viewing. Basically the chip helped a device capture, process, compress, and stream video so a person could watch it. Think action cameras, security cameras, and other video devices where the main job was making video look clean and reliable.
>
> The second generation was Ambarella's CVflow edge AI chips for CNN workloads, with ASPs ranging from $10 to $75. CVflow is Ambarella's AI processing architecture. CNN stands for convolutional neural network, which is a type of AI model commonly used for image recognition. So this generation let cameras and devices do more than record video. They could start identifying what was inside the video like a person, vehicle, object, face, lane, or movement pattern.
>
> The third generation is where things get more interesting. These are Ambarella's newer CVflow-based chips for more advanced edge AI systems, including CV72, CV75, CV7, CV3-AD, and N1. These chips are built for multi-sensor and multi-modality processing, which means they can handle more than one type of input. That could include multiple cameras, radar, lidar, or other sensors. Instead of helping one camera understand one video feed, these chips can help a robot, vehicle, drone, or edge gateway process several streams of information at once. That is crucial because these systems rarely make decisions from one camera alone. They need to combine multiple inputs to understand what is happening, avoid mistakes, and act in real time. This is why ASPs can move into double-digit to triple-digit territory.
>
> *Three-generation chip evolution — single-digit ASPs → $10–$75 → triple-digits*
> ![[cruxcapitalgroup-i-took-a-new-position-edge-ai-008.png]]
>
> That ASP progression is what makes the current product cycle interesting to me. Ambarella can grow through unit volume of course, but the more imporatnt model is unit growth plus higher content per device. As devices require more AI performance, more sensor inputs, better image quality, radar perception, and local model support, Ambarella has a structural route toward higher revenue per chip.
>
> The software side is also part of their strength. Ambarella's Cooper Development Platform scales across its edge AI portfolio, and management says customers have reached production with more than 200 different model architectures.
>
> Cooper is the toolset that helps customers build and run their AI models on Ambarella's chips. A camera company, drone company, robotics company, or automotive supplier can use it to take its own AI model and make it work efficiently on Ambarella silicon.
>
> So AMBA is selling a development platform customers can build around.
>
> *Cooper Development Platform — software stack across the edge AI portfolio*
> ![[cruxcapitalgroup-i-took-a-new-position-edge-ai-009.png]]
>
> Once a customer has trained its engineers, optimized its models, built software, and launched products around that platform, switching to another chip supplier becomes more expensive and time-consuming.

> ## The Product Cycle
>
> The product cycle is an important near-term lever for the thesis.
>
> Ambarella's new third-generation 5nm CV75 and CV72 AI SoCs already reached a high-single-digit percent of total revenue in Q4 fiscal 2026. The key point is that these are newer, more capable chips. They are designed for more advanced AI workloads at the edge, which means better performance, higher complexity, and potentially higher selling prices than older products.
>
> Management expects CV75 and CV72 to be an important source of incremental fiscal 2027 revenue as they ramp into more customer designs. CV7, the company's first 4nm chip, has already sampled and is expected to begin generating revenue in Q4 fiscal 2027. In semiconductors, moving from 5nm to 4nm generally means a more advanced manufacturing node, which can support better performance and efficiency if execution goes well.
>
> CV72, CV75, CV7, and N1 are the bridge from legacy video into higher-value physical AI systems. N1 is especially important because it targets edge AI infrastructure and more demanding local inference use cases, such as the warehouse perception hub. These are the products that can move Ambarella deeper into robots, drones, vehicles, AI vision boxes, and local edge gateways.
>
> *Third-generation product cycle — CV72/CV75/CV7/N1 bridge to physical AI*
> ![[cruxcapitalgroup-i-took-a-new-position-edge-ai-010.png]]
>
> If that bridge holds, fiscal 2028 can look meaningfully different from what the fiscal 2027 guide implies. The fiscal 2027 guide of 10% to 15% growth looks more like a transition year. The bull case is that the new product cycle starts to show up more clearly in fiscal 2028, with higher-value chips contributing more revenue and newer end markets becoming visible.
>
> There is also a semi-custom ASIC angle worth talking about. A customer may want a custom version of Ambarella's technology built for its own product or AI workload.
>
> Some customers may like Ambarella's AI accelerators, perception engine, software platform, and advanced-node chip design capability, but want those capabilities customized around their own algorithms, sensor setup, or system requirements. I view this as upside optionality rather than the core thesis today, but it could deepen customer lock-in if more design wins appear. It could also represent a higher-value engagement than standard chip sales because Ambarella would be helping a customer build more customized edge AI silicon.

> ## The Three Growth Drivers
>
> Management has been direct about where the biggest long-term opportunities sit:
>
> > "largest long-term growth opportunities in the robotics, automotive and edge infrastructure markets."
> >
> > *Management-cited long-term growth opportunities — robotics, automotive, edge infrastructure*
> > ![[cruxcapitalgroup-i-took-a-new-position-edge-ai-011.png]]
>
> Robotics, drones, and warehouse automation is the most important upside path because it takes Ambarella beyond traditional camera and security markets into faster-growing physical AI applications.
>
> *Robotics, drones, and warehouse automation as the lead growth driver*
> ![[cruxcapitalgroup-i-took-a-new-position-edge-ai-012.png]]
>
> The warehouse automation win is the clearest proof point. Management kept the customer unnamed but was specific about what is happening:
>
> > "They leveraged our N1-655 AI SoC to develop a perception hub for the warehouse floor. A fleet of these systems is being deployed to enable a high speed, accurate and efficient storage and retrieval system at their large-scale warehouses across the country."
>
> That quote does a lot of work. It shows the specific chip, the use case, the customer type, and the deployment status in one sentence. This is edge AI in production at scale, which is exactly what the thesis requires.
>
> The drone angle became more tangible this year as well. Management said Q4 was the company's first full quarter of production revenue from aerial drones, which Ambarella views as one of the highest-volume mobile robotic markets today. If robotics and drones become visible revenue drivers over the next two years, AMBA starts to look like a real physical AI platform.
>
> Automotive is the larger, slower-cycle opportunity.
>
> *Automotive opportunity — safety, telematics, ADAS today; CV3-AD radar/perception longer-term*
> ![[cruxcapitalgroup-i-took-a-new-position-edge-ai-013.png]]
>
> Ambarella's current auto revenue is mostly tied to safety, telematics, and ADAS. That means vehicle cameras and perception systems used for dash cams, fleet monitoring, e-mirrors, surround view, driver monitoring, blind-spot alerts, and forward safety features.
>
> The longer-term auto opportunity is more advanced autonomy. Ambarella's CV3-AD family targets higher-level vehicle perception systems, including central domain controllers and 4D radar software. That is where their VisLab and Oculii acquisitions become relevant. VisLab added autonomous-driving software expertise, while Oculii added radar perception. So the auto story is about Ambarella trying to build a more complete vehicle perception platform.
>
> Management said the auto opportunities it has either won or been invited to bid upon from fiscal 2027 through fiscal 2032 are approximately $13 billion. That is an opportunity pipeline rather than booked revenue of course. Still, it shows Ambarella is being pulled into larger programs across a multi-year horizon.
>
> Edge infrastructure is the most abstract growth driver, but it could be important if N1 ramps. When I say edge infrastructure here, I am talking about inference appliances, AI vision boxes, and gateways tied to sensor workloads.
>
> Ambarella is seeing two customer architectures emerge. The first is physical AI at a local edge gateway, where enterprise buyers aggregate multi-modal data from multiple sensors, pre-process it locally, and use it for fleet management, physical security, industrial robots, and related use cases. The second is digital AI at the edge, where centrally trained models are distilled, quantized, and pushed into edge nodes for low-latency automation while the cloud retains centralized control.
>
> This is the expansion path from endpoint cameras into AI vision boxes, edge gateways, and on-prem AI servers. It is also where AMBA needs to prove the category can become material beyond early proof points.

> ## Financial Setup
>
> Ambarella reports on a January fiscal year, so my calendar year-end 2027 targets are based on what investors may be willing to pay for fiscal 2028 revenue.
>
> Management guided fiscal 2027 revenue growth of 10% to 15%, which implies roughly $430M to $449M of revenue from the $390.7M fiscal 2026 base. The long-term non-GAAP gross margin model is 59% to 62%.
>
> *Financial setup — FY27 guide, gross margin model, scenario inputs*
> ![[cruxcapitalgroup-i-took-a-new-position-edge-ai-014.png]]
>
> I am using a simple EV/revenue framework because AMBA is still early in this edge AI product cycle. The two main variables are revenue growth and the multiple investors assign to that growth. These are scenario ranges, rather than exact price targets.
>
> In the bear case, AMBA grows near the low end of fiscal 2027 guidance and then slows again in fiscal 2028. Robotics, drones, and edge infrastructure remain promising, but still too early to change the growth profile. In that scenario, the market treats AMBA like a solid semiconductor company with edge AI exposure, rather than a true physical AI platform. My bear case is ~$50 per share.
>
> In the base case, fiscal 2027 is a transition year and fiscal 2028 starts to show real contribution from CV72, CV75, CV7, N1, drones, robotics, and steady auto growth. Revenue grows around 20% in fiscal 2028, and the market gives AMBA credit for becoming an emerging physical AI platform. My base case is $90 to $95 per share.
>
> In the bull case, the new product cycle works better than expected. CV7 ramps cleanly, N1 gains real edge infrastructure traction, the warehouse robotics win expands, drone revenue becomes more visible, and auto remains steady. Fiscal 2028 revenue grows 30% or more, and investors begin valuing AMBA as one of the cleaner small-cap physical AI beneficiaries. My bull case is $125 to $135 per share.
>
> At roughly a $3.1B market cap, AMBA already has some edge AI upside priced in. The base case still offers attractive upside, but the real asymmetry comes from the bull case if the new product cycle proves out.

> ## The Risks
>
> I would rank them this way.
>
> *Ranked risk list — valuation, growth deceleration, channel concentration, China exposure, robotics timing, competition*
> ![[cruxcapitalgroup-i-took-a-new-position-edge-ai-015.png]]
>
> Valuation is usually on my mind. At roughly 8x fiscal 2026 sales, AMBA already trades like investors see part of the setup. The stock needs execution.
>
> Growth deceleration is second. Fiscal 2026 revenue grew 37.2%. Fiscal 2027 guidance is 10% to 15%. The stock needs proof that the next leg can reaccelerate, and that proof requires new markets to start contributing in a visible way.
>
> Channel concentration is third and probably the most underappreciated risk in the story. WT Microelectronics, a fulfillment partner in Taiwan shipping to multiple customers in Asia, represented 73.1% of Q4 revenue and 69.7% of fiscal 2026 revenue. That concentration creates real supply-chain and geopolitical fragility that is easy to overlook when the top-line momentum is strong.
>
> China and export exposure is fourth. Ambarella has already dealt with geopolitical revenue shocks. Export controls, tariffs, Entity List risk, and the risk that China customers build dual supply chains are all real considerations. The Cantor deck flags this directly and it deserves weight in the model.
>
> Robotics and edge infrastructure timing is fifth. The use cases are promising but the revenue ramp still needs proof. One warehouse win and one quarter of drone production revenue are encouraging starting points, not a durable cycle.
>
> Competition is sixth. Ambarella competes with larger semiconductor companies across security, automotive, drones, and edge AI, including companies with broader platforms, larger engineering teams, and deeper customer relationships. The edge AI chip market is still early enough that competition will intensify as the opportunity becomes more obvious.

> ## What I Am Watching
>
> The next few quarters should answer the questions that matter most for the thesis.
>
> CV72 and CV75 revenue contribution. These third-generation products already reached a high-single-digit percent of Q4 revenue. I want to see that percentage keep rising as more customer designs ramp into production.
>
> CV7 launch timing. The 4nm chip has sampled and is expected to generate revenue in Q4 fiscal 2027. Any pull-forward in that timing would be a positive signal. Any delay would be worth understanding carefully.
>
> N1 adoption in edge infrastructure and warehouse automation. The e-commerce robotics win is the proof-of-concept. The question is whether it expands and whether other enterprise customers follow with similar deployments.
>
> Drone revenue growth. Q4 was the first full production quarter. I want to see that category grow as a visible contributor over the next two to three quarters.
>
> Auto pipeline conversion. The $13 billion opportunity figure is large. I want to see specific programs moving from invited-to-bid into awarded status and then into design phase.
>
> Gross margin stability inside the 59% to 62% model. If the product mix shifts toward higher-ASP third-generation chips, margins should hold or improve. Any sustained compression below the model range would raise questions about pricing dynamics and competitive pressure.
>
> WT concentration and China export commentary. I will be listening carefully for any customer shifts in sourcing, any new export restrictions, or any language suggesting the distribution structure is evolving.
>
> If AMBA shows progress across most of these, it can become one of the cleaner ways to own physical AI adoption in public markets.
