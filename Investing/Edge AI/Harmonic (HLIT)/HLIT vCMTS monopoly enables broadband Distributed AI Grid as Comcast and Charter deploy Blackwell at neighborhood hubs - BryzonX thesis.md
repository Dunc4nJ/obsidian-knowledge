# HLIT vCMTS monopoly enables broadband Distributed AI Grid as Comcast and Charter deploy Blackwell at neighborhood hubs - BryzonX thesis

## Key takeaways

- BryzonX frames [[Harmonic (HLIT)]] as the broadband-side mirror of the [[NVIDIA (NVDA)]]–Nokia (NOK) anyRAN trade: where anyRAN turns cell towers into mini data centers via software, vCMTS turns cable hubs into AI-ready endpoints — both routes are software-only upgrades on infrastructure operators already own, which is what makes them investable vs. tearing copper out of the ground.
- The catalyst is structural: at GTC 2026 Comcast and Charter publicly committed to moving [[NVIDIA (NVDA)]] RTX 6000 Blackwell GPUs out of distant data centers into local "neighborhood hubs," and the thesis claims HLIT has a vCMTS *monopoly* — meaning the AI Edge buildout in cable is gated on Harmonic's stack rather than competed away.
- Numbers cited: $570M backlog up 77% YoY, 3.5 book-to-bill in Q4 2025, against a $1.2B market cap — i.e. backlog ≈ half of market cap on a single-quarter print. The virtualization (SaaS) segment is growing 33% YoY at 55% gross margin while the legacy video business was divested, which is what produces the "headline numbers look horrendous because of the mix shift" framing.
- TAM/SAM frame: $5.18B CMTS TAM with only 15–20% of global broadband virtualized today; HLIT at ~$450M revenue has captured under 10% of immediate SAM despite the alleged monopoly — i.e. the thesis is not "win share" but "the category itself is pre-inflection."
- Two policy tailwinds layered on the CapEx cycle: (1) FCC "Delete Delete Delete" starting May 8, 2026 — a legal mandate to stop maintaining 30-year-old copper, which redirects MSO maintenance CapEx into network modernization; (2) $42B in federal BEAD grants for rural broadband, where deploy-speed favors a software upgrade over physical hub builds. The de-risking on customer concentration ("rest of world" now 41% of revenue, growing 33% YoY) is presented as the final piece — HLIT positioned as the global vCMTS standard, not a US-only Comcast/Charter dependency.
