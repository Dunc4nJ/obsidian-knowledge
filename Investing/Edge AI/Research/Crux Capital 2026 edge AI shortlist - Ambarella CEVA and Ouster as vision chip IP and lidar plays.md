# Crux Capital 2026 edge AI shortlist - Ambarella CEVA and Ouster as vision chip IP and lidar plays

## Key Takeaways

- Edge AI's investable surface is widening as small models, AI-sensor cameras, private wireless, and commercial drones/robots converge — the next phase of AI infrastructure connects more of the physical world to centralized compute rather than replacing it. The thesis is "physical and industrial edge AI" in factories, warehouses, ports, vehicles, drones, robots, and industrial equipment.
- [[Ambarella (AMBA)]] is the clearest pure-play vision-AI SoC story with hard proof: $390.7M FY26 revenue (+37% YoY), 42M+ SoCs shipped, ~80% of revenue from edge AI, drones now in full production, and an unnamed warehouse-robotics design-win in low-volume production. Risk is ~8x sales valuation against guided 10–15% growth — robotics + edge-infrastructure optionality has to justify the multiple.
- [[CEVA (CEVA)]] is the slower IP-royalty bet. 10 NPU agreements signed in 2025 (incl. a strategic deal with a top PC OEM building next-gen AI personal compute). Six recent NPU customers expected to enter production by end of 2026, with royalty contribution from 2027. The risk is timing — signed agreements have to convert into shipped volume.
- [[Ouster (OUST)]] is the lidar/perception play. Standalone lidar hardware has been a graveyard for investors; the bull case requires Ouster transitioning from hardware-only lidar to a sensor-plus-perception-software platform with recurring industrial / smart-infrastructure deployments.
- The three names map to distinct edge layers — local brain (AMBA), brain components for others (CEVA), eyes / 3D perception (OUST) — so picking one is a bet on which layer captures the most edge-AI value capture first. Crux is finalizing a position on a single name, with the full thesis dropping within 48 hours of this post.

> ## Why Now?
>
> We know that AI models are getting smaller and more efficient. Cameras are becoming AI sensors. Industrial customers are adopting private wireless and local compute. Drones and robots are moving into more commercial use cases. Companies are trying to reduce the cost, latency, and bandwidth of sending everything to the cloud.
>
> *Examples of emerging edge AI applications — Nest/AWS smart cameras, on-device facial recognition (Huawei/Apple), Tesla on-board AI driving, Intel vision for robots and other devices*
> ![[cruxcapitalgroup-i-want-to-invest-here-edge-ai-003.webp]]
>
> None of these trends alone creates a full investment thesis but they point toward more intelligence moving into devices and physical systems.
>
> Edge AI expands the AI surface area rather than replacing cloud compute. A smart camera may process video locally, but the useful clips, alerts, metadata, and patterns still flow into larger systems. A warehouse robot may make local decisions, but its performance data still matters to the broader automation platform. More intelligent endpoints create more data, and more data still needs to be stored, analyzed, secured, and used to improve models.
>
> The phase of AI infrastructure that we are in right now is about building the centralized compute layer. The next phase may be about connecting more of the physical world to that intelligence.
>
> The part I find most compelling at this time is physical and industrial edge AI. Factories, warehouses, ports, logistics yards, vehicles, drones, robots, and industrial equipment. These are places where AI creates a direct operating benefit like better safety, less downtime, faster inspection, more automation, earlier detection of problems.
>
> In the past I have dug into military applications of edge and I think that is an interesting space as well, but for this round of research I am going to stay focused on the aforementioned industries.

> ## The Three Companies I Am Researching
>
> The three names are Ambarella, CEVA, and Ouster.
>
> Each one sits at a different layer of the edge AI stack. Ambarella provides the local vision AI processor. CEVA licenses reusable AI, sensing, and connectivity building blocks to chipmakers and OEMs. Ouster sells lidar sensors and perception software that help machines understand the world in 3D.
>
> I am still pressure-testing all three before finalizing the position.

> ## Ambarella
>
> [[Ambarella (AMBA)]] is the clearest direct edge AI chip play I have found, that is at a point in development that I am comfortable with.
>
> *Ambarella's "AI Vision Processors For Edge Applications" hero — extracting structured data (signs, objects, vehicles) from raw video streams*
> ![[cruxcapitalgroup-i-want-to-invest-here-edge-ai-004.png]]
>
> They build low-power vision AI processors that go inside cameras, drones, robots, vehicles, security systems, and industrial edge devices. The core idea is that a camera captures video. Ambarella's chip processes the image, runs AI locally, and sends the useful output rather than the raw footage. A person detected near a restricted area. A vehicle stopped in the wrong place. A worker entering an unsafe zone. Whatever it may be.
>
> The same logic applies to drones, robots, vehicles, and industrial systems. These devices need to see and react locally. Ambarella wants to provide the chip platform that makes that possible.
>
> *Ambarella's automotive positioning — "From ADAS to Autonomy" with 360-degree object detection at long and short range*
> ![[cruxcapitalgroup-i-want-to-invest-here-edge-ai-005.png]]
>
> What makes Ambarella stand out is that this is already a commercial business. Fiscal 2026 revenue was $390.7 million, up 37.2% year over year. The company has shipped more than 42 million edge AI SoCs, generated roughly $1 billion in cumulative edge AI revenue, and has more than 370 customer AI products in production. That gives it a level of proof that many edge AI stories lack. Management said the largest long-term growth opportunities are robotics, automotive, and edge infrastructure.
>
> Q4 was the company's first full quarter of production revenue from aerial drones. There was also a warehouse robotics win already in low-volume production. Management kept the customer unnamed, but said the win validates Ambarella's perception system inside a large organization. That is the type of datapoint that is nice to hear, with edge AI moving from theory into production use cases at scale.
>
> The risk worth flagging is valuation. At roughly a $3.1 billion market cap against $390.7 million of fiscal 2026 revenue, Ambarella trades around 8x sales. Fiscal 2027 growth guidance is 10% to 15%, which is solid but slower than the 37% delivered last year. Competition is real. Auto design cycles can stretch. I am still working through whether the valuation is justified by the robotics and edge infrastructure optionality. Since this is a relatively new theme for me to research, I need to see what multiple the market has been placing on these companies.

> ## CEVA
>
> So [[Ambarella (AMBA)]] sells chips. [[CEVA (CEVA)]] sells reusable chip building blocks that other companies place inside their own silicon. An NPU block for AI inference, a DSP for audio or sensor processing, Bluetooth IP, Wi-Fi IP, cellular IoT IP. The customer integrates those pieces into its own chip design and then pays CEVA royalties when products ship.
>
> *CEVA's "Powering the Smart Edge" scale slide — 60 new CEVA-powered devices per second, 2B shipped annually, 21B cumulative*
> ![[cruxcapitalgroup-i-want-to-invest-here-edge-ai-006.png]]
>
> If Ambarella is the finished edge AI brain, CEVA is selling pieces of the brain to other companies building their own.
>
> The most important recent development is AI licensing momentum. CEVA signed 10 NPU agreements in 2025, including a strategic deal with one of the world's leading PC OEMs building a next-generation AI personal compute architecture. They shipped 2.1 billion CEVA-powered devices in 2025 and passed 21 billion cumulative devices by end of Q4.
>
> *CEVA's silicon and software IP portfolio — Connect (Bluetooth/Wi-Fi/UWB/Cellular), Sense (vision/radar/audio/voice/motion), Infer (embedded ML / classic AI / generative AI) across consumer IoT, automotive, industrial, infrastructure, PC, and mobile*
> ![[cruxcapitalgroup-i-want-to-invest-here-edge-ai-007.png]]
>
> The investment setup is a potential royalty flywheel. If more companies build their own chips for AI PCs, wearables, smart glasses, industrial devices, and automotive systems, they may need reusable IP blocks, and CEVA can collect royalties when those products ship. Management said six NPU customers signed over the last one to two years should be ready for production by end of 2026, with royalty contribution potentially starting in 2027.
>
> The risk is timing. A lot of the AI upside still sits in front of the company. The royalty ramp has to convert from signed agreements into shipped volume. That is a longer-duration bet than Ambarella.
>

>
> ## Ouster
>
> If Ambarella is the local brain and CEVA is the IP inside other brains, [[Ouster (OUST)]] is closer to the eyes. They make digital lidar sensors and perception software. Lidar helps machines understand the world in 3D, measuring depth, distance, shape, and movement in ways cameras struggle to handle alone. Traffic systems, smart cities, industrial sites, logistics yards, robots, security systems, and infrastructure monitoring are all potential use cases.
>
> *Ouster positioning as "Physical AI's First Unified Sensing and Perception Platform"*
> ![[cruxcapitalgroup-i-want-to-invest-here-edge-ai-008.png]]
>
> The edge AI thesis here is that Physical AI needs perception. Some applications need more than a camera can provide. Ouster sits in that sensing layer.
>
> They are trying to move beyond hardware-only lidar toward a broader perception platform, which I think is the right direction because hardware-only lidar companies have been difficult investments. The better version of Ouster is a company that sells sensors, attaches software, and deploys into recurring industrial and infrastructure use cases.
>
> The risk is defeinitely there as well. Lidar companies have disappointed investors before. Automotive timelines get stretched easily. Hardware margins are difficult. Customer adoption in smart infrastructure and logistics could still be early.
