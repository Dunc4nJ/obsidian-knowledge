*Verbatim from <https://cruxcapitalgroup.substack.com/p/we-need-to-talk-about-bzai>*

While I was doing my research into Edge companies, I found one that I really liked.

And now with Nokia getting major traction on X, I think that this company will start to get some exposure as well.

So I wanted to do this quick post before market opens and just show you guys what I am thinking.

My #1 Edge pick will still be announced later today.

So this is a quick impromptu post.

But my goal is to keep you guys in the loop in what I'm seeing.

**Blaize, ticker BZAI**

The setup is pretty easy to see:

Small-cap AI infrastructure name.
Edge inference.
Nokia relationship.
Hybrid AI deployments.
Low market cap.
Big revenue guide.
Fresh contract headlines.

Nokia itself is getting a lot more attention after Nvidia agreed to invest $1B into the company to accelerate AI-RAN and the transition from 5G to 6G. And they recently crushed earnings and are being seen more as an optics play. Also, Nokia's AI-RAN story is becoming much easier for investors to understand, and I think people will naturally start looking for smaller companies attached to that ecosystem.

That is where BZAI gets interesting.

Blaize is focused on programmable, energy-efficient AI inference. So basically this means running AI workloads closer to where the data is created like telecom networks, factories, cameras, smart cities, public safety systems, retail locations, logistics networks, industrial equipment, and local enterprise infrastructure.

The bull case is that inference keeps spreading outward from centralized data centers into the real world. As that happens, power efficiency, cost per inference, latency, and deployment flexibility become more important. That is the area Blaize is trying to attack.

The Nokia connection gives the story a much cleaner narrative as well.

Blaize recently announced a three-way strategic alliance with Nokia and Datacomm to deliver hybrid AI inference infrastructure across Indonesia and Southeast Asia. The release says Nokia's Singapore Innovation Lab validated a joint Nokia-Blaize reference architecture, and Datacomm cited a 50%+ increase in customer AI inference demand over the past six months.

So I think Nokia is a big part of the narrative. NeoTensr / APAC deployments are the near-term proof.

On the Q4 call, management made it sound like Nokia and the AI Services platform should start contributing more toward the end of 2026. So I would wouldn't assume Nokia is what supports the current year guide today. The Nokia relationship is important though because it gives BZAI a much cleaner story and potentially opens up strategic opportunities. But the near-term numbers still need to come from existing partners, purchase orders, deployments, and cash collection.

The revenue setup is also why this can get attention quickly. Blaize reported $38.6M of revenue in 2025, up from $1.6M in 2024. Q4 revenue alone was $23.8M, and management has guided to $130M of revenue for 2026. That is a massive step up.

So the surface-level thesis is this:

If BZAI comes anywhere close to that 2026 guide, the stock could re-rate quickly because the market might still be treating this like a fragile microcap instead of a real AI infrastructure ramp.

That said, I am treating this as a speculative setup that still needs proof, rather than my top Edge pick.

The biggest near-term issue is Q1, NeoTensr, and whether the 2026 guide is actually supported by real revenue conversion.

Management said expected Q1 revenue was only $2.7M, while still maintaining the full-year $130M guide. They blamed memory inventory and supply-chain timing, and said they had secured inventory for $10M-$12M of deliveries in late April and May.

That gives us a very clear metric to track.

If Q2 shows real acceleration, the Q1 issue starts to look like timing. If Q2 stays weak, the guide becomes much harder to trust.

NeoTensr is the bigger diligence question.

Blaize announced a new NeoTensr contract expected to generate up to $50M within the first year. They also said NeoTensr generated more than $20M of revenue for Blaize in Q4 2025.

That sounds very bullish on the surface for sure.

But there is also a short report from Pelican Way Research questioning the quality of that relationship. Their main argument is that NeoTensr's website and entity formation appear to have happened in December 2025, right around the same time Blaize says NeoTensr generated more than $20M of Q4 revenue. They also question NeoTensr's capital base and some of the product imagery.

I am treating those claims as allegations, rather than proven facts. Pelican is short the stock, so I am also reading it through that lens.

But I think I would be making a mistake to ignore it.

And it is worth pointing out that Pelican is not the only short report here. The broader short-seller argument is that Blaize has announced large APAC contracts, but investors still need to see follow-through in the form of purchase orders, cash collection, repeat deployments, and customer diversification.

That is why NeoTensr is such an important proof point.

For me, the question is:

Did NeoTensr generate real revenue, has that revenue turned into cash, and are there follow-on purchase orders behind the new $50M contract headline?

If the answer is yes, then BZAI becomes a much more interesting setup.

If the answer is unclear, then the stock can still move on sentiment, but I would be much more cautious treating it as a longer-term investment or going in with size.

The second thing I am watching is revenue quality. Blaize's 10-K shows very concentrated 2025 revenue, with Customer D at 61.5% and Customer C at 27.0% of total revenue.

That concentration is normal for some early commercial ramps, but it means I want to see broader customer validation before treating this as a cleaner long-term compounder.

The third thing is margin mix. A meaningful portion of 2025 revenue came from third-party hardware products. That can help revenue scale, but I want evidence that Blaize can move toward more valuable platform revenue over time.

On the Q4 call, management said gross margin was 11% in Q4 and 16% for the full year. They also said they expect Blaize hardware and software to become a higher mix in the second half of 2026, with Q4 gross margin moving toward 30%-35%.

That is another clear scoreboard.

If margins start moving higher, that supports the idea that this is turning into a real Blaize platform story. If margins stay low, then the market may keep treating the revenue as lower-quality hardware resale.

The fourth thing is cash. Blaize had a large operating loss and meaningful cash burn in 2025, so cash collection and funding risk need to stay on the watchlist. They ended 2025 with $46M in cash and also filed a $250M shelf, which gives them flexibility but also keeps dilution risk on the table if the ramp slips.

The fifth thing is Nokia.

I like the Nokia angle a lot, but I want to separate the narrative from the numbers.

Nokia is the clean story that can get investors interested. NeoTensr and the existing APAC deployments are the near-term proof. On the Q4 call, management made it sound like Nokia and the AI Services platform should start contributing more toward the end of 2026. So I would be careful assuming Nokia is what supports the current year guide today.

That does not make the Nokia relationship unimportant. It just means I want to see it move from reference architecture, joint go-to-market language, and Datacomm partnership headlines into actual commercial adoption.

So here is my current view:

I like BZAI.

I think it has a real shot to move on sentiment if investors start connecting Blaize with Nokia, AI-RAN, edge inference, hybrid AI infrastructure, and the broader move from training-heavy AI toward distributed inference.

But this is still a prove-it story.

I took a small position in my non-optics portfolio. But I want confirmation to size in towards 5%+.

My checklist is:

**Q2 revenue acceleration**
Prove Q1 was timing.

**NeoTensr conversion**
Show that the announced contract activity turns into revenue, purchase orders, and cash.

**Customer diversification**
Reduce reliance on a couple large customers.

**Better margin mix**
Show more Blaize platform revenue, less low-margin third-party hardware dependence.

**Nokia/Datacomm deployment traction**
Move from reference architecture and alliance language into visible commercial adoption.

**Cash collection**
Prove the revenue is collectible, especially around the large APAC customers.

That is the setup.

BZAI has the ingredients for a high-upside small-cap AI move. The narrative is strong, the Nokia connection gives investors something easy to understand, and the 2026 guide creates a major re-rating path if management delivers.

But the company still needs to prove the guide, prove the customer quality, and prove the revenue can turn into cash.

For now, I view BZAI as a speculative AI infrastructure setup with a real sentiment catalyst, rather than a fully proven long-term compounder.

That is why I started small.
