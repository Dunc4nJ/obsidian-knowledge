## Key Takeaways

**The core thesis:** Most autoresearch work focuses on building better agent researchers; Paradegma argues the real bottleneck is the *organization layer* that structures what agents discover. They call the fundamental unit of science not the paper but the experiment — and encode the full research process as a directed acyclic graph (DAG) where every node carries its hypothesis, parent experiments, and results. The paper is treated as "fanfiction of the graph," a narrative rendering of the underlying DAG structure. See [[autoresearch lets an AI agent run ML experiments autonomously overnight]] for the single-loop baseline that Paradegma's infrastructure is designed to supersede.

**Replication as first-class branching:** By modeling experiments as branchable git nodes, replication and extension collapse into the same operation. To challenge a result you branch the suspect node, rerun it, and publish the contradiction as a connected subgraph — far lower friction than writing a rebuttal paper. This directly addresses the quality-control problem [[autoresearch agents exploit unconstrained metrics and need multi-objective gates with regular human steering]] raises about reward-hacking and hill-climbing research.

**MCP as the universal agent adapter:** Flywheel exposes the entire DAG via MCP, so any agent framework (Codex, Claude Code, ChatGPT) can traverse, extend, or prove nodes without vendor lock-in. The skill layer (auto-research skill, look-ahead skill, paper-to-graph skill) sits on top and ships as composable MCP tools. This is the infrastructure abstraction layer that [[the autoresearch loop generalizes beyond ML training into a universal pattern for autonomous agent research]] points toward but doesn't implement.

**The discovery-per-joule framing:** Francesco frames Paradegma's mission around maximizing "discoveries per joule/compute" — the insight that a bad initial idea in a long-horizon experiment wastes disproportionate compute because research graphs are highly nonlinear from initial conditions. The ideator model (planned) targets this by improving *research taste*, the ability to foresee which DAG branches are worth pursuing. Compare [[distributed research swarms close the feedback loop that single-agent autoresearch leaves open]] for the multi-agent exploration angle.

**Autonomous review as the quality gate:** Rather than mechanical code-to-experiment verification, Paradegma is building adversarial review agents that challenge nodes within the DAG. The incentive structure they envision mirrors GPU-powered peer review: researchers who reproduce a result earn fractional ownership of that graph, with downstream monetization when the work generates commercial value. This closes the loop that [[three architectural fixes let AIRA2 scale AI research agents past the 24-hour performance plateau to SOTA on MLE-bench]] leaves open around result trust.

**Horizon — the global scientific knowledge graph:** The longer-term roadmap ("Horizon") involves parsing all existing papers into DAG equivalents and connecting them into a world-scale graph of scientific knowledge. The paper-to-graph skill released at time of interview is the first step. The flywheel campaigns feature (sponsors post a challenge, community spends compute) is the incentive mechanism to seed it. This parallels what [[CORAL multi-agent co-evolution beats OpenEvolve by 20% on Anthropic's kernel engineering task]] does for kernel optimization but generalized to all of science.

**Human role in the agentic research era:** Both founders converge on the same answer: humans will be bottlenecked by ideas, not execution. The PI role becomes strategic resource allocation — an RTS-game view of the graph where you direct compute to promising subgraphs. PhD students need to develop taste for long-tail, high-risk directions because the exploitation-heavy work gets delegated. Auto-research doesn't eliminate researchers; it moves the bottleneck from engineering throughput to idea quality.
