# AutoResearchClaw is a 23-stage autonomous pipeline that turns a research idea into a conference-ready paper

## Key Takeaways

The tweet frames AutoResearchClaw as the culmination of several concurrent agent infrastructure drops: MiroFish for swarm simulations, OpenViking for structured agent memory, Percepta for embedding deterministic math into transformer weights, and Andrew Ng's Context Hub for self-annotating documentation. Together they form a stack where agents can research, simulate, verify with real math, and produce rigorous outputs — the thread argues this is how you'd build a next-gen prediction market bot that actually discovers and proves things rather than guessing.

AutoResearchClaw itself is a fully autonomous 23-stage pipeline from aiming-lab. You feed it a research idea, and it produces a conference-ready paper with real literature from OpenAlex/Semantic Scholar/arXiv, sandbox experiments with self-healing code, 4-layer citation verification (no hallucinated refs), and LaTeX formatted for NeurIPS/ICML/ICLR. The [[autoresearch lets an AI agent run ML experiments autonomously overnight|autoresearch pattern]] — tight constraints, single-metric optimization, edit-one-file loops — is taken to its logical extreme here across all 23 stages.

The author's deeper point is a paradox: when generating papers costs nothing, the bottleneck shifts from production to filtering. Peer reviewers become the scarce resource, not paper authors. This echoes the broader [[tight constraints make autonomous agents more useful than open-ended freedom|constraint-amplifies-agents]] principle — the value isn't in the generator, it's in the quality gate.
