# Frontier Mathematics Target-Selection Evidence Ledger

Date: 2026-08-27  
Campaign mode: W4 priority and novelty selection, mathematics primary, ML/quant relevance scored separately  
Status: Round 2 material increment; target selection only; no mathematical result is claimed

## Decision boundary

This ledger answers a narrower question than “what are the greatest open problems?”:

> Which live mathematical targets combine enough consequence to attract serious attention with a statement and verification path that an AI-assisted research campaign can attack honestly?

The ranking is not a probability that a problem will be solved. It is a campaign judgment combining:

1. consequence if the exact target moves;
2. crispness of the finish line;
3. independent verifiability;
4. existence of a meaningful first rung;
5. tractable search or proof interfaces;
6. crowding and stale-frontier risk;
7. direct ML, training, quant, cryptography, or systems relevance.

Two leaderboards are necessary. “Headline ceiling” and “expected campaign value” are not the same variable. Ramsey R(5,5) has a far higher public ceiling than rectilinear K28, while rectilinear K28 has a one-bit gap, a compact positive certificate, and much lower apparent crowding.

## Epistemic disclosure

- The source census used primary papers, official proceedings, maintained expert surveys, and current curated tables through 2026-08-27.
- Three adversarial/scouting reviews were run. All were same-family OpenAI/GPT-5 reviews and therefore count only as T1 critique, not independent T2 corroboration.
- “No later solution found” means NO_PRIOR_FOUND under the logged searches. It never means proof that no paper, preprint, private manuscript, or newly posted result exists.
- Positive construction certificates and negative/nonexistence certificates are treated separately. A tiny witness does not imply that exhaustive nonexistence is easy.
- ML or quant “relevance” is not operational benefit. Several targets illuminate representation, algorithms, or verification without implying faster training, better forecasts, or deployable alpha.

## Headline-ceiling leaderboard

This ordering ignores probability of success and asks only how consequential a clean resolution would be:

1. R(5,5).
2. An 8-bit APN permutation.
3. Exact field-pinned 3x3 matrix-multiplication rank, or a new rank-22 upper bound.
4. The terminal shallow-ReLU depth theorem.
5. The five-dimensional kissing number.
6. Hadwiger–Nelson: a 6-chromatic unit-distance graph or a 5-coloring.
7. Distribution-independent SQ versus dimension complexity.
8. Rectilinear K28.
9. Costas-32.
10. Exact minimum Kochen–Specker size.

The first six are true moonshots. Rectilinear K28 and Costas-32 are better pilot campaigns because a positive result has a compact exact witness and a sharply bounded verifier.

## Recommended three-lane portfolio

### Lane A — quiet exact pilot

Choose one:

- Rectilinear K28 for the best one-bit gap and low apparent crowding.
- Costas-32 for the cleanest applied construction and smallest verifier.
- Weighted regression data selection for the best theorem-shaped probability of a legitimate intermediate result.

### Lane B — ML/theory flagship

Choose one:

- Terminal shallow-ReLU depth, using max11 only as the first experimental rung.
- Distribution-independent SQ versus dimension complexity.
- Fixed-dimensional attention exponent.

### Lane C — high-ceiling moonshot

Choose one:

- R(5,5).
- APN-8.
- Field-pinned 3x3 tensor rank.
- Five-dimensional kissing number.

Running all targets as one undifferentiated search would destroy falsifiability. Each launch needs a separate problem specification, cousin register, certificate contract, and stop rule.

## Evidence cards

### E-001 — shallow ReLU / CPWL

- Claim: max_n has an exact two-hidden-layer ReLU representation for n at most 10; every CPWL function on R^d inherits the result through d at most 9.
- Primary source: Rueß et al., “Shallower ReLU Network Representations via Exact Linear Algebra,” [arXiv:2607.21651](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21651).
- Local locator: papers/frontier-math-targets/core/2607.21651.txt:30, 62–101, 118–128.
- Evidence direction: supports a live next rung and exact-algebra verification.
- Qualification: unrestricted width and real weights; failure of the paper’s ansatz is not a universal lower bound.
- Confidence: high on theorem scope; freshness remains search-qualified.

### E-002 — 3x3 bilinear rank

- Claim: characteristic-general bounds remain 19 through 23; over F2 a 2026 certified result improves the lower bound to 20. A separate rank-23 algorithm uses 55 additions.
- Primary sources: [arXiv:2603.07280](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07280) and [arXiv:2607.28676](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28676).
- Local locators: core/2603.07280.txt:56, 97–127; core/2607.28676.txt:33–60.
- Evidence direction: supports the target but refutes the earlier wording “rank 22 determines exact rank.”
- Qualification: a rank-22 decomposition over Q proves only R_Q at most 22. It also does not beat Strassen recursion asymptotically: log base 3 of 22 is about 2.813, versus log base 2 of 7 about 2.807.
- Confidence: high.

### E-003 — R(5,5)

- Claim: 43 at most R(5,5) at most 46.
- Primary sources: Radziszowski’s 2026 [Small Ramsey Numbers survey](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/viewFile/DS1/pdf) and Angeltveit–McKay [arXiv:2409.15709](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.15709).
- Local locators: core/small-ramsey-numbers-2026.txt:2464; core/2409.15709.txt:25, 154–159, 573–575.
- Evidence direction: supports exact status and demonstrates verification/computation cost.
- Qualification: the 46 upper-bound project used about 30 CPU-years and about 50 more for independent replication; the authors say the same method is unlikely to move the bound without new theory.
- Confidence: high.

### E-004 — transformer rank versus depth

- Claim: Conjecture 6 rules out one fixed-size, fixed-weight rank-r<d multi-layer transformer approximating the paper’s target for every N.
- Primary source: Amsel, Yehudai, and Bruna, [ICLR 2025 paper](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/9c537882044c8b5352c363e840872ddb-Paper-Conference.pdf).
- Local locator: core/iclr2025-quality-over-quantity.txt:573–578, 660–662.
- Evidence direction: supports the exact conjecture and refutes the broader phrase “full rank is necessary for every sufficiently long fixed sequence.”
- Qualification: for each fixed N, model size may depend on N. Fixed-depth/fixed-N results are cousins unless they preserve the conjecture’s quantifiers.
- Confidence: high.

### E-005 — five-dimensional kissing number

- Claim: 40 at most tau_5 at most 44; multiple nonisometric 40-point configurations are known.
- Primary/current sources: Cohn–Rajagopal [arXiv:2412.00937](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.00937) and Cohn’s maintained [kissing-number table](https://cohn.mit.edu/kissing-numbers/).
- Local locator: core/2412.00937.txt:107–110, 194–198.
- Evidence direction: supports live status and construction diversity.
- Qualification: a floating-point Gram matrix or numerical SDP objective is not a certificate.
- Confidence: high.

### E-006 — fixed-dimensional softmax attention

- Claim: for constant d, approximate attention has an approximately n^(2−1/d) time algorithm; near-quadratic SETH hardness in the same paper begins only when d grows as 2 to an Omega(log-star n) scale.
- Primary source: Gupta et al., [arXiv:2505.14840](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14840).
- Local locator: expanded/2505.14840.txt:24–25, 139–193.
- Evidence direction: supports a genuine exponent gap.
- Qualification: B, epsilon, arithmetic/bit model, randomness, and preprocessing must be pinned; constant theoretical d is not a demonstrated production speedup.
- Confidence: high.

### E-007 — rectilinear K28

- Claim: exact values are known through n=27; the maintained current interval for K28 is 7,233 through 7,234.
- Primary/current sources: Ábrego et al., [DOI 10.1016/j.endm.2008.01.045](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.endm.2008.01.045); Schaefer’s 2026 [Crossing Numbers survey](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/download/DS21/pdf); maintained [OEIS A014540](https://oeis.org/A014540).
- Local locators: expanded/rectilinear-k27-2008.txt:10032–10075; expanded/crossing-number-survey-2026.txt:4232–4240.
- Evidence direction: supports exact known range through 27; current one-bit K28 interval is supported by maintained expert/curated sources.
- Qualification: the current interval was not independently recomputed. A nonrealizable oriented matroid does not supply a planar point construction.
- Confidence: 0.93 on status, with the stated source-quality caveat.

### E-008 — Costas-32

- Claim: all Costas arrays are enumerated through order 29; 32 and 33 are the smallest orders with no known array.
- Primary sources: Drakakis, [arXiv:1102.5727](https://arxiv.org/abs/1102.5727); Gulec and Abolghasemi, [arXiv:2602.03407](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03407).
- Local locators: expanded/1102.5727.txt:317–321, 429–434; expanded/2602.03407.txt:48–60, 239–266.
- Evidence direction: supports a live tiny-witness construction campaign and an explicit 2026 AI-search interface.
- Qualification: absence from databases means “not known,” not “does not exist.” The 2026 UCM framework does not itself produce order 32.
- Confidence: 0.90.

### E-009 — 8-bit APN permutation

- Claim: existence of an APN permutation in dimension 8 remains open; one witness is a 256-entry bijection with differential uniformity two.
- Primary sources: Beierle et al., [arXiv:2508.04644](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.04644); Kuznetsov, [arXiv:2606.11967](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11967).
- Local locators: expanded/2508.04644.txt:29–37, 273–277; expanded/2606.11967.txt:40–48, 112–125, 917–923, 982–983.
- Evidence direction: supports open status, exact verifier, and a concrete search neighborhood.
- Qualification: millions of quadratic APN functions and new CCZ classes have produced no permutation. A nonbijective APN function or a 4-uniform permutation is a cousin, not a solution.
- Confidence: 0.96 on status; low estimated tractability.

### E-010 — weighted data selection

- Claim: for min-norm linear regression, the exact worst-case ratio is infinite below d selected points, d+1 at d, and one from 2d onward; d<n<2d is open, with 1+1/d suggested at n=2d−1.
- Primary source: Hanneke et al., [PMLR 291](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v291/hanneke25e.html).
- Local locator: expanded/hanneke25e.txt:135–170.
- Evidence direction: supports an unusually crisp, theorem-sized ML target.
- Qualification: weighted and unweighted selection differ; empirical full-dataset loss is not generalization; no direct claim about LLM data pruning.
- Confidence: medium-high after bounded no-resolution searches.

### E-011 — distribution-independent deep versus linear

- Claim: COLT 2026 asks whether distribution-independent SQ learnability implies dc(H) at most C m/tau^2 and whether standard ReLU-SGD learnability with S parameters and T steps implies dc(H) at most C T S.
- Primary source: Feldman, Kamath, and Srebro, [PMLR 336](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v336/feldman26a.html).
- Local locator: expanded/feldman26a.txt:112–146, 185–207.
- Evidence direction: supports a direct feature-learning theorem target.
- Qualification: fixed-distribution separations, CSQ-only results, or architectures that encode arbitrary algorithms do not count.
- Confidence: high that it was live at publication; later-status conclusion is bounded-search T1.

### E-012 — equiangular lines in R18

- Claim: 57 at most N(18) at most 60; recent work constructs many 57-line systems but no 58-line system in R18.
- Primary sources: Greaves, Syatriadi, and Yatsyna, [arXiv:2104.04330](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.04330); Munemasa, Szöllősi, and Yoshino, [arXiv:2606.10421](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10421).
- Local locators: expanded/2104.04330.txt:28–60, 1652–1657; expanded/2606.10421.txt:76–102, 765–873.
- Evidence direction: supports a 58-line construction target.
- Qualification: non-strong maximality in a higher-dimensional ambient space does not imply an extension to 58 lines in R18.
- Confidence: 0.90.

### E-013 — sorting network S(13)

- Claim: a 45-comparator network is known; current compiled bounds list 44 through 45, while a conservative refereed lower bound is 43.
- Primary/current sources: Ehlers and Müller, [arXiv:1806.00305](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.00305); Dobbelaere’s executable [sorting-network list](https://bertdobbelaere.github.io/sorting_networks.html).
- Local locator: expanded/1806.00305.txt, especially its bounds and n=13 discussion.
- Evidence direction: supports a verifier-first finite campaign.
- Qualification: depth nine is already settled and is not the size problem. The provenance of the 44 lower bound requires a dedicated audit before claiming the interval as refereed fact.
- Confidence: 0.84.

### E-014 — minimum Kochen–Specker system

- Claim: best construction size 31 and best lower bound 24; a 2026 paper provides machine-checked geometric infrastructure but says the end-to-end 24 proof still has remaining layers.
- Primary source: [arXiv:2607.26413](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26413).
- Local locator: expanded/2607.26413.txt:10–24, 43–45, 123–136, 490–563.
- Evidence direction: supports both impact and verifier readiness while qualifying the certification status.
- Confidence: medium-high; the newest source is a fresh preprint.

### E-015 — four-uniform Erdős Matching Conjecture

- Claim: the conjecture is proved for every s at least 6,961; the authors did not optimize this threshold.
- Primary source: [arXiv:2605.26060](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26060).
- Local locator: expanded/2605.26060.txt:17–23, 87–98, 547–617, 1145–1151.
- Evidence direction: supports threshold-reduction rungs and exact finite-board checking.
- Qualification: lowering 6,961 is progress, not the full all-s theorem.
- Confidence: high on the paper’s claim; later status search-qualified.

### E-016 — ALS overparameterization

- Claim: k=O(r^2) suffices for a parallel ALS result; COLT 2026 asks whether k=o(r^2) suffices or whether k=Omega(r^(1+c)) is necessary under a pinned smoothed model.
- Primary source: Arvanitakis, Srinivas, and Vijayaraghavan, [PMLR 336](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v336/arvanitakis26a.html).
- Local locator: expanded/arvanitakis26a.txt:20–28, 80–94, 120–166.
- Evidence direction: supports a two-sided ML-theory campaign.
- Qualification: algebraic decomposition methods and pathological worst cases do not settle the stated ALS/GD smoothed question.
- Confidence: high.

### E-017 — private PAC overhead

- Claim: COLT 2026 asks for a class sequence exhibiting approximate-DP sample complexity near log-cardinality while VC dimension is much smaller, or a substantially better generic upper bound.
- Primary source: Nissim, Stemmer, and Tsfadia, [PMLR 336](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v336/nissim26a.html).
- Evidence direction: supports a crisp but difficult privacy-learning target.
- Qualification: pure DP, online learning gaps, and easy VC=1 families do not settle the approximate-DP question.
- Confidence: high at publication; no-resolution status is bounded-search T1.

### E-018 — one-bit mean interaction

- Claim: arbitrary nonadaptive measurable one-bit quantizers are not known to match the adaptive minimax rate over the finite-k-moment class.
- Primary source: Lau and Scarlett, [PMLR 336](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v336/lau26a.html).
- Evidence direction: supports an exact k=2 first rung.
- Qualification: threshold/interval lower bounds do not cover arbitrary measurable sets; a two-stage protocol is interactive and does not settle the one-shot problem.
- Confidence: high.

### E-019 — explicit deterministic RIP

- Claim: the 2026 revision breaks the square-root barrier with epsilon approximately 3.26e−7 in its specified regime.
- Primary source: Ford, Kutzarova, and Shakan, [arXiv:2108.01794](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.01794).
- Local locator: expanded/2108.01794.txt:11–45, 57–64, 220–229.
- Evidence direction: supports a live exponent-improvement target.
- Qualification: it is extremely fresh; an isolated finite matrix is not an explicit asymptotic family.
- Confidence: high.

### E-020 — KV-cache product-depth conjecture

- Claim: Conjecture 3.1 proposes a multiplicative depth lower bound for locality-respecting hard-cache transformers on pointer chasing, beyond the proved max of the cache and bandwidth terms.
- Primary source: [arXiv:2604.17935](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17935).
- Local locator: expanded/2604.17935.txt:86–102, 179–218.
- Evidence direction: supports a direct ML lower-bound target.
- Qualification: the theorem depends on hard deletion, p-bit state, a shared/locality-respecting controller, and a synthetic task. The paper explicitly says the product step is conjectural.
- Confidence: medium pending independent uptake and exact status review.

### E-021 — Hadwiger–Nelson

- Claim: the plane chromatic number remains between 5 and 7; the decisive lower-rung target is a finite 6-chromatic unit-distance graph.
- Primary source for the five-color lower bound: de Grey, [arXiv:1805.12181](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.12181).
- Current cousin evidence: [arXiv:2606.28157](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28157) improves a fractional variant, not the ordinary chromatic number.
- Evidence direction: supports enormous consequence but very poor incremental tractability.
- Qualification: a 5-chromatic graph, a Moser-free graph, or a fractional-chromatic improvement does not raise the main lower bound.
- Confidence: high on the primary result; current no-movement status search-qualified.

### E-022 — adaptive backtesting and AdamW

- Claim: these remain valuable applied families but are not yet single canonical targets.
- Sources qualifying backtesting: Pav, [arXiv:2606.01650](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01650), local locator expanded/2606.01650.txt:10–17, 37–69, 869–875.
- Sources qualifying Adam/AdamW: [arXiv:2510.26303](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26303) and [arXiv:2602.01642](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01642).
- Evidence direction: refutes broad novelty wording and demands a narrower contract.
- Required pinning for backtests: mixing class/constants, moment and variance conditions, adaptive-query interface, statistic, coverage mode, and lower-bound comparator.
- Required pinning for AdamW: architecture/loss, batch sampling, beta parameters, bias correction, epsilon, weight decay, and schedule.
- Confidence: high that the broad formulations are under-specified.

## Certificate taxonomy

1. Exact construction certificate: permutation, graph, coordinate set, tensor decomposition, code, or Gram matrix. Usually cheap to verify.
2. Finite exhaustive nonexistence: potentially checkable, but preprocessing and proof logs may be enormous.
3. Analytic lower bound: formalizable in principle, rarely reducible to one compact artifact.
4. Empirical ML/systems evidence: useful for generating conjectures, never a certificate of a universal theorem.

Every launched campaign must state which class would constitute success. “The search failed” is evidence only about the searched family and budget.

## Round 2 decision

The most defensible first discussion is not “which famous conjecture shall we solve?” It is:

- Do we want the best quiet exact pilot: rectilinear K28?
- The cleanest applied construction: Costas-32?
- The best theorem-shaped ML pilot: weighted regression data selection?
- Or the highest-ceiling moonshot: R(5,5), APN-8, field-pinned 3x3 tensor rank, or terminal ReLU depth?

That choice changes the search architecture, verifier, compute budget, and honest definition of progress.
