                                                   Abstract. We review Exoo’s 1989 paper, which demonstrates that a lower
                                                   bound for the Ramsey number Rp5, 5q is 43. We provide an efficient way
                                                   to verify the claims in the paper, adding detailed proofs. In particular, we
                                                   replace the reference to computer verification by concise arguments. Using our
                                                   understanding of the insight behind these proofs, we are also able to analyse
                                                   variations of the graph constructions to obtain, for example, colourings of K43
                                                   which have very few monochromatic K5 .




                                                                                 Introduction
                                           What is the smallest number of people that there can be in a group which guaran-
                                           tees that there will always be three people who all know each other or three people
                                           who all don’t know each other? This is a well-known puzzle whose answer is six.

                                           However, if we consider a slightly larger variation and ask “What is the minimum
                                           number of people for which we can guarantee that there exists five people who all
                                           know each other or five people who all don’t know each other?” then we have a
                                           problem whose answer is currently unknown! The answer to this question is denoted
                                           by Rp5, 5q, the Ramsey number p5, 5q, and determining its value is an open problem.

                                           There is an amusing quote from Paul Erdős regarding this problem:
                                                   Imagine an alien force, vastly more powerful than us landing on
                                                   Earth and demanding the value of Rp5, 5q or they will destroy our
                                                   planet. In that case, we should marshal all our computers and all
                                                   our mathematicians and attempt to find the value. But suppose,
                                                   instead, that they asked for Rp6, 6q, we should attempt to destroy
                                                   the aliens.
                                           It is currently known that Rp5, 5q is between 43 (Geoffrey Exoo 1989) and 48
                                           (Vigleik Angeltveit and Brendan D. McKay 2017) inclusive and there is evidence
                                           that leads to the following conjecture [MR]:
                                           Conjecture 0.1 (McKay–Radsizowski).
                                                                                  Rp5, 5q “ 43.
                                           In 1989 Exoo [Exoo] published a construction which showed:
                                           Theorem 0.1 (Exoo).
                                                                                  Rp5, 5q ě 43.

Exoo’s paper will be the focus of our study. The problem is represented using a
graph, where the vertices represent the people and the edges represent the relation-
ship between pairs of people. For concreteness, we use a complete graph, which is a
graph where every pair of vertices is joined by an edge. We then colour every edge
in this complete graph one of two colours (either red or blue) to represent whether
each pair of people knows or does not know each other.

We use the usual notation of Kn for a complete graph with n vertices. In the
following sections, we will define a red K5 to be a complete graph with 5 vertices
where all of its edges are red and define a blue K5 similarly for blue edges. Also,
we will denote an edge in the graph between vertices u, v by epu, vq.

In the first section we recall the definition of Exoo’s graph from Exoo’s original
paper. In the other sections, we provide explicit proofs for the claims whose proofs
were omitted in the original paper, making everything self contained. The highlight
of this is showing in Section 5 that no blue K5 ’s exist in Exoo’s graph, which was
left to computer verification in the original paper.

Section 1: Definition of Exoo’s graph: We recall the contents of Exoo’s paper and
organise them into statements and add some diagrams.

Section 2: Helpful observations: We state and explain some general principles
that have helped to save significant time and effort in calculations and casework.

Section 3: Red checking: We write a complete proof that there are no red K5 ’s
in Exoo’s graph by reducing it to 3 cases.

Sections 4 and 5: We write a complete proof that there are no blue K5 ’s in
Exoo’s graph.

                         1. Definition of Exoo’s graph
First, we define an edge colouring on K43 . Then, we remove one vertex and finally
change the colour of some edges.
1.1. Definition of the colouring for K43 . Exoo first defined a colouring of K43 :
Definition 1.1 (Cyclic(43)). Arrange 43 points equally spaced around a circle and
label them from 0 to 42 in clockwise order and in modulo 43 (so that for all integers
a, the vertex a is the same as vertex a ` 43). For any two distinct vertices a, b we
define the distance (denoted by dpa, bq) between them as the length of the minor
arc between the vertices (where we define the minor arc length between adjacent
vertices to be 1).

Note that dpa, bq can also be defined as the unique integer m P t1, . . . , 21u such that
either a ´ b ” m pmod 43q or b ´ a ” m pmod 43q.

We will also define the length of an edge to be the distance between the two vertices
that form the edge. Observe that all edge lengths will be between 1 and 21 inclusive.
The edges will be coloured depending on their lengths, as shown in the following
table, where each length from 1 to 21 corresponds to either the colour red or blue.

                Red 1 2 7 10 12 13 14 16 18 20 21
                Blue 3 4 5 6           8 9 11 15 17 19
Denote an edge length as a red-length or a blue-length if it corresponds to the colour
red or blue respectively in the table above.
Since this colouring is defined solely using the lengths of each edge, then this colour-
ing is cyclic over all vertices, so we will denote the graph as Cyclicp43q.

length 1, 7, 11 of Cyclicp43q.

The following proposition and its converse appears in Exoo’s paper [Exoo]. We
prove the converse in Section 3 that the only red K5 ’s in Cyclicp43q are the ones
listed below. The proof of the proposition was omitted in the paper because it was
trivial but we include it here for completeness.
Proposition 1.1. For all integers i such that 0 ď i ď 42, the 5-tuple of vertices
pi, i ` 1, i ` 2, i ` 22, i ` 23q forms a red K5 in Cyclicp43q. This yields 43 distinct
red K5 ’s.
Proof. We will check for all 0 ď i ď 42 that the lengths of the edges between all
ten pairs of vertices in each of the K5 ’s are red-lengths. Below are the edge lengths
in the i “ 0 case.
                                    dp0, 1q “ 1.
                                    dp0, 2q “ 2.
                                    dp0, 22q “ 21.
                                    dp0, 23q “ 20.
                                    dp1, 2q “ 1.
                                    dp1, 22q “ 21.
                                    dp1, 23q “ 21.
                                    dp2, 22q “ 20.
                                    dp2, 23q “ 21.
                                    dp22, 23q “ 1.


Since all the edge lengths are either 1, 2, 20 or 21 (which are all red-lengths), then
this subgraph is a red K5 . By symmetry, the edge lengths of the pairs of vertices
will be the same for the K5 ’s for all other values of i, so we obtain a red K5 for
every value of i.

For each value of i, since vertex i ` 1 is the only vertex in the corresponding K5
whose adjacent vertices are also in the K5 , then this shows that the K5 ’s for each
value of i are unique. Hence, we have 43 distinct K5 ’s.

                                2. Helpful observations
Lemma 2.1. In Cyclicp43q and Exoop42q, the edge joining vertices a and b is blue
if the difference |a ´ b| is one of the following:
             3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 11, 15, 17, 19, 24, 26, 28, 32, 34, 35, 37, 38, 39, 40.
In Cyclicp43q, all other edges are red. In Exoop42q, all other edges are red unless
the difference is 1, 21, 22 or 42 in which case the specific edge needs to be checked
against the colour change table.
Proof. The first ten differences are equal to all the blue-lengths. Also, observe
that when the latter ten differences are subtracted from 43, we obtain all the blue-
lengths, so these differences (which are distances between pairs of vertices along
the major arc) also correspond to blue edges.

By definition, all other edges of Cyclicp43q are red. In Exoop42q, colour changes
only occur for certain edges of length 1 or 21. This corresponds to the difference
|a ´ b| being either 1, 21, 22 or 42 (considering distances along the minor or major
arc).

Definition 2.1. Consider a fixed edge epa, bq in Cyclicp43q, We say that two vertices
u, v are symmetric with respect to epa, bq if
                               a ´ u ” v ´ b pmod 43q
(or equivalently a ´ v ” u ´ b pmod 43q. We say that two sets of vertices U, V of
equal size are symmetric about epa, bq if all the vertices in U can be paired with all
the vertices in V such that the vertices in each pair are symmetric with respect to
epa, bq.
Proposition 2.1 (Symmetry in Colourings). In Cyclicp43q, suppose that we are
given a pair of vertices (a, b) and another pair of vertices (x, y) satisfying
                               x ´ b ” a ´ y pmod 43q
(or in other words vertices x, y are symmetric with respect to epa, bq). Then the
edge epx, aq has the same colour as epy, bq and the edge epx, bq has the same colour
as epy, aq. In particular, if both epx, aq and epx, bq edges are red (resp. blue), then
so are the edges epy, aq and epy, bq and vice versa.
Proof. It suffices to show the differences between x and a, b are the same as the
differences between y and b, a respectively (in modulo 43) since equal differences
imply that the edge lengths are equal and hence have the same colour.

We are already given that x ´ b ” a ´ y pmod 43q. By rearranging this, we obtain
x ´ a ” b ´ y pmod 43q, so we have both of the required equalities in differences.
                                                                                
This proposition can be applied to a set of vertices X symmetric to a set of vertices
Y about a fixed edge epa, bq. A practical consequence of this symmetry is that
it reduces the effort required to determine colours of edges. For example, if the
colour of the edge epx, aq is known for some x P X, then if y P Y is the symmetric
counterpart of x, the colour of epy, bq can immediately be deduced to be the same
colour as epx, aq.
