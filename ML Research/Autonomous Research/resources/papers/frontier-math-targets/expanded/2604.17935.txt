                                         1    Introduction
                                         As context windows in large language models scale beyond 100K tokens, the key–value (KV) cache has
                                         become the dominant memory bottleneck during inference (Shi et al., 2024). This has driven extensive work
                                         on cache compression through eviction (Zhang et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023; Li et al., 2024; Cai et al., 2024),
                                         quantization (Liu et al., 2024), and merging (Wang et al., 2024); these methods report 80–90% compression
                                         with limited degradation on standard benchmarks.



                                                                                                    1
     Yet mounting empirical evidence shows that multi-hop reasoning degrades far faster than single-hop
retrieval under the same compression rates (Ananthanarayanan et al., 2026; Liu et al., 2025; Cai et al., 2025).
This raises a sharp question: how much cache must be retained for multi-step reasoning to remain possible?
     We formalize this through k-hop pointer chasing and study the depth required to solve it under a
shared KV cache of size s. Our main positive result is an unconditional max-bound lower bound, plus a
conjectured product lower bound: cache reachability (∼ k/s sequential windows) and per-window bandwidth
(∼ log n/(Hmp) layers per window) should multiply rather than compete. We identify the precise probabilistic
step (⋆) needed to turn the conjecture into a theorem and leave it as Open Problem 1; we also give a narrow
unconditional partial result in the extreme low-precision subregime s · 2mp < 8 under an additional query-only
controller assumption (Remark 4.13). Our other main results (the matching upper bound, the bandwidth
barrier, and the adaptive-vs-oblivious error analysis) are unconditional.
     The product bound is, however, quantitatively binding only in a specific parameter regime—when
precision is low or models are narrow enough that Hmp ≲ log n. In standard LLM configurations (H = 32,
m = 128, p = 16, giving Hmp ∼ 6.5 · 104 ≫ log n), the bandwidth factor ceilings at 1 and the bound reduces
to the cache-reachability constraint L ≥ ⌈k/s⌉ alone. This raises the natural question: can the product structure
be sharpened to bind in the standard LLM regime?
     Our second contribution answers: not via a broad class of current proof techniques. We formalize a barrier
theorem showing that any depth lower bound provable via per-window distinguishability counting—a class
that encompasses reachability arguments, bandwidth counting, and their combinations—saturates at ⌈k/s⌉
whenever Hmp ≥ log2 n. The barrier is structural: multi-head attention (H ≥ 2) makes per-layer bandwidth
(Hmp) exceed per-token state capacity (mp), so any counting-based per-window argument ceilings within a
single layer. Breaking this barrier would require either translating unconditional communication-complexity
lower bounds for pointer chasing (Nisan and Wigderson, 1993; Yehudayoff, 2020; Mao et al., 2025) into
Cache-Transformer depth lower bounds without the MPC conjecture (an open problem) or genuinely different
proof techniques.
     Our third contribution is a sharp, two-regime analysis of error behavior under random cache selec-
tion, which partially explains the empirical “hallucination cliff” of Ananthanarayanan et al. (2026). The
joint success probability of windowed pointer doubling over T = ⌈log2 k⌉ stages admits qualitatively dif-
ferent scaling in two regimes: for oblivious caches (chosen without seeing π), direct enumeration gives
Pr[E ] ≤ (s/(n − T ))T + 2T 3 /n, exhibiting exponential decay in T (Theorem 3.8). For adaptive locality-
respecting caches, the joint probability is exactly s/n, independent of T , and is matched by an explicit
“chain-tracking” controller (Theorem 3.9). The resulting separation, Ω((n/s)T −1 ) between adaptive and
oblivious, quantitatively explains the empirical observation (Liu et al., 2025) that heavy-hitter (adaptive)
eviction qualitatively outperforms random or recency-based eviction on multi-hop reasoning tasks.

Relation to prior work. Sanford et al. (2024) proved O(log k) depth suffices for k-hop pointer chasing
with full cache (s = n), and Ω(log k) is necessary under an MPC conjecture. Their analysis does not address
cache constraints and assumes infinite precision. The two results are complementary: theirs is conditional
and cache-oblivious; ours is unconditional (modulo locality) but finite-precision. In the s = n limit, our
bound gives L ≥ ⌈log2 n/(Hmp)⌉, which for Hmp = O(log n) matches Ω(log n) ≥ Ω(log k); in larger Hmp,
our bound is weaker and cannot recover their Ω(log k), reflecting the bandwidth barrier. Peng et al. (2024)
use communication complexity to prove a related inability of shallow Transformers to compose functions
(e.g., grandparent lookup on a genealogy), giving unconditional impossibility for low-depth models on
pointer-chasing-like tasks; their work focuses on depth vs. domain size rather than depth vs. cache size,
and does not address KV compression. Haris and Onak (2025) prove a Θ(nd) space lower bound (for
d = Ω(log n); in the low-dimensional regime d = o(log n) they show Ω(d · ed )) for KV cache compression
in attention-based autoregressive Transformers via a reduction from communication complexity, using a
Johnson-Lindenstrauss-style construction; this is orthogonal to our depth-vs-cache tradeoff, focusing on
whether sublinear space suffices at all rather than on the interaction between depth and cache size. The
concurrent empirical study (Ananthanarayanan et al., 2026) independently observed phase-transition-like
behavior in multi-hop accuracy under compression.

3.2   When the product bound binds: the bandwidth barrier
For standard LLM configurations (H = 32, m = 128, p = 16, so Hmp ≈ 6.5 · 104 ), the bandwidth factor
⌈log2 n/(Hmp)⌉ equals 1 for any realistic n (up to n ≤ 2Hmp ≫ 1019000 ), and Conjecture 3.1 reduces to the
cache-reachability bound L ≥ ⌈k/s⌉ alone—a near-trivial consequence of the fact that the query token’s
reachable set grows by at most s per layer.
    It is natural to ask whether the bandwidth factor can be strengthened. We show that, within a natural
class of proof techniques, it cannot: any lower bound obtainable via per-window distinguishability counting
saturates at ⌈k/s⌉ when Hmp ≥ log2 n.

Remark 3.7 (On the conceptual depth of the barrier). In some sense, Theorem 3.6 is a direct consequence
of the fact that the per-window output lies in [n], making it look tautological. However, its role is to
formalize a class of proof techniques (Definition 3.5) and show that the entire class ceilings at ⌈k/s⌉ when
Hmp ≥ log2 n. This is analogous to formalizing “natural proofs” in circuit complexity: once the class is
defined, the impossibility within that class is near-immediate, but the formalization itself identifies what
bypass mechanisms are needed. Our “eight handles” analysis (Section 4.4) systematically confirms that no
bypass we could identify escapes the class.

Proof. The per-window output of PCn,k is zτ j = π τ j (1) ∈ [n], which takes at most n distinct values as π varies
(regardless of any conditioning). By the definition of M j (Definition 3.5) as a lower bound on the support size
of zτ j under some conditioning, we have M j ≤ n. The inequality B ≤ ⌈log2 n/(Hmp)⌉ is then immediate, and
equals 1 when Hmp ≥ log2 n. The modification claim is immediate: any task with per-window output in a set
of size ≤ n gives M j ≤ n.

al., 2025) into Cache-Transformer depth lower bounds without the MPC conjecture (an open problem) or
genuinely different proof techniques not of the form in Definition 3.5. The barrier is not a proof gap in our
work; it is a structural obstacle on the current road to depth lower bounds in the standard LLM regime.

windowed pointer doubling. We analyze this algorithm: at each of T = ⌈log2 k⌉ stages, the algorithm needs

dt ∈ Ct , and succeeds overall (event E ) if all T stages succeed.

Remark 3.11 (What this says about adaptive vs. oblivious eviction). Theorems 3.8 and 3.9 together give a
sharp answer to why adaptive (heavy-hitter, attention-score) eviction outperforms oblivious (recency, random)
baselines in practice, as reported by Liu et al. (2025): the two regimes exhibit qualitatively different scaling
behavior with chain length. Under uniform random cache selection—the worst case for the algorithm—
oblivious caches see joint success probability decay as (s/n)T , while adaptive caches see decay as only s/n
(no T dependence). The adaptive controller exploits the structure that learning early chain values reveals
later ones via locality. Our bound is non-asymptotic and parameter-free: it applies to any locality-respecting
controller, and the achievability construction demonstrates the bound cannot be improved beyond constants.

When the bandwidth factor binds quantitatively. In the barrier regime (Hmp ≥ log2 n), the product
bound of Conjecture 3.1 would reduce to ⌈k/s⌉ and coincide with the cache-reachability bound. In the
narrow-model regime (Hmp < log2 n: e.g., H = 1, m = 4, p = 4 giving Hmp = 16; or single-head INT4 with
m = 16, p = 4, H = 1), the bandwidth factor is strictly greater than 1 and the conjectured product structure is
quantitatively binding. This regime is increasingly relevant for quantized and edge-deployed models.

4.4     Remarks on Theorem 3.6
The proof of Theorem 3.6 was given in Section 3 following the theorem statement. Here we collect additional

Remark 4.21 (Breaking the barrier). Theorem 3.6 does not rule out lower bounds outside the class of
Definition 3.5. Three natural avenues remain:
1. Unconditional communication-complexity lower bounds: the pointer chasing problem has been studied in
   multi-party communication models. Nisan and Wigderson (1993) proved a Ω̃(n/k2 ) lower bound on the
   k-round randomized communication complexity of k-hop pointer chasing; (Yehudayoff, 2020) improved
   this to Ω̃(n/k − k log n), essentially matching Klauck’s earlier randomized lower bound and tight up to
   the k log n additive term relative to Nisan-Wigderson’s Õ(n/k) upper bound; (Mao et al., 2025) closed
   the remaining log n gap to Ω(n/k + k) via gadgetless lifting. These CC bounds are unconditional. What
   remains open is the lifting: translating such a CC bound into an unconditional Cache-Transformer depth
   lower bound without invoking (Sanford et al., 2024)’s MPC-conjecture reduction, which would bypass
   Definition 3.5.


                                                            17
                                                                 (ℓ)                                       (ℓ)
2. Joint-state arguments: counting over the joint state (Xi )i∈S(ℓ) rather than the single state X0 . The
   joint state has capacity smp > Hmp whenever s > H, potentially escaping the per-layer bandwidth limit.
                                       (L)
   However, in the PCn,k task only X0 decodes to the output, so arguments coupling to joint cache states

3. Information-theoretic multi-output tasks: tasks whose output is Ω(Hmp) bits (e.g., requiring Ω(Hmp/ log n)

Remark 4.22 (Exhaustive search for bypass arguments). We considered eight alternative approaches to
escape the barrier within counting-based arguments: (i) asymmetric rounds (parallel reasoning at multiple
token positions); (ii) adversarial online streaming (cache commitment before seeing the rest of the input);
(iii) query/cache capacity asymmetry (smp vs mp); (iv) counting distinguishability at non-query tokens;
(v) pebbling-game time-space tradeoffs; (vi) direct-sum and XOR lemmas for independent task instances;
(vii) communication-complexity fooling-set arguments, in particular round-elimination lower bounds for
pointer chasing of the Duris et al. (1987) style; and (viii) average-case (random-π) hardness. Each approach
either saturates at ⌈k/s⌉ or yields a strictly weaker bound (e.g., (vii) via unconditional MPC bounds gives
log k/s, weaker than k/s for k ≥ 2). We omit the detailed case-by-case analysis; the unified conclusion is the
per-window structural limit formalized by Theorem 3.6.

5     Architecture Comparison
Full attention’s depth scales as Ω(k/s) to O((k/s) log s), with a gap of O(log s). Linear attention and RNNs
are stuck at Θ(k) regardless of cache. Full attention’s advantage depends critically on cache size.

6.4   Connection to LLM behavior
Ananthanarayanan et al. (2026) independently report a sharp hallucination cliff near 90% compression, with
multi-hop (Hops) F1 dropping from ∼31% to near zero under aggressive compression, while single-hop
retrieval degrades gracefully. This matches our framework qualitatively: k-hop capability is gated by a
product bound that involves k, so multi-hop (k ≥ 4) is hit harder than single-hop (k = 1) as s shrinks. Liu
et al. (2025) show that adaptive (heavy-hitter) eviction outperforms recency- and norm-based baselines on
reasoning; Corollary 3.10 gives a direct quantitative explanation: adaptive controllers achieve Θ(s/n) joint
success, while oblivious ones decay as (s/n)T , a separation factor of Ω((n/s)T −1 ). This is consistent with
the observation that heavy-hitter-based methods, which concentrate cache on frequently-attended positions,
provide qualitatively different error-scaling behavior than random or recency-based eviction. Caveat on
regime. Our experiments at n = 16, T = 3 do not fall within the “tight” regime of Corollary 3.10 (which
requires s ≥ (2T 3 )1/T · n(T −1)/T ≈ 24, exceeding n = 16); we view the empirical observations here as
qualitative validation of the direction of the separation rather than quantitative confirmation of the (n/s)T −1
rate. Establishing the rate empirically in the main-term-dominates regime requires moderate-to-large n (e.g.,
for s/n = 1/2 and T = 3, roughly n > 432; see Remark 3.12).


7     Related Work
Transformer depth. Sanford et al. (2023) separated attention from RNNs via communication complexity.
Sanford et al. (2024) proved Transformer–MPC equivalence. Alman and Yu (2025) gave SETH-conditional
time bounds. Kozachinskiy et al. (2025) proved infinite-precision lower bounds. We introduce cache as a new
parameter. Our finite-precision model is analogous to bounded-precision circuit models in computational
complexity; the key distinction from (Sanford et al., 2024) is that their Ω(log k) holds at infinite precision
(conditional on MPC), while our conjectured product bound addresses the finite-precision regime (under a
locality-respecting controller, which is satisfied by all standard KV-compression methods). The two results
are complementary: neither implies the other.

KV cache compression. Eviction: (Zhang et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023; Li et al., 2024; Cai et al., 2024).
Reasoning-specific: (Cai et al., 2025; Du et al., 2025). Empirical: (Liu et al., 2025; Ananthanarayanan et al.,
2026). All heuristic/empirical; we provide the first formal depth–cache tradeoff for a stylized reasoning task.
    Recent methods employ question-aware or position-aware scoring (pseudo-query-based selection, global
per-head budget optimization, head-aware risk-adaptive gating). In our framework, such strategies correspond
to smarter choices of the cache set S(ℓ) within the locality-respecting class; they may improve constant
factors in practice but are subject to the unconditional max-bound L = Ω(max(⌈k/s⌉, ⌈log2 n/(Hmp)⌉))
(Proposition 4.4), and, if Conjecture 3.1 holds, cannot circumvent the product structure L = Ω(⌈k/s⌉ ·
⌈log2 n/(Hmp)⌉). The locality assumption is satisfied by all practical methods. Systems that offload KV
entries to slower storage and retrieve on demand effectively increase s per layer (at latency cost), which our
bounds correctly capture. Soft compression (vector quantization, value merging) reduces effective precision
or merges tokens rather than evicting them; extending our counting argument to this setting is an open
direction.

8    Discussion
We conjectured a product lower bound L = Ω((k/s) · log n/(Hmp)) (Conjecture 3.1) and isolated the sole
remaining technical gap as the probabilistic step (⋆) in Remark 4.13. We unconditionally proved the max-
bound L = Ω(max(⌈k/s⌉, ⌈log2 n/(Hmp)⌉)) (Proposition 4.4), and an unconditional matching upper bound
L = O(⌈k/s⌉ · ⌈log2 (2s)⌉ · ⌈log2 n/(mp)⌉) via windowed pointer doubling, so if Conjecture 3.1 holds then
the characterization is tight up to O(H log s) in the narrow-model regime. We also proved unconditionally a
bandwidth barrier showing that the product bound cannot be sharpened in the Hmp ≥ log2 n regime by any
per-window distinguishability-counting argument, and established that adaptive cache selection does not beat
the oblivious bound at the first stage via a coupling argument. Theorems 3.8 and 3.9 (oblivious-vs-adaptive
error behavior) are unconditional.

What the bandwidth barrier means. Theorem 3.6 is the technically most significant structural statement
of the paper. It explains a puzzling feature of existing cache-compression theory: why, despite decades of work
on pointer chasing in communication complexity, we cannot get a depth lower bound for Transformers better
than the trivial cache-reachability bound in the regime of practical interest. The barrier is that per-window
output has log2 n bits of “capacity” (since zτ j ∈ [n]), while per-layer bandwidth is Hmp bits; the ratio ceilings
at 1 whenever Hmp ≥ log2 n. Breaking through requires either (i) output-informational arguments that
escape the per-window output dimension (perhaps via multi-output tasks), (ii) translating the unconditional
pointer-chasing CC lower bounds of (Nisan and Wigderson, 1993; Yehudayoff, 2020; Mao et al., 2025)
into Cache-Transformer depth lower bounds without the MPC reduction (an open lifting problem), or (iii)
joint-state counting that couples to cache-wide state capacity smp > Hmp when s > H—but this in turn
requires the task to force s cache tokens to jointly carry information.

Limitations and scope. Our model assumes hard cache boundaries, p-bit precision, and a locality-
respecting cache controller. The precision assumption is essential for Lemma 4.2 and reflects hardware
reality. The locality assumption, which rules out controllers that examine representations outside the cache, is
satisfied by all practical methods but is a genuine restriction relative to the most general adaptive controller.
Our results thus complement (Sanford et al., 2024)’s infinite-precision, MPC-conditional Ω(log k), which is
cache-oblivious but holds under unrestricted computation. When s = n (full cache), our bound gives only
L ≥ ⌈log n/(Hmp)⌉, weaker than Ω(log k) but holding unconditionally on the controller.
     In standard LLM configurations (large Hmp), the bandwidth factor equals 1, and our bound reduces to
L ≥ ⌈k/s⌉ alone—which holds unconditionally (without locality) via Lemma 4.1. The full product structure
is a structural insight—cache and bandwidth are multiplicative, not competing—that becomes quantitatively
binding in narrow or low-precision models.
     Pointer chasing is a worst-case model; real reasoning tasks have exploitable structure permitting more
aggressive compression than our bounds suggest. Our experiments use n = 16 for clarity; the hardcoded
construction’s correctness is exact (independent of n for n < 2 p ), so the sharp threshold behavior transfers to
any n within the precision range.

Concurrent work. Ananthanarayanan et al. (2026) (arXiv March 2026) is a concurrent and independent
empirical study. Their phase-transition-like behavior in multi-hop accuracy under compression is qualitatively
consistent with our theory but was discovered independently.
