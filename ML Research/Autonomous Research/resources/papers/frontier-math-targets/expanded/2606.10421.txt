                                                SETS OF EQUIANGULAR LINES IN DIMENSION 18 CONSTRUCTED FROM 𝐴35 ⊕ 𝐴41


                                                                       AKIHIRO MUNEMASA, FERENC SZÖLLŐSI, AND KIYOTO YOSHINO


                                                     A BSTRACT. In 2023, Greaves, Syatriadi, and Yatsyna found a set of 57 equiangular lines in ℝ18 , breaking the
                                                     previous record. In 2025, Lin, Munemasa, Taniguchi, and Yoshino constructed a large number of sets of 57
                                                     equiangular lines in ℝ18 as affine equiangular sets in an integral overlattice of 𝐴29 ⊕ 𝐴1 . In this paper, we construct
arXiv:2606.10421v1 [math.CO] 9 Jun 2026




                                                     further sets of 57 equiangular lines in ℝ18 from Latin squares of order 6 and Pasch configurations, realized as affine
                                                     equiangular sets in an integral overlattice of 𝐴35 ⊕ 𝐴41 . Unlike the previously known examples, these sets are not
                                                     strongly maximal. Moreover, some of them have only five distinct Seidel eigenvalues, fewer than any previously
                                                     known examples.

                                             Equiangular lines in Euclidean spaces form a classical topic in combinatorics. A set of lines through the

                                          early work of Haantjes in the 1940s [11], a central program has been to determine the maximum number
                                          𝑁(𝑑) of equiangular lines in ℝ𝑑 . The values of 𝑁(𝑑) [8, 9, 10, 11, 13, 20] and bounds for 𝑁(𝑑) [3, 7, 13]
                                          have been studied. For convenience, we summarize the known values or best bounds for 𝑑 ≤ 23 in Table 1,
                                          where arccos(1∕𝛼) denotes the common angle of a set of equiangular lines in ℝ𝑑 attaining 𝑁(𝑑).

                                          equiangular lines in ℝ𝑑 with common angle arccos(1∕𝛼). It is known that if 𝑁𝛼 (𝑑) > 2𝑑, then 𝛼 must be an
                                          odd integer [13]. Consequently, much of the literature focuses on the cases where 𝛼 is an odd integer.
                                              In high dimensions, Jiang et al. proved that for every odd integer 𝛼 ≥ 3, 𝑁𝛼 (𝑑) = ⌊(𝛼 + 1)(𝑑 − 1)∕(𝛼 − 1)⌋

                                          unknown in low dimensions for each fixed angle. Among the cases where 𝛼 is odd, the only nontrivial angle
                                          for which the problem has been completely resolved is arccos(1∕3) [13]. In this case, an infinite family

    Motivated by this situation, in this paper we investigate sets of equiangular lines with common angle
arccos(1∕5) in dimension 18. A series of improvements of upper bounds [8, 13] and lower bounds [10, 15, 19]
on 𝑁5 (18) has been obtained by a variety of methods. Earlier constructions were based on removing suitable
lines from Ψ23 , whereas more recent approaches rely on searches for integer vectors with prescribed inner
products. These methods have led to the bound 𝑁5 (18) ≥ 57. Moreover, such configurations are known to
be strongly maximal, in the sense that they cannot be extended even in higher dimensions [21]. Furthermore,
numerous non-isometric examples of 57 equiangular lines in dimension 18 have been constructed [14] as
affine equiangular sets in an integral overlattice of 𝐴29 ⊕ 𝐴1 . In the meantime, the second author found a set
of 57 equiangular lines in dimension 18 by extending a (maximal) set of 48 equiangular lines in dimension
17. It turns out that this set of 57 equiangular lines gives rise to an affine equiangular set in an integral
overlattice of 𝐴35 ⊕ 𝐴41 . Our main result, Theorem 6.1, determines all maximum affine equiangular sets lying
in this overlattice. This shows that the corresponding sets of 57 equiangular lines in dimension 18 can be
constructed from a Latin square of order 6 giving rise to 36 equiangular lines, together with three sets of 7
equiangular lines each coming from a Pasch configuration. Moreover, we prove in Theorem 7.5 that these
examples are maximal, in the sense that they cannot be extended within the same dimension. However, it
turns out that, once the dimension is increased, further lines can be added to each of these examples. In
particular, we show in Theorem 8.1 that they are not strongly maximal. These appear to be the first known
examples of 57 equiangular lines in dimension 18 with this property. Also, in Corollary 6.3, we obtain sets
of equiangular lines whose associated Seidel matrices have only five distinct eigenvalues, which is one fewer
than in previously known constructions in [14]. It is worth mentioning that despite our extensive random
generation of 57 equiangular lines in dimension 18, we were not able to find any equiangular lines not
covered by the classification done in [14] or in the present paper.
    Our approach uses the framework of affine equiangular sets to relate sets of equiangular lines to combi-
natorial objects such as Latin squares. For details on affine equiangular sets, we refer to [14]. Briefly, a set
of equiangular lines with common angle arccos(1∕5) is spanned by vectors of norm 5 with pairwise inner
products ±1. After a suitable affine transformation, these vectors become a set of vectors of norm 3 with
pairwise inner products 0 or 1, which we call an affine equiangular set. Since the lattice generated by an
affine equiangular set often has a smaller discriminant, it can be easier to handle. In fact, it is often the case
that the resulting lattice contains a root sublattice of full rank. This allows us to describe affine equiangular
sets using root vectors.
    This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we introduce some combinatorial objects and basic
definitions on lattices. In Section 3, we describe an integral overlattice of 𝐴35 ⊕ 𝐴41 , in which we found affine
equiangular sets of cardinality 57. In Sections 4 and 5, we explain our constructions in terms of combinatorial
objects. In Section 6, we summarize the previous sections and determine all maximum affine equiangular sets
in the overlattice. In Section 7, we prove that these affine equiangular sets are maximal. Finally, in Section 8,
we show that these are not strongly maximal.

Definition 2.2. A Latin square is an 𝑛 × 𝑛 array filled with 𝑛 different symbols, each occurring exactly once

2.2   Lattices
    In this subsection, we introduce some basic definitions and notation on lattices. For more information,
see [5]. A lattice is the set 𝐿 of integral linear combinations of some ℝ-linearly independent vectors
𝑢1 , … , 𝑢𝑚 ∈ ℝ𝑛 (𝑚 ≤ 𝑛). This lattice is written as ⟨{𝑢1 , … , 𝑢𝑚 }⟩. For two lattices 𝐿 and 𝑀 with 𝐿 ⊃ 𝑀, we
say that 𝑀 is a sublattice of 𝐿, and that 𝐿 is an overlattice of 𝑀. Also, denote by (𝑢, 𝑣) the inner product of
two vectors 𝑢 and 𝑣. For two lattices 𝐿 and 𝑀, we write 𝐿 ⊕ 𝑀 or 𝐿𝑀 for the orthogonal sum of 𝐿 and 𝑀.
The orthogonal sum of 𝑛 copies of a lattice 𝐿 is denoted by 𝐿𝑛 .
    A lattice 𝐿 is said to be integral if (𝑢, 𝑣) ∈ ℤ for any 𝑢, 𝑣 ∈ 𝐿. For an integral lattice 𝐿, the dual 𝐿∗ of
𝐿 is defined to be the set {𝑣 ∈ ℝ𝐿 ∣ (𝑢, 𝑣) ∈ ℤ for any 𝑢 ∈ 𝐿}, where ℝ𝐿 is the ℝ-linear span of 𝐿. The
discriminant of an integral lattice 𝐿 is defined to be the order of the quotient group 𝐿∗ ∕𝐿, and is denoted by
disc 𝐿. For each vector 𝑢, we call (𝑢, 𝑢) the norm of 𝑢. A vector is called a root if its norm is 2. An integral
lattice is called a root lattice if it is generated by roots. It is well known that irreducible root lattices are
classified into three types 𝐴, 𝐷 and 𝐸.

Definition 2.5. Let 𝜔 and 𝜔′ be affine equiangular sets with respect to a common switching root 𝑟. We say

say that a mapping between sets of vectors is an isometry if it preserves inner products.
Definition 2.6. Let 𝜔 be an affine equiangular set with a switching root 𝑟 and let 𝜔′ be one with a switching
root 𝑟′ . We say that 𝜔 and 𝜔′ are isometric if there exists an affine equiangular set 𝜔′′ with 𝜔′′ ∼sw 𝜔′ and a
bijective isometry
                                           ℎ ∶ 𝜔 ∪ {𝑟} → 𝜔′′ ∪ {𝑟′ }
such that ℎ(𝑟) = 𝑟′ .
   It is known that two affine equiangular sets are isometric if and only if their induced sets of equiangular
lines are isometric; see [14, Lemma 2.4]. Also, let 𝜔 be an affine equiangular set with switching root 𝑟 such
that 𝜔 ∪ {𝑟} is contained in a set 𝑉 . Then, we say that 𝜔 is maximal in 𝑉 if there is no affine equiangular
set in 𝑉 with respect to 𝑟 properly containing 𝜔. Here, the set 𝑉 will be taken to be a finite set of vectors,
a lattice, or a Euclidean space. If 𝑉 is not specified, it is the ℝ-linear span of 𝜔 ∪ {𝑟}. In the case where
𝑉 is this ℝ-linear span, the induced set of equiangular lines is maximal in the ℝ-linear span of these lines.
Similarly, we say that 𝜔 is maximum in 𝑉 if there is no affine equiangular set 𝜔′ in 𝑉 with respect to 𝑟 such
that |𝜔′ | > |𝜔|.
Definition 2.7. An affine equiangular set 𝜔 with respect to a switching root 𝑟 is said to be strongly maximal,
if there is no affine equiangular set with respect to 𝑟 properly containing 𝜔 even if the dimension of the
underlying space is increased.
    We see that an affine equiangular set 𝜔 with norm 𝑠 with respect to a switching root 𝑟 is strongly maximal
if and only if the induced set of equiangular lines is strongly maximal, that is, there is no set of equiangular
lines properly containing it.

7.2    Maximality in ℝ𝐿 of maximum affine equiangular sets in 𝐿
    In this subsection, we show that all the maximum affine equiangular sets in 𝐿 are maximal in ℝ𝐿.
Theorem 7.5. Every maximum affine equiangular set in 𝐿 with respect to the switching root 𝜌 is maximal in

Proof. Let 𝜔 be a maximum affine equiangular set in 𝐿 with respect to the switching root 𝜌. By Theorem 7.4,
this set 𝜔 is isometric to one which together with 𝜌 generates either 𝐿 or 𝐿54 . Without loss of generality, we
