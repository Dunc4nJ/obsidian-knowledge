                                                                                                          Abstract
                                                      We prove the 4-uniform Erdős Matching Conjecture for every matching number s ≥ 6961. The
                                                  proof has two parts. First, building on ideas from Frankl–Rödl–Ruciński [14], we formulate a general
                                                  finite-board criterion for the r-uniform conjecture. The criterion has two assumptions: the (r − 1)-
                                                  uniform cover-side bound for links with matching number at most t holds at every m ≥ nr (t), and
                                                  a finite optimization problem for mixed-size trace configurations on an (r2 + r − 1)-vertex board.
                                                  Together with the corresponding lower-uniformity input, this finite-board optimization implies the
                                                  Erdős Matching Conjecture with explicit large-matching thresholds.
                                                      Second, we verify the finite-board assumption for r = 4. The local board has 19 vertices, and the
                                                  required inequality is decomposed into three weighted local inequalities: a leading wide layer, a 15-board
                                                  layer, and an 11-board layer. The verification is reduced to exact finite optimization and certificate-
                                                  validation problems: Ferrers down-set enumerations for pair and triple traces, rational Farkas-dual
                                                  certificates for the top-star branch, integer branch-and-bound up-set hitting and pattern searches for
                                                  the no-top-star branch, and residual-cut dual certificates for the 15-board and 11-board layers.

The two examples are the complete r-graph on rs + r − 1 vertices and the family of all r-sets meeting a
fixed s-set. These examples represent the two regimes of the problem: a dense clique-type construction
near the minimum admissible number of vertices, and a cover-type construction when the ambient vertex
set is large.
Historically, the graph case r = 2 follows from the theorem of Erdős and Gallai [4]. Early large-n results
for general uniformity go back to Bollobás, Daykin, and Erdős [2], and were subsequently strengthened in
several directions, including work of Huang, Loh, and Sudakov [16], Frankl, Luczak, and Mieczkowska [13],
and Frankl [7, 8, 9]. The two extremal regimes have also been studied separately: stability and cover-side
large-n results appear in work of Frankl and Kupavskii [10, 11], while the almost-perfect, clique-side range
was advanced by Frankl [9] and Kolupaev and Kupavskii [18]. A 2026 preprint of Frankl, Lu, Ma, and Wu
[12] proves stability results toward the 4-uniform case and establishes the conjecture in the range n ≥ 5s
with n sufficiently large. The present paper gives an independent certificate-based proof in an explicit
4-uniform large-s range: all admissible n ≥ 4s + 4 once s ≥ 6961, whereas Frankl–Lu–Ma–Wu treat the
large-n range n ≥ 5s with n sufficiently large. The case r = 3 is now known in full by Frankl [8]. Luczak
and Mieczkowska [19] proved the all-n result for sufficiently large s, and Frankl, Rödl, and Ruciński [14]
gave a short proof in a large-s range. Related matching and degree versions have also played an important
role in the development of the area, for example in [1, 15, 17]. As in much of extremal set theory, shifting is
a key structural tool; we use it in the standard compressed-family form described, for instance, in Frankl’s
survey [6].

2     A finite-board criterion for general r
This section is written for a fixed integer r ≥ 2. It proves the global reduction in a form that is suited to
certificate-assisted applications. The reduction uses a selected matching rule. For every stable maximal
property-ONE r-graph, the rule chooses one s-matching avoiding vertex 1 and contained in [rs + r − 1].
The rule may impose extra deterministic tie-breaking conditions. For r = 4 we shall choose the matching
so that the two residual vertices outside 1 are lexicographically minimal; this is the source of the seed
traces in the 19-board proof.

9     Certificate bundle and exact verification architecture
This section records the proof-critical finite statements verified by the ancillary certificate bundle. The
certificate files are data, and the checkers are deterministic verifiers that regenerate finite universes or
check integer and rational certificate arithmetic. The guiding principle is that a numerical optimizer is not
accepted as a proof object. The proof object is one of the following exact finite certificates:

 (a) an exhaustive enumeration of a finite down-set universe;
 (b) an exact branch-and-bound proof that no up-set satisfying specified hitting constraints exists below
     a target size;
 (c) a Farkas certificate, namely a nonnegative rational combination of valid linear inequalities;
 (d) a residual-cut dual certificate, namely a nonnegative integer combination of local matching obstruc-
     tion rows.

The scripts either regenerate the relevant finite universe from definitions or verify the listed certificate
arithmetic. All proof-critical arithmetic is integer or rational arithmetic after clearing denominators.

The no-top-star threshold verifier checks the following exact finite conditions.

(N1) M is an up-set in V4 .
(N2) C contains no upper 3-matching. Equivalently, for every choice of permutations π1 , π2 , π3 of {1, 2, 3},
