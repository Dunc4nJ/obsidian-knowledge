                                               No involutions in the missing Moore graph
                                                                           Yawara Ishida
                                                             A.I. System Research, Inc., Kyoto, Japan

                                                                               Abstract
arXiv:2606.29183v2 [math.CO] 8 Jul 2026




                                                   The existence of a Moore graph of degree 57 is the last open case
                                               in the Hoffman–Singleton classification of Moore graphs of diameter
                                               two. We prove that such a graph, if one exists, has no involutory
                                               automorphisms; consequently its automorphism group has odd order.
                                               The proof relies on a new trace–rank identity, valid for every finite
                                               graph and obtained from the Brauer quotient of p-permutation lattices:
                                               the trace of an automorphism of prime order p on a spectral summand
                                               whose projection idempotent preserves p-adic permutation lattice equals
                                               the rank, over the residue field of characteristic p, of the corresponding
                                               idempotent restricted to the fixed vertices. For strongly regular graphs,
                                               this upgrades the classical character-value congruences for prime-order
                                               automorphisms to exact rank equalities. As further applications, the
                                               identity yields new restrictions on automorphisms of odd prime order
                                               of the hypothetical Moore graph, and it sharpens known automorphism
                                               analyses of other hypothetical strongly regular graphs.


                                          1   Introduction
                                          Moore graphs are the extremal graphs for the degree–diameter problem:
                                          regular graphs whose order attains the Moore bound for their degree and
                                          diameter. We refer to the surveys of Miller–Širáň [26] and Dalfó [12] for
                                          general background. In diameter two, the theorem of Hoffman and Singleton
                                          shows that the degree of such a graph can only be

                                                                          2,   3,       7,   57;

                                          the first three cases are the pentagon, the Petersen graph, and the Hoffman–
                                          Singleton graph, while the case of degree 57 remains open [16]. For diameter
                                          greater than two, Bannai–Ito [2] and Damerell [13] independently proved
                                          that the only Moore graphs are the odd cycles, so the degree-57 graph is the

                                                                                    1
unique Moore graph whose existence is undecided. The hypothetical graph in
the last case is often called the missing Moore graph. It would be a strongly
regular graph with parameters [16, 7]
                        (v, k, λ, µ) = (3250, 57, 0, 1).
Recent work on the existence question includes the study of integral invariants
such as the critical group [15], structural constraints on the hypothetical
graph [28, 29], and computational search [30, 31, 32].
    Even in the absence of an existence proof, the possible automorphisms
of the missing Moore graph are subject to strong restrictions. Higman’s
unpublished argument, as recorded in Brouwer–Haemers and discussed in
Cameron’s text on permutation groups, shows that such a graph cannot
be vertex-transitive [7, 9]. Makhnev–Paduchikh studied the fixed-point
subgraphs of automorphisms and the case of even group order [24, 25]; Mačaj–
Širáň proved that the order of the automorphism group is at most 375 if that
order is odd and at most 110 if it is even [19]; and Kováčiková, in a doctoral
thesis, refined the resulting restrictions for automorphisms of order 7 [17].
The main result of this paper eliminates the even-order case altogether.
Theorem 1.1. Let Γ be a Moore graph of diameter two and degree 57. Then
Aut(Γ) contains no element of order 2.
    Consequently, if the missing Moore graph exists, its automorphism group
has odd order (Corollary 6.1).
    The proof is based on a trace–rank identity for idempotent summands
of permutation lattices. Let x be an automorphism of prime order p of a
finite graph ∆, let F be its set of fixed vertices, and let O be a complete
discrete valuation ring of residue characteristic p, with field of fractions K,
maximal ideal m, and residue field κ. If E is an idempotent endomorphism
of the vertex lattice OV (∆) commuting with the action of x, with image
L = EOV (∆) , then
                     (           )         (              )
                   tr x | K ⊗O L = rankκ E[F] mod m ,
where E[F] is the principal submatrix of E on the fixed vertices (Corollary
4.2). The identity is obtained by passing to the Brauer quotient of the
p-permutation lattice L, reviewed in Section 3; in particular it applies to
every spectral idempotent of the adjacency matrix that preserves the p-adic
vertex lattice.
    For strongly regular graphs the identity becomes especially concrete. The
spectral idempotents are linear combinations
                           Eθ = αθ I + βθ A + γθ J,

and whenever Eθ preserves the lattice, the identity reads
                                        (                              )
    αθ a0 (x) + βθ a1 (x) + γθ v = rankκ (αθ IF + βθ AF + γθ JF ) mod m ,

where v is the number of vertices and

                     ai (x) = |{u ∈ V (∆) : d(u, x(u)) = i}|

are the displacement counts of x, d denoting the graph distance (Proposition
4.4). Thus the ordinary character value on the left, whose integrality is the
source of the classical congruences for a1 (x) [19, 14], is not merely constrained:
it is equal to an explicit modular rank determined by the fixed-point subgraph
(Remark 4.6). Rationality of the eigenvalues is not required: if Eθ is defined
only over a finite extension of Qp , the same argument is carried out over the
valuation ring of that extension (Remark 4.5).
     For the missing Moore graph the non-principal eigenvalues are 7 and
−8. Section 5 computes the two corresponding spectral idempotents and
determines the primes at which each preserves the p-adic vertex lattice; at
p = 2, only the summand for the eigenvalue −8 is available. Combining the
resulting rank equality with the fixed-point theorem for involutions — the
fixed subgraph of an involution t is the star K1,55 (Theorem 2.1) — forces a
negative value of the count a1 (t), a contradiction. This proves Theorem 1.1
(Section 6).
     The same comparison yields new restrictions for automorphisms of odd
prime order (Section 7). For the primes at which the known fixed-point
alternatives are precise enough, we obtain the following.
Theorem 1.2. Let Γ be a Moore graph of diameter two and degree 57, and
let x ∈ Aut(Γ) be an automorphism of prime order p ∈ {7, 11, 13, 19}. Then
a0 (x) and a1 (x) satisfy the following restrictions:

                    p        a0 (x)           a1 (x)
                    7 2, 9, 16, 23, 30, 37 7a0 (x) + 35
                    11          5               55
                    13          0               65
                    19          1               57

    For p = 7, Theorem 1.2 recovers a theorem of Kováčiková [17, Theorem
5.1] whose proof rests on computer-assisted counts of induced subgraphs; the
derivation given here uses neither the computer search nor the tabulated
bounds of [19, Lemma 12] (see Section 7.1 for a detailed comparison). For
p = 11, 13, and 19, Theorem 1.2 selects the smallest of the values of a1 (x)

allowed by the table of Mačaj–Širáň [19, Lemma 12], which appears to be
new. The remaining odd primes that can occur as orders of automorphisms
of Γ are 3 and 5 (Lemma 2.2): for these, neither spectral idempotent of Γ is
defined over the p-adic integers, and the method of this paper gives nothing.
    The trace–rank identity is independent of the Moore graph parameters.
Beyond the missing Moore graph, Section 8 applies it to sharpen known
automorphism analyses of five hypothetical strongly regular graphs, replacing
character congruences by exact displacement counts.


2     Known facts on the missing Moore graph
Throughout the paper, Γ denotes a Moore graph of diameter two and degree
57, and V (Γ) denotes its vertex set. The graph is finite, simple, undirected,
and regular.

2.1   Parameters and spectrum
The Moore bound gives

                          |V (Γ)| = 1 + 572 = 3250,

and the diameter-two Moore graph condition implies that Γ is strongly regular.
A strongly regular graph with parameters (v, k, λ, µ) is a k-regular graph
on v vertices in which any two adjacent vertices have exactly λ common
neighbours and any two distinct non-adjacent vertices have exactly µ common
neighbours. For Γ these parameters are [16, 7]

                        (v, k, λ, µ) = (3250, 57, 0, 1);

here λ = 0 because Γ is triangle-free, and µ = 1 because any two non-adjacent
vertices have a unique common neighbour. Let A be its adjacency matrix
and let J be the all-one matrix. Then

                             A2 + A = 56I + J.                            (1)

Consequently the spectrum of A is

                     Spec(A) = {571 , 71729 , (−8)1520 }.                 (2)

This spectral calculation is standard for Moore graphs and strongly regular
graphs with these parameters [16, 7].

The fixed-point calculation for involutions is part of Higman’s unpublished
argument and was further used by Makhnev–Paduchikh in their study of
Γ. For an automorphism g, write Fix(g) for the subgraph induced by the
vertices fixed by g. We need the following precise form.

Theorem 2.1 (Higman–Makhnev–Paduchikh). Let t ∈ Aut(Γ) be an involu-
tion. Then
                        Fix(t) ∼
                               = K1,55 .
In particular, t fixes exactly 56 vertices.

    The assertion that an involution fixes exactly 56 vertices, and that its fixed
vertices form a star, is proved in Cameron’s account of Higman’s argument [9,
proof of Theorem 3.13]; the count is also contained in the proof of Brouwer–
Haemers [7, Proposition 11.5.2]. The star statement is recorded explicitly by
Makhnev–Paduchikh [24, Lemma 4], who attribute the result to Higman and
cite Cameron’s account for its proof; it recurs in the fixed-point alternative of
their later paper [25, Proposition 1]. We use only this fixed-point statement
and not any classification of possible automorphism groups.
    For automorphisms of odd prime order we use the following alternative,
due to Makhnev–Paduchikh [24, Lemma 3] and stated in the form below
as Lemma 4 of Mačaj–Širáň [19], here phrased for a single automorphism.
In [19] each case carries a divisibility condition on the order of the group,
which for a group of prime order p amounts to the memberships stated below;
the underlying six-fold alternative for fixed-point subgraphs goes back to
Aschbacher’s analysis [1].

Lemma 2.2 (Makhnev–Paduchikh; Mačaj–Širáň). Let x ∈ Aut(Γ) have odd
prime order p. Then one of the following holds:

 (1) Fix(x) is empty and p ∈ {5, 13};

 (2) Fix(x) is a single vertex and p ∈ {3, 19};

 (3) Fix(x) is a star K1,1+7ℓ with ℓ ≥ 0 and p = 7;

 (4) Fix(x) is a pentagon and p ∈ {5, 11};

 (5) Fix(x) is the Petersen graph and p = 3;

 (6) Fix(x) is the Hoffman–Singleton graph and p = 5.

3    A trace formula for p-permutation lattices
We record the module-theoretic terminology used below. Let O be a complete
discrete valuation ring with maximal ideal m, residue field κ = O/m of
characteristic p > 0, and field of fractions K of characteristic 0, and let G
be a finite group. An OG-module is a module over the group algebra OG.
An OG-lattice is an OG-module which is finitely generated and free as an
O-module. A permutation OG-lattice is an OG-lattice admitting an O-basis
permuted by G.
    An OG-lattice is called p-permutation if its restriction to a Sylow p-
subgroup of G is a permutation lattice. Equivalently, it admits an O-basis
permuted by a Sylow p-subgroup. We shall use the standard characterization
that direct summands of permutation OG-lattices are p-permutation; see
Lassueur [18, Proposition–Definition 4.2]. The Brauer quotient and its basis
description for permutation modules are used in the form given by Broué
and by Bouc–Thévenaz [5, 4].
    We use this material for

                            G = Cp = ⟨x⟩,           xp = 1,

cyclic of prime order equal to the residue characteristic p of O. In this case,
a p-permutation OCp -lattice is simply an OCp -lattice admitting an O-basis
permuted by Cp , because Cp is its own Sylow p-subgroup. For an OCp -lattice
N , put
                          N = κ ⊗O N ∼   = N/mN.
We write
                                          Cp /     C
                            N (Cp ) = N          Tr1 p (N ),                  (3)
where
                        C
                     Tr1 p (v) = v + x(v) + · · · + xp−1 (v)
is the relative trace from the trivial subgroup to Cp . Thus N (Cp ) is the usual
Brauer quotient of the κCp -module N at the subgroup Cp , written so as to
record the lattice N from which it comes.

   The following observation shows that, for a lattice with a basis permuted
by Cp , the ordinary trace in characteristic zero and the Brauer quotient in
characteristic p count the same fixed basis elements.
Lemma 3.1. Let N be a p-permutation OCp -lattice. Then

                         tr (x | K ⊗O N ) = dimκ N (Cp ),

where the integer on the right is regarded as an element of K.
Proof. Since N is p-permutation and Cp is its own Sylow p-subgroup, N has
an O-basis X permuted by Cp . As Cp has prime order, the Cp -orbits on X
have size one or p.
   A fixed basis element contributes 1 to the trace of x on K ⊗O N . An
orbit of size p, on which x acts as a p-cycle, contributes the trace of the
permutation matrix of a p-cycle, which is 0. Hence the left-hand side counts
the fixed elements of X .
   After reduction modulo m, the image X of X is a κ-basis of N permuted
by Cp . If {b, x(b), . . . , xp−1 (b)} is an orbit of size p in X , then the invariant
vectors supported on it are spanned by
                                                        C
                       b + x(b) + · · · + xp−1 (b) = Tr1 p (b),

which vanishes in N (Cp ). If b ∈ X is fixed by Cp , its image survives in
the quotient. The images of the fixed elements of X therefore form a basis
of N (Cp ), as in the standard basis description of the Brauer quotient for
permutation modules [4, Proposition 2.5(4)]. Hence the right-hand side also
counts the fixed elements of X .

8     Further applications: sharpening automorphism
      analyses of hypothetical strongly regular graphs
Automorphisms of hypothetical strongly regular graphs are a well-studied
subject. Behbahani–Lam constrain automorphisms of prime order via orbit
matrices [3]; Crnković–Maksimović extend the orbit-matrix method to groups
of composite order [11]; De Winter–Kamischke–Wang derive Benson-type
congruences from ordinary eigenvalue traces [14]; Cesarz–Woldar give a
computer-free analysis of the putative Conway 99-graph [10]; and a series
of papers by Makhnev and his coauthors determines, for one parameter set
at a time, the possible fixed-point subgraphs of automorphisms of prime

                                        19
order by Higman’s character-theoretic method [20, 22, 23, 21, 27]. In this
section we use Proposition 4.4 to sharpen analyses of the latter kind. Where
a known fixed-point theorem determines the fixed subgraph but constrains
the displacement count a1 (x) only through the character congruences, the
rank equality replaces the congruence by an exact value; for one parameter
set the comparison moreover uncovers two arithmetic errors in the analysis of
[22], one of which re-opens a case believed closed (Remark 8.5). The existence
of each of the five parameter sets treated below is unresolved as of July 2026
[6, 8].

Proposition 8.6. Suppose a strongly regular graph ∆ with parameters
(1276, 50, 0, 2) exists, and let x ∈ Aut(∆) have odd prime order p ̸= 7.
Then one of the following holds:

  (1) p = 3, Fix(x) is the Clebsch graph, and a1 (x) = 0;

  (2) p = 5, and either Fix(x) ∼
                               = K1 and a1 (x) = 50, or Fix(x) is the Clebsch
      graph and a1 (x) = 70;

  (3) p = 11, Fix(x) = ∅, and a1 (x) = 44;

  (4) p = 29, Fix(x) = ∅, and a1 (x) = 58.

Proof. The fixed subgraphs in (1)–(4) are those of [27]; it remains to compute
the ranks.
    For the empty fixed subgraph both ranks vanish, and the equation available
— the second at p = 11, the first at p = 29 — gives a1 (x) = 44 and 58
respectively.
    For p = 5 and Fix(x) ∼  = K1 , the diagonal entry of 308E6 = 176I +22A−J
on the fixed vertex is 175 ≡ 0 (mod 5), so ρr = 0 and the first equation gives
a1 (x) = 50.
    When Fix(x) is the Clebsch graph, the matrix 308E6 = 176I + 22AF − JF
has eigenvalues 270, 198, 110 of multiplicities 1, 10, 5 by Lemma 8.3. Modulo
5 only 198 is a unit, so ρr = 10 and the first equation gives a1 (x) = 70; modulo
3 only 110 is a unit, so ρr = 5 and the first equation gives a1 (x) = 0.

   Parts (2) and (4) sharpen the congruences of [27] to exact values; in the
Clebsch case of part (2) the value a1 (x) = 0 allowed by the congruence is

Parts (1) and (3) recover known values: a1 (x) = 0 at p = 3 is elementary in
