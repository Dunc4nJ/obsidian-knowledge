                                                                                                    Abstract
                                                         We exhibit an explicit integer polynomial in five variables, of total degree 14 and with
                                                     constant Hessian determinant 128, whose gradient is not injective. Consequently its formal
                                                     Legendre transform is not a polynomial, and the Hessian conjecture HC5 is false. The coun-
                                                     terexample is obtained from the six-variable doubling of Alpöge’s 2026 Jacobian counterex-
                                                     ample by a one-variable Schur descent—a partial Legendre transform in a single variable.
                                                     Combined with de Bondt’s theorem that HCn holds for n ≤ 3, with the elementary doubling
                                                     and stabilization bridges relating the Jacobian conjectures JCn to the Hessian conjectures
                                                     HCn , and with Alpöge’s refutation of JC3 , this decides the Hessian conjecture in every di-
                                                     mension except n = 4: HCn is true for n ≤ 3, false for n ≥ 5, and open only at n = 4. Exactly
                                                     two statements of the two families remain unsettled, JC2 and HC4 , linked by HC4 ⇒ JC2 .
                                                     Along the way we record, as a warm-up, an explicit six-variable counterexample to HC6 with
                                                     constant Hessian determinant −4 and non-injective gradient. This note adds the five-variable
                                                     counterexample to, and updates the status recorded in, the first author’s earlier educational
                                                     preprint [7].

    This paper has two purposes. First and foremost, we construct an explicit counterexample
to the Hessian conjecture HC5 (Section 6): a five-variable integer polynomial of total degree 14
with constant nonzero Hessian determinant and non-injective gradient, obtained from Alpöge’s
map (1) by doubling followed by a one-variable Schur descent. Second, we record the resulting
status of the two families JCn and the closely related Hessian conjectures HCn introduced in [6].
Together with de Bondt’s theorem that HCn holds for n ≤ 3 [3] and two standard bridges, the
new counterexample decides HCn in every dimension but n = 4, collapsing the two dimension-
indexed families of open problems to exactly two low-dimensional statements, JC2 and HC4
(Theorem 4.1). An earlier, single-author version recording only the status portion, intended for
educational purposes, is available as [7]; the present paper adds the HC5 counterexample and
updates the status to include the truth of HC2 and HC3 , established by de Bondt [3] and not
recorded there.


2    The Hessian conjecture and the two bridges
Let φ ∈ k[x1 , . . . , xn ] satisfy det Hess φ ∈ k× , where Hess φ = ∂ 2 φ/∂xi ∂xj . Then Hess φ(0)
                                                                                  

is invertible, so the gradient map ∇φ admits a formal inverse at ∇φ(0) (after harmless affine
normalization), and one defines the formal Legendre transform φL of φ by the classical formula
φL (p) = ⟨p, x(p)⟩ − φ(x(p)), where x(p) is the formal inverse of p = ∇φ(x); equivalently,
∇φL = (∇φ)−1 as formal maps. This is the classical change of variables x ↔ p = ∇φ(x)
familiar from convex analysis and mechanics, carried out formally near the base point. In
general φL is a formal power series; see [6], where a tree formula for its coefficients is given.

Conjecture 2.1 (HCn , [6]). If φ ∈ k[x1 , . . . , xn ] has det Hess φ ∈ k× , then φL is a polynomial.

   The two conjectures are linked by two bridges. The first is immediate; the second is the

Proposition 2.2. JCn =⇒ HCn .

Proof (following [6]). If det Hess φ ∈ k× then ∇φ is a polynomial map with constant nonzero
Jacobian determinant, so by JCn it has a polynomial inverse G. By uniqueness of the formal
inverse at the base point, G coincides with the formal inverse x(p) of p = ∇φ(x) appearing in
the definition of φL . Substituting into the defining formula φL (p) = ⟨p, x(p)⟩ − φ(x(p)) gives

                                  φL (p) = ⟨p, G(p)⟩ − φ(G(p)),

a polynomial in p. Hence φL is a polynomial.

observation.

Lemma 2.4. Let φ satisfy det Hess φ ∈ k× . If the gradient map ∇φ is not injective, then φL
is not a polynomial.

Proof. Were φL a polynomial, ∇φL would be a polynomial map, and the formal identity ∇φL ◦
∇φ = id, valid near the base point, would be an identity of polynomials, hence hold on all of
kn ; thus ∇φL would be a global inverse of ∇φ, impossible for a non-injective map.


3    Two stabilization lemmas
Falsehood propagates upward in dimension, on both sides.

Lemma 3.1. If JCn is false, then JCn+1 is false.

Proof. If F : kn → kn is a Keller map, so is Fe(x, t) = (F (x), t) in dimension n + 1, with
Jac Fe = Jac F ∈ k× . As Fe acts on x and t separately, it is invertible as a polynomial map if
and only if F is. Hence a counterexample to JCn lifts to one for JCn+1 .

4     The status theorem
Theorem 4.1. Over any field of characteristic zero:

    (i) JC1 and HC1 are true; moreover HC2 and HC3 are true.

 (ii) JCn is false for every n ≥ 3.

 (iii) HCn is false for every n ≥ 5.

 (iv) Within the two indexed families JCn and HCn , n ≥ 1, over fields of characteristic zero,
      these results leave exactly two statements undecided, JC2 and HC4 , linked by

                                                 HC4 =⇒ JC2 .

Proof. (i) The one-variable cases are classical: F ′ ∈ k× forces F linear, and φ′′ ∈ k× forces φ
quadratic, hence φL quadratic. For HC2 and HC3 , de Bondt [3] proved that a polynomial in
at most three variables with constant nonzero Hessian determinant has a polynomial gradient
inverse; the argument in the proof of Proposition 2.2 then makes φL a polynomial.
    (ii) JC3 is false by (1)–(2); Lemma 3.1 propagates falsehood to all n ≥ 3.
    (iii) HC5 is false by the explicit five-variable counterexample of Section 6 (Theorem 6.1);
Lemma 3.2 propagates falsehood to all n ≥ 5. Independently, the falsity of JC3 gives the falsity
of HC6 through Proposition 2.3 (contrapositive), recovering the range n ≥ 6.
    (iv) Parts (i)–(iii) decide JCn for every n ̸= 2 and HCn for every n ̸= 4, leaving exactly JC2
and HC4 undecided; these are linked by HC4 ⇒ JC2 (Proposition 2.3 with n = 2).

The resulting landscape:

                                 n       1       2         3       4      ≥5
                               JCn     true    open       false   false   false
                               HCn     true    true       true    open    false
               The only bridge between the two surviving open statements is HC4 ⇒ JC2 .

Both survivors are open: JC2 is the classical two-dimensional Jacobian conjecture, unproven
since 1939 (see [8]), and no result known to us settles HC4 . They are linked only by HC4 ⇒
JC2 (Proposition 2.3 with n = 2), so HC4 is the stronger: proving it settles JC2 , while a
counterexample to JC2 falsifies it. That both sit in the lowest dimensions may point to a
genuinely low-dimensional phenomenon.




                                                      4
Remark 4.2 (Why the descent stops at five variables). The Schur descent that produces HC5
from HC6 (Section 6) cannot be iterated to reach HC4 : the mechanism self-terminates. Each
descent needs a pivot variable in which the potential is affine-linear with a degenerate reduced
Hessian, a degeneracy the doubled potential owes to its linearity in the dual variables. A single
descent already spends that linearity—the descended potential is quadratic in those variables—
so no further pivot of the required type remains. Reaching HC4 therefore appears to require
genuinely new ideas.

nomial by Lemma 2.4.

Remark A.4. Only a collision of F over a common last coordinate is needed—not a collision
of the doubled gradient ∇Φ, which is produced automatically at y = 0. The construction
is a genuine two-parameter family: λ ∈ k× scales the determinant, while Lemma A.2 allows
any collision lying over a value A = A0 to be transported by taking µ = t0 − λA0 in ψλ,µ =
B + λ2 A2 + µA; the choice A0 = 0 above is the simplest. When F , the points x′± , and t0 , λ are
defined over Q, a scalar multiple of ψλ has integral coefficients. Over a general field no such
integrality holds, and none is claimed.

verified independently in exact rational arithmetic. A fully expanded, line-by-line transcript—
