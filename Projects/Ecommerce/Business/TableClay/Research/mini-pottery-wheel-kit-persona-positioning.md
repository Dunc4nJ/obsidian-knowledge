# Persona Positioning: Mini Pottery Wheel Starter Kit

## Persona Overview

| Persona | Segment | Purchase Driver | Awareness Level | Priority |
|---------|---------|----------------|----------------|----------|
| The Burnout Creative | Self-care/wellness seekers | Therapeutic escape from screens/stress | Problem-Aware | Primary |
| The Intentional Parent | Parents seeking screen-free activities | Educational family bonding | Solution-Aware | Primary |
| The Perfect Gift-Finder | Gift-givers for birthdays/holidays | Unique, memorable, experiential gift | Solution-Aware | Primary |
| The Viral Curious | TikTok/Instagram-inspired impulse | "I saw this and I need it" | Product-Aware | Secondary |

## Detailed Personas

### Persona 1: The Burnout Creative

**Demographics:** 25–45, skews female (65/35), middle income ($50K–$100K), urban/suburban. Works in a desk-bound or high-stress role.
**Psychographics:** Values self-care, mindfulness, and "slow living." Has tried journaling, yoga, or meditation. Follows wellness and craft accounts on Instagram. Identifies as creative but hasn't had a creative outlet in years. Overwhelmed by screen time.

**Awareness level:** Problem-Aware — knows they need a creative/therapeutic outlet but hasn't identified mini pottery specifically.

**Current situation:**
Spends evenings scrolling phone after draining workday. Feels vaguely unfulfilled. Has thought about picking up a hobby but hasn't committed. May have tried and abandoned other crafts. The idea of pottery appeals but seems inaccessible (expensive, messy, requires a studio).

**Trigger event:**
Sees pottery content on social media (ASMR video, "first attempt" transformation), a friend mentions doing pottery, or searches for "stress relief hobbies" or "creative activities for adults." Valentine's self-care season or New Year resolution energy.

**Decision criteria:**
1. Ease of getting started — must be all-inclusive, no additional purchases needed
2. Mess containability — must fit in their apartment/home without creating chaos
3. Therapeutic value — must feel genuinely calming, not frustrating
4. Quality of results — needs to produce something they're proud of

**Objections specific to this persona:**
- "Will this actually be relaxing or just frustrating?" → Quick-win guide ensures first piece succeeds
- "I don't have space or a studio" → Kitchen-table sizing, 5-minute cleanup
- "Am I too old to start pottery?" → Multi-generational positioning, "never too late" messaging

**Belief chain:**
Unaware ("I just feel burned out") → "I need a hands-on creative outlet that isn't screen-based" → "Pottery could be that outlet — it's therapeutic and produces real results" → "A mini pottery wheel kit lets me try this at home without a studio" → "TableClay has everything I need in one box — guides included" → Purchase

**Messaging approach:**
- **Hook:** "Your phone can't give you this feeling" (visual: hands on spinning clay)
- **Angle:** Therapeutic transformation — from stressed to calm, from consuming to creating
- **Proof:** Before/after emotional state + beautiful finished piece + "relaxing ASMR" content
- **CTA:** "Your creative escape starts tonight."

**Channel preference:** Instagram (Reels + Stories), TikTok, Pinterest, Facebook (wellness groups)

### Persona 2: The Intentional Parent

**Demographics:** 30–50, any gender (slight female skew for purchase), household income $60K+, suburban. Has kids ages 6–14.
**Psychographics:** Concerned about screen time. Values educational, hands-on activities. Seeks "quality time" experiences. Willing to invest in activities that develop skills and create memories. Often researches gifts and activities thoroughly.

**Awareness level:** Solution-Aware — knows they want screen-free, hands-on family activities. May have explored other craft kits, STEM kits, or outdoor activities.

**Current situation:**
Struggles to find activities the whole family enjoys together. Kids gravitate toward screens. Previous craft kits were one-and-done. Looking for something with repeat engagement that develops real skills and produces tangible results the kids can be proud of.

**Trigger event:**
Searching for "gifts for kids who have everything," "screen-free activities," or "family craft projects." Holiday or birthday approaching. Teacher recommends tactile/sensory activities. Sees another family's pottery on social media.

**Decision criteria:**
1. Multi-age appeal — works for both kids AND parents
2. Educational value — develops motor skills, patience, creativity
3. Repeat engagement — not a single-use kit
4. Safety — non-toxic, certified, age-appropriate

**Objections specific to this persona:**
- "My kids will lose interest after one use" → "Multiple times a week for over a year" testimonial
- "Is it safe for kids?" → Non-toxic clay, safety certifications
- "How messy will it be with kids?" → Splash guard + apron + cleanup guide

**Belief chain:**
Unaware ("Kids are always on screens") → "We need tactile, creative family activities" → "Pottery develops real skills — focus, patience, fine motor" → "A mini wheel kit makes this possible at home" → "TableClay is designed for families — ages 8 to 80" → Purchase

**Messaging approach:**
- **Hook:** "The activity your kids won't put down" (video: kids absorbed in pottery, no screens in sight)
- **Angle:** Screen-free bonding + skill development. "What they learn stays with them. What they make stays on the shelf."
- **Proof:** Family activity footage, kids showing off finished pieces, parent testimonials about engagement
- **CTA:** "Make something together tonight."

**Channel preference:** Facebook (parenting groups), Pinterest, Instagram, YouTube (family activity searches), Google Shopping (gift searches)

### Persona 3: The Perfect Gift-Finder

**Demographics:** 25–55, any gender, any income. The purchaser is NOT the end user — they're buying for someone else.
**Psychographics:** Wants to give memorable, meaningful gifts — not generic ones. Values "experience" over "stuff." Willing to pay more for something unique. Often shops for partners, parents, friends, or "the person who has everything."

**Awareness level:** Solution-Aware — looking for a unique gift, may or may not have considered pottery specifically. Actively searching "unique gift ideas," "gifts for creative people," "date night gift."

**Current situation:**
Has a birthday, holiday, anniversary, or special occasion approaching. Browsing for something that stands out. Tired of giving gift cards or generic products. May have seen craft kits as ads or social content.

**Trigger event:**
Upcoming occasion + browsing gift lists + social proof (seeing others' pottery on social media or gift guides). "Best gifts for [occasion] 2026" searches. Black Friday/holiday shopping period. Valentine's Day.

**Decision criteria:**
1. Presentation/unboxing quality — must look giftable in packaging
2. All-inclusive — recipient shouldn't need to buy anything else
3. "Wow factor" — unique enough to be memorable
4. Safe bet — not too niche or skill-dependent (anyone can enjoy it)

**Objections specific to this persona:**
- "Will they actually use it?" → Show broad appeal across demographics
- "Is the packaging gift-worthy?" → Premium unboxing experience
- "What if they're not 'creative'?" → "No experience needed" messaging

**Belief chain:**
"I need a unique gift" → "Experience-style gifts are better than stuff" → "A pottery kit is a creative, memorable experience in a box" → "TableClay looks premium, has everything included, and doesn't require experience" → Purchase

**Messaging approach:**
- **Hook:** "The gift they'll actually talk about" (unboxing reveal shot)
- **Angle:** Experience gifting — give them a memory, not a thing. Premium packaging that looks thoughtful.
- **Proof:** "Bought for my [relationship] and they loved it" testimonials + beautiful unboxing content
- **CTA:** "Give the gift of making something beautiful."

**Channel preference:** Google Shopping, Amazon, Instagram (gift guide content), Pinterest (gift boards), Facebook (holiday shopping), gift guide publications

### Persona 4: The Viral Curious

**Demographics:** 18–35, skews female, any income. Active on TikTok and Instagram. Impulse buyer.
**Psychographics:** Trend-aware, tries new things. Attracted to aesthetically pleasing products. Makes purchase decisions based on social proof and visual content. Values the "experience" and shareability of a product. May or may not have a sustained interest in pottery.

**Awareness level:** Product-Aware — they've seen pottery content on TikTok/Instagram, know products like this exist, and are actively considering buying one. The ASMR and satisfying content has done the awareness work.

**Current situation:**
Has been watching mini pottery content for weeks/months. Saves pottery videos. Comments "I need this." Hasn't pulled the trigger because they're unsure which product to buy, worried it's harder than it looks, or waiting for the right moment/price.

**Trigger event:**
Particularly viral pottery video, friends doing pottery, discount/promotion, "treat yourself" moment, payday. Often seasonal (post-holiday self-gift, Valentine's solo treat).

**Decision criteria:**
1. Aesthetic/Instagram-worthy — the wheel and results must look good on camera
2. Low barrier to entry — they want to recreate what they saw on TikTok
3. Price sensitivity — more price-conscious than other personas
4. Shareability — they'll want to post their first creation

**Objections specific to this persona:**
- "Can I actually recreate what I saw on TikTok?" → Beginner guide + "first attempt" gallery
- "Which one should I buy?" → Clear positioning as the "complete kit"
- "Is $79 worth it for something I might not stick with?" → "Test the waters" framing

**Belief chain:**
"Pottery videos are so satisfying" → "I want to try this myself" → "I need a wheel that's beginner-friendly AND aesthetic" → "TableClay is the one all the creators use / recommend" → Purchase

**Messaging approach:**
- **Hook:** ASMR satisfying pottery video with product reveal at end
- **Angle:** "Recreate the TikTok pottery aesthetic at home" — bridge the inspiration-to-action gap
- **Proof:** UGC content showing real first-timers recreating viral pottery looks
- **CTA:** "Your turn." / "Stop watching. Start making."

**Channel preference:** TikTok (primary), Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, TikTok Shop
