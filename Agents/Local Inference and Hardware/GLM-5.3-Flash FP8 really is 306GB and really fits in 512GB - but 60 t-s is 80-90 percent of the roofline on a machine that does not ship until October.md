## Key Takeaways

- **Verified, and stronger than he claims: the capacity argument holds with room to spare.** The Hugging Face model card for `zai-org/GLM-5.3-Flash` reports **321.3B parameters** and **62 safetensors shards totaling 328.3 GB / 305.8 GiB** — his "320B" and "~306 GB" are both exact, and the repo is **FP8-native** (314.4B of the params stored as `F8_E4M3`, with a separate BF16 repo shipped alongside), so calling FP8 "full precision" is fair rather than sloppy: it is the reference release, not a lossy community quant. The non-obvious part he *understates* is the KV cache. The architecture is a hybrid — **34 of 45 layers are linear attention** (constant-size state, no growing cache) and only 11 are DeepSeek-style sparse attention with a **512-dim compressed KV** — so at FP8 the cache costs roughly **0.7 GiB at 128K context and ~5 GiB at the full 1M**. Weights plus a million-token context plus macOS still lands near 315 GiB in a 512 GiB pool. The fit is not marginal; it is comfortable, and it is the one part of the post nobody in 186 replies successfully attacked.

- **Not verified, and this is the load-bearing number: ~60 t/s is a roofline, not a measurement.** Because [[Step 01 - Decode is memory-bandwidth-bound (the roofline)|decode is memory-bandwidth-bound]], the ceiling is bandwidth divided by bytes touched per token. Working the config (8 of 288 routed experts plus 1 shared expert at `moe_intermediate_size` 2048 across 42 MoE layers, 3 dense layers, hybrid attention, BF16 `lm_head`) gives **~14-16B active parameters, or roughly 15-19 GB read per token** — so 1.2 TB/s caps out at **~63-80 tok/s**. Claiming 60 therefore assumes **80-90% of theoretical peak bandwidth**. That is the number that does not survive contact with measured hardware: [[buy the RAM that holds the model - a SKU-by-SKU M5 Mac guide with the CUDA tax and separate decode-prefill predictions|the SKU-by-SKU M5 guide]] puts MoE at only **~28%** of theoretical bandwidth on Metal (dense hits 70-90%), and @sethprattsf independently reports **~40% for MLX versus ~80% for CUDA**. At 28-40% the honest expectation is **~20-30 tok/s**, not 60. The one legitimate escape hatch he never invokes: the config carries `num_nextn_predict_layers: 1`, so **MTP speculative decoding can beat the single-token roofline** — which is exactly how Seth got 47 t/s on a slower M3 Ultra. Sixty is reachable *with* speculation and unreachable without it, and the post asserts it flatly with no source.

- **The hardware does not exist yet, which makes every t/s figure in the thread an extrapolation by construction.** The 512GB M5 Ultra is an October part, and the reply section caught this immediately and repeatedly — @iamdamianpala, @0x004B4D, @pkraszewski, @karololszacki, @peadar_irl and @4cruzeta all note the SKU is unreleased, with @ric_guia asking the obvious "how does he have these numbers if the new Macs aren't available yet?" and @sorrydudelmao, @0x1ee7, @artur_wala and @ji_stark all asking for a source that never arrives. Pricing is equally unsettled: replies range from @karololszacki's 256GB-at-$18,299 and @pkraszewski's 57k PLN (with 512GB projected past 100k PLN) to @4cruzeta's ~$18,500 to @MikeBradleyAI's "under $12,000" elsewhere — a spread wide enough that the **omission of the Mac's own price**, flagged by @IbexStrike and @Issakimho, is the post's second real weakness. Contrast the same-price discipline in [[at 15-20K with 512GB the real gap is bandwidth not compute - Mac Studio M5 Ultra vs 4x DGX Spark vs 4x Ryzen AI Halo|the matched-price three-way comparison]], which puts a number on both sides.

- **"10x RTX 5090" is a strawman, and the correct PC build is 4x RTX PRO 6000.** Ten 5090s at $1,999 MSRP is $19,990, so "over $20,000" is arithmetically defensible on paper — but only on paper, and only for the cards: @Dont48, @steddyman and @PapalGuard put the street build at **$40-50K**, and nobody sane assembles ten 32GB gaming cards to hold one model. @zhimin_zhang's one-liner is the real answer — **four RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell at 96GB each is 384GB at 1,792 GB/s per card**, which holds the model in four slots instead of ten and is the configuration [[tensor parallelism aggregates memory controllers so 8x RTX PRO 6000 reaches ~14.3 TB-s where one M5 Ultra has 1.2|the tensor-parallelism counterpoint]] is built around: TP shards each layer so the memory controllers read concurrently and bandwidth genuinely aggregates, rather than the sequential pipeline split that wastes multi-box setups. The power claim survives better than the price claim — 10 x 575W is ~5,750W, so "5000 W" is the right order — but against 4x PRO 6000 at ~2,400W the Mac's ~270W advantage is 9x, not 20x. @MartinSzerment's summary is the fairest reading in the thread: *"Apple didn't win the AI race, it won the electricity bill."*

- **The deepest counterpoint, from @sethprattsf: bandwidth is the cheap part of the gap, and agents buy prefill, not decode.** In a long exchange with @MikeBradleyAI he concedes the physics and then turns it around — 1.2 TB/s is *one* RTX PRO 6000's worth of bandwidth (1.792 TB/s each), the Mac realizes ~40% of peak against the GPU's ~80%, and his measured comparison on a 125B-A6B MoE is **47 t/s on an M3 Ultra vs 172 t/s on 2x RTX 6000**. Prefill is worse and is compute-bound, not bandwidth-bound: **~1K tok/s on the Mac (~60 TFLOPS fp16, no tensor cores) vs 6-10K on Blackwell (~500 fp8 TFLOPS)**, so a 150K-context agent turn takes minutes instead of seconds; and concurrency collapses on a single shared bus — **787 tok/s aggregate across 16 GPU streams vs a realistic 1-2 streams on the Mac**. His conclusion is that M5 Ultra changes the cheap variable and not the expensive ones: "*for agents the model is reading 100x more tokens than it writes*." The thread corroborates it from three directions — @stevenharms bought a 128GB MacBook and finds it slower than his DGX Spark because 90% of his work is large context, @Ender_D2023 estimates a 128K TTFT of ~3 minutes on agent loops, and @wongcken sets the usability bar at 1-2K tok/s prefill. Mitigations exist on both sides of the aisle: prefill cost is separately attackable with [[LMCache offloads paged KV to system RAM and NVMe, cutting 128K-context time-to-first-token from 68 seconds to 1.4 on 4x DGX Spark|KV offloading]], and @MikeBradleyAI's rebuttal — capacity-per-dollar, three $11K Macs running three parallel instances against one $33K Blackwell rig — is the same optionality argument as [[buy 2x 256GB Mac Studios instead of one 512GB - two boxes give 2x 1.2 TB-s parallel instances and can still be linked, but a 512GB box can never be split|two 256GB boxes over one 512GB]].

*The chart is a pure memory-bandwidth leaderboard — its figures check out (5090 and RTX PRO 6000 both 1,792 GB/s, 4090 1,008, 7900 XTX 960, M3 Ultra 819, DGX Spark 273), but it carries no FLOPS, price, or prefill column, which is precisely what @MusingSunRider, @EBdT2, @xiao_zcloak and @Rahim77771 all asked for and what @kethcode noticed it quietly omits (the 3090's 936 GB/s):*
