# Local Inference and Hardware

Running frontier-class models on hardware you own or control: DGX Sparks and Stations, consumer/prosumer GPU builds, quantization and pruning to fit VRAM budgets, throughput-vs-concurrency behavior, and the ownership math that decides buy-vs-rent. The practitioner counterpart to [[moc - Inference]] (serving engines, KV cache, speculative decoding at provider scale).

**The cost ladder so far** — all four notes below run essentially the same DeepSeek-V4-Flash class of model at very different price points, and the deciding variable in every case is duty cycle and concurrency, not peak TPS: ~$4.7K single box → ~2x DGX Spark desk cluster → $100K DGX Station → rented multi-GPU (camelAI, in the Inference folder).

## Compression & Fitting Models

- [[EXL3 3-bit plus an 18.5 percent expert prune runs DeepSeek-V4-Flash 284B at 47 tok-s on 4.7K of hardware]] — @0xSero: stacking EXL3 3-bit quantization with an ~18.5% MoE expert prune fits a 284B model into 128GB VRAM (context + activations included) at 47 tok/s and 400K context. The transferable point is that the two levers are orthogonal — bits-per-weight and expert-count — so composing them lowers the hardware floor faster than pushing either alone (**fact-check 2026-09-01:** the "$4.7K of hardware" is a single DGX Spark — 128 GB unified memory at 273 GB/s — not a GPU build)
- [[sparse experts turn cheap DDR5 into usable inference memory - Jake's MoE offload primer computes 257 GB-s blended for Qwen3.6-35B-A3B vs 122 dense]] — @JakeKAllDay's X Article, 2026-05-26: the clearest beginner explanation of why MoE offload works where dense offload does not. Stored-weight share and active-weight share diverge — Qwen3.6-35B-A3B holds ~32.3B of 35B as experts but activates 8 of 256 routed experts per layer — so under 10% of the model in VRAM can still hold most of the active token path. His weighted-bandwidth formula on an RTX 5070 (672 GB/s) plus dual-channel DDR5-5600 (~90 GB/s) gives 257 GB/s blended for MoE against 122 GB/s dense. Every number is a vendor spec or his own arithmetic; the "within 5% of my M4 Max on a 5070" result lives in a reply thread with no model, quant or engine stated (**dated context:** a 64GB DDR5-5600 kit went $191 → $1,118 in the year after he wrote it, which is the premise, not a detail)

## Caching & Serving

- [[know the difference between the harness, the model, and serving inference - Roy's longpost on why Qwen 27B flies on a 5090 in pi but feels awful in OpenCode on a MacBook or DGX]] — @usr_bin_roygbiv (Roy), 2026-06-07, 694 likes: most "model takes" are harness or serving takes. Harness (Claude Code, Codex, pi, omp, OpenCode…) decides tokens per task — "8x" and "1/4 the context" are his unverified magnitudes, direction is real; serving decides quant, speed and uptime; and the same Qwen 27B "feels awful in opencode on a macbook or dgx but flies at 10x the speed with no tool calls or thinking in pi with nvfp4 and MTP on a 5090." For a home box the durable rules are (1) silicon/bandwidth first, then kernels, config, parallelism — the roofline restated; (2) a lean harness is a hardware multiplier because every extra token is prefill time and KV memory (his corollary elsewhere: "needing over 256K context is a bad harness issue"); (3) "you can use ANY harness with ANY provider" — a local rig only needs an OpenAI-compatible endpoint, and his own pattern is API-primary with the GPUs as failover. The "Claude quantizes at peak hours" line is asserted, not shown; the vendor scorecard is opinion
- [[LMCache offloads paged KV to system RAM and NVMe, cutting 128K-context time-to-first-token from 68 seconds to 1.4 on 4x DGX Spark]] — @0xSero: store paged KV outside the GPU (system memory + NVMe) so long contexts reload instead of recompute. Same prompt/model on 4x DGX Spark, TTFT 68.1s → 1.4s at 128K and 38.3s → 0.69s at 64K; recompute climbs steeply with context while reload stays near-flat. The local answer to the fixed-prefix tax agents pay every turn — the *offload* strategy, complementary to compressing the cache or changing the architecture
